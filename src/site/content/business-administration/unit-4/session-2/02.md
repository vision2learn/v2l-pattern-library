      ### Adapt and support

      When change occurs in business there are several types of support available to help people successfully implement change. This support can be invaluable - if it is not available there can be problems.

    title: Available support during change
    theme: 
    copy: |
      During times of change, businesses can provide support to employees in a variety of ways.  Scroll through the slides below to learn about some of the support that is often available.
    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Clear communications and expectations
          text: |
            One way of supporting people during change is to make sure that they know:
            * What changes are going to be made and what the organisation will be like once the changes have been implemented.
            * How the changes will impact on the job of each person.
            * Why the changes are being made.

            If this information is provided there can be no guarantee that everyone will be contented and things will run smoothly. There is always the possibility that people will not like the proposed changes. However for most people there will be clarity and any problems will be in terms of the actual changes rather than imagined ones.

          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Counselling or coaching
          text: |
            In some organisations it may be necessary to offer more structured professional support to staff. This involves the application of personal coaching or counselling skills from trained professionals who have experience of helping people to make changes. Here specifically designed techniques may be used to help people to work through difficult transitions in their work lives.

            If an organisation thinks carefully about changes, large or small, and seeks to offer appropriate support to staff there is an improved chance that minimising the problems that could arise. This also makes people more receptive and less resistant to further changes made in the future.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Encouragement
          text: |
            A great way to support people going through a change process is to behave in ways that offer encouragement and assistance. Some typical behaviours include:
            * Smiling
            * Making encouraging statements re progress being made
            * Asking if a person needs any help
            * Being upbeat in the presence of others
            * Being willing to listen
            * Sharing experiences of successfully making changes in the past
            * Emphasising the benefits of the change
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Participation
          text: |
            At times it will not be possible or appropriate for staff to take part in the decisions on the changes that will be made and how they will be implemented. There may be times when people will actively resist change. However there may be occasions when it is possible to involve staff and have their participation in the whole change process. This can certainly help staff as they have some control of the process rather than being passive and having no control.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Effective planning
          text: |
            The support for people in an organisation can be planned to come before any actual changes are implemented. If people must deal with ill-considered and poorly planned change then the consequences may be severe. When there is an absence of planning there is a greater likelihood of chaos and conflict.

            One way managers can support staff is to make changes that contribute to the long-term well-being of the organisation and work out the most efficient and effective ways to make those changes. This shows people that they have been considered and every attempt has been made to minimise unnecessary problems.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Training or retraining
          text: |
            Many of the changes in work methods, products or services may require people to work in new ways, operate new equipment and apply new procedures. This can be a problem for people if they do not receive sufficient help in making the transition to the new ways of operating.

            To help staff to make the changes it makes sense to provide high-quality targeted training. This provides staff with knowledge and skills that will aid the transition. With these new skills staff will be more confident that they can cope successfully with the changes, as well as feel reassured that the business is allocating time and resources to support its staff.

            In addition to the formal approaches to training there is likely to be informal training where managers help their staff or co-workers help each other on the job.

    title: Working with others
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      ### Teamwork

      When a business goes through change it can cause employees to feel stressed or anxious.

      There is a danger that these feelings may cause them to become withdrawn. They may be less energetic, less likely to learn new information and less sociable with colleagues. This is harmful to the individual and the organisation.

      But there are different types of support that can help people during change. See below to learn more.
    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Different types of support
          copy: |
            * Effective planning
            * Participation
            * Training or retraining
            * Encouragement or other supportive behaviours
            * Counselling or coaching

            These activities require people to work with others to make the change successful – a big benefit to the business. 
    copy2: Perhaps the most significant benefit of working with others during change is in the area of encouragement.
