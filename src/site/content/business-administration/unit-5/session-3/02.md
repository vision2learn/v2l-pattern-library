      ### Prevention is better than cure

      The best way to deal with problems is to eliminate them from the beginning. If an event is well planned and organised then the scope for problems is significantly reduced. There are some easy ways to reduce potential problems. These include:

          title: Learn from previous events

        -
          title: Have colleagues review any plans

    title: Contingency plans
    layout: media
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      ### What if?
      
      For a sizable event, part of the planning and prevention process will include contingency plans. These plans map out actions to be taken if a problem does arise.

      The contingency plan will have identified the actions that must be taken when a particular problem occurs. If no such plan exists then there can be a substantial time lag between the problem and action being taken. In the absence of such a plan it may not be possible to solve the problem in a reasonable time.  A simple question at the heart of contingency planning is **What if?**

      What if:
      * the audio-visual equipment fails?
      * a speaker does not turn up?
      * a session runs over its allocated time slot?
      * conference packs run out?

    title: Importance and risk
    theme: t-bg-supporting3
    copy: |
      ### Balancing the two
      Although it would be possible for an event organiser to ask ‘what if’ questions about virtually all the activities in an event, it would not be practical to develop contingency plans for everything.

      It may help to think in terms of the following two factors:
      * Importance – which aspects of an event are central to its success and which aspects have a limited impact?
      * Risk – what is the likelihood of a problem developing?

    title: Case study - No Limits Adventure Holidays
    layout: media
    theme: t-bg-primary1a i-case-study
    copy: |
      During one of the talks by a guest speaker it was clear that certain attendees were having difficulty hearing what was being said and were showing signs of frustration. 
      
      A member of the support team quickly identified that attendees having the problem were hard of hearing and the amplifier system for hearing aids wasn’t working correctly. As the support team had received comprehensive training prior to the event on the various equipment to be used and backups for all systems were available, she was able to calmly and confidently re-assure the attendees that she would resolve the issue as quickly as possible. 
      
      She located the amplifier and found that its signal was being blocked by another pieces of equipment and, as soon as it was moved into a clear area, the attendees confirmed that everything was now ok.
