      ### Prevention is better than cure

      The best way to deal with problems is to eliminate them from the beginning. If an event is well planned and organised then the scope for problems is significantly reduced. There are some easy ways to reduce potential problems. These include:

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Learn from previous events
          text: If the business has organised similar events in the past, try and find out what happened. Were there any problems and how could they be avoided next time? In this way the business avoids repeating the same problem.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Get advice from experienced people
          text: There may be people inside (and outside) the business with expertise in organising a specific event. These people will have a very good idea about the pitfalls and where things can go wrong. They will almost certainly have recommendations on how problems may be avoided.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Get advice from other sources
          text: In addition to seeking advice from people it is often helpful to consult appropriate publications, websites and other sources of advice. These are produced by people with expertise and experience and they can help others to get things right the first time.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Have colleagues review any plans
          text: When plans are being developed encourage well informed and thoughtful colleagues to look at them, ask questions and suggest any improvements.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Use proven and tested suppliers
          text: These may be internal suppliers or external suppliers. The more reliable and dependable these people are the less likely there will be problems in their areas of expertise.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Use proven techniques for project planning
          text: There are a wide array of tools and techniques that have been created to help people to plan and implement projects effectively. Using these can help to eliminate potential problems.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg

          text: A disciplined and thorough approach will minimise problems. Having the patience to go through plans and arrangements to check them can often help to iron out problems that would otherwise occur on the day. The checking should extend to any equipment that will be used on the day.

      ### What if?
      
      For a sizable event, part of the planning and prevention process will include contingency plans. These plans map out actions to be taken if a problem does arise.

      The contingency plan will have identified the actions that must be taken when a particular problem occurs. If no such plan exists then there can be a substantial time lag between the problem and action being taken. In the absence of such a plan it may not be possible to solve the problem in a reasonable time.  A simple question at the heart of contingency planning is **What if?**

      What if:
      * the audio-visual equipment fails?
      * a speaker does not turn up?
      * a session runs over its allocated time slot?
      * conference packs run out?

    title: Importance and risk
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      ### Balancing the two
      Although it would be possible for an event organiser to ask ‘what if’ questions about virtually all the activities in an event, it would not be practical to develop contingency plans for everything.

      It may help to think in terms of the following two factors:
      * Importance – which aspects of an event are central to its success and which aspects have a limited impact?
      * Risk – what is the likelihood of a problem developing?

    title: Being flexible and calm
    theme: t-bg-supporting3
    copy: |
      ### Positivity in the face of problems

      A business may have a well-planned event and sensible contingency plans yet still experience problems. In these circumstances all is not lost and a positive response may solve the problem.

      When a problem occurs it helps if support staff follow the steps below:
    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Positive response steps
          copy: |
            * Remain calm
            * Be polite
            * Apologise to attendees when appropriate
            * Define the problem
            * Identify a solution or the colleague that can solve the problem
            * Take appropriate action or undertake tasks specified by the colleague in charge
            * Check that the problem has been solved
            * Ensure the problem is not repeated
          img: /images/svg/placeholder_icon.svg
    copy2: |
      This approach is likely to help solve the problem and it is an outward and visible message to attendees that the event organiser takes the problem and its solution seriously.

      When there are problems and things go wrong it may be frustrating or upsetting for attendees and delegates. However most people will respond in a measured way. If the problem appears ‘obvious’ or is the result of poor planning, and the response to it from staff is lacklustre then people experiencing the problems are likely to feel upset or angry.
