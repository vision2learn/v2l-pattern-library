    title: Recording income and expenditure
    theme: t-bg-supporting2
    copy: |
        An organisation should record accurate and timely information on its income and expenditure; otherwise, decisions will be based on misleading data. This can damage the organisation, possibly resulting in it failing. If actual information is received promptly, estimates and actual performance can be compared and conclusions drawn.

        Select the images below to see what the organisation can do by recording income and expenditure.

    subInc: 
      type: reveal
      content:
        -
          title: Monitor and control income
          copy: |
            If good records are kept, the organisation and the business operations will know the income that is flowing in. The actual figures can then be compared with the estimates. If income records are not kept, monitoring and controlling income is impossible.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Reallocate budgets where necessary
          copy: |
            When an organisation has good records of income and expenditure, the actual and estimated figures can be compared. It is then possible to identify those business operations with a surplus and those that may be short of funds.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Enable auditing to take place
          copy: |
            An audit is an unbiased examination and evaluation of the financial statements of an organisation. The better the quality of the records of income and expenditure, the better the information that is available for the auditors.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Provide openness and accountability over financial matters
          copy: |
            Without good quality records of income and expenditure, it is not possible for an organisation to be open about its financial matters and be accountable for them.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Maintain its reputation for sound financial management
          copy: |
            If an organisation has poor records of income and expenditure – or no records at all – then it is impossible for it to have a good reputation for sound financial management. Without records of this information, the organisation will appear unsound financially and both its financial and general reputation will suffer.

    title: Monitoring budgets
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      Collecting accurate and timely information on income and expenditure allows an organisation to present the estimated and actual performance figures in the budget. 
      
      This enables managers and staff to monitor performance, which can be divided into three parts:

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Measuring actual performance
          text: |
            The first part of monitoring the budgets and performance is to have available the data on actual income and expenditure. Whether the actual figures are good, bad or indifferent, it is important that managers know what they are.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Comparing actual performance with budgeted performance
          text: |
            Once the actual figures have been placed in a budget, comparisons can be made between what was estimated and what has actually happened. This comparison shows if the figures are exactly the same (a rare event), or if there is a difference between the actual and estimated figures. The difference between the two types of figures is called a variance.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Analysing any variances
          text: |
            A key part of the monitoring process is a thoughtful analysis of any variances. An organisation should seek to understand any differences, whatever the size. By assessing why variances have occurred, organisations can identify any actions that need to be taken. They may also discover ways to ensure that estimates are more accurate in the future.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
