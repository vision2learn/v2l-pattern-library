        -
          title: What is a budget
          copy: |
            A budget is an **estimate of income and expenditure** of an organisation over a set period of **time**, presented under **specific headings**.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Estimates for the future
          copy: |
            When the budget is created it focuses on **estimates** for the future. Over time, it becomes possible to **compare** the **estimates** with the **actual levels** of performance. The aim is to make sure that there is no overspending and the targets are achieved.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Management accounting
          copy: |
            Budgeting is a part of **management accounting** and focuses upon the preparation and use of financial plans within an organisation. These plans are a management tool for monitoring and controlling an organisation, or a function / department within an organisation.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Financial tool
          copy: |
            For organisations, budgeting can be a very powerful **financial tool**. Maintaining good financial plans can help an organisation to **control its resources**, rather than be controlled by them. Budgets can be used for short, medium or long term planning.

    title: Why do business organisations use budgets
    theme: t-bg-supporting3
    copy: |
      slider or activity???

      Budgets are essential to an organisation when it comes to managing and controlling financial resources in order to achieve organisational objectives. Scroll through the slides below to learn more about some of the most common uses of budgets.
    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Meeting organisational objectives
          text: |
            Budgets help businesses to **achieve their objectives**. Objectives can include maximising profits, minimising costs, developing new products, satisfying customers and managing risk.

            In all of these cases, the organisational objectives should influence the way budgets are used and the budgets should then help the organisation to achieve those objectives.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Planning and decision making
          text: |
            A budget can provide a **plan** to help the organisation achieve its objectives and **make key decisions** for the future.

            When a budget is used as part of the business planning process, it should provide a fully costed description of what the organisation is going to do over a twelve month period. This information can really help the people in an organisation to **focus** on what it plans to achieve, how it will achieve the **desired outcomes** and the **resources** that are required.

            Budgets can be used to compare planned and actual performance. This **aids decision making** and can highlight areas where **alternative actions** may be required.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Monitoring and controlling finances
          text: |
            A well-formed budget enables people in the organisation to **control costs, income and cash** and to make confident **financial decisions**.

            For example, a budget makes it possible for staff of an organisation to **monitor financial performance**. They can **compare** planned estimates with actual performance (this difference is known as **variance**). When there is a major variance between estimates and actual performance, the organisation will have to explain it and take actions if required. If the variances are small, they are likely to be noted but significant actions would be unlikely.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Co-ordination and communication
          text: |
            The budgeting process can encourage **co-ordination and communication** across an organisation. For example, if budgets are used throughout an organisation (for different functions, departments or units), this process works best when people in the organisation **communicate** with each other and **co-ordinate their efforts** so the budgets are **complimentary**.

            To achieve co-ordination, people will need to speak to each other across different functions and departments. This can really improve organisational communication and sharing of information.

            Similarly, once budgets have been prepared, it is important that details of these budgets are communicated clearly to staff members and others.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Managing resources
          text: |
            One of the benefits of a budget building process is that it can help an organisation to **manage its resources effectively** (for example, identifying resources that may be limited or in need of careful management). This can help the organisation to **operate more efficiently**.
            
            A budgetary process can help to allocate resources in a way that maximises the ability to achieve the best outcomes for the whole organisation and its customers.
