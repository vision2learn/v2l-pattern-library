    title: Negotiating and agreeing a budget
    theme: 
    copy: |
      When drawing up a budget there may be many perspectives and draft estimates, but eventually a final budget must be agreed.

      There are many reasons why organisations will take action to achieve agreement on budgets. The organisation can create a foundation for good management and work practices, whereas the failure to have clear and agreed budgets can be very damaging.
      
      Select the images below below which show the benefits of agreeing budgets.

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Managers fully understand budgetary constraints and organisational plans
          text: |
            The agreed budget will provide precise information on estimated income and expenditure. These figures will define the limits on what may be done, what is expected to happen, and what would not be permissible.

            The specific nature of an agreed budget enables staff to understand better what the organisation has planned for their part of the business and what is expected in the year to come.

            The agreed budget provides a specific measurable framework for communicating key information through the organisation. The agreed budget can be used to monitor and control performance. You will explore this later in the course.
          img: ba_u6_s2_p4_slide_1.svg
        -
          title: They enable managers to explain budgetary decisions to staff
          text: |
            When managers have taken an active part in the budgetary process, and have the final agreed figures, they are well placed to communicate effectively with their members of staff.

            The managers can confidently inform staff of the exact figures that will influence their work over the year; often month by month. This will help staff members to understand precisely what is expected of them in terms of the content of the budget.

            The managers are also able to explain the rationale underpinning the figures. They can explain how the operational activities link with the broader organisational objectives. This helps all staff to understand how their efforts contribute to delivering the aims and objectives of the organisation.
          img: ba_u6_s2_p4_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Managers feel ownership of resourcing decisions
          text: |
            In many organisations, the manager and staff preparing budget estimates will know the aims of the organisation and will have a meaningful role in the estimation process.

            When people participate in this way, they invest their energy and effort into developing the budget. If the staff are listened to respectfully, they will be committed to the budget even if it is not exactly as they expected. This level of participation leads to managers feeling that they ‘own’ the budget decisions.

            When a budget is simply imposed on staff, there is a danger that it will be resented and people will want it to fail, or do little to keep it on track.
          img: ba_u6_s2_p4_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: They encourage motivation and positive working relationships
          text: |
            When the organisation agrees budgets, it can provide the foundation for staff being motivated and colleagues having positive working relationships. Staff may be motivated by having a sense of ownership. They are engaged by their involvement in the process and have invested in delivering the outcomes of the budget.

            Furthermore, managers and staff will know that their performance may be formally or informally evaluated in terms of the delivery of the budget outcomes. This too can motivate staff. The agreed budgets provide performance measures on an annual, or monthly, basis.

            The process of working towards and with agreed budgets can make a contribution to positive working relationships in the workplace.
          img: ba_u6_s2_p4_slide_4.svg

    title: Summary
    theme: t-bg-primary i-summary
    copy: |
      There should be little doubt that the agreed budgets can be very beneficial for the internal running of an organisation and make a significant contribution to the results the organisation may achieve.

      They enable managers to:

      * Understand budgetary constraints and organisational plans
      * Feel ownership of resourcing decisions
      * Explain budgetary decisions to staff.
      
      They also encourage **motivation** and **positive working relationships**.
