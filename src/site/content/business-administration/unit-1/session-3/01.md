      ### Communicating effectively

      The ability to communicate effectively is essential in any business environment.
      Indeed, it is impossible for an organisation to function without communication.
      You can communicate with work colleagues in many different ways, ranging from "off the cuff" brief verbal communications through to formal written documents distributed to employees. The communication can range from one-to-one conversations through to large meetings and presentations.

        -
          title: 1. Focus on a person when communicating
          text: |
            * As far as possible, be in an environment that minimises distractions. If you are discussing something particularly detailed, a quiet office may be needed.
            * Make sure you have enough time to give and receive the relevant information.
            * Look at the speaker and be determined to focus on what they are saying.
            * Do not interrupt. Note any questions and ask them at the appropriate time.
            * If your mind wanders, switch back to the discussion. Maybe ask for a summary if you think you have missed anything.
            * Focus on details.
            Recap to check that you have understood correctly.
          img: ba_u1_s3_p1_slider_1.svg
        -
          title: 2. Prepare information for a presentation
          text: |
            * Make sure you know exactly what information is required.
            * Obtain the information and check it.
            * Find out how the recipient would like the information to be presented.
            * For written information, find out which type / format, etc is preferred. 
            * When preparing a presentation have a clear introduction, develop key themes and conclude with a summary / action plan.

          img: ba_u1_s3_p1_slider_2.svg
        -
          title: 3. Structure and present written information clearly
          text: |
            * Follow the house style for the type of document you are writing (this will help you to see how emails / reports / letters are set out). You could also check filed copies for guidance if they are available.
            * Where appropriate, use headings to indicate what is coming next. The use of sub-headings can break a lengthy document into more manageable chunks. 
            Use paragraphs to separate large sections of text. 
            * The first paragraph should introduce a topic. Subsequent paragraphs develop key points. The final paragraphs should describe actions and summarise the whole topic.
          img: ba_u1_s3_p1_slider_3.svg
        -
          title: 4. Structure and present written information clearly
          text: |
            * Use bullet points for key items that need to stand out. If a lengthy document is to be discussed / referred to, then it may help to number the paragraphs. 
            * If any graphics / charts / diagrams are used, give them a heading / reference number so they link to the text. 
            * In most cases, documents will be dated and the authors named. 
            * Be sure to check your spelling, punctuation and grammar.

      ### Other ways to communicate
      
      A lot of the information we communicate is done non-verbally. When a person is speaking, the words they say are only a part of the message.

      Examples of non-verbal communication include 
      - Posture (sometimes called body language)
      - Gestures
      - Facial expressions
      - Eye contact / movement
      - Voice tone.

    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Non-verbal communication examples
          copy: |
            - When asked if he took a bar of chocolate, a child says “No” but looks away and mumbles his answer
            - A husband says he likes his wife’s new dress, but his voice tone is unenthusiastic
            - A person says she is happy, yet her face looks sad and her voice is flat.
            
            In these examples, you are more likely to notice the non-verbal communication rather than the content of communication. When communicating with people, it helps to think about two simple questions with regard to non-verbal communication - 
            1. Am I doing anything non-verbally that distracts people from my message?
            2. Am I noticing mixed messages between verbal and non-verbal communication? (If anything is noticed you should probe further to clarify the mixed message.)
