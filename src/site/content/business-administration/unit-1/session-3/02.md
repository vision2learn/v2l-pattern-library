      Which method will you use?
      
      With so many communication methods to choose from, the main challenge is to select the most effective option.
      When you are choosing the right communication method, there are some important factors to consider. It's clear that there is not a single communication method that can meet all needs. Instead, you need to think carefully about which one is appropriate. Spoken communication, for example, works better in face-to-face contexts than written communication. If you're not sure, ask a manager or experienced colleague.
      
      To communicate successfully with others, you should
      - Try and understand what the recipient wants / needs to know.
      - Try and understand how they like / need the information to be presented.

    title: Active listening
    theme: 
    copy: |
      ### Show you're focused

      Sometimes, people will 'turn off' when they are being spoken to. For example, they might -
      - Switch focus to what is being said in other people's conversations
      - Get distracted by noises they hear around them
      - Start focusing on their internal thoughts rather than the external information.
      
      When this happens, vital information can be missed. If there is written documentation to support the verbal information, you could check the notes to see what has been missed. However, this is not always possible so it's important to listen carefully to what is being said!

      ### Are you taking it in?

      Generally, you are a good (active) listener if you can accurately repeat back what you have heard...but some people do find this difficult!
      Can you think of a situation when you tried to communicate something important, but the listener was distracted, disinterested or kept interrupting?

      In circumstances like this, the outcomes may include -
      - The message is not communicated
      - You become frustrated / angry with the other person
      - You are less motivated to communicate with that person in the future.
      - All of these things can undermine organisational effectiveness.

          title: Focusing on others when communicating
          copy: |
            Focusing on others when communicating
            Here are some handy tips to help you listen actively to others when they are communicating - 
            - As far as possible, be in an environment that minimises distractions. If you are discussing something particularly detailed, a quiet interruption-free office may be needed
            - Make sure you have enough time to receive the information
            - Look at the speaker and focus on what they are saying.
            - Don’t interrupt. Note any questions and ask them at the appropriate time
            - If your mind wanders, switch back quickly. Ask for a summary if you think you have missed anything
            - Focus on details
            - Recap to check that you have understood correctly.

            Ask questions

            - How does this help?
            - Questioning is part of the listening process.
            - When receiving information, there is always the potential for confusion and misunderstanding. This can happen for a number of reasons 
        - 
          title: Questioning tips
          copy: |
            
            - If you hear anything you don’t understand (a word / statement), ask for further explanation.
            - Double check names / addresses / correct spellings and numbers / dates.
            - If a person assumes you know something – when you do not – explain their error to them.
            - If they speak too quickly / quietly, etc tell them so they can change the way they are speaking.
            - Try and put your questions in a logical order.
            Make written notes to help you remember what you want to ask.
            - Take time to repeat back to the person what you have understood from the discussion. Check with them that you have understood things correctly.
            - Thank the person for answering your questions and making things clear.
            - If the person you are speaking to recognises that you are doing all you can to understand them, they will welcome your questions.

            ### What else do the DSE regulations say?

            In addition to the UK legislation, a European Directive on the subject states that - 

            1. Workers should have eye tests before starting display screen work; at regular intervals after that; if they experience visual difficulties.
            2. Employers should contribute towards the cost of these eye tests.
            3. Employers should regularly evaluate how healthy computer workstations are, on-site or otherwise.
            4. Employers should comply with minimum specified standards relating to screens, keyboards, work desks or space, work chairs, space, lighting, glare, noise, heat, radiation, humidity, ventilation and software.
            5. Workers should have adequate health and safety information about their workstation.
            6. Workers should receive training before starting any display unit work, and should take frequent, short breaks.
            7. There is also legislation that covers working time. Here employees need to control the number of consecutive days and hours people should work and they should ensure that there are adequate rest breaks.
