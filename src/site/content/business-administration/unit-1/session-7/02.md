    layout: media
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      There are many ways of dealing with workplace problems. You can use your knowledge and experience, ask an experienced colleague, or refer problems to an appropriate member of staff. For minor problems you can use your own knowledge and experience to come up with a workable solution. But there are circumstances when it's best to check your solution with an appropriate manager before you take action.
            
      Some problems will be of a size and scale that they must be referred to a senior manager. In this instance, you should inform a senior manager as soon as possible.

      - It is important to decide whether to act on a problem or pass it to someone else.

            Imagine you have a task that must be done by 1.30pm. The finished product must then be transported elsewhere. The task is not quite finished when your lunch time comes round. You could work through the first part of your lunch break to complete the task, then have lunch afterwards.

            Imagine the supplier of an important component cannot deliver it in time for an important order to be completed. You have a potential solution – there is another supplier who can provide what is needed but they are more expensive. You don't have the authority to make such a decision but at the same time you can't opt to do nothing at all. In this instance you should bring it to the attention of an appropriate manager.

The machine needs to be repaired and the whole production line needs to be organised as efficiently as possible. You should inform your manager, as it's their responsibility to resolve this problem.
