    title: Why follow procedure?
    theme: t-bg-supporting2
    copy: |
      ### Having a framework in place

      By having health, safety and security procedures, an organisation has a framework for making sure that the business environment is a healthy, safe and secure place for everyone.

      These procedures should clarify who is responsible for what. Care should also be taken to ensure that people are informed and trained in a way that makes a safer environment more achievable. Scroll through the slides below to learn more.

    subInc:
      type: slider
      title: none
      slides:
        -
          title: Legal
          text: |
            In this Session, you have learned that there are many laws relating to the creation of safe and secure business environments. To comply with the law, an organisation must undertake specific actions and people must follow specified procedures. A failure to follow procedures may mean that the law is being broken. This can result in reprimands, fines, closures and even prison sentences.
          img: ba_u1_s2_p3_slider_1.svg
        -
          title: Moral
          text: |
            An organisation’s health, safety and security procedures are created to ensure that people do not come to any harm. If procedures are not followed, people inside or outside the organisation may suffer in some way. For this reason, employers and employees have a moral obligation to do the right thing and follow procedures. In this way, harm may be minimised.
          img: ba_u1_s2_p3_slider_2.svg
        - 
          title: Organisation rules
          text: |
            
            Each organisation will have procedures for staff to follow. Many of these will be determined by legal requirements. If staff or management fail to follow these procedures, they are likely to be subject to organisational grievance / disciplinary procedures. Ultimately, these can result in people being dismissed. These actions do not rule out further legal action.
          img: ba_u1_s2_p3_slider_3.svg
        -
          title: Economic
          text: |
            Some of the main economic consequences for failing to follow appropriate health, safety and security procedures include:
            - Employment contracts
            - Legislation
            - Sources of information
            - The role of representative bodies supporting employees
            - Equality and diversity

            If a worker in a factory has an accident, the factory may be fined, closed until things are put right and could even lose customers. These are all economic consequences of not following procedures.
          img: ba_u1_s2_p3_slider_4.svg
    copy2: By following procedures correctly, an organisation shows that the wellbeing of staff, customers and others is important. This is a very positive message and it may also have long term economic benefits.

      Security systems are put in place to protect buildings, equipment, staff, personal possessions and information.

            Some of the key reasons for maintaining security and confidentiality are to -

      ### The specific workplace policies covering your job role. 
      
      Scroll through the slides below to learn more about workplace polices.
    subInc:
      type: accordion
      content:
        -
          title: What are workplace policies?
          copy: |
            This covers the documentation prepared by the employer on the procedures to be followed regarding health and safety matters. It could be the employer's safety policy statement, or general health and safety statements and written safety procedures covering aspects of the workplace that should be drawn to the employees' (and "other persons") attention. 

            Now you should take time to discover what the policies say in terms of your job role. What policies and procedures apply to your day-to-day activities?

          img: /images/svg/ba_u1_s2_p3_slide_1_hns.svg
        -
          title: Suppliers and manufacturers’ instructions
          copy: |
            When using equipment, materials or products to do your work it is advisable to check any advice or instructions offered by the suppliers or manufacturers as they have a detailed knowledge of the items you are using. Their advice on its safe use should be adhered to. 
            These instructions can be anything from the advice on using a detergent – found on the back of the container – through to very detailed instruction manuals. 

            It is important that you apply safe working practices in your job role. A failure to do so increases risks to you, colleagues and third parties. 

            Many organisations have procedures, manuals and formal training to ensure people apply safe work practices. In others they are ‘taught on the job’. Sadly, some employers do not bother at all. 
            Remember, by failing to do something that is reasonable, you could jeopardise the health and safety of other people and yourself!

          img: /images/svg/ba_u1_s2_p3_slide_2_instructions.svg
        - 
          title: Your scope and responsibility for rectifying risks
          copy: |
            
            All organisations should have workplace procedures for handling risks. They should tell you what to do if you lack the skills to reduce to the risk, timescale for reporting risks and who should be contacted if you are unable to deal with the risk.   

            It is important to know your scope and responsibility for rectifying risks. Sometimes this information will be formally recorded in documents. At other times this information may be provided verbally. 

            If you have a responsibility to rectify a risk and the skills to do so, you can correct the problem but you may still wish to report it later so the issue is recorded. 

            Sometimes a person has a responsibility to do something but not the skills to do so. In this situation you should proceed with caution. Ideally get some training before the risk arises so you can deal with it. However, if you lack the skills it is advisable to speak to a line manager rather than trying to rectify it. 

          img: /images/svg/ba_u1_s2_p3_slide_3_risks.svg
        -
          title: Personal presentation
          copy: |
            You should not wear clothing or items of jewellery that may cause a health and safety problem. Likewise, you should wear protective clothing – when required – that reduces the risks of a health and safety problem.  Take a look below to see some examples -

            **Personal presentation** - Clara works in the packaging department of a manufacturing company. She likes to wear dozens of loose bangles and bracelets. For her job this is problematic as the bangles could easily snag the sealing machine. 

            **Protective clothing** - Gary's job requires him to do small amounts of soldering each day. Gary cannot be bothered to put on goggles for a 30 second job. 

            Both cases may have an increased chance of being hurt. In every person's job it is important that their personal presentation maximises their health and safety. 

          img: /images/svg/ba_u1_s2_p3_slide_4_protection.svg
        -
          title: Personal conduct
          copy: |
            One of the most significant influences on risks in the workplace is the behaviour of staff. How people conduct themselves can have a significant impact on their own health and safety and that of others. Take a look at the example below - 

            One thing that has a significant influence on personal conduct is alcohol. When people have been drinking, they often tend to have impaired perceptions, are prone to being more reckless and have poorer co-ordination. All the symptoms can impair performance and cause people to behave dangerously. This is why most businesses have very strict rules banning drink in the workplace.

          img: /images/svg/ba_u1_s2_p3_slide_5_conduct.svg

      ### Keeping things confidential
      
      In most cases, there are many simple things that all employees can do during their daily work.

      Select below to see some examples before you move on.

    subInc:
      type: accordion
      content:
        -
          title:  Improving security and confidentiality
          copy: |
            To improve security and confidentiality in the workplace, you should -

            - Not allow computer screens to be seen by unauthorised people
            - Ensure people can't see confidential documents that aren't meant for them
            - Log off your computer if it is unattended
            - Use computer passwords that are not easy to guess
            - Double check outgoing emails before you send them
            - Never gossip or share confidential information
            - Err on the side of caution when disclosing information
            - Keep sensitive documents in folders and lockable drawers
            - Comply with your organisation’s security procedures
            - Report any lapses in security
            - Close windows before leaving empty offices/rooms
            - Log off computers that have been left on by colleagues
            - Close windows before leaving empty offices/rooms
            - Lock away valuables/confidential papers
            - Ask colleagues to be more careful if sensitive information is being discussed with inappropriate people.
