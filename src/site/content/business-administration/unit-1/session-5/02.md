            ### What are your priorities?

            When deciding what to do first it helps to prioritise your work.

            To do this, it helps to think about two factors
            - Urgency
            - Importance
            
            The diagram on the right shows how you can use these factors to prioritise tasks.
            
            When you know which tasks will be given priority, you can break them down into sub-tasks and decide how long each one will take and the resources required.

            Each individual can also look at their priorities – if they have not already been set.

        title: Prepare for the unexpected
        theme: 
        copy: |
            ### Factor in extra time

            When you are planning the time you have to spend on your tasks, be aware that unexpected things can happen to hold you up.
            For example - 
            - A machine failure
            - A fire drill
            Time spent talking to a colleague about another work related issue / problem.

            Try to build a little 'slack' into your plans so that minor changes can be handled without anyone else being disrupted. And, if there are no disruptions, the job will get done a little earlier and few people will complain about that outcome.
