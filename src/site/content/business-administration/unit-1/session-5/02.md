            Now let's take a look at some practical steps you can take when planning work for a team / group.
            In order to plan the work of a team / group, a systematic approach is often best.
            Select the icons below to learn more about three key steps to take when planning teamwork.
            
            ### REVEALS
            
            Step 1  Know your objectives
            Make sure everyone knows what the objectives are. In particular, discuss the outcomes that have to be achieved in given timescales.

            BA_U1_S5_p2_Reveal_1_ToDo.svg

            Step 2  Identify actions / timings
            Work backwards from the final objectives and identify the actions / timings of what has to be done in order to achieve the objectives. Identify the high priority tasks first.

            BA_U1_S5_p2_Reveal_2_Timing.svg

            Step 3  Allocate responsibilities
            Allocate responsibilities to team / group members for carrying out the actions in a timely manner. Ensure employees have the appropriate skills and know what is expected of them.

            BA_U1_S5_p2_Reveal_3_Team.svg

            Creating a plan will help you to identify your high priority tasks / critical activities. You will also be able to look at each part of the plan and check whether you have the resources to make it work. The next step is to prioritise your work and schedule your tasks.

            ### What are your priorities?

            When deciding what to do first it helps to prioritise your work.

            To do this, it helps to think about two factors
            - Urgency
            - Importance
            
            The diagram on the right shows how you can use these factors to prioritise tasks.
            
            When you know which tasks will be given priority, you can break them down into sub-tasks and decide how long each one will take and the resources required.

            Each individual can also look at their priorities – if they have not already been set.

        theme: 
        copy: |
            ### Factor in extra time

            When you are planning the time you have to spend on your tasks, be aware that unexpected things can happen to hold you up.
            For example - 
            - A machine failure
            - A fire drill
            Time spent talking to a colleague about another work related issue / problem.

            Try to build a little 'slack' into your plans so that minor changes can be handled without anyone else being disrupted. And, if there are no disruptions, the job will get done a little earlier and few people will complain about that outcome.
