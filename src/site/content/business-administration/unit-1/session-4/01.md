    copy2: | 
      ### Example
      
      Imagine that 'Employee 3' has to deliver a document to a client by a specified time. If 'Employee 2' or 'Employee 1' fail to deliver work to an appropriate standard, then it may be impossible for 'Employee 3' to do his / her work properly and deliver the document to the client on time.

      If you are unclear on your work standards, you should speak to an appropriate manager and find out what they are.

      ### The challenge of change

      It is unlikely that a business will continue to do exactly the same thing for years and years.
    
      Instead, businesses need to change on a regular basis.

    subInc:

        -
          title: Pressures for change
          copy: |
            There are many reasons for an organisation to change. Examples include 
        
            - The changing requirements of their customers
            - The introduction of new technologies / working practices
            - Social and legal changes that influence the organisation
            - New colleagues / managers entering the work environment.
            - In the above cases, and many others, an organisation will face challenges and reasons for change.
          
        -
          title: Different types of changes
          copy: |
            Sometimes, changes are sudden while at other times they can be introduced slowly over time. In either case, the people involved are faced with many challenges and changes.
        
            These challenges and changes may not always be sweeping in their nature. It could be something quite simple such as answering the telephone in an office during the lunch break or learning to use a new dishwasher.
            
            While these may appear to be trivial examples, it is clear that if these jobs were not to be done, then the organisation or business would be less effective.

        My employer says I seem very capable of adapting to change and wishes more people would be more like me! Should I take this as a compliment?:
          answers:
            Yes: true
            No: false
          feedback: As an employee, if you're willing to take on new challenges you are potentially more valuable to the organisation than someone who resists change. You're likely to enjoy the inevitable challenges rather than be upset by them.
        So how do I prepare myself for change?:
          answers:
            By asking questions such as "How can I best approach the challenge?" and "How can I adapt successfully?" you are likely to cope more effectively.: true
            Don't face up to challenges or try and hide away so the problem is not solved.: false
          feedback: Those people who welcome change are likely to get more opportunities for variation in their work. If a person wishes to learn then they will need to face up to new information and new situations. If a person will not change and adapt then there is little scope for learning.
        What happens if I resist change?:
          answers:
            Employees who resist or deny changes will find themselves at odds with colleagues and managers. : True
            Their skills and attitudes may become redundant. This may result in them no longer fitting in with the business. : True
            My employer will understand my resistance and allow me to continue working in the ways I am comfortable with. : False
          feedback: For those people wanting to advance within an organisation there must be a willingness to take on new challenges and adapt. A new job or a role with added responsibility cannot be done if a person resists changes and challenges.
