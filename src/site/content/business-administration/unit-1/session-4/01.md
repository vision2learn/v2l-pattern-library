      ### Work and behaviour
            
      You can set work standards in many different ways.
            
      For example, standards can be set using SMART (specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time objectives where specific outcomes are agreed between staff and managers.

      ### Commonly used work standards

      Some examples of commonly used work standards include
      
      - Quality standards – for assessing whether a product or service is fit for purpose
      - Wastage standards – specifying what levels of waste are acceptable
      - Behavioural standards – acceptable behaviours in the work environment (this can also include time keeping or appearance requirements, etc).

    copy: |
      ### Challenges and changes in the workplace

      Please see the following questions on the subject of change in the workplace. 

      Question 1 - My employer says I seem very capable of adapting to change and wishes more people would be more like me! Should I take this as a compliment?  
      Answer - Yes
      
      Feedback - As an employee, if you're willing to take on new challenges you are potentially more valuable to the organisation than someone who resists change. You're likely to enjoy the inevitable challenges rather than be upset by them.


      Question 2 - So how do I prepare myself for change?  
      Answer By asking questions such as "How can I best approach the challenge?" "How can I adapt successfully?"

      Feedback - By asking questions such as "How can I best approach the challenge?" "How can I adapt successfully?" you are likely to cope more effectively. When people don't face up to challenges or try and hide away, the problem is not solved.
      Those people who welcome change are likely to get more opportunities for variation in their work. If a person wishes to learn then they will need to face up to new information and new situations. If a person will not change and adapt then there is little scope for learning.

      Question 3 - What happens if I resist change?  
      Answer - You will will find yourself at odds with colleagues and managers
      
      Feedback - Employees who resist or deny changes will find themselves at odds with colleagues and managers. Their skills and attitudes may become redundant. This may result in them no longer fitting in with the business. In the short term, this form of resistance can be painful and cause anxiety. In the longer term it can result in people losing their jobs.

      For those people wanting to advance within an organisation there must be a willingness to take on new challenges and adapt. A new job or a role with added responsibility cannot be done if a person resists changes and challenges.

      ### Respecting colleagues
            
      At times it is easy to respect the views and actions of people who agree with you. For good quality, productive relationships, the challenge is to respect views and actions in others when they differ from your own.
              
      It is important here to appreciate that you are not being encouraged to respect unethical views / actions or those that you are strongly opposed to. However, there are many times when people will just have a different perspective.
              
      By respecting other perspectives, you encourage an atmosphere of trust, mutual respect and tolerance. This leads to openness and a more productive environment.

      Showing consideration involves listening to different views, not ignoring these views and looking for the positive.

      It is common to see people who are inconsiderate of others. However, making the effort to be considerate can be very beneficial in an organisation.
