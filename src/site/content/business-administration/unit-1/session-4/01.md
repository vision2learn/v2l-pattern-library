      ### Work and behaviour

      You can set work standards in many different ways.
      For example, standards can be set using SMART (specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time) objectives where specific outcomes are agreed between staff and managers.

      ### Commonly used work standards

      Some examples of commonly used work standards include

      - Quality standards – for assessing whether a product or service is fit for purpose
      - Wastage standards – specifying what levels of waste are acceptable
      - Behavioural standards – acceptable behaviours in the work environment (this can also include time keeping or appearance requirements, etc).

      ### More on work standards

      You can click on the icons below to learn more about work standards.

      Standards of work for the individual employee
      If an employee doesn't have a standard of work, or if the standards are very vague then it is difficult for them to know what they should be doing and when they should be doing it. The absence of standards makes it impossible for an employee to effectively monitor his / her own performance.

      #img: BA_U1_S4_p1_Reveal1_Individual.svg

      Standards of work for the employee and manager
      If work standards are set and known by managers and employees, it is possible to monitor progress and performance. Each party will know exactly what outputs are expected. If precise work standards don't exist, then things are open to personal interpretation and this can lead to conflicting viewpoints.

      #img: BA_U1_S4_p1_Reveal2_Manager.svg

      Standards and working with others
      In a business organisation, an employee does not operate in isolation. Employees will receive inputs from colleagues and provide outputs to colleague. The colleagues depend on each other. If some colleagues work to lower standards, it may adversely affect the work of others.

      #img: BA_U1_S4_p1_Reveal3_Team.svg

      Example  Imagine that 'Employee 3' has to deliver a document to a client by a specified time. If 'Employee 2' or 'Employee 1' fail to deliver work to an appropriate standard, then it may be impossible for 'Employee 3' to do his / her work properly and deliver the document to the client on time.

      ### Challenges and changes in the workplace

      Please see the following questions on the subject of change in the workplace. 

      Question 1
      Q
      My employer says I seem very capable of adapting to change and wishes more people would be more like me! Should I take this as a compliment?
      A
      Yes - As an employee, if you're willing to take on new challenges you are potentially more valuable to the organisation than someone who resists change. You're likely to enjoy the inevitable challenges rather than be upset by them.

      Q2 So how do I prepare myself for change?
      A
      By asking questions such as "How can I best approach the challenge?" "How can I adapt successfully?" you are likely to cope more effectively. When people don't face up to challenges or try and hide away, the problem is not solved.

      Those people who welcome change are likely to get more opportunities for variation in their work. If a person wishes to learn then they will need to face up to new information and new situations. If a person will not change and adapt then there is little scope for learning.

      Q3 What happens if I resist change?
      A
      Employees who resist or deny changes will find themselves at odds with colleagues and managers. Their skills and attitudes may become redundant. This may result in them no longer fitting in with the business. In the short term, this form of resistance can be painful and cause anxiety. In the longer term it can result in people losing their jobs.

      For those people wanting to advance within an organisation there must be a willingness to take on new challenges and adapt. A new job or a role with added responsibility cannot be done if a person resists changes and challenges.

      ### Respecting colleagues

      The way you behave in the workplace is very important.

      For example, think about the following saying "Behaviour breeds behaviour."

      If a person is unfriendly and unhelpful towards you, how would you behave towards them? The chances are that you would behave in a similar way. On the other hand, if a person is helpful and supportive, you are far more likely to make an effort to help them.

            text: |
              Being honest involves telling the truth (even when it is tough to do so) and keeping your word.

              When you are honest with people, they know that they can trust you. They will not assume that you have an agenda or that you will stab people in the back. People are happier to share information with honest colleagues and to help them if it is needed.
      
              There may be times when something is confidential and it cannot be shared with others. This can cause conflict between being honest and not disclosing something confidential. In these situations, you should be upfront and acknowledge that the information cannot be disclosed and tell people you're not trying to keep them out of the loop on purpose.

            title: Show respect for others
            text: |
              Being respectful towards others involves recognising differences, appreciating them and tolerating them.
            
              At times it is easy to respect the views and actions of people who agree with you. For good quality, productive relationships, the challenge is to respect views and actions in others when they differ from your own.
              
              It is important here to appreciate that you are not being encouraged to respect unethical views / actions or those that you are strongly opposed to. However, there are many times when people will just have a different perspective.
              
              By respecting other perspectives, you encourage an atmosphere of trust, mutual respect and tolerance. This leads to openness and a more productive environment.

            img: BA_U1_S4_p1_Slider2_Respect.svg
          -
            title: Show consideration for others
            text: |
              Showing consideration involves listening to different views, not ignoring these views and looking for the positive.
      
              It is common to see people who are inconsiderate of others. However, making the effort to be considerate can be very beneficial in an organisation.
      
              This does not mean that you always have to agree with another person's viewpoint. However, the willingness to allow other people to express their views and to listen to them respectfully is a very smart way to behave.
      
              By being considerate of others, you encourage them to be considerate when you speak. People will be more inclined to let you have your say.
        
              Being considerate also encourages people to express their views. This can sometimes lead to new ideas and approaches being uncovered which benefit everybody.
