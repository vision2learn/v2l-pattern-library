<section class="t-bg-primary">
  <h2>Why have laws?</h2>

  <p>
    Laws are created in business to protect employers, employees, customers and third parties.
  </p>
  <p>
    If laws didn’t exist, people would be unprotected and things may get out of control. There would be no clear way to resolve differences or difficulties.
  </p>
  <p>
    Laws help businesses to have rules of conduct that apply to all relevant parties. Other methods can also be used to resolve differences of opinion or behaviour that break these rules – such as courts / tribunals.
  </p>
  <p>
    Courts use an objective, evidence-based approach to solve problems to do with breaking the law, rather than using force or power to resolve an issue.
  </p>
</section>

  <div class="l-media__body">
    <h3>Support for staff</h3>

  <p>
    One of the ways that employees can receive help and support in the workplace is through representative bodies.
  </p>
  <p>
    The best known examples of representative bodies are trade unions.
  </p>
  <p>
    Non-trade union representation may also be found in certain organisations.
  </p>
  <p>
    According to ACAS, there are 50% trade union and 50% non-trade union representatives in the UK.
  </p>
  <p>
    There are also other sources of support, including staff associations and trade associations.
  </p>
