    theme: t-bg-supporting2
    copy: |
      ### Why minimising is important
      Most businesses seek to keep waste to a minimum. This is because too much waste can have consequences for the long-term viability of the business.

      These include too much money being spent on dealing with waste and a wasteful culture among employees.

      See below to learn more
    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Definitions of waste
          copy: |
            Waste in business can be said to be:
            1. Resources used up by inefficient processes or non-essential activities.
            2. Unwanted materials left over from production of a product.
            3. A product that does not meet usability tests.
            4. A process or material that does not (from the viewpoint of the customer) add value to a product or service.
            5. Materials that cause damage to the environment.

    title: Identifying waste
    theme: t-bg-supporting2
    copy: |
      ### Root out the causes
      Reducing waste in business is an important objective.

      Businesses that reduce waste have the opportunity to:
      * Be more efficient
      * Be more able to satisfy customers
      * Be more likely to have a long-term future.
      The challenge is to be able to identify the causes of waste and then minimise them.

      The type of waste produced by businesses varies depending on a number of factors. See below to see examples of these.
    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:

          title: Factors that affect waste in business
          copy: |
            * Type of industry
            * Type of department
            * Size of organisation/business
            * Management culture
            * The mix of staff, such as age, gender, culture and value
            * Market conditions
            * Use of waste reduction approaches.
