      ###  What are customers?

      A customer can be defined as:

      _Any person who has the reasonable expectation that you will provide him or her with a product or service._

      Sometimes customers are known as clients, guests (especially in hotels) or patients (for example in hospitals and clinics).

      When thinking about customers who you deal with at work, it helps to group them into two categories:
      * Internal customers
      * External customers

    title: Customer types - Internal and External
    layout: media
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      It helps to think about **internal** customers as:

      _Individuals and teams within the business you work in who receive the outputs of your work._
      
      **External** customers can be thought about as:

      _Individuals outside the business you work in who receive the product or service that the business produces._

      There are also internal and external **suppliers** fulfilling demands from customers:
      * Internal suppliers are those working for the same employer as yourself. 
      * External suppliers are other businesses, organisations or companies that supply you or the business you work for.

The marketer is the internal customer and the designer and copywriter are the internal suppliers.

      Now imagine you're in an administrative role and you're at work. 
      1. You word process a report and print a copy. You give this to your colleague, Mark.
      2. Mark makes five copies of the report and binds them, then returns them to you.
      3. You deliver the reports to the heads of the marketing, customer service and technical departments, and send two copies to a customer.

      Now have a think about who are the internal and external customers in the process.
