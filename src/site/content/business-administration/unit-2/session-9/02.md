    subInc:

      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Non verbal communication
          text: When the visitor arrives, acknowledge them, make eye contact and smile. This can be done even if you're dealing with someone else face to face or on the phone. This non verbal communication sends an important message to the visitor.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Greeting
          text: |
            As soon as possible verbally greet the visitor. Some businesses will have a specified greeting. If this does not exist then ‘Hello’ / ‘Good morning’ / ‘Good afternoon’ followed by asking the visitor ‘ how can I help’ will be a very good way to start.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Known visitor
          text: If the visitor is a regular one, it may be appropriate to greet him / her by name. (Mr / Mrs / Ms followed by surname)
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: New visitor
          text: If the visitor is new then politely try and get his / her name as soon as possible.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Visitors' log
          text: If the business has a visitors' log for signing in and out tell the visitor what information is required and where the information should be written.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Appointment
          text: If the visitor has an appointment then the appropriate colleague(s) should be informed that the visitor has arrived.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Short wait
          text: If the visitor has to wait a very short time then conversation about neutral topics may be appropriate (e.g. the weather / the visitor's journey to the organisation).
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Long wait
          text: |
            If the visitor has a long wait it may be appropriate to point out relevant facilities such as reading materials or access to refreshments and toilet facilities.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Maintain contact
          text: Maintain contact with the visitor while he / she waits. Do not let the visitor feel neglected.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg

          title: Appearance

            * Clean

            * Clean clothes (style appropriate for the business)
            * Clean shoes
            * Avoid over-powering aftershave/perfume
        - 
          title: Non-verbal cues
          copy: |
            * Smiling
            * Making eye contact
            * Looking attentive when a visitor is speaking
        - 
          title: Communication
          copy: |
            * Speak clearly
            * Speak at a sensible speed (not too quick or too slowly)
            * Ask questions relevant to the reception role
            * Listen carefully to answers
            * Keep the visitor informed
        - 
          title: Behaviour
          copy: |
            * Polite
            * Friendly
            * Attentive
        -
          title: Professionalism
          copy: |
            Deal with visitors promptly  
            Efficiently work through reception processes such as:
            * Get visitor details
            * Completing paperwork
            * Issuing badges
            * Signing people in and out
            * Effectively use technology such as telephone and computer systems
    copy2: |
      The receptionist role can be very demanding. There can be many people wanting to be dealt with at the same time, and some may feel they deserve to be seen sooner than others.

      The trick for a receptionist is to project a positive image and deal calmly with demands of visitors in order to minimise potential conflicts.

      Some businesses use a checklist to help receptionists check whether they are presenting a positive image. This is a quick and effective way of getting employees to project the right image to visitors.

      * Smile
      * Look pleasant and cheerful
      * Dress appropriately smart for the organisation
      * Be ‘well scrubbed’ and have good personal hygiene
      * Always be polite
      * Always treat all visitors with respect
      * Be willing to make polite conversation if the visitor has to wait
      * Keep visitors informed
      * Never be over familiar with visitors
      * Never make critical comments about the organisation or colleagues
