    theme: t-bg-supporting3

      ### Keep people in the loop

      Some meetings will require information and support.  The types of information and support you may be asked to provide will depend on the content of that meeting. Often, it will be to reinforce or confirm information that has been issued with the original agenda and documents.

      In many meetings, it helps to have spare copies (or quick access to additional copies) in case some people lose or forget their paperwork. There may be other activities, such as providing refreshments or making sure everyone is ready to start at the right time. The aim is to have a friendly and efficient approach.

      One of the most common support activities will be to make an accurate record of the meeting. We'll cover this in greater detail later on.

      See below to see some examples of other information and support activities

      ###  A how-to guide
      If you're responsible for organising meetings, you will have a significant influence on the meeting's success and failure.

      Get it wrong and you might expect the following results:
      * Wasted time and resources
      * Frustration
      * Conflict
      * Poor decisions or no decisions at all.
      But if you get it right, the meeting can contribute to a more efficient and effective business.

      The specific role of the person responsible for organising and supporting a meeting may vary from organisation to organisation, department to department or even meeting to meeting.

            ### Steps 1 - 10
            1. Find out about and confirm the meeting brief.
            2. Confirm the venue, equipment and catering requirements.
            3. Produce the agenda and meeting papers.

      ###  What are the outcomes?

      Meeting outcomes are important for a business.  In order to ensure the outcomes are as clear as possible, it is very important that discussions in meetings, and any agreed actions, are recorded accurately.

      This type of record is known as the **minutes** of a meeting.

      Minutes are usually taken by a designated person, such as a secretary or assistant.  They are then typed up and passed onto a manager or director for approval, before being made available to the people who attended the meeting.
