        ###  A wide choice

        There is a wide choice of telephone systems for businesses.

        The type of system your employer has will depend on the following things:
        * Business size
        * Number of employees
        * Number of employees who need to use the telephone often
        * Number of employees who don't need to use the telephone as often
        * The amount of business done over the telephone
        * Whether it needs to have phone and computer systems integrated with one another
        * The features needed on the system.

          title: Scenario
          text: |
            Your boss has been waiting for an important call all day about a big contract. They go out for some lunch, leaving you and your colleague to answer the phones. The phone rings and your colleague answers it. The conversation goes something like this:

            Colleague:	"Hello, ABC Businesses."
            Caller:     "Hi can I speak to Doug please?"
            Colleague:  "Sorry, he's just nipped out for lunch and I don't know when he'll be back. Bye."
            
            The consequences of not identifying the caller meant that Doug didn't get the contract, which meant the business lost out on hundreds of thousands of pounds.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg

          title: Identifying the caller
          text: |
            When you answer the phone, it helps to do the following:

            * Have a pen/pencil and paper to hand when you answer the phone.
            * Be ready to make notes immediately.
            * If the caller says his/her name, make a note of it in writing that can be read by others.
            * If they don't say their name, ask who is calling
            * For unusual names, ask how to spell them. Some letters sound the same on the phone, so check if there is any doubt.
            * If the caller is from another business, make a note of the business name.
            
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: The caller's needs
          text: |
            Employees often take calls and transfer them to the appropriate extensions. But it's still important to find out why the caller wants to speak to a particular person, as by knowing what the caller wants, you can:

            * See if you can deal with the issue or whether you need to pass it on.
            * Connect him/her to the best person who can help them.
            * Brief the recipient of the call about why the caller wants to speak to them.
            * Make an informative note if the recipient is currently unavailable.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Confidential environments
          text: |
            Most callers are happy to say what they want. In more confidential environments people may be reluctant to give a precise reason for calling. Here good judgement should be applied. It is possible that people are calling for confidential reasons.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Give the best service
          text: |
            Once a caller has explained what they want, the employee must give the best response possible. Sometimes though, callers will not need to speak to anyone, they will just be selling something. Some typical responses might be:

            * Politely decline cold callers.
            * Politely decline people who want inappropriate or confidential information.
            * Provide approved and scripted information to cold callers
            * Make a note of the caller's needs and ensure the colleague knows who is calling and why they are calling.
