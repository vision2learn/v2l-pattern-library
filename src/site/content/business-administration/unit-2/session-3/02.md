      ###  Figuring out how things work

      Office equipment is usually supplied with instructions. These provide guidance on the setup and functions of a piece of equipment.

      It's best practice to follow manufacturers' instructions in order to fully understand the equipment and get the most out of it.

      Not following manufacturers' instructions can result in damage being caused.  Some of the most important reasons for following manufacturers' instructions are:

      * Health and safety
      * Efficiency
      * Maintenance
      * Manufacturers' guarantees.

            It is also likely that materials may be wasted. All these factors are examples of inefficiencies that ultimately provide a poorer service / product to customers. The manufacturers’ instructions will describe the most efficient way to use the equipment.

            It would add to the costs of the business organisation to put these things right. The manufacturers’ instructions will describe the way to use the equipment if guarantees are to be honoured.

      ###  Think about your colleagues

      You must take into consideration other users of equipment.

      In lots of businesses, people work in communal areas and share equipment. Sometimes, users leave the equipment in a poor state, which affects the next user.

      When things are not kept clean, tidy and ready to use by other people, this can result in employees adopting a sloppy attitude.

      It can also mean that productivity is affected, as time must be spent on solving problems such as messy areas and equipment that does not function correctly.

      And if the same people are left to clean up after their colleagues, this can result in them becoming demotivated.

    title: Activity - Leaving equipment in good working order
    theme: 
    
    copy: |
      In lots of business environments people are working in communal areas and either occasionally or frequently share equipment. In some workplaces the user of a piece of equipment will use it and then leave it in a ‘poor state’. This results in the next user being delayed and it shows a lack of respect for other work colleagues.

      As a rule of thumb people should leave things as they would hope to find them when wanting to do their job efficiently. Some organisations go so far as to produce official standards/codes of behaviour.

      When things are not kept clean, hygienic and ready for use, it also creates a ‘work culture’ of being sloppy and not caring about the needs of work colleagues. There can be a danger that even diligent people get tired of cleaning up after less motivated people and a work environment deteriorates.

      Conversely when everyone is diligent and respectful of colleagues the work environment is a nicer place to be for everyone.
