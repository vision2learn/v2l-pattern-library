        ### Producing documents from notes

        When documents are created they will often be based on a collection of notes. Notes tend not to be written in well-structured, composed sentences. Instead they tend to be:
        * Key points of information
        * Key dates/times
        * Key names.

        ### What are the benefits?

        The great advantage of having formats is that it provides an excellent structure for note-taking.

        The key headings are already established and they will indicate the information that needs to be noted for later. This helps the note taker to focus on what information needs to be recorded.

        When preparing documents it is helpful to make some notes before creating a document. The author needs to present the information effectively within the appropriate document.

        ### Simple steps

        Once notes have been taken, the author is ready to produce the required document. There are some simple steps to follow if the best document possible is to be produced:

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: What is the purpose of note-taking?
          text: This reminds the author why a document is being produced and the reason why notes have been taken. It provides a focus for note-taking activities.
          img: ba_u3_s4_p4_slide_1.svg
        -
          title: What is the format of the document being produced?
          text: |
            When the format is specified this provides a structure for the written document. It will enable the author to identify which notes will be used for which purposes.
          img: ba_u3_s4_p4_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Are the notes clear and easy to understand?
          text: Most notes are abbreviated forms of writing. It's important that the author can comprehend what has been noted. This may be particularly so when the notes are hand-written, if the hand writing is illegible then the notes are meaningless.
          img: ba_u3_s4_p4_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Is the required amount of detail available?
          text: |
            If only some of the required details are found in the notes then there is every danger that the final document will be inadequate and deficient. 
            
            Sometimes the person writing up a document will not know everything that is required. This will lead to him/her assuming the notes have 100% of the details. They could be wrong. When the details are put into the document is the required amount provided?
          img: ba_u3_s4_p4_slide_4.svg
        -
          title: Have the notes been checked for accuracy?
          text: |
            If the notes can be understood then they ought to be checked for accuracy. In an earlier section the importance of making sure those details in the notes are correctly transferred to the report. 
            
            In this case it is important that details from external sources (such as people's comments and book references) are accurately noted. Is a high level of accuracy maintained when the information is put into the document?
          img: ba_u3_s4_p4_slide_5.svg
        -
          title: Is it known where the final copy will be stored?
          text: |
            Where will the final copy of the document - and the initial notes - be stored? Will it be backed up with an electronic copy?
          img: ba_u3_s4_p4_slide_6.svg
    copy2: |
        These questions can be used to form the following:
        ### A simple procedure

        1. Establish the purpose for the note-taking.
        2. Establish the format for the document.
        3. Check the notes for clarity and make amendments if necessary.
        4. Check that the detail in the notes is sufficient for producing the document.
        5. Check the facts.
        6. Produce the document.
        7. Check the final document.
