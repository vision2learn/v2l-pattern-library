      ### Why check documents?

      In most businesses a finished document will be checked in some way for accuracy and correctness before it is given to the customer. This is usually done to ensure the document is fit-for-purpose.

      When the checking phase is not completed, then mistakes and incorrect or insufficient information may make their way in to the final document. This could result in inefficiencies and dissatisfied customers.

    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: 
    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Author or someone else?
          copy: |
            The first person to check the document is likely to be the author. They should take the time to ensure the document meets the requirements of the recipient. However, it's often best to have someone else to look over the document as they won't have been working with it as closely as the author.
          img: /images/svg/placeholder_icon.svg

        - 
          title: Spelling, grammar and technical terms...
          copy: |
            On all word processing systems there are spell checking and grammar checking facilities, which can be used to check the accuracy of what has been written. 
            
            However, these spell checkers don't pick up everything and it's best to use them in conjunction with reading a printed version of the document. For example, a spell checker won't recognise 'tow' as incorrect if the author meant to write 'two', as both are words.

            Technical terms should be checked thoroughly and independently if there is any doubt.
