The philosophy of lean management and manufacturing is a generic set of ideas that have been adapted to fit different kinds of manufacturing and business processes.

Across all these different businesses, attempts to improve productivity and quality using lean organisation techniques have the same set of principles at heart.

### 1\. Specify what creates value for customers

Here, value is the quality of service provided to the end customer. Only a small fraction of the time and effort spent working in a business directly adds value for the customer. By specifying activities in the work process that add value for the customer, it becomes easier to identify non-value activities that could be minimised or removed.

### 2\. Identify steps along the process chain

The process chain contains all the activities and processes that help deliver the product or service to the end customer. This represents, from start to finish, how value is delivered to the customer.

### 3\. Make processes flow by eliminating waste

Once activities and processes have been identified, the challenge is then to find ways to remove or minimise non-value waste. You’ll learn about the seven different types of waste later in this Session. 

### 4\. Respond to customer demand

Check what the customer wants so that the business produces what is wanted exactly when the customer wants it. This can help to make sure that any changes and attempts to minimise waste are working. 

### 5\. Strive for perfection by continual improvement

A lean business that doesn’t want to stand still must constantly be on the lookout for ways to improve. The aim is to introduce lean management techniques throughout the business so that the employees are always looking for ways to improve efficiency and minimise waste.
