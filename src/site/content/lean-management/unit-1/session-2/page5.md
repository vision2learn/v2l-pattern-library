The previous page gave examples of different measures of lean organisation.  

The two case studies exemplified that measures relevant to one organisation may not be as relevant to another organisation. In other words, **measures are relevant to the individual organisation.**

Anyone interested in organisational improvement and lean methodologies should not fall into the trap of thinking that there are a few measures that must always be used.

### Organisation levels

#### Scales of organisation

It is possible to look at an organisation as operating at a minimum of four scales or levels.  

ClickTap each of the images below to learn more.  

![Individual](../../images/unit01/session02/pg5-graphic1.svg) ![team](../../images/unit01/session02/pg5-graphic2.svg) ![process \(or department\)](../../images/unit01/session02/pg5-graphic3.svg) ![organisation](../../images/unit01/session02/pg5-graphic4.svg)

There may be measures that are suited to different scales of the organisation. In well-run and efficient organisations an attempt will be made to ensure that measures are consistent at all levels. In basic terms, if an individual delivers on their key outputs then this will help their team to deliver on its targets.

As teams deliver, this will enable processes to run efficiently and the organisation will be more successful.  

#### Measuring key points

Furthermore, an organisation may be measuring many things. It would be a surprise if an organisation was not measuring key outputs. However, this should not lead to the conclusion that everything that gets measured is of equal importance. Some measures will be more important than others. Some may be vital while others may have moderate value and others may be virtually pointless.

#### Correct measuring

There is an old saying that ‘what gets measured gets done’. There is a degree of truth in this statement and for this reason it is important that the right things are measured. This means resources, effort and energy can be dedicated to areas which will benefit the organisation and the key stakeholders. If the wrong things are measured then it is possible that vital resources will be wasted, while important areas are overlooked.

### In summary ![Summary](../../images/icons/icon-summary.svg)

So it is possible to conclude that there are many things that may be measured. The measures may be done at different levels of an organisation. The measures will vary in their importance.
