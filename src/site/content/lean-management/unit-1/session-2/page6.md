Who is involved in the Productivity Needs Analysis process?

As we saw on [page 3](../../unit01/session02/pg3.asp), staff turnover is a common key area measured by organisations in order to evaluate its own productivity.  

However, staff are not the only people involved in the PNA process.

ClickTap along the slider to the right to learn more.

  * ![Slide number 1 ](../../images/unit01/session02/pg6-slider1.svg)

#### Stakeholders

A stakeholder is someone with a vested interest in an organisation. These may include managers, board members, shareholders, employees, suppliers, government and community.

  * ![Slide number 2 ](../../images/unit01/session02/pg6-slider2.svg)

#### Customer voice

As well as staff and stakeholders, customers are another fundamentally important group of people who may be involved in the PNA process. It is important for a lean organisation to ensure that the voice of the customer is given the highest priority.

  * ![Slide number 3](../../images/unit01/session02/pg6-slider3.svg)

For example, a publically funded hospital may have some very specific targets on waiting lists, quality of treatment and budgets set by politicians (a key stakeholder).  

  * ![Slide number 4](../../images/unit01/session02/pg6-slider4.svg)

A private sector organisation may have targets set by an owner (a stakeholder) on profits, market share and dividends.  

  * ![Slide number 5](../../images/unit01/session02/pg6-slider5.svg)

In both cases there may also be specific things that patients and customers expect.

### Scorecards

When organisations wish to collect information or collate feedback from customers they may develop a scorecard. This provides a numerical and visual record of how customers experience the product or service.

### Case study ![Case Study](../../images/icons/icon-case-study.svg)

An organisation is concerned about its customer’s opinions regarding their **reliability** and **delivery**. To collect information on these and other areas, they develop the following scorecard. Roll your mouse over Tap the scorecard to gain an understanding of its features.

Please mark with a black “X” how you feel about our product/service.

X

X

Poor Average Excellent

They decide that **reliability** should score **5** and **delivery** should score **4** in order to meet general customer demand.

ClickTap to see an example of average scores given by customers on the products made by an organisation.

In this example it is clear that improvements in **reliability** and **delivery** are necessary, in order to meet with rating demands. This would result in the organisation looking closely at those processes linked to **product reliability** and **product delivery**.

There may be times when there are conflicts between what different stakeholders require. In this case the organisation must seek to have a very clear description of what really matters. Those things that matter are the ones where **measurement** is likely to be **most important**.
