### Which measures should be prioritised?

When prioritising measures, organisations and people within these organisations should follow the **four step** approach. This way, consistent approaches will be used throughout the organisation.  

### The four steps of priority

Remember to **think four** when talking about how organisations prioritise measures.

Determine the **organisational** objectives and state them in measurable and time bound terms. This may require a period of collecting the views of stakeholders, including customers, and then deciding upon the key objectives. The objectives should not conflict. If some are more important than others this should be clearly presented.

Identify and prioritise the **organisational processes** that deliver the outcomes in step 1. Specify the measures used to evaluate the outputs of these processes.  

Where applicable, based on the desired process outcomes, identify and prioritise **team objectives** and the methods used to measure performance.  

Where applicable, based on desired process and / or team objectives, identify and prioritise **individual objectives** and the methods used to measure performance.  

By following the **four step** approach, measures are selected and prioritised consistently.  

The most important measures are most likely to contribute to increasing productivity and other important organisation objectives.

For a lean organisation, the areas identified using the four steps will also be those areas where the elimination of waste will result in improved performance and a greater likelihood that objectives will be met.
