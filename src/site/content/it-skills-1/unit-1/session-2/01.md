      ### Your page layout

      Before you start typing your document, you can check to see if the pages are set up to your liking. By default, Microsoft Word will assume that you’re using A4 sized paper in portrait orientation (with the short sides of the paper at the top and bottom). If these default settings aren’t what you need for the document you are creating, you can change them.

            Once you start typing your document, it is useful to be able to split your text up into paragraphs. This makes your text easier to read and allows you to separate different pieces of text into relevant sections.

            When you reach the end of a page, Word automatically inserts a page break to take you onto a new blank page. If you want to insert a page break before the end of the page, you can insert a manual page break.
            
            To do this, simply insert your cursor where you want the current page to end and the new page to begin, and then select Insert from the top menu and Break from the drop down menu. Then select Page break and click OK.

            ## Line spacing
            
            Open up a letter document that you have created and follow these instructions to find out how to alter the line spacing.
              1. Select and highlight a section of text in your document.
              2. Go to Page Layout on the top menu then open the Paragraph box.
              3. In Indents and Spacing select an option from the Line Spacing drop down menu

      When you have finished typing a document, you can use the Spelling and Grammar tool in Word to check the accuracy of the text you have entered. To start using this tool, simply select Tools – Spelling and Grammar from the top menu ( Review – Proofing – Spelling & Grammar in Microsoft Word 2007).

      The spelling and grammar check will now work through the text in your document and check for any spelling and / or grammatical errors. When an error is found, a dialog box appears with a number of options. Click on the image below to see an example of the Spelling and Grammar dialog box:

      Spelling and Grammar dialog box

      The problem word (or phrase) is shown in the upper box and suggestions for the correct spelling are shown in the lower box. The buttons on the right hand side of the dialog box give you a number of options:

      Ignore – Ignore this word and move on to the next word for correction.
      Ignore All – Wherever this word occurs in the document, it will be ignored by the spell check.
      Add – Add this word to the dictionary. Doing this means that the word will not be picked up by future spell checks.
      Change – Change the problem word to the highlighted word in the Suggestions box.
      Change All – Wherever the problem word occurs in the document, change it for the highlighted word in the Suggestions box.
      AutoCorrect – Change the problem word to the highlighted word in the Suggestions box and, in future, automatically correct this mistake after it is typed.

      The spell checker does have its limitations, so you shouldn’t rely on it to spot every error in your document.

      You should always take care to ensure that the data you enter into your documents is accurate – this will cut down on the number of errors in your work and make your document more professional.

      As you won’t always be able to ensure 100% accuracy and your spell checker may have missed certain mistypes, you should always proofread your document. You can begin by checking your work on-screen; however, it is often easier to proofread your work once you have printed the document out.

      A handy way to proofread is to lay your document on a flat surface and then hold a ruler under each line of text as you read it. This way, you should not miss any mistakes.

      As well as checking the spelling of text in your document, you should also check the way your document looks. For example, pay attention to the page layout, margins, line breaks and page breaks. These things all make a difference to the way your finished document looks, so it’s important to get them right. Remember, you can use the Print Preview option to check the layout of your document.
