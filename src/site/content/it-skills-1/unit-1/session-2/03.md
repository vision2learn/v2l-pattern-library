      ### Headers and footers

      When you add a header or footer to your document, it will appear on every page.
      
      It is often very useful to include a header or footer in your document, as it saves having to type the same text on every page – Word will automatically do it for you. For example, if you wanted to include a reference that appears at the bottom of every page in your document, you could use a footer to do this.

          copy: |
            As we saw on the previous page, you can include page numbers if you create a header / footer in your document. However, if you don’t want to use the header / footer option, then you can simply follow these steps to add page numbers to your work:

            1. Select Insert – Page Numbers from the top menu (In Microsoft Word 2007 go to Insert – Header & Footer – Page Number).
            2. The Page Numbers dialog box will appear. Click on the image below to see an example of the dialog box:
            3. You can use the two drop-down lists headed Position and Alignment to change the way page numbers are displayed in the document.
            4. If you don’t want the number 1 to appear on the first page of your document (for example, this might be a title page), simply click to remove the tick from the Show number on first page check box.
            5. Click OK to insert page numbers in your document. The page numbers will now automatically appear in the chosen position on each page of your document.
            
          img: /images/svg/placeholder_icon.svg

          title: Date / Time
          copy: |
            As we saw on the previous page, you can include date / time information if you create a header or footer in your document. However, if you don’t want to use the header / footer option, then you can simply follow these steps to add the date / time to your work:

            1. Select Insert – Date and Time from the top menu (In Microsoft Word 2007, go to Insert – Text – Date & Time).
            2. The Date and Time dialog box will appear. Click on the image below to see an example of the dialog box:
            3. From the options that appear in this dialog box, you can choose a suitable format from the list on the left-hand side. Once you have chosen a format, click OK to insert the date / time in your document.

            In the Date and Time dialog box, you will see a check box marked Update automatically. If you tick this box, then the date / time you insert in your work will change to show the current date / time whenever you open the document.

          title: Tabs and indents
          text: |
            To make your text easier to read, you can apply indents to it. You can do this in a number of ways.

            1. You can use the Tab key at the beginning of a paragraph to apply Microsoft Word’s Auto Format function, which will move the first line of a paragraph in front of the left margin automatically. The Tab key can also be used to add spaces in your work, although this is not technically known as indenting.
            
            2. To apply indents to larger pieces of text, you can highlight the text and then click and drag the hanging indent icon at the top of the page. This will move all of the highlighted text to the new indented position.

            As well as checking the accuracy of your spelling and grammar, you can also check your work by counting how many words are in your document – and there is a tool in Microsoft Word that you can use to do this.
