    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      ### External pressure to change
      There are continuous pressures for change to be implemented in business. It is helpful to consider **reactive changes** when the business changes as a response to **external pressures** and **proactive changes** when the business changes due to internal demands.

      One helpful way of thinking about the factors influencing change is to use the PEST acronym. This highlights four of the main influences on the operation of a business. Select the images below to see what each part of the acronym represents with examples:

    title: Case studies
    theme: t-bg-primary1a i-case-study
    copy: |
      See below four case studies for each of the P E S T factors influencing change.
    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        -
          title: P - Political unrest
          copy: |
            ### Consider a tour operator

            A sustained period of political unrest in a popular tourist destination will have significant implications for a tour operator. The operator will have to deal with many people wishing to cancel and/or move their holidays. There will also be less demand for holidays to a place that is experiencing civil unrest.

            Such an external change can have a dramatic impact on the operation of an organisation. In the worst case, if the organisation fails to adapt it may go out of business.
          img: /images/svg/ba_u4_s1_p2_accordion_1.svg
        -
          title: E - Economic uncertainty
          copy: |
            Changes to the economy can have a significant impact on businesses both large and small. 
            
            Company A produces components for the car industry and is vulnerable to the cost of raw materials as well as changes to exchange rates and import/export duties. Failure to plan and respond effectively to economic uncertainty can result in less investment and reduced competitiveness.
          img: /images/svg/ba_u2_s8_p1_accordion_1.svg
        -
          title: S - Social changes
          copy: |
            Changes to the way that people shop and buy products and services have changed dramatically in recent years.  
            
            A small clothing store located on the local high street has seen a 50% reduction in visitors over the last 4 years.  It is clear that unless they take the time to understand changes in customer types and their preferences as well as the impact of social media in marketing and customer satisfaction, they will continue to see a decline in customers visiting the business.
          img: /images/svg/ba_u2_s8_p1_accordion_2.svg
        -
          title: T - Technological change
          copy: |
            Rapid advances in technology have created one of the biggest challenges for business in recent years. 
            
            Business A has a computer system, software and a website that has not been updated in several years. They are unable to take electronic payments on their website and processing customer orders causes severe delays.  It is becoming clear that if they fail to make changes and to keep up to date with technological advances, customers will take their business elsewhere.
          img: /images/svg/ba_u2_s9_p3_reveal_2b.svg

    title: The impact of external changes
    theme: 
    copy: |
      There are a vast number of potential influences on an organisation that will create pressure for change. This does not mean that an organisation has to change but the consequences of not doing so may be dire.

      Scroll through the slides to see how a failure to change can influence an organisation.
    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Innovation
          text: |
            **Change** - Customers want continuing innovation in the products they buy.

            **Organisational consequences of not making any changes**  
            Failure to innovate with products can lead to customers going elsewhere.
          img: ba_u4_s1_p2_slide_1.svg
        -
          title: Office IT equipment
          text: |
            **Change** - The use of new office IT can improve the speed, cost and quality of the work that is done.

            **Organisational consequences of not making any changes**  
            The failure to use this technology results in less efficient business performance.
          img: ba_u4_s1_p2_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Law
          text: |
            **Change** - The Government makes changes in the law.

            **Organisational consequences of not making any changes**  
            The organisation breaks the law resulting in fines / business closure / prison sentences.
          img: ba_u4_s1_p2_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Money
          text: |
            **Change** - Consumers expect the price of certain products to fall through time.

            **Organisational consequences of not making any changes**  
            Failure to drive down price can lead to customers going elsewhere.
          img: ba_u4_s1_p2_slide_4.svg
        -
          title: Production
          text: |
            **Change** - New computer aided production systems can lead to substantial cuts in production costs.

            **Organisational consequences of not making any changes**  
            Organisations unable to match the new technology improvements may decline or go out of business.
          img: ba_u4_s1_p2_slide_5.svg
        -
          title: Economy - Recession
          text: |
            **Change** - The recession has drastically reduced the demand for an organisation’s services.

            **Organisational consequences of not making any changes**  
            Excess spending compared with demand for services / continued production when it is not justified by demand / losses and ultimately potential closure.
          img: ba_u4_s1_p2_slide_6.svg
        -
          title: Economy - Exchange rate
          text: |
            **Change** - Changes in exchange rates make imports from competitors far cheaper.

            **Organisational consequences of not making any changes**  
            Fall in demand for products necessitates a cut in production and new efforts in sales and marketing - if this does not happen, the organisation will suffer.
          img: ba_u4_s1_p2_slide_7.svg
        -
          title: Delivery
          text: |
            **Change** - A Government agency cancels a large order.

            **Organisational consequences of not making any changes**  
            Overproduction / wasted resources / unsold products / cash flow problems / potential business failure.
          img: ba_u4_s1_p2_slide_8.svg

    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      ### Change from within
      The other source of change comes from **within a business - internal change**.

      Here a business can be operating effectively and satisfying its customers yet still be making changes within. This is driven by a desire to improve.

      See below for some examples of internal changes:
