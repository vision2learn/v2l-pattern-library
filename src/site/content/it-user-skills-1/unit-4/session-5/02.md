            In most cases, you will choose to print your spreadsheet to show the values that you have either entered or calculated. However, you may occasionally find it useful to display the formulae you have used in your printed spreadsheet.

            You should bear in mind that displaying formulae may change the layout of your final printed document. The formulae you create will often take up more room than the resulting values, so your printed output may need some adjustment in order to fit onto the correct number of pages, etc.

          text: |
            You can choose to either print a chart on a worksheet by itself, or print the chart along with its accompanying data from the worksheet.

            As before, you should take care to check the layout of your work before printing it. For example, if you have a small chart within a worksheet and you choose to print it on a separate page, the size and format of the chart will change to fill this page. So, as we have already seen, it’s always good to preview your work before printing.

    title: Page Layout and Setup
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      You can easily change the layout and appearance of your spreadsheet by editing features such as the margins, page orientation, page size and page breaks. This can help to make your spreadsheet more professional. When you are editing these options, you are adjusting the page setup for printing.

      You can change the layout of your spreadsheet on the *Page Layout* tab, however, further options can be found within the *Page Setup* dialogue box - you will see four tabs across the top of this dialog box. Scroll through the slides below to learn more.

            You can also use the Scaling section to change the size of your document so that it fits onto a certain number of pages when printed.

            This is often useful if you want each page to accommodate more data when printed – by reducing the size of the margins, your spreadsheet will be able to display more information.

      When you’re happy with your final spreadsheet, you can print your work. However, it is always a good idea to check how the spreadsheet will look before you commit to printing. See below to learn more.

    subInc:

      content:
        - 
          title: Preview and print
          copy: |
            You can use this feature to preview the layout of your document and see what it will look like when you print it. To do this:
            1. Click the *File* button (*File menu* on a Mac).
            2. Click the *Print* option.
            3. In the window displayed you can preview each page of your document before you print it.
            4. Here you can select a number of options, including: output type (printer or PDF), the Printer, Paper size and Number of copies that you would like to print. 
            5. If you wish to create a PDF on a Mac, you would select *Save as PDF* from the *PDF* dropdown box at the botton of the Print options window.
            6. You can also set the scaling options to ensure that the data is displayed on the correct number of pages.  For example, ‘fit sheet on one page’ will resize all of the data so that the entire content of the selected worksheet will fit on one page when printed. 

            Under the *Settings* heading in the *Print* section of the *File* tab, you can select whether you would like to print just the active sheets (the ones you have used), the whole workbook (all of the worksheets it includes) or the current selection (any highlighted data).
