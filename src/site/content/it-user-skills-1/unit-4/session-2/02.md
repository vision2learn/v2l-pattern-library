    title: Working with multiple cells
    layout: media
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      You can quickly and easily replicate formulae across multiple cells by clicking and dragging using the autofill or fill tool.

      By highlighting the cell containing the formula you want to replicate and then dragging up / down a column or across a row, Excel will populate the cells that you want to include in the selected formula.

      This is a very useful technique to remember when creating spreadsheets. You can see how much quicker it is to get Excel to replicate your formula – and it means you don’t have to worry about any typing errors or inaccuracies.

    title: Relative and Absolute cell references *LEVEL 2 - modify*
    theme: t-bg-supporting3
    copy: |
      Relative cell references are references to cells in relation to the position of the formula. For example, if you typed =B3 into cell C5, then Excel will search for the value located two cells above and one cell to the left of C5 – this is known as a relative reference and the examples we have looked at so far in this Session have all been relative cell references.

      When you copy a formula containing relative cell references, Excel will automatically update the cell references relative to the position of the formula.

      If you don’t want Excel to update your cell references when copying a formula, you would use an absolute reference.
