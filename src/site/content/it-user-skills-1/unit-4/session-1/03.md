    title: Key components in a spreadsheet
    theme: 
    copy: |
        Every worksheet (or spreadsheet) is made up of cells that are organised into columns and rows.

    subInc: 
      type: reveal
      content:
        -
          title: Cells
          copy: |
            In a spreadsheet file, the series of rectangular boxes - labelled with letters along the top line, and numbers down the left-hand side are called cells.

            In these cells is where you will insert the information (text, numbers, etc.) that you want to include in your spreadsheet.

          img: it2/it2_u3_s1_p2_reveal5.svg
        -
          title: Columns
          copy: |
            Columns are the lines of cells that run down the worksheet.

            The letters along the top mark the columns. These are used to structure the content in cells.
          img: it2/it2_u3_s1_p2_reveal6.svg
        -
          title: Rows
          copy: |
            Rows are the lines of cells that run across your worksheets.

            The numbers down the left mark rows. These are used to structure the content in cells.
          img: it2/it2_u3_s1_p2_reveal7.svg
        -
          title: Tabs
          copy: |
            When you launch a new spreadsheet file, you will see that it consists of a number of worksheets. Each worksheet will be indicated at the bottom of the screen by a tab – clicking a tab will take you to that worksheet.
          img: it1/it1_u4_s1_p3_reveal4.png
        
    copy2: Each cell in your spreadsheet has its own unique reference made up of the column letter and the row number. For example, C4 would be the reference for a cell located in column C, row 4.

    title: Amending your spreadsheet
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      You can change the format and layout of your spreadsheet by inserting, deleting, hiding and clearing your rows and columns. Scroll through the slides below to learn more.

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Inserting rows and columns
          text: |
            You can insert rows and columns into your spreadsheet by selecting the row or column, right-clicking with your mouse and selecting *Insert* from the shortcut menu.
          img: it1/it1_u4_s1_p3_slide1.png
        -
          title: Deleting rows and columns
          text: |
            As well as inserting new rows and columns into your spreadsheet, you can also delete them.
          img: it1/it1_u4_s1_p3_slide2.png
        -
          title: Hiding rows and columns
          text: |
            If you want to remove rows and columns from your spreadsheet without deleting them altogether, you can choose to hide them instead. 
            
            Once these are hidden it is a simple step to display them again.
          img: it1/it1_u4_s1_p3_slide3.png
        -
          title: Clearing rows and columns
          text: |
            In some cases, you might want to remove content from your spreadsheet without deleting or hiding the actual rows or columns. To do this, you can *clear* rows and columns.

            You can clear the contents of an *individual cell* in a similar way. Simply click to select the cell you want to clear and then press the Delete key on your keyboard.
          img: it1/it1_u4_s1_p3_slide4.png

    title: Editing techniques
    theme: 
    copy: |
      As you add text and data to your spreadsheet, you might need to edit and amend your work from time to time – and there are many ways in which you can do this. One technique for moving and amending content is to use cut, copy and paste.  Scroll through the slides below to see more.

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Cut, copy and paste
          text: |
            Cutting and pasting allows you to move content from one place to another (either within the same spreadsheet or to another spreadsheet altogether).

            Copying and pasting is very similar to cutting content but, in this case, the original content remains in place and a duplicate is made of it.
          img: it1/it1_u1_s3_p2_slide1.svg
        -
          title: Fill or Autofill
          text: |
            Another quick way of copying (or replicating) cell content is by using the *Autofill* option or by pressing the *Fill* button located on the Home Ribbon.

            Imagine you are creating a spreadsheet of customer contact details, and you know that they all live in London. While the Name, Address and Postcode fields will be different for each customer, you can quickly and easily populate the City field by either pressing the Fill button or dragging the cell content down using Autofill.
          img: it1/it1_u4_s1_p3_slide2b.png
        -
          title: Find and replace
          text: |
            If you make a mistake throughout a spreadsheet, like forgetting to use a capital letter for the name of a company, you don’t need to alter all of these mistakes separately – Find and Replace is a much quicker option.
          img: it1/it1_u1_s3_p2_slide2.svg
        -
          title: Undo / Redo
          text: |
            When you are working on a document, you can quickly and easily undo the last thing you have done (such as a word you have typed, format you have applied, etc).

            If you undo a particular action but then decide against it, you can redo it.
          img: it1/it1_u4_s1_p3_slide4b.svg
