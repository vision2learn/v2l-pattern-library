        When it comes to deciding on the layout of your presentation, remember to check if there are any organisational guidelines that you need to follow. For example, there might be guidelines around including a company logo on each slide or using a particular colour scheme, etc.

        old page 3: 

        There are some key points to consider when you are adding content to your presentation. For example:

        Copyright laws – These laws apply to the use of content that belongs to someone else. So, if you copy text or an image from somewhere, you will need to make sure that you have permission to use it before you add it into your presentation.

        Acknowledgement of sources – If you do use words or images that belong to someone else in your presentation (remember to get permission before you do this), you should always include a reference to show where you got this information from. This shows that the information is not your own work and it acknowledges the original owner of the content.

        Avoiding plagiarism – If you copy someone else’s content into your presentation and don’t acknowledge the source of this content, this is known as plagiarism. You should always acknowledge the source of any content you have obtained from others.

        Organisational guidelines – You should check to see if there are any specific organisational guidelines that you should follow when adding content to your presentation. For example, there might be an agreed company format for you to stick to when acknowledging sources of content, etc.

      When creating your presentation, it is important to make sure that your content is accurate. Your slides should always be professional, and any errors or inconsistencies will not give this impression.

      While you may take great care when entering content in your presentation, it is useful to check your finished work and make sure it meets expectations. For example, you might have a list of points you need to cover in your presentation, so check your finished work to make sure you have included everything you need to cover.

      One particularly useful way of checking the accuracy of your presentation is to run a spell check.

      While PowerPoint won’t automatically check your grammar for you, you might find it useful to check your work before inserting it into your presentation. For example, you could use the spelling and grammar check in Word first, and then copy your text into PowerPoint once you are sure that it is correct.
