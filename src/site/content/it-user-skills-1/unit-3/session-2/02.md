    title: Changing the order of your presentation
    theme: 
    copy: |
      You can change the order of slides in your presentation and it can be very useful to be able to do this. For example, if you are putting together a particularly detailed presentation, you might lose track of the content you have added to each slide. It is often only when you finish the presentation and review your work that you realise that some information might be better placed elsewhere.
    subInc:

          title: To do this - 
          copy: |
            1. Click on the image you want to order, and then click on the Format tab.
            2. Use the Bring Forward and Send Backward buttons in the Arrange group to change the order of your images.

            You can access some of these formatting options by selecting the object to be formatted and then clicking on the Arrange button in the Drawing group of the Home tab.

    title: Formatting text
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
        You can format the content of your presentation in order to change the way it looks. The first thing you need to do before formatting any text is to select it, so that PowerPoint knows what you are trying to do. To do this, point with your mouse at the word you want to select and then either double-click with the left mouse button to select a single word, or drag the mouse over the word (or words) to select. Your selected text will appear highlighted on the screen. Once you have highlighted the text, you are ready to format it.

    title: Editing your text
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      As well as formatting the content of your presentation, you can also apply a range of editing techniques if you need to rearrange your content. As before, you must first click and drag to select the text you want to edit. Once you have selected the text, you can begin to edit.

      Scroll through the slides below to find out more about the editing tools you can use.

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Cut, copy and paste
          text: |
            Cutting and pasting allows you to move text from one place to another (either within the same file or to another file altogether).

            Copying and pasting is very similar to cutting text but, in this case, the original text remains in place and a duplicate is made of it.

            While this is a nice easy way of editing text within your presentation, you need to be careful not to paste into the wrong position on a slide or cut some content that you need to keep.

          img: it1/it1_u1_s3_p2_slide1.svg
        -
          title: Delete and backspace
          text: |
            To delete text from your presentation, you can either use the Backspace key (by positioning your cursor at the end of the text) or, the Delete key (by highlighting your text).

            Be careful when using the Backspace key – if you hold it down too long, you may end up deleting text that you don’t want to delete!
          img: it1/it1_u1_s3_p2_reveal3.svg
        -
          title: Find and replace
          text: |
            PowerPoint has a handy ‘find and replace’ tool that you can use to update your presentation content by replacing specified text. For example, you may want to update your presentation by finding all references to January and changing them to February.

            This is a much easier way of updating content than manually going through and amending the entire presentation. However, you should take care when using the find and replace tool, as PowerPoint will make no exceptions to the content it will replace.
          img: it1/it1_u1_s3_p2_slide2.svg

          title: Bulleted sub list
          text: |
            You can create sub-points within an existing bulleted list. The main bullet points are known as 1st level text, while the slightly indented sub-points are 2nd level text.

            Just as with the main text in your presentation, you can change the format of a list (or part of a list) in your presentation by altering the line spacing between each bullet point. For example, you might want to apply 1.5 line spacing to the main bullet points in a list, and 0.5 line spacing to any sub-points.
