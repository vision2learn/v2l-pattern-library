      With the ever increasing use of IT within both the workplace and at home, information security threats are a problem for businesses as well as individuals. Viruses, worms, Trojans, spyware, adware and spam are widely known security threats, but they are just a few of many. Other common information security threats include, rootkits, and logic bombs. In recent years the number of security threats has increased extensively and it has become a challenge for IT professionals to stay up to date and ensure that IT systems are able to keep information secure.

    title: Types of system security threats
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      Scroll through the slides below to learn about the different types of system security threats.

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Virus
          text: |
            A virus covers many different types of threats but it is simply a program or piece of code that has the ability to replicate itself and infect multiples computers. Viruses are commonly transmitted using networks and removable media such as usb drives. Viruses can harm computers by: 
            * Using disk space 
            * Erasing all the data on a hard drive
            * Making changes to data and text files
            * Cause the computer to stop working.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Worm
          text: |
            A worm is a specific type of virus. Unlike a typical virus, it's goal isn't to alter system files, but to replicate them so many times that it consumes hard disk space or memory. Worm victims will notice their computers running slower or crashing.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Trojan
          text: |
            Trojan horses, commonly referred to as Trojans, are programs that work as normal, safe applications, but their mission is to allow a hacker remote access to your computer. In turn, the infected computer can be used as part of a denial of service attack and data theft can occur. 
            
            A nasty example of a Trojan is a keystroke logger that can be used to capture passwords, credit card numbers, and other sensitive information.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Spyware
          text: |
            Spyware usually invades computers through software downloads. Shareware and freeware downloads, in addition to peer-to-peer file sharing are typical infection points. Like Trojans, spyware can steal sensitive information, but they are often used as advertising tools as well.

            Their purpose is to gather a user's information by monitoring Internet activity and transmitting that to an attacker.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Spam
          text: |
            Some view spam is more of an annoyance than a threat. Spam is unsolicited junk mail. It comes in the form of an advertisement, and in addition to being a time waster, has the ability to consume valuable network bandwidth.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Adware
          text: |
            Adware, is similar to spyware in that it is used to observe a user's internet browsing habits. However, the purpose of Adware is to determine the type of adverts a user may be most likely to view or interact with. It automatically generates targeted ads in order to generate revenue for the person or company that created it. It is normally directed at individuals rather than companies and it monitors, records, and uses your online activity for promotional purposes.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg
