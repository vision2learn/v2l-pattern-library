          title: Entering text
          text: |
            It is often assumed that spreadsheets are all about numbers and calculations. However, you need to include text in your spreadsheet to help it make sense.

            For example, text could be used to add headings to columns / rows as well as to explain what the data in your spreadsheet shows.

            The text you insert into your spreadsheet can be made up of letters, characters and spaces. Any text entered is automatically left-aligned in your spreadsheet cells (numerical data is right-aligned).

          img: it2/it2_u3_s1_p3_slide1.png
        - 
          title: Entering numbers
          text: |
            The numerical data that you add to your spreadsheet includes the numbers 0 – 9 and a range of characters that are associated with numerical information, such as currency symbols (£, $ etc.), percentage signs (%), full stops (.) and commas (,).   If you want to type a negative number, you just have to type a minus sign (-) before the number. 

          img: it2/it2_u3_s1_p3_slide2.png
        - 
          title: Inserting images
          text: |
            You probably won’t add images to your spreadsheet work very often, but it is still useful to know how this is done! The images that you add to your spreadsheets can be pictures, photographs, Clip Art items or simple shapes.

          img: it2/it2_u3_s1_p3_slide3.png
        -
          title: Multiple formatting
          text: |
            You can save yourself a great deal of time by working with multiple parts of your spreadsheet at the same time. For example, you could apply the same font or formatting across a range of cells or insert data into more than one worksheet.
        
            When adding data to your spreadsheet, you don’t always have to enter information into each cell one by one. Instead, there are a number of shortcuts that you can use to insert data into multiple cells at the same time.

          img: it2/it2_u3_s1_p3_slide4.png
