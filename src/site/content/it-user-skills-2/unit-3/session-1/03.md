          title: Entering text
          copy: |
            It is often assumed that spreadsheets are all about numbers and calculations. However, you need to include text in your spreadsheet to help it make sense!

            For example, text could be used to add headings to columns / rows as well as to explain what the data in your spreadsheet shows. You might even create a spreadsheet that doesn’t contain any numbers at all, just columns / rows of text.

            The text you insert into your spreadsheet can be made up of letters, characters and spaces. Any text entered is automatically left-aligned in your spreadsheet cells (numerical data is right-aligned). To insert text into your spreadsheet:

            1. Left-click with your mouse to select the cell you want to add text to
            2. Type the text. As you type, your text will appear in the Formula Bar at the top of your spreadsheet
            3. When you have finished typing, press the Enter key or the TAB key on your keyboard. Your text is added to the cell and is automatically left-aligned.

          img: /images/svg/placeholder_icon.svg
        - 
          title: Entering numbers
          copy: |
            The numerical data that you add to your spreadsheet includes the numbers 0 – 9 and a range of characters that are associated with numerical information, such as currency symbols (£, $ etc.), percentage signs (%), full stops (.) and commas (,).   If you want to type a negative number, you just have to type a minus sign (-) before the number. 

            To insert numerical data into your spreadsheet:
            1. Left-click with your mouse to select the cell you want to add data into
            2. Type in the numerical data. As you type, your data will appear in the Formula Bar at the top of your spreadsheet
            3. When you have finished typing, press the Enter key or the TAB key on your keyboard. Your data is added to the cell and is automatically right-aligned.

          img: /images/svg/placeholder_icon.svg
        - 
          title: Inserting images
          copy: |
            You probably won’t add images to your spreadsheet work very often, but it is still useful to know how this is done! The images that you add to your spreadsheets can be pictures, photographs, Clip Art items or simple shapes.

            To insert a picture into a spreadsheet:
            1. In the worksheet that you want to add the picture to, left click to select a cell close to where you would like the picture to appear
            2. Click on the Insert tab
            3. In the Illustrations group, click on the Picture icon 
            4. The Insert Picture dialog box will open. Here, you can search through files and folders on your computer to find the picture you want to add to your spreadsheet 
            5. When you have found the picture you want to insert, left click on it to select it and click on the Insert button to insert it into your spreadsheet. 
          img: /images/svg/placeholder_icon.svg

        ## Copy and paste

        When adding data to your spreadsheet, you don’t always have to enter information into each cell one by one. Instead, there are a number of shortcuts that you can use to insert data into multiple cells at the same time. Scroll throuhg the slides below to learn more.

            To copy a data range:

            1. Select the cells that you want to copy.
            2. On the Home tab, in the Clipboard group, click the Copy icon.
          img: placeholder_icon.svg

          title: Pasting data
          text: |
            To paste your data range to a new location (e.g. a new worksheet or workbook):
            1. Click on the worksheet, cell or workbook that you want to copy your data to.
            2. On the Home tab, in the Clipboard group, click the Paste icon:
            3. Your data will now appear in its new location.

          img: placeholder_icon.svg
        -
          title: Avoid copying blank cells
          text: |
             To avoid copying blank cells, follow the steps for selecting and copying data as normal, however when it comes to pasting, use these steps instead:
             1. Click on the worksheet, cell or workbook that you want to copy your data to.
             2. On the Home tab, in the Clipboard group, click the arrow next to the Paste icon.
             3. From the menu that appears, select Paste Special.
             4. The Paste Special dialog box will appear, check the box marked Skip Blanks.
             5. Click OK to copy your data into your chosen data. The blank cells will not have replaced any of your existing data.

      ### Linking data and creating hyperlinks

       When you copy and paste data, it may be useful to create a link between the source file and the destination file. If information is linked in this way, whenever you update the original information (source file), the data in the destination file will be automatically updated. 

       Just as in Word, you can add hyperlinks to your spreadsheet documents. For example, you could create a link to another worksheet within that document, to another document or even to a web page.
