title: Evaluating the use of IT
intro: |
    Once you have planned and completed a task using IT, you need to evaluate your work to decide whether you can improve your work.

    It’s important that you know whether the IT method you have used was fit for purpose, and if you have met the requirements for the task.

      It’s important to check the strengths and weakness of your work so that you have an overall view of how effective your initial response to the task is. Once you have identified the strengths and weakness, you can start thinking about improving the final outcome. Scroll through the slides below to learn more.

    subInc:

      slides:
        -
          title: Format
          text: |
            It’s important to check the format of your work when you are evaluating. This means checking things like text style, size and colour and things like borders, tables and graphs. There are two key questions that you need to ask yourself:
            * Is the formatting appropriate?
            * Has the correct software been used to produce the format?

            Effective formatting should make your work easier to read and follow. For example, in a presentation for a meeting you wouldn’t use light green font on a yellow background, as this would be difficult to read. 
          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_1.svg
        -
          title: Styles
          text: |
            Styles should make applying consistent formats much easier! Styles should always add to the presentation of a piece of work. There are two key questions that you need to ask yourself when evaluating the use of styles:
            * Do the styles used add to the presentation and improve readability?
            * Do the styles used comply with the house style?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Structure and Layout
          text: |
            A good structure and layout should make a document easier to read, navigate and understand.

            There are three key questions that you need to ask yourself when you’re thinking about structure and layout:
            * Is the information presented in a logical way?
            * Is the information well laid out?
            * Do features like headers, footers and titles make it easy to find key information?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Quality
          text: |
            A high-quality document should look professional, and it should impress the people who see it!

            There are three key questions to ask yourself when you are thinking about the quality of a piece of work:
            * Does your outcome look professional?
            * Has your task been completed to a high standard?
            * Does your outcome look impressive?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_4.svg
        -
          title: Accuracy
          text: |
            An accurate outcome will present information correctly, without errors such as spelling mistakes, formula errors or unlabelled charts preventing users from extracting reliable information from the document.

            There are three key questions to ask yourself when you are thinking about the accuracy of your document:
            * Have you checked for spelling / grammatical errors?
            * Have graphs and charts been created using the right information?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_5.svg
        -
          title: Clarity for Audience
          text: |
            A task that has clarity for the audience is a task that makes it very easy for the audience to understand the information. It will not be confusing or difficult to read.

            When thinking about how clear your outcome is, there are two key questions that you should consider:
            * Is your final outcome clear?
            * Could your task confuse anyone?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_6.svg

    title: Improving your work
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      Once you have identified strengths and weaknesses, it’s important to consider how you can improve your work. The first thing you should do is try and correct mistakes yourself, however you might not always be able to do this by yourself. Scroll through the slides below to learn more.

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Correcting mistakes
          text: |
            Making changes to the format, style, structure or layout aspects of your work can help to make your work more clear. This could be by changing a font size / colour to make it more readable, or restructuring a page so that it is in a logical order.

            You can improve the quality and accuracy of your work using tools like Spell Check, and the Excel Error Checker. Using Print preview and proofreading your finished work can also help. 
          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_7.svg
        -
          title: Working with others
          text: |
            When you’re working with other people, it’s really important that you keep everyone informed of the changes that need to be made.

            Feedback is really important and if people don’t know that changes are being made, they can’t help you with them! 
          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_8.svg
        -
          title: Working with others - Limited time for improvements
          text: |
            If you don’t have much time to make the improvements, it can be useful to get other people to help you make them!

            For example, say you needed to completely reorganise a financial report. This is a big task and if different people worked on different sections, then it could help you to complete the report in time! 
          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_9.svg
        -
          title: Working with others - Its a team outcome
          text: |
            If the task has been completed by a number of people, then the outcome is a team effort. In this case, it may be useful to delegate any improvements depending on who has completed which part of the task.
            
            For example, the formatting needed to be improved in a sales presentation, the person who is responsible for this should be the one to improve it. Similarly, if the graphs used in the presentation need to be improved, the person with the technical ability to do this should be the one to make the changes. 
          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_10.svg
        -
          title: Improving efficiency and effectiveness
          text: |
            A great way of improving your work is to find more effective and efficient ways of doing things. This will leave you with more time to concentrate on improving content, proofreading and seeing what other people think of your work.
          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_11.svg
        -
          title: Training
          text: |
            There may be occasions when there is a task or a project that you would like to be involved in but don't currently have the required skills.  Completing training to improve your skill-set will enable you to undertake new projects and tasks and improve your productivity and efficiency.
          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_slide_12.svg

          title: Quality of information
          copy: |
            The quality of your final outcome is really important. When assessing the quality of information used you could ask yourself:
            * Has the quality of information improved now that you have made changes?
            * Is the information given of a high enough standard?
            * Is the task still fit for purpose?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_reveal_1.svg
        -
          title: Drafing
          copy: |
            A good way of reviewing your final outcome is to compare it to drafts that you have created before you finished the final task. Ask yourself three key questions:
            * Did you complete drafts?
            * Should you have?
            * Has the drafting process helped you to minimise mistakes?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_reveal_2.svg
        -
          title: Compare to the original plans
          copy: |
            Take a look at your final task alongside the original plans and ask yourself the following questions:
            * Does the outcome of the task do everything that it is supposed to?
            * Have you sacrificed some parts of the plan in order to meet deadlines?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_reveal_3.svg
        -
          title: Check with audience
          copy: |
            You can check with the audience to see whether the task has been completed effectively, for example could the outcome of a presentation be improved. Two key questions you could ask yourself: 
            1. Are the audience happy with the outcome? 
            2. Did you get it to them within the timescale?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_reveal_4.svg
        -
          title: Effect of mistakes
          copy: |
            You should review any mistakes that have been made and the effect they have on the task. Two key questions you could ask yourself:
            * Did you make any mistakes during your completion of the task?
            * How did these tasks impact on the final outcome of the task?

          img: it2/it2_u2_s6_p3_ipu_reveal_5.svg
