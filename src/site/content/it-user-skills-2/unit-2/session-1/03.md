    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      It’s really important to consider the budget, time, skills and resources available to you when approaching a task. By considering what you have available to you, you will have a better idea of how realistic any deadlines or budgets are.

      Scroll through the slides below to learn more

          title: Skills
          text: |
            In your plan, you need to make a list of the different skills that you will need to complete the task. This will help you to decide whether the skills that you already have are enough to complete the task on your own, or if you will need help and specific training. 
          img: it2/it2_u2_s1_p3_ipu_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Timescales
          text: |
            Have you been given a timescale of when the task must be finished? The time that you have available is one of the most important things you have to bear in mind throughout your work.

            For example, if you don’t have very much time to complete something, you might have to leave out some of your more detailed ideas to make sure that you can meet your deadline.

          img: it2/it2_u2_s1_p3_ipu_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Resources
          text: |
            Do you have the resources that you need to complete the task effectively? If not, you might have to use some of the budget to buy additional resources or gain extra help from professional experts or other staff.

            This is particularly important when completing a task using IT. For example, if you needed to create a large financial report, not having access to a computer with Excel would have a huge impact on the task and how quickly it could be completed.

          img: it2/it2_u2_s1_p3_ipu_slide_4.svg
        -
          title: Audience needs
          text: |
            Before you start any kind of IT work, you should always think about who you are producing the work for and what their needs are. Different audiences would mean totally different outcomes.

            For example, if you were preparing two café menus, one for adults and one for children, they would be very different. A menu aimed at children would be more colourful and probably have less text. A menu for adults would have more text and be formatted in a more sophisticated manner.
          img: it2/it2_u2_s1_p3_ipu_slide_5.svg

      ### Example 2
      Sarah is at work and she has to complete a list of tasks before the end of the week. She realises that she won’t get all of her work done in time unless she delegates some of the work to her colleagues

      She decides to create a list of the tasks with instructions on whose tasks are whose and how to complete each task. She’ll then send everyone a copy. Sarah needs to decide the best way to create this list and the best way to send everyone a copy. She must consider time constraints and whether using IT is the best way to display her list.
