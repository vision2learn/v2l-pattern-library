    subInc:

      slides:
        -
          title: Resizing objects
          text: |
            The first thing you need to do to resize an image, table, shape or chart is click to select it.

            When the object is selected, it will appear with small white circular (square on a Mac) handles around the edge. To resize your object, simply click and drag any of these handles, then release your mouse button once your image is the size you want it to be.

          img: it2/it2_u2_s3_p3_slide1.png
        -
          title: Image proportions
          text: |
            ## Image proportions
            When resizing your objects, it is important that you maintain the original proportions. This keeps the object quality at a decent level and also ensures that the object's content is easily visible.
            
            To resize your object while maintaining proportions, simply hold down the CTRL key on your keyboard while clicking and dragging the circular handles to resize.
        -
          title: Formatting shapes
          text: |
            When formatting shapes, you can also click on the shape with your right mouse button and select Format Shape from the menu that appears – this will give you the option to change the way your shape looks (for example, by changing the colour of the shape or rotating it).

          img: it2/it2_u2_s3_p3_slide2.svg
        -
          title: Positioning objects
          text: |
            ## Positioning objects
            If you want to move your object around the slide, first click on the object so that it is selected (you will see the small white circular handles around the image).

            Now, click on the main object and drag the object into its new location on the slide. When you’re happy with the position of the object, release your mouse button to ‘drop’ it into place.
        -
          title: Cropping objects
          text: |
            To crop your object, click on it so that the Format tab appears. Then click on the Crop button in the Size group.
            
            Your image will now appear with black handles around it – use these to crop your image as you want.

          img: it2/it2_u2_s3_p3_slide3.svg
        -
          title: Order
          text: |
            Sometimes, it may be useful to change the order of any images that you insert into your presentations. To do this:

            1. Click on the image you want to order, and then click on the Format tab.
            2. Use the Bring Forward and Send Backward buttons in the Arrange group to change the order of your images.

            You can access some of these formatting options by selecting the object to be formatted and then clicking on the Arrange button in the Drawing group of the Home tab.

          img: it2/it2_u2_s3_p3_slide4.svg
        -
          title: Grouping
          text: |
            You can also group your objects so that they remain in the same position in relation to one another. To do this:
            1. Hold down the CTRL key and select the objects that you want to group.
            2. On the Format tab, in the Arrange group, click on the arrow next to the Group icon, then click on Group.
            3. Your images will now remain in the same position in relation to one another.

            To Ungroup your icons, right-click on the grouped items and select Group – Ungroup from the menu that appears.

          img: it2/it2_u2_s3_p3_slide5.svg

      When you are planning a task, it’s really important that you consider that factors that might stop you from completing it. Planning for certain restrictions is just as important as planning the task itself. 

      Scroll through the slides below to learn more.

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Access to information
          text: |
            It’s important that you have full access to all the information that you need before you start working, otherwise you will have to keep stopping to collect information. A lack of information could really affect the timescale of your task, and analysing the information that you need is one of the most important parts of the planning process.

            For example, if you needed sales figures to produce a report, it would be best to collect this data before you started. Otherwise it could mean that you take longer to finish the task, or that your work is a lower standard. 
          img: it2/it2_u2_s3_p3_ipu_slide_1.svg
        -
          title: Organisational guidelines
          text: |
            Organisations often have specific guidelines that you need to stick to when working on a task. For example, you may need to follow an organisational house style or a set of specific brand guidelines.

            Brand guidelines are very similar to house styles. They give you information to ensure that your work reflects the image that the organisation wants to appear as. This might include guidance on how to use the company logo or apply company colours to text etc. 
          img: it2/it2_u2_s3_p3_ipu_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Data protection
          text: |
            There are also some legal considerations which may affect the task. Any personal data that you include in your work is covered by The Data Protection Act 2018. If you need to include personal information in your work, you should make sure that you have permission to use it.

          img: it2/it2_u2_s3_p3_ipu_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Copyright
          text: |
            If your work includes any content that is not your own, you need to be aware of copyright laws. If you do want to use content (some text, an image, etc.) that belongs to someone else, you may need to ask their permission before using it. The owner of the content might also want you to include an acknowledgement in your work, in order to confirm the source of the content.

            The Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988 is the main piece of legislation relating to the use of other people’s content.

          img: it2/it2_u2_s3_p3_ipu_slide_4.svg

    title: Read - Organisational guidelines
    theme: t-bg-primary2 i-read
    copy: | 
      Often, when you are producing a presentation for an organisation, you will be provided with a House style sheet. A house style is a set of guidelines for the particular style that a company wants to apply to all of its written documentation and correspondence. It’s used to ensure that presentations are formatted consistently.
      
      Especially in large companies, house styles are very important. If hundreds of employees are creating documents in different styles and layouts, the company’s branding would not be very consistent.
