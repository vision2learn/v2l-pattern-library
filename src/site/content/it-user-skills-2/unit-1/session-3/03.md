    title: Styles and guidelines
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      Microsoft Word comes equipped with a number of Styles. These styles are pre-determined sets of formatting that can help you format your document quickly and consistently. Each specific style is a different set of paragraph and character formatting settings.

      Styles can be accessed from the Quick Styles Gallery. This is located in the Styles group under the Home tab.  Styles allow you to choose a paragraph alignment, font names, sizes, colours and shading in one simple click.

      When you are receiving advice on your own work or giving advice to others about their own work, you may have to consider certain guidelines. These guidelines can take a number of different forms and will help you to improve productivity and efficiency.

          title: Using styles
          text: |
            Styles are mainly used to format headings and body text, but they can also be used in lists and tables. To apply a style to a paragraph or line of text:

            1. Highlight the text that you want to apply the Style to.

            2. In the Styles group, under the Home tab, click on the Style that you want to apply.

            You can apply different styles to different parts of text; simply use the steps above for different sections within your document. For example, you may want to apply one Style to the heading of your document, and one to the body text.

          title: House styles
          text: |
            These guidelines are often provided by an organisation.  A ‘house style’ is a particular style and layout that an organisation wants to apply to all of its written documents. This makes sure that all documents are formatted in a consistent way. This is very useful for large organisations, where hundreds of employees may be creating documents in a variety of different fonts, styles and layouts.

            Typically, a house style will specify criteria for the formatting that is to be applied to a document. You can use this to help you to check the consistency of your work, and during proofreading. You will learn more about proofreading in session 6.

      ### Styles, Templates and guidelines

      Templates are documents that are already formatted so that the ‘look’ of the document is already in place. This includes elements such as margins, line spacing, text styles and colour schemes.
      
      Templates can be used for a large number of things, such as a letter, memo, fax, agenda, calendar, CV, mailing label, report or even a web page!
