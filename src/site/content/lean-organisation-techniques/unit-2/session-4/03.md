    title: Standard operating procedures
    theme: t-bg-supporting2
    copy: |
      The use of standard operating procedures may bring considerable benefits to an organisation. They provide people with all the safety, health, environmental and operational information necessary to perform a job properly and to enable quality standards to be maintained.

      See below for some more reasons for creating SOPs:
    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: They ensure that 
          copy: |
            * production operations are performed consistently to maintain quality control of processes and products
            * processes continue uninterrupted and are completed on a prescribed schedule 
            * no failures occur in manufacturing and other processes that would harm anyone in the surrounding community 
            * approved procedures are followed in compliance with company and government regulations. 
        - 
          title: They serve as 
          copy: |
            * a training document for teaching users about the process for which the SOP was written
            * a checklist for co-workers who observe job performance, and as a checklist for auditor 
            * a historical record of an existing process, so there is a factual basis for revising those steps when a process or piece of equipment is changed 
            * an explanation of steps in a process, so they can be reviewed in accident investigations. 
    copy2: |
      By creating SOPs, it is possible to develop a safer and more **efficient** environment where products and services are delivered to consistent **standards**.

      SOPs provide a framework for **continuous improvement**. They can be monitored, reviewed and amended where necessary. By recording these actions, an organisation may safeguard against repeated errors. 

      Some activities are not standardised in this way, such as the discussions between sales staff and customers, and sales prices, which need to allow for variation and flexibility.

            Other forms of documentation are used alongside SOS sheets. The use of multiple complementary improvement techniques is a commonly used business improvement technique.

    title: Summary
    theme: t-bg-primary i-summary
    copy: |
      Standard operating sheets (SOS) are used by organisations to keep a record of their processes as part of their commitment to standardisation. 
      
      Standard operating sheets record a description of the work activity, the distance moved, the tools used and the time the activity takes. Other forms of documentation, such as maps, photographs and checklists, are usually used alongside standard operating sheets. The sheets are reviewed, updated and amended over time.
