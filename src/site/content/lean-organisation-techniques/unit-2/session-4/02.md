title: The benefits of standardisation
intro: | 
  Organisations seek to standardise in order to improve reliability and cut wasted time, materials and labour.

  The elimination of waste and errors, and the consistent delivery of a product or service, improves the quality of the outputs of an organisation.

    title: Standardisation benefits
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      Consider a multi-plant organisation where each plant uses different software systems. Information transfer takes a long time and costs a lot of money to move around. There is also scope for errors in transfers. However, if all the plants used the same standardised systems, information could be transferred almost instantaneously, at very little cost and with few or no errors.

      Another potential benefit of standardisation is reduced stock levels carried in organisations. If one component can be standardised and used for all models produced by a business, reducing stock, then storage, monitoring and the potential for error can be achieved cost-effectively.

      The automotive and hotel industries are both examples of industries which use standardisations to make themselves more competitive.

    title: Reduced costs, problem elimination and health and safety
    theme: 
    copy: |
      In addition to improving reliability and eliminating waste, standardisation is also used to **reduce costs**, **eliminate problems** and aid in **health and safety**

      The process of standardisation can make a significant contribution to **quality**, **efficiency** and **safety** in the workplace. It may be used in all types of organisation and for all types of business functions.

      Scroll through the slides below to learn more about each of these benefits.
    subInc:
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Reduced costs
          text: One aim of standardisation is to **reduce costs**. In a manufacturing environment, one way to reduce costs is to use the same component for several models. This creates demand for a larger volume of the component and makes it possible to get economies of scale and volume discounts. It also makes it possible to carry lower overall inventory (over the full range of models). 

          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U2_S4_P2_Slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Problem elimination
          text: In both product and service industries, standard procedures can help to eliminate the likelihood of problems recurring, and provide the basis for a consistently delivered process. A standard procedure makes it possible to track the process and identify anything that may cause undesirable outcomes. 

          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U2_S4_P2_Slide_4.svg
        -
          title: Health and safety
          text: Most organisations have clear instructions procedures for the operation of equipment and handling of potentially hazardous substances. If instructions are ignored, people’s health and safety can be put at risk. There are legal, moral and commercial reasons for maximising health and safety. It is logical to reduce variation and standardise health and safety processes. For example, organisations may emphasise the need for appropriate protective clothing for carrying out certain tasks. 

          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U2_S4_P2_Slide_5.svg

      There is a tension between standardisation and **customisation** Whilst customers want to have the benefits of standardisation, they often like to have things that express their individualism. Often, organisations seek to standardise as much of the production process as possible and then make provision for some customisation at the end.
