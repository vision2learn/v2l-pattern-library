    title: Case study
    theme: t-bg-primary1a i-case-study
    copy: |

      Consider two companies called Company A and Company B. They produce very similar products of comparable quality and price.

      Company A consistently delivers its products year after year.

      Company B seeks to continuously improve its production and delivery. As a result of this, the products improve by 5% each year and their prices come down by 5% each year.

      If all other things are equal, what are the consequences for Company A and Company B? See the below ‘consequences’ to learn more. 
    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Consequences
          copy: |
            It is most likely that more and more customers will go to Company B and fewer to Company A. This is because Company A’s product remains unchanged, whilst Company B’s product gets continually better and cheaper.

            The consequences of these differences are not only experienced by customers. They will also have an impact on security and future employment prospects. An expanding company is better placed to offer promotion opportunities, higher earning potential, better pensions and other similar benefits. As organisations grow, they provide the basis for a stronger and more competitive national economy.

            There are many ways that Company B may have achieved its improved performance. Two common methods are through **innovation** and **continuous improvement**.

    title: Customer Feedback
    theme: t-bg-supporting3
    copy: |
      As the phrase suggests, **continuous improvement** involves seeking to improve a product or service continuously over time.

      For continuous improvement to be effective, an organisation must know how to define ‘**improvement**’. The definition of improvement must be determined by the **customers**. 

      ### Asking for customer feedback 

      In order to determine what is meant by improvement, a company must ask its customers the following two questions:

        1. What changes in product / delivery / quality / price would be an improvement?
        2. What should be changed as a priority?

      Select each stage to learn more about using customer feedback for continuous improvement.
    subInc: 
        type: Accordion
        content:
            -
                title: Stage 1. The continuous improvement process
                copy: |
                     It would be foolish and wasteful for an organisation to allocate resources to make changes and improvements that the customer does not desire. The first stage in the continuous improvement process is to ask the customer about which aspects of the company they would like to see improved.

            -
                title: Stage 2. Acting on customer feedback
                copy: |
                    Once the customer feedback has been collected and analysed, the organisation can identify the work activities and processes that have the most significant impact on the desired outcome of customers. This is the second stage in the continuous improvement process.
            -
                title: Stage 3. Implementing change
                copy: |
                    Once an organisation has identified the most significant activities and processes, it can look for ways to change them in order to improve the product or service. This is the third stage of the continuous improvement process.
