title: The 5C/5S technique
intro: | 
  A systematic approach focuses on the **factors that influence a more organised workplace**. One such approach is the **5C/5S technique**.

  The 5C/5S technique is based on five Japanese words: seiri, seiton, seiso, seiketsu and shitsuke.

    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      When aiming to create a clean, orderly and logical workplace, it helps to have a structured, **systematic approach** in order to identify where and how improvements can be made. 

      The **5C/5S technique** approach enables workers to set appropriate workplace **standards** then identify any negative variations or abnormalities. Once these have been identified, it is possible to take action to correct them. 

      ### Factors that influence an organised workplace

      * The people
      * The jobs that are done
      * The order in which jobs are done
      * The physical space required to do jobs
      * The tools, equipment and materials needed to carry out jobs
      * The use of shared resources
      * Storage space

    title: Summary
    theme: t-bg-primary i-summary
    copy: |
      The 5C/5S technique is a systematic approach that is used within organisations to stabilise, maintain and, ultimately, improve the work environment.
      
      It enables workers to set workplace standards which are then used to identify and correct any negative variations or abnormalities. This contributes towards the continuous improvement of the organisation. 
      
      The 5C/5S technique is made up of five key stages: Clear out, Configure, Clean and Check, Conformity and Custom and Practice.

    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Factors to consider
          copy: |
            The organisation must:
            * Clearly define the boundaries of a chosen area.
            * Make sure they check all health and safety requirements, work permits, risks, hazards, protective clothing, preventative actions, etc.
            * Make sure that machinery and power is isolated (electrical, hydraulic and pneumatic).
            * Ensure that relevant parties agree on the clear out area.
            * Determine how unwanted items and waste will be disposed of.
            * Plan the timing and duration of the activity.
            * Communicate to all parties that the activity is taking place and the effect it will have on production.
            * Brief the team involved and have a clear definition of the tasks.
            * ‘Red Tag’ any items which are unsuitable (you will find out more about tagging on the next page).
          img: /images/svg/LeanManagement/LM_U2_S2_P2_Accordion_1.svg
        - 
          title: Benefits of the 5C/5S technique 
          copy: |
            The 5C/5S approach is more than just ‘good housekeeping’. Here are some of the benefits that the 5C/5S technique can offer.

            * The flow of work (products/services/administration activities) and any deviation from agreed standards can be seen immediately.
            * The standard procedures are easily understood and readily available in the workplace.
            * The workplace is organised so that people can reach anything needed with ease.
            * Appropriate parts and supplies are located near to the operator for immediate use.
            * People can retrieve information or equipment within a defined minimum time.
            * The levels of stock can be identified immediately and labelled so that re-ordering may be automatic.
            * Any waste or abnormalities can be immediately recognised.

            The 5C/5S approach is not limited to particular workplaces, shift patterns or workloads. It can be applied anywhere work is carried out.

            Although a clean and tidy workplace is a by-product of the 5Cs/5S approach, it is not the primary objective.
