    title: Check sheets
    theme: t-bg-supporting2
    copy: |
      Standards are listed on **check sheets**. Check sheets enable an organisation to collect and collate data, thereby allowing it to make and maintain improvements. 
      
      Check sheets are therefore an essential part of stage 4: Conformity (standardise) as they allow an organisation to check that the standards established in stages 1-3 are being consistently maintained. 
      
      In this way the check sheets can be used as part of the **continuous improvement process.**

    title: PDSA
    layout: media
    theme: t-bg-supporting3
    copy: |
      The most commonly used approach to continuous improvement is the **PDSA (Plan, Do, Study, Act) cycle**.  Plan, Do, Study, Act (PDSA), or Plan, Do, Check, Act (as it is sometimes known) is a very simple concept used by many businesses. 

      * **Plan** is the first stage in the process. Planning involves collecting data and pinpointing exactly what will be improved and how the changes will be implemented.  
      * **Do** is carrying out the test or implementing the changes that will result in an improvement.  
      * **Study** is monitoring the data before and after the change has been made to establish whether there has been an improvement and to reflect on what has been learned.  
      * **Act** is to carry out full implementation of the new process if there has been an improvement, or to plan the next change. 

      When aiming to improve a system, these steps must be repeated until the desired outcome is achieved. It is essential that all four steps are **balanced**.

    title: Using check sheets in the PDSA cycle
    layout: media
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      It is unrealistic to expect change to produce the right result every time. Therefore, it is **always** necessary to check that predicted improvement has actually happened. 

      The check sheets used as part of the PDSA cycle are used to score current and future performance. The aim is to **make and maintain improvements**.

      If abnormal conditions are regularly identified and logged, the check sheet may indicate a persistent underlying problem. The company’s aim would then be to discover the root cause of the problem and correct it. There is a continued effort to reduce the number of items from the **red tag system**.

      To get everyone to work to the same standards, the best approach is to have **standard operating procedures** that clearly spell out what is required. All relevant staff should know what these are.

      Once you have completed the fourth stage **(Conformity/standardise)**, you are ready to move on to the final fifth stage **(Custom and Practice).**

      The fifth and final stage of the 5C/5S approach is **Custom and Practice (Sustain/Self-discipline)**. 

      The aim of this stage is to **embed** the 5C/5S approach within an organisation by **communicating its benefits** and **gaining the active participation of all the employees**. 

      ‘Custom and Practice’ means that ‘every employee follows the rules, understands the benefits and contributes to the improvement’.

            The fifth and final stage of the 5C/5S approach is **Custom and Practice (Sustain/Self-discipline)**. 

            The aim of this stage is to **embed** the 5C/5S approach within an organisation by **communicating its benefits** and **gaining the active participation of all the employees**. 

            ‘Custom and Practice’ means that ‘every employee follows the rules, understands the benefits and contributes to the improvement’.  

            ### Embedding Custom and Practice 

            Companies embed the 5C/5S approach by:

            * Keeping it simple and involving everyone
            * Developing routines that are realistic for staff
            * Auditing (inspecting and reviewing)
            * Conducting staff training
            * Using high visibility
            * Carrying out random inspections and checking safety equipment
            * Providing sufficient management resources
            * Ensuring managers praise and encourage appropriately.

            By systematically implementing the 5C/5S approach, the company improves efficiency and effectiveness in the workplace and this, in turn, improves productivity. 
        - 
          title: Auditing
          copy: |

            IMAGE

            Part of the **Custom and Practice (Sustain/Self-discipline)** stage of the 5C/5S approach is **auditing**. Organisations carry out **audits** on a regular basis to ensure that the new standards do not deteriorate. 

            Once the standards have been established during the first three stages of 5C/5S, a **scoring system** can be developed. The score indicates how closely the standard is matched. Often a score of ‘0’ is used for substandard and a score of ‘1’ for the matched standard.

            Auditing achieves four important results:

            1. It sets a baseline figure for comparison
            2. It identifies and quantifies the areas where improvements could be made
            3. It designates responsibility for individual improvement actions
            4. It monitors progress.

            An audit every month helps to maintain standards.

            The **results** of audits are often presented in a **radar chart**. The radar chart provides an excellent way of **measuring and presenting performance**. Have a look at the example below.

            Two further ways of embedding and supporting 5S/5C are to use **visual management techniques** and **standard operating procedures**. Both of these will be explained in Session 3.

    copy: |
      The first three stages of the 5C/5S technique (Clear out, Configure and Clean and Check) are concerned with establishing the standards which provide a structured approach to the work environment and work activities. The fourth stage, Conformity, is concerned with maintaining the standards and improving workplace organisation. The purpose of the fifth stage, Custom and Practice, is to embed the approach within the organisation and use auditing to ensure that standards do not deteriorate.
