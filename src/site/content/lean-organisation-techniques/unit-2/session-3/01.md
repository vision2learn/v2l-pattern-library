    copy2: |
      ### Why visual information is so useful

      Effective visual information helps to implement and maintain workplace organisation. It provides all workers with the relevant information to do their job as efficiently as possible. Visual information can provide information such as what belongs where, where things should go, how many there should be, is the process being followed or is something unusual happening.

      Really effective visual information provides people with all the information they need at a glance, allowing people to immediately work out whether working conditions and processes are running as they should.

    title: Beginnings of visual management techniques
    layout: media
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      Visual management techniques go all the way back to the early 19th Century with the industrialist Robert Owen’s silent monitors.

      Owen didn’t like punishing his workers so came up with silent monitors as a way to improve motivation and productivity.

      Silent monitors were coloured, wooden blocks next to each worker that showed how hard they had been working and operated on a colour coded system which you can see below. Just like modern forms of visual information, they allowed supervisors to see at-a-glance how productive workers were.

          title: Helps control the process
          text: Visual indicators such as instructions on a wall or markings on the floor help to control and standardise work processes. They help to reduce any variations by making sure everyone is carrying out tasks in a similar way and as efficiently as possible.

          img: LM_U2_S3_P1_Slide_1.svg
        -
          title: Provides feedback
          text: Visual displays on machinery, charts and graphs all help to provide employees with feedback about work processes. Indicators such as markings on machinery can help inform employees if things are functioning correctly or markers on stock can help keep on top of stock replenishment. 
          img: LM_U2_S3_P1_Slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Informs the initiation of action
          text: Visual indicators can also help to initiate work processes or help inform employees about when to start different processes. For example, a pallet used for recyclables has a visual marker on it where, once the recycling reaches that marker, the pallet must be emptied.
          img: LM_U2_S3_P1_Slide_3.svg

        -
          title: Keeps people informed 
          text: By visibly displaying any changes, updates or modifications to work processes you can make sure that all employees are kept informed with the most up-to-date information. For example, if someone is a newly appointed fire warden, their photo should be added to a photo wall or similar so that employees are aware of the change. 
          img: LM_U2_S3_P1_Slide_4.svg

        -
          title: Focuses on performance measures and issues 

          text: Displays that show current targets and current productivity help to keep employees focused on their own efficiency and productivity. Updating this information in a timely fashion also helps them to adjust their own workload if necessary to meet performance measures and targets. 
          img: LM_U2_S3_P1_Slide_5.svg

        -
          title: Provides the same message to everyone 
          text: Often visual indicators are communal and visible to everyone. In this way they provide the same, standardised message to everyone. For example, think about the difference between a notice that displays safety measures and a manager telling each employee verbally. 
          img: LM_U2_S3_P1_Slide_6.svg

    title: How we learn visually
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      While it is generally thought that different people learn more effectively in different ways, visual learning still has a number of significant benefits over other forms of learning – particularly in the workplace!

      Visual indicators can be very useful for quickly and clearly displaying a huge variety of different things in the work environment including too much or too little inventory, whether processes are working normally or abnormally and even if machinery is either functioning or faulty.

      By having a clear and easy to follow visual system in place, employees are able to know exactly what is going on at a glance. It also allows workers to be more directly involved in problem solving as they can see what is going on without having to ask questions.
