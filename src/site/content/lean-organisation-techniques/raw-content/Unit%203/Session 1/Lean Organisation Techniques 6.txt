# Types of teams in the workplace

In order for teams to be effective, it is very important that team members know exactly what their **goals** are and what they must to do achieve them. 

Teams can ensure that they do this by adhering to seven key steps, as you saw on the previous page.

There are many types of team that may be found in organisations. The types of teams will vary according to their roles, objectives and operating timescales. ClickTap on each heading to learn more about five different types of team.

#### Improvement teams

Improvement teams are created to make **targeted improvements** in a work environment. These teams focus on specific processes that team members know most about. For example, in the public sector teams may improve the way patients are ‘processed’ in A&E (Health). In the private sector, the team may focus on improving the delivery of a product. When an approach called **Total Quality Management** was popular, the improvement teams were often referred to as **Quality Circles.**

These teams may be in continuous existence or they may be created to address a particular process that needs to be improved.

#### Management teams

Many organisations have groups of managers who come together **periodically** to exchange information and points of view. This type of management team is often ineffective.

However, some management teams specify issues and timescales (objectives), and ensure that managers have clear roles and responsibilities within the team. The managers have the ability to look at the way many tasks interact and how processes relate to each other. Managers can ensure that an organisation’s processes work well together so that everything runs efficiently. 

#### Project teams 

A project team is usually created to address a specific problem or challenge that cannot easily be addressed by a group of employees currently working together. 

The project team should have a clearly defined **objective** and the team members should possess the skills, resources and authority to achieve it. Once the objective has been achieved, the project team is likely to be disbanded. Sometimes, organisations have project teams who move from one project to another. 

#### Task teams 

Task teams are **time-limited teams** made up of people from across two or more functions. Often, the objective of the task team is to resolve an issue that overlaps between two or more groups, teams, departments or processes. 

The task team may implement changes or make recommendations to senior decision makers. In a rapidly changing environment, task teams may stay ‘on call’ to address any new problems or challenges that may arise.

#### Work unit teams 

In work unit teams, the team members tend to work together most of the time in a particular business function or process. These teams may have ongoing objectives – such as continuously improving performance – or have a specific remit/task.

These teams often have names that relate to their functions, such as ‘customer service team’, ‘the sales team’ or the ‘quality inspection team’. They tend to be permanent teams.

### In summary ![Summary](../../images/icons/icon-summary.svg)

Although types of team vary, they share one main aim – to improve the performance of the organisation and enhance the experience of the customer. Within each team, there are employees with different skills. Therefore, team members usually have different **team roles**. These roles make a significant contribution to the effectiveness of the team.
