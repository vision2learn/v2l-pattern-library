# Team effectiveness: McGregor’s key areas

  * __

Before we look at the types of team that may be found in the workplace, and the benefits of team working, we must identify the factors that contribute towards **effective team performance.**

Professor D.M. McGregor, author of the influential book _The Human Side of Enterprise_ and expert on organisational behaviour and performance, provides a detailed description of effective and ineffective teams.

McGregor’s findings can be summarised into **ten key areas** which highlight the characteristics of effective and ineffective teams. 

ClickTap along the slider below to learn more about Professor McGregor’s findings concerning **effective** and **ineffective** teams. 

  * ![Effective and Ineffective teams](../../images/unit03/session01/pg3-graphic1.svg) Effective teams Ineffective teams
  * #### 1\. Atmosphere – effective teams

The atmosphere is informal and relaxed. People behave professionally but are still friendly with each other and feel able to express themselves. 

#### 1\. Atmosphere – ineffective teams

The atmosphere is uncomfortable and tense. People may not talk to each other and may be afraid to express themselves. 

  * #### 2\. Discussion and participation – effective teams

There is plenty of relevant discussion with a high level of team participation.

#### 2\. Discussion and participation – ineffective teams

There is limited relevant discussion and limited participation.

  * #### 3\. Objectives and commitment – effective teams

The team objective is understood by the team, who are committed to achieving it. 

#### 3\. Objectives and commitment – ineffective teams

There is no clear objective or commitment.

  * #### 4\. Listening – effective teams

Contributions to discussions are welcomed. Team members listen to one another, demonstrating that they value what their colleagues have to say. 

#### 4\. Listening – ineffective teams

Team members tend not to listen to one another. Team members may even interrupt each other in conversation, or do not take their colleagues’ suggestions on board.

  * #### 5\. Conflict – effective teams

Conflict is not avoided but dealt with constructively in the open. As there is a friendly, relaxed atmosphere there is more effective communication between team members and thus conflict is minimised.

#### 5\. Conflict – ineffective teams

A lack of communication means that conflicts often fester or build up and thus lead to destructive interactions. 

  * #### 6\. Decision making – effective teams 

Decisions are made effectively and efficiently through group consensus. Everyone is involved in the decision-making process and the structure is non-hierarchical. 

#### 6\. Decision making – ineffective teams 

Decisions are made via other means and there is indecisiveness which results in wasted time. Employees have little or no input in the decision-making process. Decision making is a top-down, hierarchical approach where leaders have complete control. 

  * #### 7\. Ideas – effective teams

Ideas are openly shared and expressed.

#### 7\. Ideas – ineffective teams

Ideas are withheld or may be subject to open criticism. 

  * #### 8\. Leadership – effective teams

Leadership is not overly hierarchical or formal. There is greater focus on consensus. 

#### 8\. Leadership – ineffective teams

Leadership is focused on a formal leader. 

  * #### 9\. Progress and behaviour – effective teams

The team examines and monitors its own progress and behaviour. 

#### 9\. Progress and behaviour – ineffective teams 

There is little or no examination of progress or behaviour by team members. 

  * #### 10\. Employee turnover and absenteeism – effective teams

There is low employee turnover and absenteeism. 

#### 10\. Employee turnover and absenteeism – ineffective teams

There is higher staff turnover and absenteeism.

#### Work teams and lean

A team-based approach has become increasingly prevalent in UK workplaces because of the application of **total quality management initiatives.** The application of **lean methodologies** lends itself to teams of employees working together to make activities and processes less wasteful.

For these types of organisational teams, the most important way to check effectiveness is to assess whether the team and the organisation achieve their **objectives and goals. **

### In summary ![Summary](../../images/icons/icon-summary.svg)

Some characteristics of effective teams, highlighted by McGregor, include a good atmosphere, lots of discussion, a clear objective, resolution of conflict, decision-making through consensus, shared ideas, non-hierarchical leadership, self-monitoring of progress and behaviour and low employee turnover and absenteeism. Effective teams are also based around good working relationships, clear instructions and effective communication.
