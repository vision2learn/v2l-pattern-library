On the previous page we looked at the types of team that may be found in an organisation, such as, task, management or improvement driven teams. In addition to these different teams which are found in the workplace, people can also adopt different roles in these teams. We’ll now briefly look at the different roles within teams.

### Case study ![Case studyk](../../images/icons/icon-case-study.svg)

First let’s look at the different positions individuals may hold in a team.

#### Hospitality company

The hierarchy found in teams such as those in bars, pubs or clubs.

The team will focus on the same overall team objectives but will have different individual objectives. In this case bar staff (full and part time) are responsible for serving customers, keeping the bar clean and tidy and other duties such as changing kegs and restocking fridges. The bar and area managers will be concerned about profit, how much money they make each night and how they can encourage people to come to the bar.

#### Distribution company 

Such as a team in an online shopping company.

A team may also operate across an entire company, especially in small businesses. In this example, if one person were to neglect their role, then the customer may receive an incorrect order or they may not receive their order at all. The delivery officer relies on the post room coordinator to give them the correct post with the correct addresses, both team members rely on the packaging coordinator to ensure items are packaged appropriately for delivery.

The packaging coordinator relies on the order picker to find the right items in the warehouse and send them through to product packaging. All rely on the order processor to make sure the correct information is distributed to all teams. No position has more or less important as they are all needed to complete the order. 

#### Telesales company 

The hierarchy within a call centre which is sales driven.

Similar to organisations like bars and restaurants team members in call centres will have individual targets such as call time and number of sales. The managers, directors and CEO will have other objectives such as how to increase staff productivity, attract customers or additional business and how to make the organisation more profitable.

* * *

There are many different ways to classify team roles. All classification systems identify the fact that in a team, certain things need to be done in order for the team to be effective and healthy. These may be done by different teams as in the examples above or they may be carried out by different people depending on their role within their team.

Two main approaches to team roles are Team Management Systems (TMS) and Belbin.

TapClick to see some examples of the team roles within each approach.
