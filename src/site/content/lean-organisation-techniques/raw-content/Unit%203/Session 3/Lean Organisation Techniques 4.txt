# Styles of leadership

Every leader will have a different combination of the management competencies and qualities that you have learnt about so far in this Session. The ways in which managers lead their teams can be broadly categorised into three leadership styles.

TapClick through the slider to see more about each leadership style.

#### McGregor’s theory

We covered McGregor’s theory in Session 1 but we’ll revisit here. ClickTap the slider to learn more about McGregor’s X and Y theories.

  * ![Democratic](../../images/unit03/session03/pg4-graphic1.svg)

#### Democratic

This style of leadership is based upon mutual respect among colleagues regardless of their position. Here people are developed and encouraged to participate. There is an open approach to discussion and debate.

  * ![Autocratic](../../images/unit03/session03/pg4-graphic2.svg)

#### Autocratic 

Here the leader is not inclined to discuss or debate. Instead there are top-down orders. This has the benefit of leading to fast and decisive decisions.

  * ![Permissive](../../images/unit03/session03/pg4-graphic3.svg)

#### Permissive 

This is also sometimes called laissez-faire. The team members are 'free' to do their own thing. The team leader does not impose on others.

This table shows that one style is not more ‘correct’ than any other, but that all have their advantages and disadvantages.

Styles Frustration Benefits

Democratic
Action-oriented team members dislike ‘discussions’
Input and ideas from the many

Autocratic
‘Participative’ staff are alienated
Speed, clarity of purpose

Permissive
People may be purposeless
Creative people enjoy the freedom

It can be easy to try and put team leaders and managers into a single category. More successful team leaders treat these styles as options. They may identify certain team members who respond best to one style over the others and work situations that may require a particular style. Being able to change from one style to another gives a team leader a higher chance of success.

  * ![Theory X/Y assumptions](../../images/unit03/session03/pg4-graphic4.svg) Theory X  Theory Y 
  * #### Theory X assumptions

    People have an inherent dislike of work and avoid it if possible
    People prefer to be told what to do
    People try to avoid responsibility
    People are not ambitious
    People want security and nothing else
    People tend to require being controlled
    Control is achieved by the use of punishments and rewards

#### Theory Y assumptions 

    People can exercise self-control
    People can demonstrate imagination, ingenuity and creativity for the betterment of the organisation
    People in the workplace are attracted to both intrinsic rewards (self-fulfilment) and extrinsic rewards (pay / promotion / good working conditions)

  * #### Theory X assumptions

Manager motivates by:

    Authority
    Micro supervision
    Rules and regulations
    Punishment (threat of punishment).

#### Theory Y assumptions

Manager motivates by:

    Fairness and involvement
    Rewarding performance via bonuses and incentives.
