# Qualities to deliver team leadership

Pages:

  * __
  * __

In addition to the competences and functions of a team leader which you learnt about on the previous page, there are also the qualities required to lead a team and get things done. It is recognised that despite holding the competences or functions of a team leader, it is important that they also have some important personal qualities to be a good leader.

In her book _People and Self Management_, Sally Palmer describes the qualities of a team leader. They should be seen as complementary to the managerial competences rather than as alternatives to them. TapClick through the slider to learn about the ten team leader qualities.

  * ![Drive and enthusiasm](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic1.svg)

#### Drive and enthusiasm

For a team to work effectively, the team leader must have the drive and enthusiasm to keep things moving. If a team leader is enthusiastic it will have a direct impact on the levels of enthusiasm of team members. An unenthusiastic leader can have a negative impact on their team.

  * ![Honesty and integrity](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic2.svg)

#### Honesty and integrity

If the team leader does not have integrity and honesty, there will be mistrust within the team. Mistrust can lead to the withholding of information, suspicion and potential conflict which results in underperformance. When there is trust, team members will share information, support each other and cooperate. It is important that the team are led by the example of their leader.

  * ![Honesty and integrity](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic3.svg)

#### Reliability and dependability

A team leader needs to be trusted by everyone that they work with. This can be achieved through honesty as well as through being reliable and dependable. If team members know a leader will keep their word and always strive to do what is expected of them, it sets a good standard for working relationships.

  * ![Reliability and dependability](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic4.svg)

#### Fairness

A fair team leader will not favour certain people or behave in a biased way. The leader will seek to be impartial and not take sides. Their focus is on team performance, facts and evidence rather than individual personalities.

  * ![Communication skills](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic5.svg)

#### Communication skills

Communication underpins many team-based activities and actions. The efficient flow of information through an organisation depends on effective communication. The willingness to listen carefully and to take care to be understood are important attributes for a team leader. This commitment should apply to everyone in the team. 

  * ![People skills](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic6.svg)

#### People skills

A team leader needs to be a ‘people person’. The ability to work with people is just as important as focusing on objectives and completing technical tasks. The team leader must be able to work well with team members and key people outside the team. These people skills will include liking people, supporting and developing them.

  * ![Ability to make difficult decisions](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic7.svg)

#### Ability to make difficult decisions

When the team leader needs to make a decision they will gather facts and discuss them with the appropriate parties (such as team members and / or other team leaders). There will usually be options and the team leader should use their judgement to select the one which is most likely to deliver the team objectives.

  * ![Confidence](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic8.svg)

#### Confidence

A team leader should be openly confident in themselves and in the team. This external display of confidence encourages and energises everyone in the team. The team leader should always be on the lookout for confidence dropping and take action to rectify the situation. 

  * ![Vision](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic9.svg)

#### Vision

While team members should have a broad view of what the team is trying to achieve they can focus primarily on their personal tasks and responsibilities. The team leader needs to be able to see the ‘big picture’. This is in terms of the team and also in relation to other parts of the organisation. 

  * ![Sense of humour](../../images/unit03/session03/pg3-graphic10.svg)

#### Sense of humour

Having a sense of humour is not mandatory for a team leader. However, it can add value to a team because it helps to keep things in perspective and minimise overreaction. It can be encouraging for everyone if the team leader and team members are able to laugh at themselves!

## Theories of leadership qualities

Other experts have influenced the work of Sally Palmer and there is often overlap with the qualities identified necessary for good leadership. TapClick the headers to read about some other theories. 

#### Peter Scholtes

In his book _The Leader’s Handbook: Making Things Happen, Getting Things Done_, Peter Scholtes listed the traditional ‘competences’ of team leadership as:

  * Forcefulness
  * Motivation
  * Decisiveness
  * Wilfulness
  * Assertiveness
  * Results and bottom line orientated
  * Task orientated
  * Integrity and diplomacy

These qualities are still dominant in leadership and they are not necessarily incorrect, but they are seen as offering too little and not taking into account new ideas about people and organisations. 

#### Marilee B. Sprenger

New approaches have identified principles of leadership from the perspective of how people’s brains work. In her book _The Leadership Brain for Dummies_, Marilee Sprenger has identified leadership attributes found in great leaders. 

ClickTap to see these qualities.

1. **Be in touch. **Team members should know that that the team leader is with them. 

2. **Be visible.** Team members should see their leader regularly.

3. **Hone communication skills.** The team leader has to be a good listener as well as a good speaker. 

4. **Keep emotions in check. **Team leaders must lead themselves as well as the team members. 

5. **Inspire teams. **A leader needs followers. The team leader should walk with the team, not in front of them. 

6. **Sharpen decision-making skills.** Being able to make rational decisions and quick decisions is part of leading others.

7.** Place others’ needs above their own.** If team members believe their leader will help them, they will be more willing to try and achieve team objectives.

8. **Lead people where they want to go.** Often the team leader’s best work is the removal of team members’ self-imposed limitations.

This brain based approach highlights the personal and interpersonal attributes needed by a team leader. If these attributes are absent the team is very likely to underperform.
