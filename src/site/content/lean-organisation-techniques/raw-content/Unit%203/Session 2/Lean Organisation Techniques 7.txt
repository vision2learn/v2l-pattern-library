# How team members and leaders deal with process problems

### Processes

Issues relating to processes can affect the overall productivity of the organisation as well as team efficiency.

TapClick each heading to the right to learn more about process issues and how team members and team leaders might deal with them. The words written in bold refer to the attitudes and behaviours outlined on [page 3](pg3.asp).

TapClick through the slides below to see some examples of the way in which team leaders and team members might address some workplace relationship issues.

#### Authority levels for decision making are unclear. 

Team members Team leaders

Team members should ask other team members and the team leader for clarification.   
  
There is a focus on **communication, listening, evidence** and **feedback**. 
If the decision-making authority is clear, the team leader needs to take time to work with team members to ensure that they understand it.   
  
If authority levels are unclear, team leaders may be able to clear them up themselves, but if the team leaders do not have the authority to make changes, they must speak to the appropriate managers.  
  
There is a focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **team context**. 

#### Cross-team communications fail to function. 

Team members Team leaders

Initially, team members should seek to instigate the appropriate communications with other teams.   
  
The focus should be on **openness, trust, communication, constructive feedback, listening** and **evidence.**
If the communication is not resolvable by team members or is due to structural or technical problems that need greater authority to rectify them, the team leader can speak to other team leaders and/or managers   
  
If the team leader finds that the problem is due to the negative behaviour of team members, then they may be subjected to disciplinary actions.  
  
There is a focus on **openness, trust, communication, constructive feedback, listening, evidence** and **team context. **

#### Conflict resolutions processes are substandard.

Team members Team leaders

Team members can highlight problems and offer ideas but rarely have the authority to change processes unless they have a significant degree of authority.   
  
Focus on **trust, evidence, communication** and **cooperation**. 
In many teams, the team leader may be able to refine or re-create conflict resolution processes.   
  
If the organisation already has a conflict resolution process, then the team leader could make managers aware of the issues and suggest possible improvements.   
  
Focus on** openness, trust, communication, constructive feedback, listening, evidence** and **team context.**

#### Co-ordinating activities and monitoring processes are not adhered to. 

Team members Team leaders

Team members can professionally point out, with appropriate evidence, the ways others are not coordinating, or monitoring, in the correct way. They can explain the correct approach and encourage and help others to do things correctly. If certain team members continue to fail to comply with processes, their colleagues may bring it to the attention of a team leader.  
  
Focus on **trust, openness, constructive feedback and communication** and **evidence**. 
A team leader is responsible for ensuring that key activities and processes are adhered to. If the team leader notices non-compliance, then action should be taken. First, the issue will be brought forward and training may be offered. Ultimately, a failure to comply may be a disciplinary issue.  
  
If a team leader fails to ensure key activities and monitoring processes then they may be dealt with by other team leaders or managers.  
  
Focus on **openness, trust, communication, constructive feedback, listening, evidence, team objectives** and Focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **listening**.

### In summary ![summary](../../images/icons/icon-summary.svg)

__

__

__

__

Conflict and difficult situations can be hard to deal with. Remember that many of the solutions available to team members are equally available to team leaders. However, team members and team leaders may face different problems: team members may face more productive or time pressure related issues where as team leaders may deal with more operational or directional problems in addition to internal team problems. Both members and leaders should focus on using open and honest communication, and when team members are unable to deal with issues themselves they should raise the issue with their team leader. They may need to provide evidence when bringing issues to the attention of the team leader.
