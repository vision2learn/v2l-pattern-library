# How team members and leaders deal with relationship problems.

Pages:

  * __
  * __

Workplace relationships are important within organisations as they help to positively support the countless interactions that can occur in the workplace.

When dealing with relationship related issues team members should focus on trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence and listening. There is a team leader focus on trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence, listening, cooperation and team context.

TapClick through the slides below to see some examples of the way in which team leaders and team members might address some workplace relationship issues. Each word written in bold refers to the attitudes and behaviours outlined on [page 3](pg3.asp).

  * ![Team members have negative feelings about one another](../../images/unit03/session02/pg6-graphic1.svg) Team members have negative feelings about one another. 
  * #### Team member

The team members with the problem may try to address it themselves. The aim should be to make the other aware of any negative feelings and search for solutions in terms of the team objectives. 

Focus on **trust, team objectives, evidence, mutual respect** and **listening**.

#### Team leader

The team leader may have to work with the unhappy team members to get them to focus on team and individual objectives rather than personal differences or disagreements.

They should emphasise **openness, listening** and **communication**. 

  * ![Team members do not believe that their individual needs are being met](../../images/unit03/session02/pg6-graphic2.svg) Team members do not believe that their individual needs are being met. 
  * #### Team member

Team members should make notes or provide evidence outlining how they feel their needs are not being met, or how this can be resolved, and raise the issue with their team leader.

Focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **listening**.

#### Team leader

The team leader can explore potential unmet needs. 

It may be possible that the team leader has the authority to make changes that address the issue(s).

If the issue needs greater authority, the team leader can communicate with an appropriate manager. 

  * ![Team climate discourages openness and sharing of information](../../images/unit03/session02/pg6-graphic3.svg) Team climate discourages openness and sharing of information. 
  * #### Team member

Team members could try and address this but the lack of openness may work against them. 

Focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **listening**.

#### Team leader

A team leader has the responsibility to promote openness and information exchange. 

If openness is not encouraged the team leader should take action using skills in **listening** and **communication**.

  * ![Teams](../../images/unit03/session02/pg6-graphic4.svg) Personality clashes are given a priority by team members. 
  * #### Team member

Team members should seek to communicate constructively. They should try to provide evidence of personality clashes and show how it may undermine the team. 

Focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **listening**.

#### Team leader

Team leaders should show evidence of personality clashes and how they can undermine the team or performance. The team leader can explain that these occurrences will not be tolerated and ultimately could result in sanctions or disciplinary action.
