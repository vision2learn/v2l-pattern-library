# LEAN ORGANISATION TECHNIQUES

Unit 3: Working in business teams

# How team members and leaders deal with purpose problems 

Pages:

  * __
  * __

Problems can occur within organisations when employees do not understand or do not accept the **purpose** or **key objectives**.

Open and honest communication is vital when establishing, understanding and agreeing team purpose. There is a focus on listening to others and working within a team context. Each word written in bold refers to the attitudes and behaviours outlined on [page 3](pg3.asp).

#### The team purpose is not **understood**

![Thumbs down](../../images/unit03/session02/pg4-graphic1.svg) ![Thumbs up](../../images/unit03/session02/pg4-graphic2.svg)

#### Team member 

If team members do not understand the team purpose they may ask other team members or the team leader for more information. 

Individual team members can help other members should they become confused.

If there is a disagreement in team purpose between team members, then a team leader or other authority should confirm the true purpose clearly and make sure everyone understands. 

Team members should use shared knowledge, co-operative behaviour, openness, listening skills and effective communication.

#### Team leader 

The team leader should brief team members and check that they understand the team purpose.

If the members still don’t understand, the team leader could make an effort to explain the purpose in a different way.

If there is no team purpose, action should be taken to establish one. 

There should be a focus on team context, openness, communication and listening.

#### The team purpose is not **accepted**

![Thumbs down](../../images/unit03/session02/pg4-graphic3.svg) ![Thumbs up](../../images/unit03/session02/pg4-graphic4.svg)

#### Team member 

All members should make an effort to accept the team purpose and encourage those who do not. 

Team members may try to emphasise to others the value of the team purpose. 

Others may explain why they do not accept the team purpose.

**Communication and team context is important when dealing with this issue.**

#### Team leader 

Team leaders should seek to work with team members to establish an acceptable team purpose. If there are thoughtful objections based on evidence, the team leader may communicate these to managers outside the team. If the view of the organisation and team is that the purpose will stay as it is, then the team members who do not accept it will either have to accept the purpose or work outside the team. 

There is a focus on **team objectives, trust and openness, communication, listening and team context.**

#### **Team objectives** are not understood or accepted.

![Thumbs down](../../images/unit03/session02/pg4-graphic5.svg) ![Thumbs up](../../images/unit03/session02/pg4-graphic6.svg)

#### Team member 

Team meetings or discussions may be helpful in this case. Team members may ask colleagues or a team leader for explanations and clarifications. If the team member understands the objective, but does not accept it, they may explain why they do not accept it.

There should be a focus on **openness, communication, listening** and **sharing knowledge**.

#### Team leader 

The team leader will work with team members to explain the key objectives. If there are thoughtful objections based on evidence, the team leader may communicate these to managers outside the team. If the view of the organisation and team is that the key objectives will stay as they are, the team members who do not accept them will have to learn to work to them or work outside the team. 

There is focus on** trust, key objectives, openness, communication, listening** and **team context.**

Notice how the same recurring approaches and perspectives provide ways of solving different problems.
