# Self-management

The effectiveness of a team is also partly dependent on **self-management**. Self-management should be reviewed alongside team and individual contributions. 

It helps to think of three simple phrases: 

![Self-management](../../images/unit03/session02/pg10-graphic1.svg)

There are several methods and techniques that may be used to help employees manage their time – and themselves – effectively. Three of the most commonly used are **planners, to-do lists** and **diaries**.

TapClick on the images below to learn more about each tool. 

![oneshow](../../images/unit03/session02/pg10-graphic2.svg) ![twoshow](../../images/unit03/session02/pg10-graphic3.svg) ![threeshow](../../images/unit03/session02/pg10-graphic4.svg)

#### Planners

Once an individual has planned out their potential tasks and activities, they should be able to set **priorities**. They should also be able to calculate how much time will be required to do each task. With this information, they can then plan their day and week, and create a written document that maps out what they will do, when they will do it and how long it should take. Once the plan has been drafted, it makes sense to check it is acceptable to a supervisor or manager. 

#### To do lists

A quick and easy technique is to take work tasks and list them in order of priority. The task with the highest priority goes at the top of the list and the tasks diminish in priority as they move down the list. Once the list has been written, the individual simply works their way through the highest task on the list. When it has been completed they move to the next task. This offers a clearly defined structure for work-based activities.

#### Diaries

Keeping an accurate record of tasks, how well they are done and how long it takes to do them can be a valuable time management tool. This may be done by making notes at the end of a working day while the activities can still be easily recalled. Another method is to have a structured hour-by-hour log, like this:

Time Activity/task Performance

09.00

10.00 

11.00

12.00

13.00

* * *

### The importance of reviewing time management 

By reviewing a diary or time log, an employee can assess whether they were being **efficient** and **effective**. They can decide whether things need to change, and may discuss their observations with their team leader, a supervisor or a manager. They should aim to **learn** from this process and make **appropriate changes** to future plans. 

At all times, it is important that individuals keep others informed about their progress. This is because their work may have a direct impact on co-workers, other departments and, ultimately, customers. If they are having difficulty hitting deadlines, the **sooner** they can inform managers, the quicker actions can be taken to prevent delays.

### In summary ![summary](../../images/icons/icon-summary.svg)

Remember that in lean organisations, reviews of team and individual contributions are undertaken so that effective contributions can be acknowledged, poor performance can be corrected and further improvements can be made. Teams are reviewed according to objectives based on their expected roles, goals and behaviour. It is also important that team members manage themselves effectively and review their self-management. 

### Assessment key point ![assessment](../../images/icons/icon-assessment-flag.svg)

For your Assessment, you must be able to (2.3) review a team’s overall effectiveness and individual contributions.

When thinking about the effectiveness of certain team members, you should think about the objectives of the team and consider how well each team member contributes to achieving those objectives.
