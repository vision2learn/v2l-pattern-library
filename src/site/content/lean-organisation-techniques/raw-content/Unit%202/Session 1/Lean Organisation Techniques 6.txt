# Kaizen

An organisation can develop a mind-set and approach that seeks to continuously find and implement improvements.

There has been a significant commitment to **continuous improvement** in Japan. During the post-World War II period, American business experts like William Shewhart and W.E. Deming shared their ideas with Japanese industrialists. A significant outcome of this union was the emergence of **Kaizen**.

### What is Kaizen?

The term ‘**Kaizen**’ means ‘gradual and orderly continuous improvement’. It comes from the words ‘kai’ (which means ‘continuous’) and ‘zen’ (which means ‘good’). 

In Japan, the word carries the meaning of **change, renewal and spirit**. Adopting a Kaizen approach in the workplace brings a spirit of renewal. This means the organisation should operate within a culture where change and continuous improvement are the norm.

### Continuous improvement

Kaizen encourages organisations to continually seek to improve their organisation and processes. Businesses should always be looking to make all areas of their organisation better.

### People

Employees need to have a voice and decision-making should not just be restricted to management. Information should flow between the customers, people in the organisation and the suppliers.

### Change

Change is good! Change should not be feared and avoided, but should be gradual. It is necessary for developing and improving businesses.

### Eliminating waste

The less waste, the better! Resources should be used as efficiently as possible. As waste is eliminated in a business, value is added.

The term ‘Kaizen’ means ‘gradual and orderly continuous improvement’. Adopting a Kaizen approach in the workplace means that the organisation should operate within a culture where change and continuous improvement are the norm. Kaizen is a new way of thinking. Decisions about day-to-day activities may be made by the people doing the activities, rather than by the managers at the top of the organisation. Therefore, information flows both up and down and back and forth throughout the organisation, rather than simply from the top-downwards.
