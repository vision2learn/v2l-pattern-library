# Planning an observation

Pages:

  * __
  * __

Every workplace must ensure that the work environment is organised. One of the most effective ways of making this happen is by using the **5C/5S technique**. 

The 5C/5S technique is a systematic **approach** that can be used to stabilise, maintain and, ultimately, **improve the work environment**.

You will learn more about the 5C/5S technique on Page 4. First, clicktap to learn about the first stage of improving workplace organisation: **planning an observation**.

### Steps in the planning process

1.The workplace environment must be defined.

2.Any health and safety requirements must be checked.

3.A ‘clear-out’ area must be established.

4.Categories for observation must be considered (for example, equipment, people etc.).

5.The people who will be involved must be considered.

6.Necessary equipment must be gathered.

7.A timescale of the observation (and reasons for that timescale) must be decided upon.

Once the planning steps have been completed, the organisation may devise an **observation checklist**, which looks like this:

The purpose of the organisation checklist is to identify **potential problems** and consider **potential solutions** and the **likely benefits**. Ultimately, the checklist is used to keep finding ways to improve the workplace organisation.

Once the observations have been completed, it helps to review the findings and prepare a report. The report will summarise the **main observations, findings** and **suggested solutions**. It may also identify **priorities for action**.
