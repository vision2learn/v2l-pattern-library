You have already learnt about the five stages that make up the 5C/5S approach and the various factors that must also be considered.

Now, let’s learn about the first three stages of the 5C/5S approach in more detail. 

The first three stages focus on **setting and maintaining the initial standards**.

Clear out 

Configure 

Clean & Check 

The first stage, ‘Clear Out’ is all about separating essential items from non-essential items. The aim of this stage is to clarify what is necessary and what is unnecessary in a given work area. 

Essential items should be checked to ensure that they are safe and fully operational. Any unwanted items (such as surplus raw materials, equipment and spare parts) should be disposed of in an appropriate manner. 

ClickTap here to see the steps that organisations must take as part of the ‘Clear Out’ stage. 

  1. It should be ensured that the workplace is safe and that health and safety procedures are fully understood.
  2. All items must be moved to a ‘clear out’ area.
  3. Items should be separated out into two categories: essential and non-essential.
  4. A designated quarantine area should be created for items that are not needed in the work area.
  5. The workplace should be cleaned down and assessed. Essential tools and machinery should also be assessed.
  6. ‘Red tags’ should be attached to any abnormal conditions.
  7. A red tag log should be used to monitor tagging activities and help detect repeat problems.

Once you have completed the Clear Out phase, you are ready to move on to the **Configure** phase.

The purpose of this second stage is to **find a place for everything** in order to achieve **improved efficiency**. Improved efficiency is the result of more effective use of the workspace, equipment and inventory. 

ClickTap here to see the typical steps that make up the ‘Configure’ stage.

  * Essential items must be returned to the workplace.
  * When locating items, safety should be evaluated.
  * Frequency of use (ergonomics) should be considered.
  * Clear visual management systems should be created.

The workplace should be organised in such a way that minimises any unnecessary reaching, walking and bending. When deciding where items should be located, the safety and protection of staff and the prevention of damage are of paramount importance.

Any stored items should be **accessible, visible, classified and labelled**. Cords should be attached to tools, items should be stacked correctly and colour coding should be implemented. The size of containers and the way they are moved is very important and **double handling** should be avoided.

The aim of this stage is to **clean all the essential items and equipment**. This makes it possible to **identify any abnormal conditions**. For example, some surface liquid on the floor could be the result of a spillage that needs to be avoided or a leak that must be repaired.

ClickTap here to see the steps that make up the ‘Clean and Check’ stage.

  1. The current conditions of the workplace must be reviewed.
  2. A check sheet must be devised.
  3. The workplace should be manually cleaned and checked.
  4. Any abnormal conditions should be recognised.
  5. Abnormal conditions should be eliminated (with immediate effect).
  6. Action should be taken to eliminate red tags.

Once the ‘clean and check’ activities have been completed, the new **standard conditions** are established. These are the operating standards of the clean and organised work environment.

With the development of new standard conditions, a **check sheet** can be created. The check sheet is used to assist with audits. Each employee is responsible for cleaning and checking the appropriate items listed on the check sheet, and for maintaining their own neat and tidy workplace.

### Stats and facts ![Stats and facts](../../images/icons/icon-stats.svg)

  * In many organisations which apply the 5C/5S technique, there is a **5-minute clean up routine**. Each person develops their own ‘Clean and Check’ routine for each work day, made up of a list of timed and documented actions. 
  * A simple way to assess the work area before and after the application of the 5C/5S technique is by taking before and after photographs.
