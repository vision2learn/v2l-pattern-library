# The 5C/5S technique 

Pages:

  * __
  * __

When aiming to create a clean, orderly and logical workplace, it helps to have a structured, **systematic approach** in order to identify where and how improvements can be made. 

A systematic approach focuses on the **factors that influence a more organised workplace**. One such approach is the **5C/5S technique**, which enables workers to set appropriate workplace **standards** then identify any negative variations or abnormalities. Once these have been identified, it is possible to take action to correct them. 

### Factors that influence an organised workplace 

  * The people
  * The jobs that are done
  * The order in which jobs are done
  * The physical space required to do jobs
  * The tools, equipment and materials needed to carry out jobs
  * The use of shared resources
  * Storage space

### The 5C/5S technique

The 5C/5S technique is based on five Japanese words: seiri, seiton, seiso, seiketsu and shitsuke.

Organisations use the 5C/5S technique to create an efficient and safe work environment. The selection of work areas to be addressed is often prompted by a work environment which is noticeably untidy and cluttered.

ClickTap here to see some more examples of means by which areas in need of improvement may be identified.

  * Issues highlighted by safety inspections
  * Quality reviews
  * Late, partial or erroneous delivery
  * Low productivity
  * Problems arising from 6 Sigma/SPC/problem solving techniques
  * Workplace observations or audits.

The 5C/5S technique enables all employees to take ownership of their immediate workplace. Any abnormal conditions may be identified and corrected, and this provides a solid foundation for any **continuous improvement programme**.

__

The 5C/5S technique is a systematic approach that is used within organisations to stabilise, maintain and, ultimately, improve the work environment. It enables workers to set workplace standards which are then used to identify and correct any negative variations or abnormalities. This contributes towards the continuous improvement of the organisation. The 5C/5S technique is made up of five key stages: Clear out, Configure, Clean and Check, Conformity and Custom and Practice.
