#### Standard operations

This is an element of an overall task that is carried out **repetitively**. A standard operation does not offer information about how to carry out a task. 

In car assembly, a standard operation may be to ‘fit the steering wheel’.

#### Standard operating procedures

This gives instructions about how to complete the task in the safest, most efficient manner with the minimum of variance.

The SOP would offer detailed instructions about how to fit the steering wheel. 

* * *

#### Standard operating procedures 

The use of standard operating procedures may bring considerable benefits to an organisation. 

TapClick to take a look at some reasons for creating SOPs.

1.They provide people with all the safety, health, environmental and operational information necessary to perform a job properly and to enable quality standards to be maintained. 

2.They ensure that production operations are performed consistently to maintain quality control of processes and products. 

3.They ensure that processes continue uninterrupted and are completed on a prescribed schedule. 

4.They ensure that no failures occur in manufacturing and other processes that would harm anyone in the surrounding community. 

5.They ensure that approved procedures are followed in compliance with company and government regulations. 

6.They serve as a training document for teaching users about the process for which the SOP was written. 

7.They serve as a checklist for co-workers who observe job performance, and as a checklist for auditors. 

8.They serve as a historical record of an existing process, so there is a factual basis for revising those steps when a process or piece of equipment is changed. 

9.They serve as an explanation of steps in a process, so they can be reviewed in accident investigations. 

By creating SOPs, it is possible to develop a safer and more **efficient** environment where products and services are delivered to consistent **standards**.

SOPs provide a framework for **continuous improvement**. They can be monitored, reviewed and amended where necessary. By recording these actions, an organisation may safeguard against repeated errors. 

Some activities are not standardised in this way, such as the discussions between sales staff and customers, and sales prices, which need to allow for variation and flexibility.
