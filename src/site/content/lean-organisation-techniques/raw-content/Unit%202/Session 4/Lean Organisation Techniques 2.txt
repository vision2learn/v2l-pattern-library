# Consistency and predictability 

Pages:

  * __
  * __

Consistency and predictability are very important within organisations. A lack of consistency and predictability is undesirable to customers and employees. When people operate in impractical ways, and supplies have a high degree of variation, there is the potential for chaos.

Read on to learn more about the importance of consistently and predictability.

Imagine a customer going to a fast food franchise and ordering a specific set meal. When their meal arrives, the burger is 4oz (not 8oz), the drink is a small milkshake rather than a diet Cola, and no fries are provided. The customer would be disappointed and would probably choose to go elsewhere the next time they decide to go out for fast food.

![](../../images/unit02/session04/pg2-graphic1.svg)

When there are high levels of variation in a business process, such as in the fast food franchise, the likelihood of problems and dissatisfaction is increased. Fast food franchises – like all organisations – must provide consistency and predictability through standardisation. In this way, internal and external customers get exactly what they expect and need, enabling the organisation to operate more effectively.

#### Case study ![](../../images/icons/icon-case-study.svg)

Tom is a chocolatier who owns a company that creates a range of products. When Tom first started his business, he received some complaints from customers about one particular chocolate bar product that often varied in size. Following the complaints, Tom made an effort to ensure that the size of the chocolate bar in question was standardised. Since doing so, he has received no more complaints and sales of the chocolate bar have increased.
