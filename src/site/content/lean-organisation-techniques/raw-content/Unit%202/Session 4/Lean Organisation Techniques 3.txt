# Standardisation

Pages:

  * __
  * __

Standardisation is in operation in every aspect of organisations. There are certain standards that we all tend to accept automatically. These standards are described as universal For example, photocopiers have a setting for A4 paper and organisations usually use A4 copier paper. This consistency and predictability saves people time and energy. 

Organisations may also use other standards in addition to these ‘universal standards’. 

TapClick the slider below to learn more about these additional standards. 

  * ![](../../images/unit02/session04/pg3-slide1.svg)

#### Standardisation and design 

Components may be designed so that they could be used in a range of products/models. For example, a car door lock may be standardised so that it could be used in a variety of cars and models. 

  * ![Standardisation and Processes](../../images/unit02/session04/pg3-slide2.svg)

#### Standardisation and processes 

Using similar methods to produce similar components makes it possible to standardise machine tooling. 

  * ![Standardisation and Systems](../../images/unit02/session04/pg3-slide3.svg)

#### Standardisation and systems 

Using the same systems (such as computer systems) across plants and/or countries enables companies to use standard approaches to training, maintaining and upgrading.

  * ![Standardisation and Components](../../images/unit02/session04/pg3-slide4.svg)

#### Standardisation and components 

It is possible to have components that are identical in shape and size but made in different materials. They could be colour-coded for prompt identification. 

  * ![Standardisation and Procedures](../../images/unit02/session04/pg3-slide5.svg)

#### Standardisation and procedures 

Organisations may introduce standard procedures in order to solve problems, reduce set up times or identify improvements. The **PDSA** approach (Plan, Do, Study, Act) is often used in business improvement organisations.
