# Standard operating procedures (SOP)

Pages:

  * __
  * __

You now know all about standard operating sheets. Now let’s look at **standard operating procedures** in more detail. 

Standard operating procedures make a significant contribution to organisations. They provide a foundation for **lowering variability**, **enhancing consistency** and **improving efficiency **within an organisation. 

TapClick through the slider to see some of the features of SOPs and what they’re used for in organisations.

  * ![Slide 1](../../images/unit02/session04/pg8-slide1.svg)

A written document is at the heart of most SOPs. However, they can also include charts, graphs, process maps and photographs. 

  * ![Slide 2](../../images/unit02/session04/pg8-slide2.svg)

SOPs can be developed in all types of businesses and any type of work area, such as providing food in a fast food restaurant, vehicle assembly and processing invoices. 

  * ![Slide 3](../../images/unit02/session04/pg8-slide3.svg)

In some organisations, SOPs are used from the design to production and distribution process. This provides a blueprint for recreating processes, created by a multi-disciplinary team. 

  * ![Slide 4](../../images/unit02/session04/pg8-slide4.svg)

One danger with the use of SOPs is that employees may become bored with doing the same repetitive task over and over. This would need to be checked in reviews or audits.

### Sample SOP for serving food

**PURPOSE:** To prevent food borne illness by ensuring that all foods are served in a sanitary manner.

**SCOPE:** This procedure applies to food service employees who serve food. 

**INSTRUCTIONS:**

  1. Train food service employees on using the procedures in this SOP. Refer to the ‘Using and Calibrating Thermometers SOP’. 
  2. Follow state or local health department requirements.
  3. Follow the employee health policy.
  4. Wash hands before putting on gloves, each time the gloves are changed, when changing tasks, and before serving food with utensils. Refer to the ‘Washing Hands SOP’.
  5. Avoid touching ready-to-eat foods with bare hands. Refer to the ‘Using Suitable Utensils when Handling Ready-To-Eat Foods SOP’. 
  6. Handle plates by the edge or bottom; cups by the handle or bottom; and utensils by the handles. 
  7. Store utensils with the handles up or by other means to prevent contamination. 
  8. Hold potentially hazardous food at the proper temperature. Refer to the ‘Holding Hot and Cold Potentially Hazardous Foods SOP’.
  9. Serve food with clean and sanitised utensils.
  10. Store in-use utensils properly. Refer to the ‘Storing In-Use Utensils SOP’. 
  11. Date mark and cool potentially hazardous foods or discard leftovers. Refer to the ‘Date Marking Ready-to-Eat, Potentially Hazardous Foods, and Cooling Potentially Hazardous Foods SOPs’.

**MONITORING:** A designated food service employee will visually observe that food is being served in a manner that prevents contamination during all hours of service.

**CORRECTIVE ACTION:**

  1. Retrain any food service employee found not following the procedures in this SOP. 
  2. Replace improperly handled plates, cups, or utensils. 
  3. Discard ready-to-eat food that has been touched with bare hands.
  4. Follow the corrective actions identified in the Washing Hands; Using Suitable Utensils When Handling Ready-To-Eat Foods; Date Marking Ready-to-Eat, Potentially Hazardous Foods; Cooling Potentially Hazardous Foods; and Holding Hot and Cold Potentially Hazardous Foods SOPs.

**VERIFICATION AND RECORD KEEPING:** The food service manager will periodically check the storage and use of utensils during service. In addition, the food service manager will complete the ‘Food Safety Checklist’ daily. The ‘Food Safety Checklist’ is to be kept on file for a minimum of 1 year.

**Date Implemented: _____________________ By: _____________________ **

**Date Reviewed: _____________________ By: _____________________ **

**Date Revised: _____________________ By: _____________________ **
