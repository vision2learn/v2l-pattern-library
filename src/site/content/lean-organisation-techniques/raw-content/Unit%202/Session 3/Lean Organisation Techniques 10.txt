# Utilising effective VMTs 

Pages:

  * __

As you have already learned, there are a wide range of different visual management techniques that a business can utilise depending on their needs and circumstances. 

No matter which VMTs are chosen there are still commonalities between them when it comes to utilising them effectively.

ClickTap the sliders on this page to learn more. 

### Clarity of display

#### What is it?

Whatever visual data is being displayed it should be clear and visible to everyone who needs to see it. This not only means that it should be placed in a position where employees can see it, but also that the data shown should be clear and straight to the point.

Also, symbols like arrows or other visual aids should not be confusing, but be understood by everyone.

![Thumbs down](../../images/unit02/session03/pg10-thumbsdown.svg) ![Thumbs up](../../images/unit02/session03/pg10-thumbsup.svg)

#### When it’s effective

Employees in an office have asked for a more efficient way of displaying current sales figures and targets so that they can work more effectively. 

As a result, management began displaying the previous week’s figures on screens dotted around the office using computer software. The data was made as clear as possible, with large fonts and clear wording so that employees could understand exactly what was being displayed.

#### When it’s ineffective

The sales department at a stationery supplier prints out weekly sales data with copies left lying around in the staff room. This rather haphazard approach has proved problematic as copies are always going missing or accidentally getting thrown away.

On top of this, the data is presented in its raw form with no context provided such as how it compares to previous figures or targets.

### Updated where necessary

#### What is it?

Depending on the information being displayed, it may or may not need updating over time.

Information like productivity figures need updating at regular intervals so that employees remain informed and able to act on the most up-to-date information.

Other forms of VMT may need updating due to wear and tear or might no longer be relevant.

Nowadays, information on screens and computers can easily be updated. However, it is still necessary to update by hand things like handwritten notices and signs. 

![Thumbs down](../../images/unit02/session03/pg10-thumbsdown.svg) ![Thumbs up](../../images/unit02/session03/pg10-thumbsup.svg)

#### When it’s effective

A recruitment firm currently has an efficient visual system in place for the categorising and disposal of office waste. 

In a recent drive to be even more efficient and meet new environmental and data protection standards, this visual system has had to be updated.

There are now designated areas for paper waste that must be shredded for security reasons while more visual indicators have been added for different types of recyclable waste. 

#### When it’s ineffective

In a factory, new markings were introduced to designate where parts should be disposed and inventory should be stored. Often, the working conditions in the factory will get very hot from the heavy machinery and constant movement of parts.

This system also makes it difficult for employees who are out of the office to stay up to date with current sales figures.

As a result, markings and arrows have begun to peel off work areas and are increasingly difficult to read which can make things confusing for employees.

Currently, there is no system in place to oversee the replacement of VMTs or to maintain them.

* * *

### Trends

#### What is it?

Showing trends is all about putting information into context and presenting it in the most efficient way possible.

Presenting data in a simple graph or chart provides employees with information at-a-glance, giving an idea of general trends without getting bogged down in numbers and statistics.

![Thumbs down](../../images/unit02/session03/pg10-thumbsdown.svg) ![Thumbs up](../../images/unit02/session03/pg10-thumbsup.svg)

#### When it’s effective

Workers in a warehouse have a range of general output targets that they must meet on a rolling weekly basis. In order to keep employees informed about current targets and whether they’re being met, managers prepare charts and graphs from output data which are displayed on computer screens.

The charts include data from the previous two months so employees can see how figures are faring in both the long and short-term. 

By providing easy to understand charts, those in the warehouse don’t have to spend precious time pouring over numbers and data.

#### When it’s ineffective

In a different warehouse, management have opted to show weekly output statistics by comparing the last two week’s numbers.

Not only does this make it difficult for the workers to get a real idea of current trends and how their output is faring, it also means that the information is limited to the last two weeks only.

### In summary ![In summary ](../../images/icons/icon-summary.svg)

__

__

__

__

For visual management techniques to be effective they should be updated as and when required and display information in a clear and understandable way. 

If the visual indicator relates to information such as sales or output data then it should be presented in a way that provides an at-a-glance look at how well things are performing.
