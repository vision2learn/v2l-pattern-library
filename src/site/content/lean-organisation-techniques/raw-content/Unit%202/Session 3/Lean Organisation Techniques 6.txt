# Visual management techniques

Pages:

  * __
  * __

Visual management techniques (VMT) should form a major part of any business’ attempt at continual improvement. Better VMT helps work to flow and encourages work processes to become more efficient. The implementation of visual management techniques can come broadly under three sections – **inputs**, **process** and **outputs**. 

Different businesses have different VMT requirements as their inputs, processes and outputs differ.

### Inputs

#### People

VMTs for employees should focus on presenting them with relevant information that helps them to do their job effectively. Visual indicators such as photos of fire wardens and holiday charts are easily overlooked but, when implemented properly, can have a very real impact. 

Examples include:

  * Photographs of team members
  * Skills matrix
  * Holiday chart

### Inputs

#### Machines

Various VMTs can help ensure machinery is operated correctly and efficiently. Clear, visual data can prevent improper operation and cut down on damage and failure. This approach isn’t just relevant for manufacturing either; it can be applied to any piece of machinery in the workplace including computers, phones and even communal fridges! 

Examples include:

  * Shadow boards
  * 5C check sheet
  * Maximum / minimum levels on dials

### Inputs

#### Materials

When thinking about VMTs in the work environment, visual indicators on materials are possibly the first thing that comes to mind. Properly labelling materials and where they should go is both practical and good practice for health and safety. Whilst inventory indicators help to keep stocks consistent and prevents important tools and materials from running out. 

Examples include:

  * Maximum / minimum level markers
  * Inventory indicators
  * Location areas
  * Material identification

### Process

A crucial aspect of lean management is making work processes flow better so that they’re more efficient. VMTs relating to process can help by standardising how work is carried out and sharing best practice with all employees. Examples include easily understood operating instructions as well as clear safety displays. 

Examples include:

  * Standard operations
  * Operation / control limits
  * Visual aids / photographs of acceptable outputs

### Outputs

Outputs can be any outcome or service that the business provides. For example, the output at a car manufacturing plant will be completed cars off the production line, whilst in an IT department output could be completed service calls or repairs. Effective VMTs can keep employees updated on how the business is performing and encourage them to adapt their work processes if necessary. 

Examples include:

  * Sales charts
  * Productivity graphs
  * Customer satisfaction numbers
