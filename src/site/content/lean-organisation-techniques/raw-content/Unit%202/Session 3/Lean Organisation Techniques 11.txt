# Getting the cost right 

Pages:

  * __

On the previous page you thought about some of the potential costs of implementing a visual management system. It’s likely your ideas included the three main types of cost – **materials**, **training** and **staff**. 

Such costs are an inevitable consequence of implementing new visual management techniques. However, businesses can still look for ways to do things as efficiently as possible.

#### Whiteboards

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg11-slide1.svg)

When costs are being considered, businesses will often look into the cost of alternatives such as black and white printing over colour printing to make VMTs more cost-effective.

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg11-slide2.svg)

Similarly, techniques like attaching marker pens and erasers to whiteboards can ensure they are available and do not go missing. This sort of attention to detail can go a long way to making things more efficient.

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg11-slide3.svg)

If members of staff need to process information and turn it into graphs / diagrams or simply interpret the visual information, then they may need specific training. There may also be time allocated to induction training.

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg11-slide4.svg)

Once members of staff have been trained in the creation and interpretation of visual information, then there will be staff time required to collect, analyse and use the information.

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg11-slide5.svg)

One of the ways to offset some of the cost is to explore possibilities of applying the visual management systems in other parts of the organisation, or making adaptations for it to be applied elsewhere.

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg11-slide6.svg)

By taking a systematic approach that considers the views of all parties, it is possible for teams to identify visual management systems that may enhance performance but still be efficient and cost-effective.

While businesses should ensure that cutting costs doesn’t impact upon the quality of service provided there should still be attempts made to save money where possible. Central to continuous improvement is the concept of doing more with less; making processes more efficient and cost-effective while still improving the quality of service.
