# Introducing effective VMT

It pays to prepare systematically before introducing new visual management techniques to a team, department, process or any particular unit in a business. This is so that any new visual information is both relevant and necessary.

### Precisely define the information required

Preparation should begin with precisely defining the information that is required to maintain and improve performance. Each department, office or unit of the business will have its own set of valuable information that could help improve performance, but this is likely to differ throughout the business.

### Decide if presenting information visually will be effective

Almost all information can be presented in a visual way. However, it is still important to consider whether presenting certain information visually is the most effective thing to do. This can even stretch to whether it’s even necessary to present the information at all. Think about, as an example, a business putting up a ‘No swearing’ sign in its reception area. Some people might consider such a sign to be over the top and unnecessary as they already intuitively know not to do this anyway!

### Select an appropriate visual option

For any given problem, there is usually a wide-range of different visual options that can be used, although not all VMTs are suitable for all situations. Displaying important information incorrectly, or in a way that is unsuitable, can be counter-productive as employees may not take notice of other visual indicators as a result.

### Consider how often the information needs to be updated

The purpose of the information will determine how regularly it needs to be updated. If amendments and refinements need to be made quickly, as is the case with daily or weekly sales figures or charts, then updates will be required more regularly. However, if some things are stable and unlikely to change, then the visual information rarely needs to be updated.

### Think about where the information should be presented

Employees should be able to readily see and use information that is relevant to their role. It doesn’t make any sense to bombard employees with pointless or irrelevant information! If the information that needs to be displayed is general and has to be seen by everyone, then it needs to be placed in an area that is frequented by all employees.

### Keep in mind any future refinements

As with any other aspect of the work process, businesses looking for ways to continuously improve should also keep in mind any future refinements with their VMTs. Businesses cannot assume that the implementation of visual indicators will be done exactly right the first time; reviewing and updating VMTs can help to find alternatives or make improvements.
