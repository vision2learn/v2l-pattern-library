# Guiding principles of visual management

Pages:

  * __
  * __

As you’ve already learnt, there is a wide variety of different visual indicators that can be used in the work environment. The tricky part of visual management is choosing the visual methods that are appropriate and effective.

In order to choose appropriate visual indicators, it’s important to carefully consider exactly what outcomes the business requires. 

ClickTap to see some of these principles in more detail.

  * ![Correct](../../images/unit02/session03/pg4-slide1.svg)

#### Correct

First and foremost, any visual information should be correct. Incorrect visual indicators can come in all forms, from incorrectly labelled shelves to directional arrows that point the wrong way. Clearly, if visual methods are incorrect they can add extra time onto work processes, cause employees to not trust what is being displayed and even be a hazard to health.

  * ![Relevant](../../images/unit02/session03/pg4-slide2.svg)

#### Relevant

Providing target figures and work feedback from a different department isn’t going to be much use if it has no impact on someone’s work. In a more extreme example, something like a tyre pressure indicator that displays the temperature of the room is hardly going to be any use to a car mechanic!

  * ![Important](../../images/unit02/session03/pg4-slide3.svg)

#### Important

Visual information and feedback must be carefully chosen so only the most important information is displayed. Displaying irrelevant or unimportant information can not only cause employees to not pay attention to signs, displays and charts but can also cause them not to pay attention to the really important stuff as well! 

  * ![Keeps people informed](../../images/unit02/session03/pg4-slide4.svg)

#### Keeps people informed 

By visibly displaying any changes, updates or modifications to work processes you can make sure that all employees are kept informed with the most up-to-date information. For example, if the place where A4 photocopier paper is stored changes, then visual indicators relating to this should be changed as soon as possible.

### In summary ![Summary](../../images/icons/icon-summary.svg)

__

__

__

__

While visual management may sound like a rather simple aspect of lean, it can actually be quite a tricky thing to get right. All visual indicators must be carefully chosen so that they add value to the work environment and work processes.

Poorly chosen visual indicators can have a negative impact on the workplace as employees end up thinking that the information is not under their control. They may not understand the information or they might think it is irrelevant.
