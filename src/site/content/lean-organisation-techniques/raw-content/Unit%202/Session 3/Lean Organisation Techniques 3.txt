# Benefits of visual information

Pages:

  * __
  * __

Any work environment dedicated to continuous improvement should look for ways to present visual indicators as effectively and efficiently as possible. Practising good visual management brings with it a wide-range of real benefits to a business. 

ClickTap through the slider to learn about some of these benefits. 

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg3-slide1.svg)

#### Helps control the process

Visual indicators such as instructions on a wall or markings on the floor help to control and standardise work processes. They help to reduce any variations by making sure everyone is carrying out tasks in a similar way and as efficiently as possible.

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg3-slide1.svg)

#### Provides feedback

Visual displays on machinery, charts and graphs all help to provide employees with feedback about work processes. Indicators such as markings on machinery can help inform employees if things are functioning correctly or markers on stock can help keep on top of stock replenishment. 

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg3-slide1.svg)

#### Informs the initiation of action

Visual indicators can also help to initiate work processes or help inform employees about when to start different processes. For example, a pallet used for recyclables has a visual marker on it where, once the recycling reaches that marker, the pallet must be emptied.

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg3-slide1.svg)

#### Keeps people informed 

By visibly displaying any changes, updates or modifications to work processes you can make sure that all employees are kept informed with the most up-to-date information. For example, if someone is a newly appointed fire warden, their photo should be added to a photo wall or similar so that employees are aware of the change. 

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg3-slide1.svg)

#### Focuses on performance measures and issues 

Displays that show current targets and current productivity help to keep employees focused on their own efficiency and productivity. Updating this information in a timely fashion also helps them to adjust their own workload if necessary to meet performance measures and targets. 

  * ![Lean manufacturing](../../images/unit02/session03/pg3-slide1.svg)

#### Provides the same message to everyone 

Often visual indicators are communal and visible to everyone. In this way they provide the same, standardised message to everyone. For example, think about the difference between a notice that displays safety measures and a manager telling each employee verbally.

### How we learn visually

While it is generally thought that different people learn more effectively in different ways, visual learning still has a number of significant benefits over other forms of learning – particularly in the workplace!

Visual indicators can be very useful for quickly and clearly displaying a huge variety of different things in the work environment including too much or too little inventory, whether processes are working normally or abnormally and even if machinery is either functioning or faulty.

By having a clear and easy to follow visual system in place, employees are able to know exactly what is going on at a glance. It also allows workers to be more directly involved in problem solving as they can see what is going on without having to ask questions.
