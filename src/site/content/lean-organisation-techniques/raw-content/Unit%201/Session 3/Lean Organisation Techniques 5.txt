# Basic and detailed process maps

Pages:

  * __

The basic steps for creating a process map are the same whatever the type of map you are creating. The aim is to use a level of detail that meets the needs of your problem solving. Too much detail and you are bogged down with detail…too little and there is little useful information. 

There are considerable similarities between flowcharts and process maps. Some quality specialists do not differentiate between the two; they simply see them both as ways of understanding and improving business processes. 

### Creating a process map

To create a process map, you should follow ten steps. Each slide below contains a step, clicktap along to see each one:

  * ![Review the process](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide1.svg)

Review the process being studied and its boundaries as defined by your project. 

  * ![Have participants identify the steps](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide2.svg)

Have participants identify the steps in the process. Write each step on a card or Sticky note. 

  * ![Working as a team](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide3.svg)

Working as a team, arrange the steps in order (placing the cards / notes in order). 

  * ![Eliminate any duplicates](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide4.svg)

Eliminate any duplicates and agree on the wording being used. 

  * ![Agree and maintain the level of detail](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide5.svg)

Agree and maintain the level of detail to be used. 

  * ![Keep the process-flow moving](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide6.svg)

Keep the process-flow moving in one direction, usually left to right or top to bottom. You should only go in the opposite direction if a step needs to be repeated. 

  * ![Discuss the results](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide7.svg)

Discuss the results. Does it match the real situation that you know? Are adjustments required? 

  * ![Number the tasks sequentially](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide8.svg)

When steps 1 to 7 have been done, number the tasks sequentially through the most direct route. Then number the offline tasks. 

  * ![Transfer the whole process map to paper or computer](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide9.svg)

Transfer the whole process map to paper or computer. 

  * ![Date the map and record the names of those involved](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-slide10.svg)

Date the map and record the names of those involved.

### The basic structure of a process map

Below is a basic example of a basic process map. Remember, the functions represented by **circles are connectors** and the functions represented by **rectangles are activities**.

![Flowchart](../../images/unit01/session03/pg5-flowchart.svg)

A basic map will outline the major steps of a process. It is valuable when an overview is required. It is possible to go into greater detail when more information is needed. For a lean organisation, it is highly likely that more information will be required.

For a lean organisation, the processes are looked at in terms of them being value adding / non value adding or wasteful. To make these judgements, it is important to have accurate information flowing from the customer through the organisation.
