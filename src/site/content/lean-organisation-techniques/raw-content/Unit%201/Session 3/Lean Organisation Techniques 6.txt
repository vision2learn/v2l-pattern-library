Before we look at a practical example of a company using process mapping to improve productivity, it is important that we first consider the role customers have. 

So far we’ve looked at the basic structure of a process map. Next, we’ll aim to take a closer look at the process and build upon what we’ve learnt so far. 

### Taking a closer look at the process

#### Voice of the customer

Once the processes are mapped out, it is possible to look at them with the ‘voice of the customer’ in mind. If the feedback from the customer is analysed, it is possible to identify the factors that ‘add value’ for the customer. Once the factors are singled out, it is possible to look at the organisation’s processes that have the greatest impact on the customer experience of a product or service. 

![Voice](../../images/unit01/session03/pg6-voice.svg)

#### Collecting information 

Once the key processes are identified, it is possible to look at them in greater detail. Part of this activity may include the information collected during a Productivity Needs Analysis. This information results in:

  * High quality information on productivity outputs
  * Enables mapping of key organisational processes
  * Provides clear indications on what customers value

![Voice](../../images/unit01/session03/pg6-collecting.svg)

#### Value stream map

A process map that shows how a key organisational process delivers value to a customer is often referred to as a value stream map.

With this information, it is possible to look at the elements of a key process and ask whether the steps within it add value or are wasteful. If there is scope for improvements to be made then the staff involved can:

  * Identify the actions that need to be undertaken to make improvements
  * Organise the actions into an action plan
  * Take action
  * Monitor the effectiveness of the changes that have been made.

![Key](../../images/unit01/session03/pg6-key.svg)

### In summary ![In summary](../../images/icons/icon-summary.svg)

__

__

__

__

Imagine for a moment an organisation that has not:

  * Collected productivity data
  * Mapped out the key processes
  * Listened to the voice of the customer.

This organisation would have little understanding of what they should be doing to improve performance. Any one of the activities above will have some potential benefits. However, doing all three can have a significant impact on customers and the long term viability of the organisation.
