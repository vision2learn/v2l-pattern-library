### Case study ![Case study](../../images/icons/icon-case-study.svg)

On the next two pages we will look at a real life example of an organisation using a process map. 

Jarvis Marketing Company (JMC) is a marketing company that, among other services, sends out direct mail for a variety of clients. While a large proportion of clients are happy with the service they receive and there is a significant amount of repeat business, **there has been an increasing number of complaints and customers wanting reductions in their bills because of a poor service**.

When Bryan Connolly, a manager at JMC, spoke to the unhappy customers there was actually a lot of positive feedback.

They liked the design and content of the mailshots. Customers also thought that JMC were competitive on price.

The central complaint was that recently the mailshots were arriving late at the target market. Often, people would be contacted as a follow up to the mailer only to discover that it had not yet been received.

There was also some reference to the mailers being ‘stuffed’ into envelopes and appearing unprofessional.

More than one customer stated they would only use JMC again if they were given assurances that it would not happen again.

### Brainstorming session

As a starting point, a director of JMC decided that it would help to ‘get a picture’ of the mailing process for large quantities of envelopes. In no particular order, the list of the main stages of the mailing process were made in a ‘brainstorming session’. TapClick the first note to reveal the results of the session.

### Findings

In light of the brainstorming session, the manager found that there were no measures being recorded on how mailers were processed. The process had grown on an ad hoc basis and grown significantly in the last couple of years. If a large job came along people just did their best to get the mailer out.
