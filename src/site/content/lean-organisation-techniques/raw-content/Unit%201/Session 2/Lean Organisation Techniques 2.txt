# Conducting the PNA

A Productivity Needs Analysis (PNA) measures current productivity so that processes can be designed and implemented in order to create improvements. 

The PNA process helps us to understand ways that businesses can systematically, regularly improve their own productivity.

There are five steps which form the PNA process. 

Look at the five steps on the right to learn more about each of these steps.

### Measuring relevant info areas

In order to gauge whether any improvement has been made it is necessary to begin by measuring the relevant information areas. There are various types of information which indicate business performance and which can be measured, these will differ from business to business and across different sectors.

### Prioritise measures

Once the information areas have been measured these measures are then prioritised. There are four steps to prioritising measures. This will be covered in more detail on page 7 of this Session. 

### Set objectives

At this stage objectives will be set in order to make improvements.

### Processes to reach objectives (across scales of organisation).

Once productivity objectives have been set, specific processes will then be established across different scales of the organisation in order to achieve the set objective(s). 

### Deliver required outputs to reach desired productivity.

The aforementioned processes are now implemented and the required outputs are delivered to reach the desired levels of productivity.

  


The Productivity Needs Analysis is a great way to thoroughly identify gaps between how an organisation is currently performing and how it could be performing at its most productive. You will learn more about each of these five steps throughout the rest of this Session. Read on below to find out more about the first step.

## Beginning the process

If the productivity of an organisation is to be improved, then its productivity needs to be measured. 

The first step in the PNA process, and the first step to improving productivity, is to measure how productive the organisation currently is. We measure productivity by collecting and analysing information. 

Learn more about measuring performance and productivity by clickingtapping along the slider to the right.

  * ![Slide number 1](../../images/unit01/session02/pg2-slider1.svg)

At the start of any lean initiative it is vital that the organisation identifies the relevant measurements used to assess performance.

  * ![Slide number 2](../../images/unit01/session02/pg2-slider2.svg)

Most organisations will collect and monitor various forms of information to show business performance. You will look at these areas in detail on the next page.

  * ![Slide number 3](../../images/unit01/session02/pg2-slider3.svg)

However, for some organisations, there may be the need to begin measuring things that were previously unconsidered. This is hardly surprising as there are any number of things that may be measured. 

  * ![Slide number 4](../../images/unit01/session02/pg2-slider4.svg)

Many organisations rely on financial measures and little else. In lean organisations, a wider range of measures are usually used. 

  * ![Slide number 5](../../images/unit01/session02/pg2-slider5.svg)

Financial measures used by traditional organisations will often be driven by owners, shareholders and senior managers. 

  * ![Slide number 6](../../images/unit01/session02/pg2-slider6.svg)

However, in lean organisations the measures will be heavily influenced by current and potential customers.
