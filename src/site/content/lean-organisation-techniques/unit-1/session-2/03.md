    title: The processes of a Productivity Needs Analysis
    theme: t-bg-supporting2
    copy: |
      Once an organisation has a clear understanding of what it is seeking to achieve it can identify the **key processes, performances** and **outputs**. The organisation can also decide upon the way prioritised outputs will be measured. 

      With the measures in effect, an organisation is well placed to conduct a Productivity Needs Analysis. To understand the basic ideas underpinning a Productivity Needs Analysis, it is helpful to consider it with reference to a more familiar training needs analysis. 

      The slider below compares a simplified training analysis model to a Productivity Needs Analysis; Scroll through the slides to see the similarities and differences.
    subInc:
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Training Needs Analysis
          text: |
            1. Identify tasks to be performed by an organisation and the skills required to perform those tasks.
            2. Assess the employees’ ability to perform the required tasks.
            3. Where gaps exist, take action to remove the gap.
            4. Check the action has resulted in the gap being closed.

          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S2_p3_slider_6_process.svg
        -
          title: Productivity Needs Analysis
          text: |
            1. Identify the **objectives** to be achieved by the organisation and the **outputs** needed (in processes, teams and by individuals) to deliver the objectives. 
            2. Identify any gaps between the required outputs and the actual output capability (the productivity gap). 
            3. Where gaps exist, take action to remove the gap.
            4. Check the action has resulted in the gap being closed.
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S2_p3_slider_7_process.svg

            With further consideration, the organisation had an objective of delivering:

            From these objectives the organisation would look at current measures of returned products and on time deliveries. (If they did not already exist then these measures would have to be established for the first time.)
        - 
          title: PNA
          copy: |
            A Productivity Needs Analysis would then be done. It would find out the current performance of the organisation. In this case the measures showed the following:

            * **The number of returned defective products is currently 7 in 100,000**
            * **On time deliveries are currently at 87.7%**

            In both cases the Productivity Needs Analysis shows there to be a detrimental gap. There are too many returned products and too few on time deliveries.

        - 
          title: Outcome
          copy: |
            As the Productivity Needs Analysis has highlighted inadequate performance, the organisation should recognise that action must be taken in order to improve. The organisation should identify the processes and tasks that have an impact on these returns and deliveries outcomes. These processes and tasks will also have relevant measurable outcomes that may form a part of the Productivity Needs Analysis.

      ### Reviewing the Productivity Needs Analysis process

      In this session we have seen what a Productivity Needs Analysis is.

      Let’s take a final recap by looking at the steps below:

      1. Measuring relevant info areas
      2. Prioritise measures
      3. Set objectives
      4. Processes to reach objectives (across scales of organisation)
      5. Deliver required outputs to reach desired productivity
