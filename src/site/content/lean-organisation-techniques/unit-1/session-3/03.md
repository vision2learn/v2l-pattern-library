    copy: |
      Before you begin to put your flowchart together click on the shapes below to remind yourself of some of the most commonly used symbols in flowcharts.
    subInc: 
        type: reveal
        content:
            -
                title: Start or End flow of activity
                copy: |
                    The oval shape represents the start or the end of a flow. For example, if the flowchart represented a complaint process, it would start with the complaint being received and end with it being dealt with.

                img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_Symbol_StartEnd_1.svg
            -
                title: Process or activity
                copy: |
                    Rectangles are usually the most common symbols found in flowcharts as they represent processes or activities. Examples can range from making phone calls in an office to cutting metal in a factory.
                img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_Symbol_Process_1.svg
            -
                title: Decision – yes or no outcome
                copy: |
                    A diamond symbol represents decisions in a flowchart. You’ll see these a lot as they lead to yes or no outcomes, shaping the rest of the process.
                img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_Symbol_Decision_1.svg
            -
                title: Document
                copy: |
                    A document symbol can sometimes be seen in flowcharts. For example, it may feature when reports must be completed or when forms need filling out.
                img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_Symbol_Document_1.svg
            -
                title: Storage
                copy: |
                    The downwards triangle symbol represents storage in a process. For example, this could be the storage of documents or the stacking of envelopes in a mailing process.
                img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_Symbol_Storage_1.svg
            -
                title: Database
                copy: |
                    You may see a database symbol used in flowcharts that deal with data. For example, this may be in the form of stock files or sales records.
                img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_Symbol_Database_1.svg

      A supermarket has decided to create a new ‘make your own’ pizza section in store. At the counter, customers can create a pizza of their own choice, providing an alternative to buying pre-packaged pizza. Fresh foods manager, Valerie, collects customer feedback to investigate how successful the new foods section is operating. It seems whilst many customers are more than satisfied with their pizzas, there remains some dissatisfaction. In some cases, customers complained about their waiting time although the biggest complaint seemed to be with the highly popular advertised ‘special sauce’ option often being unavailable.
