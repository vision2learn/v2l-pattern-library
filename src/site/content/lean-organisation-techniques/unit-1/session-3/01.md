    layout: media
    theme: t-bg-primary1b
    copy: |

      In a workplace an **action** or **activity** does not take place in isolation. Instead, there is a **sequence** of interconnected and interrelated activities that lead to an outcome. These linked activities form a process.

      However, it can be difficult to get an understanding of how the various activities are linked. One of the most effective ways to understand and improve workplace performance is to get a ‘picture’ or **visual representation** of what is happening.

      In this Session you will gain a better understanding of processes through the use of visual representations.

    title: The flow chart process
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      ### Main Steps

      1. Identify all of the key processes or activities. You may write these on cards or sticky notes.
      2. Sequence these processes on a large piece of paper.
      3. Work through the sequence from start to finish, identifying key decisions and resulting documentation.
      4. Finalise the sequence. Focus on links between processes, decisions and outputs.
      5. Produce the final flowchart, checking you’ve involved all key personnel.

    title: Basic and detailed process maps
    theme: 
    copy: |
      The basic steps for creating a process map are the same whatever the type of map you are creating. The aim is to use a level of detail that meets the needs of your problem solving. Too much detail and you are bogged down with detail…too little and there is little useful information. 

      There are considerable similarities between flowcharts and process maps. Some quality specialists do not differentiate between the two; they simply see them both as ways of understanding and improving business processes. 

      ### Creating a process map

      To create a process map, you should follow ten steps. Each slide below contains a step, select through to see each point:
    subInc:
      type: slider
      title: Ten steps
      slides:
        -
          title: Step 1 
          text: Review the process being studied and its boundaries as defined by your project. 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step1.svg
        -
          title: Step 2 
          text: Have participants identify the steps in the process. Write each step on a card or Sticky note. 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step2.svg
        -
          title: Step 3
          text: Working as a team, arrange the steps in order (placing the cards / notes in order). 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step3.svg
        -
          title: Step 4
          text: Eliminate any duplicates and agree on the wording being used. 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step4.svg
        -
          title: Step 5
          text: Agree and maintain the level of detail to be used. 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step5.svg
        -
          title: Step 6
          text: Keep the process-flow moving in one direction, usually left to right or top to bottom. You should only go in the opposite direction if a step needs to be repeated. 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step6.svg
        -
          title: Step 7 
          text: Discuss the results. Does it match the real situation that you know? Are adjustments required? 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step7.svg
        -
          title: Step 8 
          text: When steps 1 to 7 have been done, number the tasks sequentially through the most direct route. Then number the offline tasks. 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step8.svg
        -
          title: Step 9
          text: Transfer the whole process map to paper or computer. 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step9.svg
        -
          title: Step 10
          text: Date the map and record the names of those involved. 
          img: /LeanManagement/LM_U1_S3_p1_slide_Step10.svg
