title: Behavioural styles, skills and capabilities in teams
intro: | 
  Within teams, team members rarely do exactly the same things as one another. Instead, different team members use different skills.

    copy: |
      It is not just clearly defined teams and team roles that contribute to effective team working.  The behavioural styles and skills and capabilities of each team member also play a big role.

      You can read about some of the different behavioural styles in the below accordion.
    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Behavioural styles
          copy: |
            There are no fixed rules about which behavioural styles work best in teams. Effective teams usually have a diverse and balanced selection of people with different behavioural styles. 

            Depending on what is required, certain behavioural styles may be suited to carrying out tasks more effectively than others. For example, someone with an analytical mindset would be ideally suited to carrying out data analysis.

        - 
          title: Skills & capabilities
          copy: |
            Effective teams also need a good blend of skills and capabilities among team members. Not only must people with the correct skills be matched with appropriate tasks, but an organisation must also ensure it is making the most of the skills and capabilities of its staff. 

            Skills and capabilities can be anything from specific skills such as computer literacy or wider life skills such as written and verbal skills. Which skills and capabilities are required will depend on what a team is expected to achieve and it is vital that team members are matched to tasks that fit their skills.

    title: Summary
    theme: t-bg-primary i-summary
    copy: |
      It is vital that there is a good mix of different behavioural styles, skills and capabilities for a team to operate effectively. Having a wide range of different people will mean a team is more versatile and able to tackle different tasks effectively.

      However, this can still be a fluid process as people’s skills and capabilities develop over time or they may have skills they did not realise they have.

    title: Benefits of team working
    theme: 
    copy: |
      Many organisations use teams as a means of delivering desired outputs. In **lean organisations**, teams are often tasked with the **elimination of waste.**

      There are many potential benefits that team working can bring to organisations and their customers. 
      
      Select through the slides below to see some of the most commonly found benefits of teamwork.
    subInc:
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Increased efficiency
          text: Team collaboration allows each employee to focus on their own specialist skills. Any team that takes the time to establish working patterns and perseveres is likely to achieve increased **efficiency** and **productivity**. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_1.svg
        -
          title: Improved performance 
          text: When employees focus on what they do best and don’t have to deal with tasks that are beyond their skills or capabilities, they produce higher quality work.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Increased competency 
          text: While collaboration enables team members to focus on their core competencies, it can also help to increase competences. By collaborating with others, team members learn more about what they do, and gain a broader understanding of the system they work within.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Trust is built 
          text: Strong teams cultivate a foundation of trust. Trust maintains open lines of communication between all team members which encourages people to share information. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_4.svg
        -
          title: Reduced stress
          text: When employees try to do too many tasks, it can negatively affect their productivity and health. In a team, employees can support each other, reducing the likelihood of burnout. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_5.svg
        -
          title: It enables management focus on the big picture
          text: An organization may struggle to be successful if its leaders are forced to spend hours dealing with task or process level administrative or operational matters. Having capable teams to handle these tasks frees up senior managers to guide the medium and long term strategy.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_6.svg
        -
          title: It leads to innovation 
          text: Most innovation comes as the result of the effort of a group of people, rather than an individual. Most lasting innovations start with the creative process, which then moves through development, refinement, execution, and revision phases. Innovation usually involves input from many employees. The different experiences and perspectives of different teams can be utilised. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_7.svg
        -
          title: Improved service
          text: In any successful organisation, there tends to be a strong **team spirit** that is appreciated by its internal customers, external customers and supporters. Positive team working leads to positive attitude and a strong sense of commitment to the organisation. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_8.svg
        -
          title: It brings mutual support
          text: When teams work well together they are also supportive of one another. Mutual support can encourage people to achieve goals they may not have realised they could reach on their own. Furthermore, teams offer an environment that provides a sense of belonging. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_9.svg
        -
          title: It brings job satisfaction
          text: The advantages of teamwork may lead to improved organisational efficiency and customer satisfaction. It also contributes to employees’ job satisfaction, through learning, reduced stress, more support and better use of skills. When employees are more satisfied, they tend to be motivated, less prone to absenteeism and more productive. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p5_slide_10.svg
