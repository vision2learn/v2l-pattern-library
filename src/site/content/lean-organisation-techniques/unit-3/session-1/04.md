        Select the slides below to learn more about five different types of team. 
    subInc:
      type: slider
      title: 
      slides:
        -
          title: Improvement teams
          text: |
              Improvement teams are created to make **targeted improvements** in a work environment. These teams focus on specific processes that team members know most about. For example, in the public sector teams may improve the way patients are ‘processed’ in A&E (Health). In the private sector, the team may focus on improving the delivery of a product. When an approach called **Total Quality Management** was popular, the improvement teams were often referred to as **Quality Circles.**

              These teams may be in continuous existence or they may be created to address a particular process that needs to be improved.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p4_slide_1.svg
        -
          title: Management teams
          text: |
              Many organisations have groups of managers who come together **periodically** to exchange information and points of view. This type of management team is often ineffective.
              
              However, some management teams specify issues and timescales (objectives), and ensure that managers have clear roles and responsibilities within the team. The managers have the ability to look at the way many tasks interact and how processes relate to each other. Managers can ensure that an organisation’s processes work well together so that everything runs efficiently. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p4_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Project teams
          text: |
              A project team is usually created to address a specific problem or challenge that cannot easily be addressed by a group of employees currently working together. 
              
              The project team should have a clearly defined **objective** and the team members should possess the skills, resources and authority to achieve it. Once the objective has been achieved, the project team is likely to be disbanded. Sometimes, organisations have project teams who move from one project to another. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p4_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Task teams
          text: |
              Task teams are **time-limited teams** made up of people from across two or more functions. Often, the objective of the task team is to resolve an issue that overlaps between two or more groups, teams, departments or processes. 
              
              The task team may implement changes or make recommendations to senior decision makers. In a rapidly changing environment, task teams may stay ‘on call’ to address any new problems or challenges that may arise.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p4_slide_4.svg
        -
          title: Work unit teams
          text: |
              In work unit teams, the team members tend to work together most of the time in a particular business function or process. These teams may have ongoing objectives – such as continuously improving performance – or have a specific remit/task.
              
              These teams often have names that relate to their functions, such as ‘customer service team’, ‘the sales team’ or the ‘quality inspection team’. They tend to be permanent teams. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p4_slide_5.svg

    theme: t-bg-primary i-summary
    copy: |
      Although types of team vary, they share one main aim – to improve the performance of the organisation and enhance the experience of the customer. Within each team, there are employees with different skills. Therefore, team members usually have different **team roles**. These roles make a significant contribution to the effectiveness of the team.

          title: Hospitality company
          copy: |
            The hierarchy found in teams such as those in bars, pubs or clubs.

            The team will focus on the same overall team objectives but will have different individual objectives. In this case bar staff (full and part time) are responsible for serving customers, keeping the bar clean and tidy and other duties such as changing kegs and restocking fridges. The bar and area managers will be concerned about profit, how much money they make each night and how they can encourage people to come to the bar.

          img: /images/svg/LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p4_casestudy_1.svg
        - 
          title: Distribution company
          copy: |
            Such as a team in an online shopping company.

            A team may also operate across an entire company, especially in small businesses. In this example, if one person were to neglect their role, then the customer may receive an incorrect order or they may not receive their order at all. The delivery officer relies on the post room coordinator to give them the correct post with the correct addresses, both team members rely on the packaging coordinator to ensure items are packaged appropriately for delivery.

            The packaging coordinator relies on the order picker to find the right items in the warehouse and send them through to product packaging. All rely on the order processor to make sure the correct information is distributed to all teams. No position has more or less important as they are all needed to complete the order. 

          img: /images/svg/LeanManagement/lm_u3_s1_p4_casestudy_2.svg
        - 
          title: Telesales company
          copy: |
            The hierarchy within a call centre which is sales driven.

            Similar to organisations like bars and restaurants team members in call centres will have individual targets such as call time and number of sales. The managers, directors and CEO will have other objectives such as how to increase staff productivity, attract customers or additional business and how to make the organisation more profitable.

    title: Approaches to team roles
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      There are many different ways to classify team roles. All classification systems identify the fact that in a team, certain things need to be done in order for the team to be effective and healthy. These may be done by different teams as in the examples above or they may be carried out by different people depending on their role within their team.

      Two main approaches to team roles are Team Management Systems (TMS) and Belbin.
