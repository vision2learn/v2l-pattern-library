    title: Qualities to deliver team leadership
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
       In addition to the competences and functions of a team leader which you learnt about on the previous page, there are also the qualities required to lead a team and get things done. 

        In her book _People and Self Management_, Sally Palmer describes the qualities of a team leader. They should be seen as complementary to the managerial competences rather than as alternatives to them. Scroll through the slides to learn about the ten team leader qualities.

    subInc: 
      type: slider
      title:
      slides:
        -
          title: Drive and enthusiasm
          text: |
              For a team to work effectively, the team leader must have the drive and enthusiasm to keep things moving. If a team leader is enthusiastic it will have a direct impact on the levels of enthusiasm of team members. An unenthusiastic leader can have a negative impact on their team.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_1.svg
        -
          title: Honesty and integrity
          text: |
              If the team leader does not have integrity and honesty, there will be mistrust within the team. Mistrust can lead to the withholding of information, suspicion and potential conflict which results in underperformance. When there is trust, team members will share information, support each other and cooperate. It is important that the team are led by the example of their leader.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Reliability and dependability
          text: |
              A team leader needs to be trusted by everyone that they work with. This can be achieved through honesty as well as through being reliable and dependable. If team members know a leader will keep their word and always strive to do what is expected of them, it sets a good standard for working relationships.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Fairness
          text: |
              A fair team leader will not favour certain people or behave in a biased way. The leader will seek to be impartial and not take sides. Their focus is on team performance, facts and evidence rather than individual personalities.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_4.svg
        -
          title: Communication skills
          text: |
              Communication underpins many team-based activities and actions. The efficient flow of information through an organisation depends on effective communication. The willingness to listen carefully and to take care to be understood are important attributes for a team leader. This commitment should apply to everyone in the team. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_5.svg
        -
          title: People skills
          text: |
              A team leader needs to be a ‘people person’. The ability to work with people is just as important as focusing on objectives and completing technical tasks. The team leader must be able to work well with team members and key people outside the team. These people skills will include liking people, supporting and developing them.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_6.svg
        -
          title: Ability to make difficult decisions
          text: |
              When the team leader needs to make a decision they will gather facts and discuss them with the appropriate parties (such as team members and / or other team leaders). There will usually be options and the team leader should use their judgement to select the one which is most likely to deliver the team objectives.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_7.svg
        -
          title: Confidence
          text: |
              A team leader should be openly confident in themselves and in the team. This external display of confidence encourages and energises everyone in the team. The team leader should always be on the lookout for confidence dropping and take action to rectify the situation. 


          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_8.svg
        -
          title: Vision
          text: |
              While team members should have a broad view of what the team is trying to achieve they can focus primarily on their personal tasks and responsibilities. The team leader needs to be able to see the ‘big picture’. This is in terms of the team and also in relation to other parts of the organisation. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_9.svg
        -
          title: Sense of humour
          text: |
              Having a sense of humour is not mandatory for a team leader. However, it can add value to a team because it helps to keep things in perspective and minimise overreaction. It can be encouraging for everyone if the team leader and team members are able to laugh at themselves!

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_slide_10.svg

    title: Theories of leadership qualities
    theme: 
    copy: |
      Other experts have influenced the work of Sally Palmer and there is often overlap with the qualities identified necessary for good leadership. See below to read about some other theories. 

    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Peter Scholtes
          copy: |
            In his book _The Leader’s Handbook: Making Things Happen, Getting Things Done_, Peter Scholtes listed the traditional ‘competences’ of team leadership as

            * Forcefulness
            * Motivation
            * Decisiveness
            * Wilfulness
            * Assertiveness
            * Results and bottom line orientated
            * Task orientated
            * Integrity and diplomacy

            These qualities are still dominant in leadership and they are not necessarily incorrect, but they are seen as offering too little and not taking into account new ideas about people and organisations. 
        - 
          title: Marilee B. Sprenger
          copy: |
            New approaches have identified principles of leadership from the perspective of how people’s brains work. In her book _The Leadership Brain for Dummies_, Marilee Sprenger has identified leadership attributes found in great leaders. 

            Qualities of great leaders:

            1. **Be in touch.** Team members should know that that the team leader is with them. 

            2. **Be visible.** Team members should see their leader regularly.

            3. **Hone communication skills.** The team leader has to be a good listener as well as a good speaker. 

            4. **Keep emotions in check.** Team leaders must lead themselves as well as the team members. 

            5. **Inspire teams.** A leader needs followers. The team leader should walk with the team, not in front of them. 

            6. **Sharpen decision-making skills.** Being able to make rational decisions and quick decisions is part of leading others.

            7. **Place others’ needs above their own.** If team members believe their leader will help them, they will be more willing to try and achieve team objectives.

            8. **Lead people where they want to go.** Often the team leader’s best work is the removal of team members’ self-imposed limitations.

            This brain based approach highlights the personal and interpersonal attributes needed by a team leader. If these attributes are absent the team is very likely to underperform.

          title: Team 2
          copy: |
            The team leader is very strong on the 7 managerial competences and weak on the 10 team leader qualities.

            The team is well organised and focused, though it is not as energised as team 1. Targets are met when things run smoothly but the response of team members when a problem occurs can be disappointing. While team members acknowledge the leader’s technical competence and do not think them dishonest, there is a lack of trust and a reluctance to put extra effort into team tasks.
          img: /images/svg/LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_casestudy_2.svg

          title: Team 3
          copy: |
            The team leader is very weak on the 7 managerial competences, but generally strong on the 10 team leader qualities.

            The team lacks organisation and focus, with it being chaotic at times. The objectives and targets set for the team are rarely met because the team leader has a vision but no idea how to get there. Initially team members would work very hard since the leader was universally liked, but the constant underperformance became frustrating. The team members trust their leader; they just don’t think they can get things done.

            The organisation has noticed that the team is underperforming and will have to take action which could mean replacing or rapidly training the team leader.

            The team is utterly chaotic and rive with infighting. Targets are not met and resources are wasted. There is distrust throughout the team and no confidence in the team leader.

            The organisation has noticed that the team is underperforming and will have to take action with the most likely option to replace the team leader.

    title: Styles of leadership
    theme: t-bg-primary
    copy: |
      Every leader will have a different combination of the management competencies and qualities that you have learnt about so far in this Session. The ways in which managers lead their teams can be broadly categorised into three leadership styles.

      ### McGregor’s theory

      We covered McGregor’s theory in Session 1 but we’ll revisit here. Select the below images to learn more about McGregor’s X and Y theories.

    subInc: 
      type: reveal
      content:
        -
          title: Democratic
          copy: |
              This style of leadership is based upon mutual respect among colleagues regardless of their position. Here people are developed and encouraged to participate. There is an open approach to discussion and debate.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_reveal_1.svg
        -
          title: Autocratic
          copy: |
              Here the leader is not inclined to discuss or debate. Instead there are top-down orders. This has the benefit of leading to fast and decisive decisions.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_reveal_2.svg
        -
          title: Permissive
          copy: |
              This is also sometimes called laissez-faire. The team members are 'free' to do their own thing. The team leader does not impose on others.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s3_p2_reveal_3.svg
  -
    title: 
    theme: t-bg-primary
    layout: media
    copy: |
      This table shows that one style is not more ‘correct’ than any other, but that all have their advantages and disadvantages.

      It can be easy to try and put team leaders and managers into a single category. More successful team leaders treat these styles as options. They may identify certain team members who respond best to one style over the others and work situations that may require a particular style. Being able to change from one style to another gives a team leader a higher chance of success.

            * People have an inherent dislike of work and avoid it if possible

            * People are not ambitious

            * Authority
            * Micro supervision
            * Rules and regulations
            * Punishment (threat of punishment).

            * People in the workplace are attracted to both intrinsic rewards (self-fulfilment) and extrinsic rewards (pay / promotion / good working conditions)

            Manager motivates by:
            * Fairness and involvement
            * Rewarding performance via bonuses and incentives.
