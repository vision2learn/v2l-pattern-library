      Problems can occur within organisations when employees do not understand or do not accept the **purpose** or **key objectives**.

      Open and honest communication is vital when establishing, understanding and agreeing team purpose. There is a focus on listening to others and working within a team context. Each word written in bold refers to the attitudes and behaviours outlined on the previous page.

          title: The team purpose is not understood
          text: |
            ### Team leader

            The team leader should brief team members and check that they understand the team purpose.

            If the members still don’t understand, the team leader could make an effort to explain the purpose in a different way.

            If there is no team purpose, action should be taken to establish one. 

            There should be a focus on team context, openness, communication and listening.

          title: The team purpose is not understood
          text: |
            ### Team member

            If team members do not understand the team purpose they may ask other team members or the team leader for more information. 

            Individual team members can help other members should they become confused.

            If there is a disagreement in team purpose between team members, then a team leader or other authority should confirm the true purpose clearly and make sure everyone understands. 

            Team members should use shared knowledge, co-operative behaviour, openness, listening skills and effective communication.

          title: The team purpose is not accepted
          text: |
            ### Team leader

            Team leaders should seek to work with team members to establish an acceptable team purpose. If there are thoughtful objections based on evidence, the team leader may communicate these to managers outside the team. If the view of the organisation and team is that the purpose will stay as it is, then the team members who do not accept it will either have to accept the purpose or work outside the team. 

            There is a focus on **team objectives, trust and openness, communication, listening and team context.**

          title: The team purpose is not accepted
          text: |
            ### Team member

            All members should make an effort to accept the team purpose and encourage those who do not. 

            Team members may try to emphasise to others the value of the team purpose. 

            Others may explain why they do not accept the team purpose.

If there are thoughtful objections based on evidence, the team leader may communicate these to managers outside the team. If the view of the organisation and team is that the key objectives will stay as they are, the team members who do not accept them will have to learn to work to them or work outside the team. 

            There is focus on **trust, key objectives, openness, communication, listening** and **team context.**

            There should be a focus on **openness, communication, listening** and **sharing knowledge**.

    title: How team members and leaders deal with role problems
    theme: t-bg-supporting2
    copy: |
      Remember **role** may refer to the role of a **member within a team** or to the role of a **team within an organisation.** Roles provide details about what people must do to achieve goals and objectives.

      Take a look below to see how team members and leaders can approach common role related problems and difficulties. The words written in bold refer to the attitudes and behaviours outlined on the previous page.

    subInc: 
      type: accordion
      content:
        - 
          title: Confusion about what team members should expect from one another
          copy: |
            ### Team member

            Members should communicate clearly and listen carefully to their team members. They should seek to clarify expectations and record what is found.

            Focus on **trust, key objectives, openness, communication, listening** and **team context**.

            ### Team leader

            The team leader should use team briefing sessions, one-to-one conversations and written communications to clearly outline or explain expectations. Questions should be encouraged to ensure understanding.

            There should be a focus on **trust, key objectives, openness, communication, feedback, listening** and **team context**.
          img: /images/svg/LeanManagement/lm_u3_s2_p2_accordion_1.svg
        - 
          title: Inconsistencies between team objectives and individual objectives
          copy: |
            ### Team member

            When members recognise inconsistencies they may point them out to the team leader (or senior management).

            Focus on **trust, key objectives, openness, communication, evidence** and **listening**.

            ### Team leader

            Team leaders should take the lead and work with members and relevant people, inside and outside the team, to resolve any inconsistencies.

            There should be a focus on **trust, key objectives, openness, communication, evidence** and **listening**.

          img: /images/svg/LeanManagement/lm_u3_s2_p2_accordion_2.svg
        - 
          title: There is a duplication of effort due to overlapping roles
          copy: |
            ### Team member

            If an overlap of roles occurs within a team it can be discussed and the problem should be alleviated. If not, team members should seek out evidence of the duplication and alert a team leader or senior manager.

            Focus on **openness, trust, evidence** and **feedback**.

            ### Team leader

            If team members cannot resolve the problem, then the leader may recommend a solution or work with team members to eliminate any overlap. 
            
            There should be a focus on **openness, communication, trust, co-operation evidence, feedback** and **team context**

    title: Case study
    theme: t-bg-primary1a i-case-study
    copy: |
      Karly is part of an editing team for a publishing company. The team are given targets and deadlines and are told they need to have two books ready for printing in two weeks’ time. One has already been sent to the printers. Jason is working on the other which is nearly complete. Karly has been asked to edit a marketing leaflet by the end of the month. 
      
      While the other team members prioritise helping Jason to finish the novel within the fortnight, by editing the book with him or taking on his other commitments for him, Karly continues to work on the task she has been given. As a result Jason’s novel is still a few days away from completion when the deadline arrives. This is recorded as the whole team missing its target for that month.

      If Karly had prioritised the team objective over her individual objective the team may have reached their target on time.
