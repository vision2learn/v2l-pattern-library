      Workplace relationships are important within organisations as they help to positively support the countless interactions that can occur in the workplace.

      When dealing with relationship related issues team members should focus on trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence and listening. There is a team leader focus on trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence, listening, cooperation and team context.

      Select through the slides below to see some examples of the way in which team leaders and team members might address some workplace relationship issues. Each word written in bold refers to the attitudes and behaviours outlined on page 1.

          title: Team members have negative feelings about one another.
          text: |
            ### Team member
            The team members with the problem may try to address it themselves. The aim should be to make the other aware of any negative feelings and search for solutions in terms of the team objectives.  Focus on **trust, team objectives, evidence, mutual respect** and **listening**.

            ### Team leader
            The team leader may have to work with the unhappy team members to get them to focus on team and individual objectives rather than personal differences or disagreements.  They should emphasise **openness, listening** and **communication**. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s2_p3_slide_1.svg
        -
          title: Team members do not believe that their individual needs are being met.
          text: |
            ### Team member
            Team members should make notes or provide evidence outlining how they feel their needs are not being met, or how this can be resolved, and raise the issue with their team leader.  Focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **listening**.

            ### Team leader
            The team leader can explore potential unmet needs.  It may be possible that the team leader has the authority to make changes that address the issue(s).  If the issue needs greater authority, the team leader can communicate with an appropriate manager. 

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s2_p3_slide_2.svg
        -
          title: Team climate discourages openness and sharing of information.
          text: |
            ### Team member
            Team members could try and address this but the lack of openness may work against them.  Focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **listening**.

            ### Team leader
            A team leader has the responsibility to promote openness and information exchange.  If openness is not encouraged the team leader should take action using skills in **listening** and **communication**.

          img: LeanManagement/lm_u3_s2_p3_slide_3.svg
        -
          title: Personality clashes are given a priority by team members.
          text: |
            ### Team member
            Team members should seek to communicate constructively. They should try to provide evidence of personality clashes and show how it may undermine the team.  Focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **listening**.

            ### Team leader
            Team leaders should show evidence of personality clashes and how they can undermine the team or performance. The team leader can explain that these occurrences will not be tolerated and ultimately could result in sanctions or disciplinary action.

        - 
          title: Authority levels for decision making are unclear
          copy: |
            ### Team members
            Team members should ask other team members and the team leader for clarification.   
  
            There is a focus on **communication, listening, evidence** and **feedback**. 

            ### Team leaders
            If the decision-making authority is clear, the team leader needs to take time to work with team members to ensure that they understand it.
  
            If authority levels are unclear, team leaders may be able to clear them up themselves, but if the team leaders do not have the authority to make changes, they must speak to the appropriate managers.  
              
            There is a focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **team context**. 

        - 
          title: Cross-team communications fail to function
          copy: |
            ### Team members
            Initially, team members should seek to instigate the appropriate communications with other teams.   
  
            The focus should be on **openness, trust, communication, constructive feedback, listening** and **evidence.**

            ### Team leaders
            If the communication is not resolvable by team members or is due to structural or technical problems that need greater authority to rectify them, the team leader can speak to other team leaders and/or managers   
  
            If the team leader finds that the problem is due to the negative behaviour of team members, then they may be subjected to disciplinary actions.  
              
            There is a focus on **openness, trust, communication, constructive feedback, listening, evidence** and **team context. **

        - 
          title: Conflict resolutions processes are substandard
          copy: |
            ### Team members
            Team members can highlight problems and offer ideas but rarely have the authority to change processes unless they have a significant degree of authority.   
  
            Focus on **trust, evidence, communication** and **cooperation**. 

            ### Team leaders
            In many teams, the team leader may be able to refine or re-create conflict resolution processes.   
  
            If the organisation already has a conflict resolution process, then the team leader could make managers aware of the issues and suggest possible improvements.   
              
            Focus on** openness, trust, communication, constructive feedback, listening, evidence** and **team context.**

        - 
          title: Co-ordinating activities and monitoring processes are not adhered to
          copy: |
            ### Team members
            Team members can professionally point out, with appropriate evidence, the ways others are not coordinating, or monitoring, in the correct way. They can explain the correct approach and encourage and help others to do things correctly. If certain team members continue to fail to comply with processes, their colleagues may bring it to the attention of a team leader.

            Focus on **trust, openness, constructive feedback and communication** and **evidence**. 

            ### Team leaders
            A team leader is responsible for ensuring that key activities and processes are adhered to. If the team leader notices non-compliance, then action should be taken. First, the issue will be brought forward and training may be offered. Ultimately, a failure to comply may be a disciplinary issue.  
  
            If a team leader fails to ensure key activities and monitoring processes then they may be dealt with by other team leaders or managers.  
              
            Focus on **openness, trust, communication, constructive feedback, listening, evidence, team objectives** and Focus on **trust, constructive feedback and communication, evidence** and **listening**.

    title: Summary
    theme: t-bg-primary i-summary
    copy: |
      Conflict and difficult situations can be hard to deal with. Remember that many of the solutions available to team members are equally available to team leaders. 
      
      However, team members and team leaders may face different problems:  

      * Team members may face more productive or time pressure related issues where as team leaders may deal with more operational or directional problems in addition to internal team problems.
      * Both members and leaders should focus on using open and honest communication, and when team members are unable to deal with issues themselves they should raise the issue with their team leader. They may need to provide evidence when bringing issues to the attention of the team leader.
