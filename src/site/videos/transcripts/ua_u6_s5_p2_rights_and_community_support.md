JOE: There are many charities, organisations and community support groups who work to support the needs of individuals with autism and their families.  They may be involved in purely providing support, guidance and advice or may be involved in campaigning for improved rights, services and opportunities like the ‘National Autistic Society’.

BETH: It doesn’t matter how big or small the organisation or group is, their main aim is usually to help to create a better functioning society for individuals with autism and their families. 

JOE: Autism charities can offer a large amount of information on their sites, with links to other charities support agencies and online forums. Some even have directories giving lists of services available.  The ‘NHS’ website itself has a lot of information and links to other organisations.

BETH: Social media such as ‘twitter’ and ‘facebook’ can help give links to information for many different kinds of support, along with personal advice and event information. Often charities will have their own facebook page or twitter feed.

JOE: There are many people with experience of autism offering support and sharing their stories on forums and social media. It’s not always necessary to talk to others in online groups, but reading or hearing about other people's experiences, can help make it easier to understand some of the problems, and how they may relate.  

BETH: Comments on social media are often based on personal experience of individuals and families and should be taken in the context in which they are written, and not as advice that will help someone directly.

Also, any information on the internet, unless it’s from a registered or official source, should always be double checked and cross referenced, to make sure that it is correct and supported by the appropriate authorities. 

JOE: Local councils can help with advice and support and maybe able to organise financial benefits, depending on situation.

For children and young people under 25 councils have a ‘local offer’ which is support for young people with special educational needs. 

For adults with autism or parents and carers, a council can do a ‘needs’ assessment or a ‘carers’ assessment to find out what problems there are, and if any support or financial benefits might be available.

BETH: Some people might be wary of the internet or about letting people know their situation, but there is a lot of information, help and support available online or by phone. Also support doesn’t always have to be given online, once links are established, face to face support
can develop, if this preferred.
