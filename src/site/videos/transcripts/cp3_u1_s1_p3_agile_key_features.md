**Leah:** What is Agile development? 

**Chris:** Let’s have a look at this in more detail. 

**Leah:** ‘Agile Development’ which is also more commonly known as Agile Software Development, is an umbrella term for a range of frameworks, such as ‘Scrum’, and practices such as the ‘daily stand-up meeting’. It adopts the principles set out in the ‘Agile Manifesto’ and the ‘Agile 12 Principles’.

**Chris:** The Agile Manifesto is a short document, which was created in 2001 from a group of leading professionals who met to discuss the future of software development. Its focus is on value to individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration and responding to change. The Manifesto itself is short and made up of 68 words and has since been embraced by many different teams and companies, and it is agreed by many that it is still relevant today.

**Leah:** The 12 Principles of Agile Software, expand on the values within the Manifesto. The focus is on the customer, continuous delivery of product, change, frequency, team collaboration, motivation, support, communication, measurement, excellence, simplicity, self-organisation, and reflection.

Agile came about as a process to address issues that software developers faced, focussing on a project being flexible, so it could respond to any changes and deal with any uncertainty. 

**Chris:** Agile is people-oriented and result-focused in respect to software delivery and instead of delivering all the product at the end of the project, the software is built incrementally from the start in time-boxed 'sprints'. It is an iterative approach used in software development projects with a focus on continuous releases that takes customer feedback into consideration. 

Development tasks are broken down into bite size chunks, which can be achieved in a certain period known as a ‘sprint’. Each task would be assigned a measurement of time or complexity. The self-organised agile team itself would normally consist of different skillsets, working independently and collaboratively, to achieve the tasks within that set time.

**Leah:** Agile development’s iterative approach means that continuous testing and continuous improvement takes place throughout the entire product lifecycle this gives early warnings of quality issues, reduces the risk of failure, and gives opportunities to find new ways to fix those issues and or processes. There is an emphasis on frequent continuous releases with customer transparency and visibility on the work in progress. 

**Chris:** Project planning is also ongoing throughout the project which is known as just-in-time [also a method used in ‘LEAN’]. Estimates for each stage or ‘Sprint’ are relative and these are refined as the project progresses. Features also begin more general and details are added later. As not all the details are available at the beginning of the project, features being added later are often influenced by other members or the team and the customer.
