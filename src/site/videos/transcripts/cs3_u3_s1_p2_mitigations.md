**Abraham:** So how does an organization decide what type of cyber testing they should do?

This will depend on many different things. 

**Nicole:** This may be related to the size of the organization, the experience of its IT department and the nature of the business itself.

**Abraham:** A Pen test is best used when an organisation wants to focus on vulnerabilities in a new system or project. They are a more useful way to identify specific vulnerabilities rather than how they would respond to a real attack. They can identify smaller vulnerabilities that on their own might seem insignificant in the scheme of things, but these can be used by attackers as an entry into a system. 

**Nicole:** By following the processes hackers do pen testers can find these points of entry. Pen testers will generate detailed reports which meet the client’s specific needs ranking and rating any vulnerabilities.

However, if a pen test is not done correctly, it can cause damage by crashing servers, exposing sensitive data or even corrupt crucial data. Therefore, it is imperative that a pen tester can be trusted and understands the needs of the organization they are working for.

**Abraham:** Vulnerability scanning is often used for checking systems like web servers, VPN endpoints and internet connections and as part of the software development lifecycle. It can help identify vulnerabilities early as many attacks use bots to search for and exploit known vulnerabilities.

**Nicole:** By using the same tools, weaknesses can be discovered in advance and if many vulnerabilities are evident, it shows a system is flawed. Automated scans save time and money and can be repeated regularly. By doing this an organization can demonstrate they are meeting standards and GDPR regulations. 

**Abraham:** However, some vulnerabilities will be missed as there are always those that a scanner will not pick up and it may bring up false positives meaning that specialist help is still needed to identify results.

**Nicole:** Red team testing is used to create as near as possible how real attackers would act. It can’t replace vulnerability scans or penetration tests, but is more useful to see what would happen in the event of a real attack and is a good way of testing the ability of an IT security team or Security Operations Centre to detect particular types of attacks. 

**Abraham:** However, it only usually targets the vulnerability needed to achieve the goal of the attack and may not show all issues. It can also involve the high costs of using specialists.

**Nicole:** Social engineering testing covers areas that the other forms of testing don’t. They are also very common forms of attack, and many organisations will fall foul of them at some time. This form of testing helps the workforce in understanding the part they play in the vulnerability of their system both in their personal life as well as at work. However, this type of testing doesn’t work in isolation and should be combined with other forms of testing.

**Abraham:** Cyber security testing is only part of the process of safeguarding a system or network. Once testing has been done this should be followed up by mitigations that respond to any risks that have been identified during testing.

So, what exactly are mitigations? 

**Nicole:** Mitigations are actions that can help reduce the potential damage. Let’s look at some of these in more detail.

**Abraham:** If hackers gain entry to a system, the threat needs to be stopped before it can escalate. Having a plan in place can help with this by making critical decisions such as taking a system offline before damage escalates. A mitigation plan can help with identifying who has control in these circumstances and can make the appropriate decisions. Early containment is key in reducing any impact.

**Nicole:** This can be done by performing regular updates and patching any known vulnerabilities. Keeping malware protection up to date and installing firewalls to block unsecure access along with ensuring a robust password protection policy and multi factor authentication, can also help to mitigate any potential damage.

**Abraham:** Limiting user’s permissions by only allowing access to files and folders necessary for them to do their work can also help. A user access system can also verify the identity of users, stopping hackers from taking advantage of uncontrolled administrative privileges. This can also help to protect from insider or social engineering attacks.

**Nicole:** Those with access to sensitive or highly available data should be the first to be identified as at risk and this approach should then be implemented through the system and all other users.

**Abraham:** And finally, the vulnerability of employees rather than hardware and software systems. By training staff on how to mitigate threats by being fully aware of what role they play in the cyber security of their organisation, can help to reduce the issue of human error. Regular training in how and why cyber security is important can help to develop a culture of security across all employees, by helping to make them all aware of potential attack by social engineering.

**Nicole:** Within a business or organisation everyone has a role to play to make sure that cyber security is given the importance it deserves by making them aware of the impact the lack of it can have.
