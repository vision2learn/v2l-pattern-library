LEAH: Nearly 10 percent of children and young people experience behavioural or emotional problems growing up and for most, these will resolve in time but for others, it won’t, and they will need more professional support.

RHIANNON: It can be difficult to know when a child or young person is suffering but significant changes in behaviour, difficulties with sleep, withdrawal or not wanting to do things they usually like to do, will be a potential sign. 

LEAH: Children with long term medical conditions are more likely to suffer from emotional problems or disturbed behaviour. They may start to rebel or withdraw from social settings.

RHIANNON: They may be susceptible to using other methods to control their problems such as self-medicating.

Teenagers as they grow up, start to get moody and worried about how they look, what other people think of them and this can affect the way they behave. They may change their eating habits even becoming anorexic or bulimic, and indulge in risky behaviours. 

LEAH: Self-harm is more common in teenage girls than in boys, but boys and young men are at a higher risk of suicide, however suicide rates are on the increase with young women also. Cutting can become habit forming and many people who self-harm, take care to hide any scars and self-harmers can be unpredictable with their ‘tools’.

RHIANNON: When it comes to addiction, young people will not often seek help but will present with other problems such as difficulties at school, inappropriate sexual behaviour or signs of depression. They may even start lying or stealing to feed their drug habit. 

LEAH: Behavioural and conduct problems may be related to other complaints, such as ADHD, specific learning difficulties, abuse, or social influences. This can have a negative impact, not only on the child or young person but on the whole family.

RHIANNON: Eating disorders not only affect the individual mentally, but they also affect them physically and in extreme cases can cause death. Anorexia in girls, causes problems with their periods stopping and other health problems. Bulimia and the excessive use of laxatives can cause physical damage and regular vomiting can dissolve and erode the teeth over time. 

LEAH: Psychosis can be caused by substance abuse or can be a signal of an underlying physical illness such as a severe infection, or a severe mental illness like bipolar disorder or schizophrenia.

Children and young people are often not aware that they have a problem, until it becomes a problem and this is where parents, carers, friends, family, teachers and anyone who knows them well, should be aware of changes in behaviour or mood. 

RHIANNON: If they are starting to behave differently or show distress or mood swings it should be a warning that something isn’t right, and it might be time to talk to someone about it before things go too far.
