JOE: Different environments and different situations can cause individuals with sensory problems to suffer sensory overload, and this has to be considered when architects and designers are creating spaces as they need to factor this into their designs.

Small changes to an environment can make changes to how an individual with autism responds to stimuli. However, all environments cannot be made suitable for the sensory needs of all individuals with autism. 

BETH: There are things that can be done in the home, schools and workplaces and public buildings that can help, such as changing the environment by addressing any possible causes of over stimulation which may cause sensory overload.

The most important thing is to be aware of the sensory stimuli that the individual responds to and how it affects them. Sometimes preparing an individual for some form of potential stimuli can help them to be prepared.

JOE: In the home, making sure that lights are not too bright and don’t flicker, and sounds of electrical goods are minimalised can relieve problems. 

Sunglasses could help to combat bright lights and blackout curtains could help with sleeping and hiding visual distractions from view can improve a workspace.

An oversensitivity to sound can be helped by shutting doors and windows and eliminating unnecessary sound such as mechanical noise. The problem of everyday smells in the home can be solved using low odour products.

Individuals who find textures or patterns of clothing or soft furnishings intolerable, can be helped by making sure that patterns are kept to a minimum and cushion covers, and other soft furnishings and clothes have acceptable textures.

BETH: Each individual with autism has their own sensory issues and if these can be addressed and alleviated to some extent, they can make a world of difference to their lives.
