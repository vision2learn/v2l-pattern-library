**Abraham:** There are many terms that we will discuss throughout this course that are used in cyber security and some of them we hear more often than others let’s look at some of the most common used terms.

**Nicole:** Threat -as we have already discussed is the ability of hackers or threat actors to target and adversely impact organisational systems, operations, and assets. Threats take many different forms such as malware, data breaches and DoS attacks. 

**Abraham:** Vulnerabilities-refers to any week spot within the cyber security of an organisation that allows anyone to enter their systems and steal anything of value like data that may compromise and adversely impact an organisations operation. Hackers use these in various ways to gain access and attack a system. Vulnerabilities are also known as the attack surface. 

**Nicole:** Software flaws are some of the most common vulnerabilities such as, bugs or backdoors which enable access by bypassing normal security measures. These may be as a result of a poor system design processes or mistakes made when the system was set up and can go undetected for some time. 

User error can make a system vulnerable for example when the user unwittingly clicks on a malicious link in an email or text message. They may also consistently use the same obvious email for everything easily guessed by the hacker. Hackers can easily trick users into giving away information by a variety of different phishing attacks. 

**Abraham:** When an attack happens on the day a weakness is discovered in software this is classed as a zero day attack. This is because the hackers are aware of the flaw and launch their attack before the software company can release the patch. 

**Nicole:** Risk- refers to the level of impact a cyber attack may have on an organisation and its operations for example financial loss, disruption, and damage to reputation. It usually involves a combination of threat, vulnerability, and impact. Organisations may face a greater risk if they have more valuable information or financial assets. Add to that a flaw or a vulnerability then the risk of attack would be high. Malicious threat actors also like to target companies and organisations they don’t like so as to damage their reputations.

**Abraham:** Exploits- are codes that take advantage of a software vulnerability or flaw. They allow unauthorised access remotely, gaining elevated privileges or to move deeper into the network. Exploit tools can also be embedded into compromised webpages and as the user navigates through them the tool scans their device for vulnerabilities and inserts the malware. They can also be used as part of a multi-component attack.
