**Jack:** To make sure that we are communicating correctly we need to first seek feedback and not just expect it to come to us.

The first thing to think about when seeking feedback is what do we want from the feedback? Is it to know whether the communication was understood, was clear to everyone? Or to be more specific, such as getting individuals to feedback directly with data and immediate responses to particular questions.

The things that we most commonly seek feedback about are suitability, relevancy, and usefulness. We want to see if the message we were trying to get across was received by all and was clear, effective, and informative.

**Leah:** So, who do we seek that feedback from?

Within a programming environment the other parties that we may seek feedback from are clients with whom we may be communicating about their new software, colleagues, and team members who we may be collaborating with on a development project, and managers, stakeholders, and any third parties who may be involved at some stage with a project. It doesn’t matter who they are, their feedback will often help with the process of communication. 

**Jack:** So how can we get feedback that meets our requirements and is timely?

The person who is communicating should set out the guidelines for feedback and make sure that they ask for it. They should also make everyone aware that feedback is an important and helpful way of them confirming their understanding and in turn for gaining a response.

For a more 

**Leah:** efficient process they should enable feedback to be given by means of creating a mechanism for it. Feedback can be given by a variety of means such as by informal and formal meetings, email questioning, polls, surveys, focus groups and questionnaires. The approach will depend on the feedback that is wanted and how quickly it is needed.
