**Leah:** Agile and Scrum 

Scrum is a framework of Agile so the strengths and weaknesses for each one is very similar. There are further ones that are specific to Scrum, but the following ones we are going to look at here are the main ones.

Let’s first look at the Strengths

**Chris:** Flexible and adaptable. Because the requirements do not need to be defined at the beginning of the project, changes can take place quickly and easily in Agile and Scrum development.

**Leah:** Creative and innovative-Team members are more creative and innovative as there is more freedom for change due to it being flexible and adaptable with less rigid planning.

**Chris:** Reduced time-to-market-Due to the shorter start-up time, frequent reviews, and the iterative approach, Agile and Scrum developments have quicker product releases.

**Leah:** Higher productivity of team and improved quality. Because Agile and Scrum prioritises requirements there is less risk that the project specification will increase. Higher productivity and improved quality are also due to the team being more focused, self-managed, responsible for own tasks, and having job satisfaction due to frequent iterations and completions of tasks.

**Chris:** Customer satisfaction-Customers are more satisfied as they participate in the development process, their feedback is valued, and they can see where this is being used in the product.

**Leah:** Organisation transparency-Agile and Scrum enables other employees [who could also be stakeholders] within the organisation to be included in the project which supports partnerships and trust.

**Chris:** Now let’s look at the Weaknesses 

Training needed-If Agile and or Scrum haven’t been used before then there would need to be training so all the team members are comfortable with the principles, ceremonies, practices, and roles.

**Leah:** Complexity of skills required-Depending on the project there would be a need of a mix of roles and technical ability such as Developers, Testers, Designers, and a Scrum master.

**Chris:** Organisation - initial change needs-If the organisation does not support Agile or Scrum, then the team would be working in isolation. Less understanding and support would be given from the wider organisation to the team members which could result in ‘scope-creep’ and an increased workload for the Scrum Master whose role as a ‘Servant Leader’ would be to protect the team.

**Leah:** Not suitable for certain types of projects-Agile and Scrum are not suitable for certain types of projects, especially larger scale ones which require a high level of certainty so a lot of planning would be required.

**Chris:** Agile is the opposite of Waterfall, its more flexible, good for urgent projects, higher level of complexity, with varying tolerance for change and bugs. It is often used when the team needs to know something new, such as for Apps, games, etc. 

Companies that have used Agile include ‘Philips’, ‘VistaPrint’, ‘JP Morgan Chase’.

Scrum can be used for projects that need flexibility as it can easily adapt to unexpected changes. It is a good framework to use for projects that are urgent, complex need quick deliverables, and a team who are already comfortable with this framework.

**Leah:** Examples of Scrum projects include ‘Saab Defense’ to develop a fighter jet for mission critical software, ‘Intronis online backup services’ for increased call centre productivity, and ‘Yahoo!’ Who use scrum for over 100 Software teams.
