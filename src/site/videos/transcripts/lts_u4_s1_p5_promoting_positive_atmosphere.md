Whenever you have a group of people working together, sooner or later there will be some form of conflict that will have to be resolved, even if it’s only minor issues. So, in this video we are going to be discussing how important it is that a manager promotes a positive atmosphere to minimise the adverse effects of conflict.

Although conflict is inevitable, steps can be taken to help prevent conflicts from emerging in the first place. If you do this, you can save time, energy and resources that would otherwise be spent minimising and resolving the conflict once it’s already broken out.

One of the best things you can do to make conflicts less likely to occur, and less harmful when they do occur, is to create a positive atmosphere in the workplace

A negative workplace atmosphere can often be a consequence of an unresolved conflict or conflicts causing people to have lower morale. This can in turn lead to the change of future conflict becoming more likely. Employees won’t be as enthusiastic about work and will be less willing to find solutions to their problems with their colleagues.

With a positive workplace atmosphere, things are different. Morale is higher, people will feel happier and more energised, more enthusiastic about work, and often more valued. It also minimises the chances of conflicts occurring, as people will be more open with each other and more focused on their jobs. 

So, what steps can be taken to promote a positive atmosphere at work?

Arrange Social Events and Mixers

This will encourage people in the team to unwind and let off some steam, helping to make them more relaxed and happier. More importantly, it can also encourage members of a team to learn more about each other, outside of the work context. This can help form friendships and improve interpersonal relationships, helping them see each other as real people instead of just work colleagues.

Team Building Activities

Time and budget permitting, running team building activities like workshops and away days can help put people together in alternate situations and encourage them to solve problems. By putting them in an environment which is not work related and they have the opportunity to safely fail and people can learn more about each other and learn to trust each other more. It also creates a shared experience and memory that they can refer back to later.

Get to Know Your Team 

As a team leader, it’s important to spend time and make the effort to get to know your team better. This can help to make you more approachable to them and help break down the barriers that can make the relationship between you and your team impersonal. If the relationship is friendlier and warmer, the general attitude of your team will be improved.

Encourage Collaboration in the Team

Impress on your team how important it is for everyone to work together and collaborate. This will help encourage the team to think of itself as unified, rather than as divided. You can do this with a communications campaign, which can be as simple as emails and posters, or involve workshops and presentations, whatever works best for you and your team.

Be available to your team

Make sure your team knows you are accessible and how to reach you if they need to. Proactively check in with people to see how they’re doing. Ask questions and be there to answer questions that are posed to you. This will make your team feel like they can rely on you and make it more likely that they’ll come to you first if a conflict is starting to develop, so you can help to nip it in the bud.
