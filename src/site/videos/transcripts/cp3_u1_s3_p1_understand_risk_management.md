**Leah:** Risk is often defined as an event or activity that could have a positive or negative outcome. When doing something that involves risk we often have to weigh up if the potential reward is worth the risk! Just think of some of those risky activities like ‘skydiving’ or ‘swimming with sharks’ to something as simple as ‘crossing the road’! In all cases we have to identify the risks and minimise them. This is risk management!

**Chris:** So, what do we mean by risk management? 

In business risk management can be defined as the process used to ‘identify, assess and control threats to the organisations financial stability’. 

In coding we are referring to a ‘potential situation arising that can have a direct impact on a projects progress’. 

**Leah:** Some potential risks may seem rather extreme such as a ‘global pandemic’, ‘terrorist attack’ or ‘war’! however in today’s world these are becoming more plausible as we all know! But on the whole, all projects will have more realistic risks attached such as, project failure, too much time spent on the project, overspend on budget or possibly a poor-quality product. 

**Chris:** The role of the project manager is to identify the major risk factors that may affect the project, evaluate these, and prioritise them, and then plan how these risks can be managed or mitigated. They are also responsible for reviewing and monitoring for any new risks that might arise and updating all stakeholders.

**Leah:** Risks can be split up into two types, Pure risks and Business risks.

Pure risks are extreme and often involve the ultimate use of the software in high-risk environments where failure of the software may have dire consequences. These don’t directly affect the software development but have to be considered regarding its deployment.

**Chris:** Business risks are more likely to be encountered and often involve risk associated with financial aspects such as cost cutting exercises and a lack of vision.

Every project has risks, but software projects are special. So, what are the potential risks that could affect a projects delivery?

Let’s take a look at the most common. 

**Leah:** Experience 

A business risk may involve using less experienced developers to save money. This lack of experience could affect the quality and deadline for the project. However, the developer may rise to the challenge and proves to be an asset and of greater value in this and future projects. 

**Chris:** Time and costs

Time estimates need to be realistic. If a project team struggles to meet the deadlines, quality could suffer, which means work may need to be redone and more delays.

In software development the largest expense is usually tied to the developers’ time. So, If the time estimates are out then so will be the cost estimates. As costs pile up, stakeholders, primarily management, may start to lose faith, putting pressure on for completion, resulting in errors, lost time, and lost money!

**Leah:** Scope

Scope is the agreed body of work that the project will capture. ‘Scope creep’, also called ‘mission creep’, can occur when stakeholders begin to sneak in changes that circumvent the identified change request system and time and cost are affected. Scope changes may also stem from the project team not having a clear understanding of what’s considered ‘in scope’ and what’s ‘out of scope’. Scope changes should be avoided unless absolutely necessary but should always be reflected in the time and cost estimates.

**Chris:** Feasibility

some projects are unrealistic in regard to scope, schedule, and budget. It’s the project manager’s job to identify this unfeasibility or face the consequences.

**Leah:** Quality

what good is a project if it’s completed on time and on budget but the software is full of bugs, errors, and crashes all the time? 

**Chris:** Stakeholder expectations. title on screen

You must remember to maintain communication channels. Failure to ensure that the project is moving in alignment with stakeholder expectations can risk not meeting their vision.

**Leah:** Human Resources

Knowledgeable technical resources are always in great demand. You may run the risk of your top developers being recruited by other firms therefore it’s important to consider employee retention strategies to mitigate this.
