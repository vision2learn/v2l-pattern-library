**Jack:** So, let’s have a look at Scrum Events in a little more detail, starting with ‘The Sprint’

**Leah:** The sprint is a fixed length event which has a sprint goal. All events are time-boxed so once a sprint begins, it is fixed. The sprint itself, involves a collection of ‘tasks’ for the team to achieve. Each task or ‘user story’ is allocated a level of complexity called a ‘story point’. A sprint project board which is also known as the ‘Sprint Backlog’ is used to track the tasks, and sub-tasks are positioned in three columns - ‘To do’, ‘In progress’, and ‘Done’.

**Jack:** Sprint planning is the first meeting of the sprint. It is where achievable items or user stories, are identified from the product backlog to be moved to the next sprint. The team would decide in this meeting, what can be done within the next sprint, and then refine the user stories; for example, they would determine story-points, how tasks will get done, the definition of done, dependencies, and sub-tasks. 

The Scrum Master who ensures that the framework is followed, would predominately manage this meeting, taking into consideration priority, capacity, and the wider business expectations. However, before this meeting can take place, the Product Owner would need to determine the priority tasks and decide on the sprint goal. Team members would also need to allocate their own tasks on the product backlog.

**Leah:** The Daily Scrum, also known as the ‘Daily Standup’, is a 15 minute meeting for the internal scrum team, which is arranged at the same time, each working day of the sprint. Its main objective is to determine progress and update the project board, also known as the ‘sprint backlog’, and adapt the ‘product backlog’ if needed. The focus is on progress and actions, and self-management is encouraged, as well as promoting collaboration, improvement, and transparency amongst team members.

It is ‘not’ meant to be a ‘status’ meeting but is meant to be about achieving valuable outcomes. It is also supposed to eliminate the need for other meetings, improve communication and decision-making, and identify blockers.

**Jack:** The Sprint Review is where the scrum team present their results from the work they have done in the current sprint, to the stakeholders and/or the customers. It is where feedback is given, and adaptations, alterations, changes and or improvements can be planned for. The sprint review is a working session, not just a presentation and the product backlog is adjusted to reflect new information and or feedback. It is usually one of the last events of the sprint. 

**Leah:** The Sprint Retrospective or retro is the last meeting and concludes the sprint. This is where the team determine what went well, what could be improved, what needs greater understanding, and the actions that need to be taken, moving forward. 

The retro is a safe place where the team can come together, give support and productive feedback. The purpose of the retro is to act on what has been reviewed, and plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness. One of the biggest benefits of working in Agile is ‘continuous improvement’, and in Scrum the driver of improvement is the ‘Retrospective’.
