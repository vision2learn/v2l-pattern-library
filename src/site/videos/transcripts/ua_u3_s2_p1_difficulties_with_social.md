JOE: Individuals with autism typically struggle with fully engaging in conversations. They may interrupt the person talking or decide the conversation is over and walk away while the other person is still speaking.  

They can suffer from Social imagination difficulties by not having the ability to imagine what another person may be feeling, thinking or experiencing and therefore lack the ability to relate to others.

They may have trouble with eye contact which is important for communicating interest in having a social interaction with someone, and this difficulty can often be misconstrued by others as disinterest or inattention.

To individuals with autism social interactions can appear unnatural and very stressful so many may try to avoid them. They suffer from social inhibition, consciously or subconsciously avoiding social interaction because of the possibility of disapproving reactions from others. 

It can be difficult for individuals with autism to make or keep friends and they may say they don't need or want friends and isolate themselves socially.

They may have less interest in social interaction for its own sake and be more interested in things than in people, and are often content when left alone to pursue their own interests

Some choose to be social, however their issues in social understanding and empathy can prevent them from smoothly engaging with others. An individual may know they are supposed to use words to start a conversation with others however, they may not know quite how to use them, and may end up having life-long difficulty navigating through social situations.
