**Abraham:** Let’s discuss the possible motives for cyber crime. 

The motives for cyber crime are similar to those for crime in general only they involve the benefits of 'online anonymity'. Cyber crime can be on more of a 'small' scale where it can impact an individual directly such as cyber bullying or phone hacking or a small company that has their whole computer system taken over. This may be to gain control of their data, holding them to ransom or adding malware. However, it can also be on a large scale where governments and large organisations can be affected by state sponsored attacks on their security with world-wide repercussions.

The motivation for cyber criminals can be wide-ranging, for example those linked to ransomware, phishing, internet and financial fraud, password cracking, hacking and malware are mainly motivated by financial gain but can also be associated with pranks, activism, cyber theft and espionage.

Cyber crimes against people such as cyber stalking, bullying, trolling and revenge pornography are likely to be motivated by hatred or to intimidate or inflict harm.

Additionally, some cyber crime is committed solely ‘because they can’!

Often criminals will hack a website or computer just to prove to other hackers that they can and to cause mayhem. These are usually younger people just getting into hacking with spare time on their hands. An example of this is the hacking group LulzSec, who would often breach news outlets like The Sun and The Times and create fake news stories.
