There are certain barriers that an organization is likely to run into when they’re trying to change and innovate and, in this video, we are going to look at these and how to go about overcoming them.

So, what could these barriers be? 

• Fear of the unknown

• Unclear goals

• Lack of leadership

• Unwillingness to change

• Lack of planning 

There are some practical ways these barriers can be overcome and some of these solutions can be applied to more than one barrier. So, let’s have a look at these in a little more detail.

Fear of the unknown

Uncertainty and anxiety around change are very common and should be factored into any innovations you’re planning. Although part of the reality of change is that people will initially feel some discomfort around it, it’s the uncertainty of what change involves that can be the real driver behind the fear. Everyone who is affected by the changes should understand what those changes are, when they’ll be applied, and what they’ll mean for them. You can do this with efficient and clear communication.

Unclear goals 

Something that will always make change and innovation more difficult, is when it isn’t made clear why the changes are necessary. Without clearly defined goals, people may feel like change is being pursued just for the sake of it. It is important to make sure that the goal of all the changes and innovation, such as increased efficiency, embracing a new technology, or increasing profits, is made clear to everyone involved. This will help them get behind the change and put all their energy into it. Again, clear, and effective communication, like regular meetings and question and answer sessions can help do this.

Lack of leadership 

If it isn’t clear who’s in charge of leading people through the change, or even worse if no one is actually in charge, there will be confusion, mistrust, and possibly even chaos. The nature of the leadership needed will depend on the nature of the change in question. You might need a whole dedicated leadership team if it’s a big, sweeping change that effects everyone, or, you might just need a change champion in each team or department. What’s most important is that everyone knows who is in charge of the change, so they can act as a point of contact and a source of reassurance.

Unwillingness to change 

This can be a difficult barrier to overcome as it involves winning over peoples’ hearts and minds. In order to inspire willingness to change, you need to have already overcome many of the barriers previously identified. There should be no uncertainty about what the change involves, with clearly defined the goals, and a leadership structure that is clear to everyone. 

You can also help overcome the unwillingness some people may feel to change by giving them an avenue to express their worries, such as through private meetings with a change leader or through a dedicated channel on the Intranet. This will mean that they will feel listened to, and if appropriate, you may even consider giving them some responsibilities in achieving the innovation, helping them to feel like they’re on the inside, not the outside, of the change.

Lack of planning 

Without some kind of plan, a change is often doomed to fail. People won’t know what they’re doing, when, or why! The solution to this is simple, make sure there is a plan. This should be prepared in advance of the change and clearly define what will happen at each step. 

It will need to be flexible enough to absorb shocks from unexpected occurrences, but not so flexible that it has no shape or structure. It should be prepared with input from the right stakeholders in the organization, but if the planning involves too many people, this may make it too complicated. 

What’s most important is that the plan accurately describes the innovation, includes a communications strategy, and clearly identifies who is responsible for what aspects of the change.
