**Chris:** When working in ‘Agile’ development you will constantly hear the terms ‘backlog’, ‘Epics’, ‘Features’, ‘Themes’ and ‘User Stories’, so what do these different terms mean? 

**Leah:** Let’s first take a look at backlogs. 

There are two types of backlogs, product backlogs and sprint backlogs. 

**Chris:** Product backlogs contain the list of work items that need to be completed for the project. Items are ranked in order of importance, so that the team knows which items need to be completed first. Simply put, the ‘product backlog’ is like a ‘To-Do list’ of tasks, that helps to drive progress and keeps the development always moving forward. 

When there is capacity, members of the team move items from the ‘product backlog’ into the ‘sprint backlog’. A good thing to note is that the ‘product backlog’ is not set in stone, when working in ‘Agile’, projects are constantly evolving and the ‘product backlog’ is changed to reflect this. 

**Leah:** So, what about the sprint backlog?

The ‘sprint backlog’ is where the items taken from the ‘product backlog’ are placed. Once in the ‘sprint backlog’, the items are included in the current sprint, and must be completed by the ‘scrum team’. This may be over a period of 1, 2, 3 or more weeks but for software development projects only, it is typically 2 weeks.

Unlike the ‘product backlog’ items in the ‘sprint backlog’ can’t be changed during the sprint, any items that are not fully completed in the current sprint, will carry over into the next sprint. 

**Chris:** So, what about Epics, User stories and Features? 

All three of these terms are integral to each other, so let’s look at Epics first. 

Epics are large tasks that may need several sprints to complete and will contain the ‘what’s and why’. For example, you could be asked to create a shopping cart for an online store. If you remember back to our first video in session 2 when we talked about the process of ‘requirements elicitation’, you will begin with gathering the high-level requirements for the project [Epics] which will then be broken down and refined into more detailed requirements [User Stories]. There will be several elements to this, that can then be broken down into smaller chunks called stories. 

**Leah:** So, what are Stories? 

Stories are small chunks of work written in non-technical language and will give a simple overview of what is expected. Stories should be a complete narrative, and provide a tangible user benefit end to end, whilst also small enough to complete in one sprint. Looking at our previous example, the online shopping cart is classed as an ‘Epic’. A ‘User Story’ relating to this could be selecting the payment method. As you can see this is a ‘subtask’ of the creation of the shopping cart. 

**Chris:** And Features what are these?

If we look at the shopping cart again, we have identified one of the User Stories as the customer wanting to select payment options. A ‘feature’ in this instance describes a specific solution to this requirement. So, in our example a ‘feature’ could be a drop-down selector that offers different payment options. Another Feature may be another drop down option for selecting different postal rates. 

**Leah:** So, what does the term Themes relate to? 

So far, we have talked about Backlogs, Epics, User Stories and features but what is a Theme? When working on a project there will be Epics and or User Stories that share common characteristics, these can be grouped together as a Theme. In the examples we have been using this ‘theme’ would relate to E-commerce functionality and personalisation. 

**Chris:** As you can see there are many terms that are used when working on ‘Agile’ projects and it can take some time to fully understand them. Don’t worry, as you become more comfortable with Agile development, understanding, and using these terms will become very familiar to you.
