LEAH:  
As well as barriers to seeking a diagnosis, some children and young people may be reluctant to have a diagnosis or any medical intervention because of a range of different concerns. Let’s have a look at some of those concerns.

 
RHIANNON:  
There can be stigma associated with receiving a mental health diagnoses, and although this is lessening, as young people are more open to exploring mental health issues, there are still those that would discriminate.

 
Being labelled with a mental health diagnosis, might impact on how others, including family and friends may interact with someone.

 
LEAH:  
Parents of a child or young person, may become overly cautious around a mental health label. This may unintentionally reinforce the underlying problem, such as not encouraging an anxious child to try new things.

 
For some a diagnosis can put an emphasis on the problem being a part of the child or young person and maybe less likely to take into account other external factors such as the family dynamic and peer interactions.

 
RHIANNON:  
Some diagnoses are more helpful than others, diagnoses of depression or obsessive-compulsive disorder [OCD] can validate suffering, however some individuals might be sceptical of any diagnostic label placed on them, as they may feel dismissed by others who choose to use their diagnosis as a way of explaining away their valid emotions.

 
More severe diagnoses, such as schizophrenia or borderline personality disorder, can be used to damage the credibility of the sufferer, silencing their voice.

 
LEAH:  
Some families of children and young people, may fear that a diagnosis will impact on their progression or opportunities, such as not being able to handle University or not being able to have that job role they want, due to their mental health issues. This could then impact their ambition for their future.

 
They may fear that the information held about their diagnoses, may be available to others, including potential employers. They may wish to keep parts of their life, or their mental health issues private, however this is not the case due to data protection laws.

 
RHIANNON:  
They may be concerned about being put into a box, based solely on their symptoms, rather than looking at the cause of the symptoms themselves. Sometimes it is an experience of trauma, abuse, or neglect that brings on the symptoms of mental health conditions. They may not wish to address these issues and feel that medical intervention, may cause things that have been suppressed to resurface.

 
LEAH:  
How a diagnosis is received is also mediated by the individual’s life experiences and culture. If the diagnosis is placed upon the patient with no space for the individual’s thoughts, queries, or concerns, then it is likely that the individual will reject the diagnosis and rebel against any potential treatment plans.
