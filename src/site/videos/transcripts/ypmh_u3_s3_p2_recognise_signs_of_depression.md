RHIANNON: Whatever is causing the problem, it should be taken seriously. But first we need to know that there is something wrong, and how to identify the signs or symptoms of ‘Depression’ in children and young people.

Let’s look at some of the signs and symptoms of depression in children and young people.

Feeling sad, in a low mood that doesn’t go away, or being irritable or grumpy all the time.

Constantly feeling tired, sleeping more than usual or maybe having trouble sleeping all.

Interacting less with friends and family and not being interested in things in general.

Eating less than usual or overeating and having big changes in weight.

Unable to relax or being more lethargic than usual or unable to feel emotions.

Lacking confidence or talking about feeling worthless.

Self-harming, or even talking about suicide.

LEAH: We can see from these signs and symptoms that depression and anxiety which we have looked at previously are very similar in the way the present themselves. This is because some children and young people with anxiety also develop depression and vice versa.

RHIANNON: If children or young people are resistant to discussing their feelings or admitting that they may be depressed, they should be encouraged to talk to someone they trust, such as a family member, a friend or someone at school or should see their GP and or be referred to specialist services.
