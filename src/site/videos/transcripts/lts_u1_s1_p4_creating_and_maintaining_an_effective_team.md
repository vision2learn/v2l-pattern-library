Knowing how to create and maintain an effective team is an important part of team leadership.

But it’s important to realise that the evolution of teams and their good running isn’t always a clear and linear process. It can often be unpredictable and can even be a little chaotic! 

Using a model is a good way to prepare for the likely events you’ll encounter, but there’s always going to be some small distance between the content of the model and reality. 

Let’s look at the Tuckman’s team development model and some of the general principles around creating and maintaining an effective team 

Tuckman’s model is really helpful for plotting out the evolution that a team goes through, and it breaks this down into four main steps:

• Forming

• Storming

• Norming

• Performing

There is also a fifth step called Mourning which is also known as adjourning.

Let’s look at these in more detail.

Forming refers to when the team starts to come together. There’s a lot of uncertainty at times so strong leadership and clear goals are especially important at this point.

Storming is when the work has begun. The team now must reckon with reality and will inevitably experience resistant of some kind or another. Team members will also be working with one another, and this will probably produce some conflicts and raise some problems too.

Norming is when the team has started to get its act together. Differences within the team are being reconciled, realistic expectations around team goals have been established, and the real work is finally being done.

Performing is when is the team at its peak. It’s working as a well-oiled machine, hitting its targets, and operating efficiently.

Mourning the fifth step refers to the point when a team is being wound down. Some teams may not go through this step and continue indefinitely, but ones that reach an end point, for whatever reason, will undergo a period of reflection and probably a little sadness. 

Tuckman’s model is useful as it covers the whole team lifecycle, and it gives you the landmarks of team development and maintenance to look out for. By regularly reviewing how your team is functioning, you can determine which of the Tuckman’s stages have been reached, as well as the next steps you need to take to develop your team onto the next level. But remember that this isn’t always a linear process and teams can go “backward” as well as “forward”, going down to a previous level, instead of onwards to the next.

Let’s look at some more general principles for creating and maintaining a team.

It is important to make sure that the team’s purpose is clearly defined. This might be a one-off project, or it could be permanent working group. Whatever it is, it should be made clear, because it will affect your choices when it comes to choosing the right people.

So, who are the right people? You should try and find a balance of fresh perspectives and experience. Look out for people with the right skills and talent. Also, you should think hard about the pool you’re drawing from, do some of the people work better together or maybe they should be placed with others? These are important factors to consider. 

Make sure you schedule regular reviews and catchups, so that everyone is clear on their duties and responsibilities, but not so regular that they become a nuisance. 

And finally, it’s really important, that you have multiple channels and methods for giving and receiving feedback. Your team should develop a culture of giving each other feedback, with some kind of formal process around this, especially when it comes to giving feedback about team leadership.
