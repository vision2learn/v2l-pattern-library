JOE: The use of parental controls with online media, can solve many problems with regards to protecting children with autism, from unpleasant or inappropriate content. This, however, will probably only work short term, because the individual may become more proficient at bypassing these controls, as they get older, especially if they are good with technology. Parents may also stop monitoring the children as they feel that there isn’t a need anymore.
 
BETH: Most of the time the use of IT, video games and social media in moderation, can be positive activities, as long as sensible guidelines are followed. In the same way safety checks are done when children and young people are out and about, then the same should apply on the internet.

However, when is an individual with autism, deemed old enough to make their own choices and to be trusted online, should parents still enforce their controls on adults because of a perceived need to protect them? 

JOE: It can be more successful if parents share internet experiences with their children, having regular open discussions with them about their safety in general, but especially online. As the internet means so much to many autistic children, parents taking an interest seems natural and supportive and a way to develop the parent-child bond, rather than a way of spying on them.

BETH: Addiction especially can be a concern for parents of autistic children and adults, as their obsessive nature can lead to internet gaming and social media dependency. An individual with autism may often find sanctuary from the real world, in an alternate world that appears safe, and offers an opportunity to meet and communicate comfortably in their own home with people more like themselves, with the same interests.

JOE: Many individuals with autism who suffer loneliness and isolation due to problems with social interaction, can find communicating via a computer a much less stressful and a more enjoyable way of maintaining friendships. Social media allows for an almost instant response and can be helpful in communicating with other individuals with autism and their families, friends and carers.

BETH: It enables charities and organisations offering help and advice to reach people more easily with online support available often 24/7, not just during office hours. 

The internet can have both positive and negative impacts on individuals with autism and their families, however If used appropriately and in moderation, can be of great benefit in terms of offering a different form of socialisation. It can also be beneficial in terms of accessing information and being able to share interests. 

JOE: Negative things can happen using social media and the internet as they can in all aspects of life, but as long as individuals with autism are made aware and prepared for those risks, they can develop the tools to cope with them.

BETH: The positive thing about computers and the internet is that they are potentially the greatest asset for individuals with autism, opening up a new world of potential friends, opportunity and employment. The internet is here to stay so it is important to make it a positive and beneficial experience for all, however they can most effectively use it.
