Good communication in the workplace should be effective, but there are often barriers that can affect this, so let’s have a look at what they are and how we can overcome them.

Cultural differences-These often occur when the kind of language being used, such as the use of vocabulary or style, is unsuitable or unfamiliar to the receiver. This might be because the two people involved are used to different workplace cultures, so may find one another’s style of communicating unfamiliar and challenging. 

The important thing about overcoming language barriers is to establish a common ground that both sender and receiver are familiar with. This will make communication smoother and more reliable. It's also important to bear in mind who the audience is and what you can do to make the language more appropriate for them?

Inattention and a lack of engagement- This may mean that neither the sender, the receiver, or both really care about communicating, so not enough attention is being paid. Communication doesn’t work if the people involved don’t care about what’s being communicated. If they don’t care, they won’t make the effort to communicate properly, or care about the message they receive.

It's important that the sender invests time and energy into making the communication relevant to the receiver making it clear why it is important for them. In turn receivers should try to avoid distractions when they receive a message, so they can focus on it fully and ask follow-up questions if needed.

Information overload- This can involve too much information being communicated, and this could either be because the wrong channel is being used, or the information hasn't been coded correctly. If we try to communicate too much information in one go, it won’t work because the person receiving may feel overwhelmed, with all the facts being thrown at them. Some methods are more flexible with how much information they can effectively transmit, than others.

Senders need to be mindful of how much information they need to communicate and how much they are communicating. It's easy to go into too much detail about irrelevant points sometimes. So remember in general, the shorter the message, the easier it is to understand [though that doesn't mean you should omit important details if they're necessary].

Distractions and noise- These can be literal, such as if you're trying to have a sensitive conversation in a noisy business area, or it can be more general, like if an email is cluttered with unnecessary pictures. Distractions and noise pull the ‘receiver’ away from the point of the communication, meaning they might walk away not realising or understanding what the point was meant to be. 

Keep communications clear and to the point. Don't throw in visuals or graphics or fancy phrases just for the sake of them. Everything in the message should have a reason to be there. And remember to make sure that conversations happen somewhere quiet and private, especially if they're on a sensitive subject.

Emotions- In nonverbal and oral communication, it’s easy to let our emotions get the better of us and undermine our message. Although there’s often a place for some emotional expression in communication, it can also sometimes inhibit effective communication. That’s because the way we’re feeling, can often cloud our rational mind, meaning we’re not receptive to what’s actually being said. We might feel that something unfair has been said, meaning we don’t want to listen to valid criticism. Or we might feel so frustrated about a situation that our communications sound angry and aggressive. 

It’s important to remember human beings aren't robots and there's nothing wrong with your emotions coming through in communication! But you need to consider how you're expressing your feelings, and if it's appropriate to do so at that time. Being too emotive or trying to provoke an emotional response in the receiver, might not always be appropriate.

Lack of clarity- This could be due to poor encoding or an inappropriate channel, the information in the message just isn't clear to the receiver or isn’t interpreted in the way you meant it to be. If this happens then this is a failed communication. 

If a communication is too vague or too long, the receiver may feel like they're time is being wasted. They'll lose focus and miss important information. Your communications should be direct and to the point. Avoid using complicated imagery or metaphors and be clear about the purpose of the communication.

Wrong style and tone -The message could be using the right channel, but is too formal and impersonal, or the opposite too informal and casual. This means that the receiver won’t be as receptive to the message because it won’t feel right to them and will undermine the communication, making it less effective.

Everyone has their own style of writing and speaking, and that's fine. In fact, a bit of personality can often make your communication easier to understand. But be mindful of your tone and style and think about what your audience is expecting from you, and what you need to say. Think about some of the communications you have received in the past and how you might have said things differently.

So, there are a range of barriers to communication in the workplace, but these barriers can be overcome if you just take some time to consider how they might be received and if you could communicate in a more effective way.
