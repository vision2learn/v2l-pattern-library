Sensitive data that includes PII [Personally identifiable information], PHI [Protected health information] and personal information, is held by many organisations. It may be low level or high level and should be kept secure accordingly.

**Nicole:** Personal Identifiable Information [PII] is information that can be used by a cybercriminal to identify or locate an individual. This could be a name, address, phone numbers, data of birth, IP address, location details, or any other physical or digital identity data. 

Think about how much of this data is shared online especially through social media. 

Birthday details, age, phone number etc. and then think about what could happen if this data got into the wrong hands. The less personal details and information shown on social media, the less PII information is available to cyber criminals.

**Abraham:** Protected Health Information [PHI] is [PII] that has been collected through healthcare services. This may relate to physical or mental health of an individual, their access to health care services, and any information about their health status, including medical histories, test results, mental health information and even case studies. PHI could be stolen by hackers and sold or ransomed until the healthcare organisation pays a fee if they not adequately protected. 

**Nicole:** Personal information in the UK is covered by GDPR G.D.P.R. and all personal data must be processed securely using the appropriate organisational measures. To make sure this happens companies must consider risk analysis regarding the safety of data both physical and digital, making sure that all data is kept secure and encrypted.

**Abraham:** There are many kinds of cyber security incidents but those that involve personal data breaches need to be reported to the ICO [Information commissioners office] within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. If this affects an individual’s rights and freedoms, then they must also be told without delay.

**Nicole:** Where appropriate this will be shared with the National Cyber security centre [NCSC] and other law enforcement and cyber crime agencies or regulators such as NCA National Crime Agency [NCA], the Financial Conduct Authority [FCA], and the Payment Cards Industry [PCI].

**Abraham:** A key principle of the UK General Data protection regulation is that personal data is processed securely and means that all systems and services should have the correct security to maintain confidentiality.

**Nicole:** Intellectual property is defined as the creation of something, that could be an invention, literary work, artistic work, designs, symbols and images that are used in everything around us, including our websites and domain names. The access, distribution of and or use of without permission is classed as intellectual property crime and falls under cyber crime. Intellectual property needs to be protected by cyber security as when private data is gained and sold on, it can lead to counterfeiting, database rights issues, trademark and copyright misuse, impersonation etc. 

Intellectual property usually belongs to the author or creators of the asset, but it belongs to an employer if the asset is created whilst under their employment. If a third party creates something for another organisation the intellectual property rights will be with the third party but, the responsibility for its safety will also remain with the other organisation. 

**Abraham:** There are two important pieces of legislation concerning IP rights. The Copyright Design and Patents Act 1988 [CDPA] deals with copyright, including copyright in databases, and defines what a database is. The Copyright and Rights in Databases Regulations 1997 [CRDR] establishes what database rights are.
