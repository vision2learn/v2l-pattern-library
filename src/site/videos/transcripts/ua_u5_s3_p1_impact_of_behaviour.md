BETH: It’s common for some individuals on the autistic spectrum to behave in challenging ways. Once the reasons behind this are known, and there usually is a reason for behaviour that challenges, there are ways that this can be managed to the benefit of everyone concerned.

When individuals with autism can manage their own behaviour, they can learn how to get along better with others.

JOE: Some individuals with autism might refuse or ignore requests from others and behave in a way which is socially inappropriate such as disrobing, showing aggression or injuring themselves. This behaviour may challenge the feelings and thoughts of others they interact with.

When those who interact with individuals with autism understand why they behave this way, such as having trouble with verbal and non-verbal communication and the frustration it can cause, they are more likely to respond more positively. If they are aware of what might trigger the individual with autism, they can try to alleviate any stressors which could develop into triggers to behaviour that challenges others.

BETH: Challenging behaviour is in itself not always a problem for an individual with autism, as it can often be a way of them gaining satisfaction and control over a situation, however it is challenging to those around them, as it does not fit with what is recognised generally as ‘normal’ or acceptable behaviour.

JOE: Sometime a particular person is may become a target for behaviour that challenges, it could be a peer, a family member, a teacher or a support worker. 

BETH: Whoever it is, it is important to think about what the trigger might be. It may be the clothes they wear such as the pattern, texture or colour, it may be their perfume is overwhelming to the individual or maybe the person is associated with a distressing place or activity.

JOE: Maybe they don’t like the fact that the person spends too much time with others, in their eyes, as they want them solely to themselves, or they may simply clash with the person for no obvious reason.

This can be remedied if the person is a support worker as other people could replace them but if it is a family member it is more of a problem.
