**Abraham:** So, what exactly is social engineering and why do cyber criminals like to use it?

Social engineering is the name given to techniques used by cyber criminals to manipulate and trick people into giving out confidential information, transferring money or downloading malware. 

**Nicole:** Social engineers use psychology to manipulate a victim’s feelings of curiosity or fear to draw them into a trap. They often find it is easier to exploit a person’s willingness to trust rather than go to the trouble of trying to discover ways to hack their software.

As technology and subsequently cyber security gets more advanced, cyber criminals are now turning more to social engineering, to make their initial attack on a system by exploiting the weakest link in an organisational chain the user. 

**Abraham:** Unlike cyber attacks that rely on system vulnerabilities to gain access to unauthorised devices, social engineering targets human vulnerabilities. They can use a variety of methods both online and offline such as phone calls, emails, text messages or other means to con unsuspecting users into compromising their own systems or their work-based systems by installing malware and handing over access to their sensitive and personal information and potentially that of their employer. 

**Nicole:** We have already looked at OSINT or open source intelligence information in previous videos but let’s have a quick recap and look at how open source information can help cyber criminals with social engineering.

**Abraham:** Open-source information is information that is publicly available, and anyone can obtain by request, purchase or observation. Information to be considered open source can be published or broadcast for a public audience, available to the public by request, subscription or purchase. It can also be seen or heard casually or made available at a public meeting or by visiting a place or event open to the public. 

**Nicole:** Cyber criminals can use open-source intelligence to identify weaknesses to exploit in networks such as accidental leaks of information through social media, open ports or unsecured internet connected devices. 

Cyber criminals can also access publicly available information through social media sites. The list is endless and gives plenty of information to cyber criminals. That's just a small portion of the information that is available.

So how do cyber criminals use the information they have obtained by open source intelligence and what do they do with it?

**Nicole:** Social engineering is a way of manipulating people into breaking their normal security protocols. The cyber criminal uses these tactics to exploit an individual’s trust so that they can hack into their systems and software.

**Abraham:** So how can they do this and how does this affect us?

**Nicole:** By using OSINT, cyber criminals can make their attacks look legitimate, they do this by lowering your defences. They check out your details on social media and have a back story prepared.

**Abraham:** Cyber criminals will build an image of who they are pretending to be, so they can talk confidently about it and make you feel for them to increase your sense of familiarity. They make you more likely to do what they want, such as click a link. This is called pretexting. They may, for example, research Instagram and see that you have a pet dog and when they make contact, could talk about how they’ve been working at the dog shelter. This will build an emotional connection and establish themselves as a good and trustworthy person. 

**Nicole:** So what useful information can be gained through social engineering?

Cybercriminals through social engineering use multiple tactics to gain information such as passwords, usernames and banking details. They create websites which look legitimate using trademarks and logos. They rely on an individual’s trust that it must be 'legit'.

By accessing one person’s email password they also have access to all their contacts and many people use the same password so they may have access to their social media and all their social media contacts.

Think of all those emails about winning money or prizes, be eligible for a tax refund, helping someone out abroad by accepting their money into your account. They are all ways social engineering takes place so just think have you ever been a victim.

**Abraham:** As well as traditional social engineering where the attacker contacts the victim. There is also another tactic called reverse social engineering.

In reverse social engineering the cyber criminal will use OSINT to manipulate a victim into contacting them. 

**Nicole:** This could first start with a phishing link which invites the victim to respond, maybe they want to upgrade their energy supply, or have a parcel redelivered. If they click on the link and put in their details, they are hooked. 

**Abraham:** The attackers also use ‘domain squatting’ which involves setting up a fake website that resemble one the victim uses regularly but with a slightly different name. They wait for the victim to misspell the web address and click on the familiar looking link and add their username and password. When they submit, they are redirected to the legitimate site and never know they have had their details stolen. 

**Nicole:** Other forms of reverse social engineering involve posting repeatedly on a victim’s social media site until the victim becomes curious and visits the bogus site, therefore the link is made.

So how can social engineering attacks be prevented?

**Abraham:** Threat actors will always be able to find a way to exploit the vulnerability of users but if the appropriate cyber security measures are in place and people are educated and informed about potential threat of attacks through social engineering the incidence may reduce. 

**Nicole:** We all must remember to be more vigilant in everything we do especially online, not randomly put information out there to be exploited, learn to not trust so readily, and think more carefully about what we click on, we might lessen our chances of becoming a victim.
