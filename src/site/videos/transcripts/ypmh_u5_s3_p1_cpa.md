The Care Programme Approach is a package of care that may be used to plan mental healthcare and is there to support young people’s recovery from mental illness.

 
It is a framework used to assess young people’s needs, and make sure that they have the correct support for those needs from specialist teams of mental health professionals such as mental health nurses and psychiatrists.

 
LEAH:  
Different mental health services will have policies about who is able to get help under CPA and a care coordinator will monitor that care and support with regular reviews, to see if needs have changed and a crisis plan must be included.

 
A CPA aims to support mental health recovery by helping an individual to understand their strengths, goals, support needs, and difficulties. It should be available if a child or young person has a wide range of needs from different services or are thought to be a high risk.

 
A child or young person may get help or be offered support under a CPA if they have a severe mental disorder, are at risk of suicide, self-harm or harm to others. They may also be eligible if they neglect themselves, are vulnerable due to various reasons or misuse drugs and alcohol.

There are other things that may qualify them such as needing the support of a carer or having their own caring responsibilities or having been detained under the mental health act.

 
They may also get help if they have recently been put in touch with a crisis team, have physical health problems or disability, housing problems or ethnicity issues. Local teams will have their own policies, but these will be closely linked to the general guidelines.

 
LEAH:  
Young people who have been detained under the Mental Health Act will be supported through the CPA after they are discharged from hospital.

 
A care plan is drawn up and this a written plan of how their care needs will be met including information about treatment and any other areas of their life that may need supporting, such as housing, education or employment. 

 
RHIANNON:  
Other things likely to be considered are personal circumstances, medication, therapy, physical health needs, problems with drugs and alcohol and any risks of harm to themselves or others.

 
A multi-disciplinary approach will be used to assess needs and make the care plan and a copy of this plan will be available for the individual or a parent or carer if they wish. It will also have full details of what to do in a crisis and once a young person turns 17 and they need to move to adult services this will be planned as part of their care plan.
