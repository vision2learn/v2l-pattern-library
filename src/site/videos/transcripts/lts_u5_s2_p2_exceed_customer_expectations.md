In this video we are going to look at the expectations of customers. So, what are customer expectations and why is it important to meet or exceed them?

Customer expectations are just what they sound like, what the customer expects from a service or a product. These will be formed by a culmination of different factors, including experience with your organisation, the opinions they’ve heard expressed about the product or service you offer, and the general reputation your organisation has overall. 

Meeting and exceeding customer expectations translates into loyal customers and an increase in word-of-mouth sales.

It’s important that you’re able to identify exactly what the customer’s expectations are and you can do this through formal channels like surveys and feedback forms, or more informally through natural conversations and observations. It’s not just about a flashy website or how good an organisation looks on paper, it is also about the face-to-face response from staff that makes for a good customer experience as well as meeting and exceeding expectations. 

When you’ve established what a customer expects, then you can then start to plan how you’re going to meet and exceed those expectations. Customers don’t just purchase products and services they are often looking for a good feeling, solutions to problems and good aftercare.

When a customer’s expectations are met, it means that they have had the experience they wanted to have when interacting with your organization and purchasing the product or service you offer. This makes it more likely the customer will probably become a return customer themselves or may recommend you to others.

However, meeting expectations of the customer is not as good as exceeding their expectations. Although they may be happy that they got what they wanted, by going that extra mile the experience could be even better, which in turn would mean that they are more likely to recommend you and your services or products with confidence. 

When a customer’s expectations are exceeded, you might very well have made a customer for life! When a customer makes the comment “my expectations were exceeded” what they are really saying is “I was impressed”. Exceeding their expectations will make them feel like they can rely on you or your organisation to always provide them with a quality service and offering, boosting the likelihood of them becoming a return customer and recommending your organization to other people.

Unfortunately, though there may be times when a customer may feel that their expectations were simply not met, and this may be for a lot of reasons, including the possibility that the customer simply had unrealistic expectations about what was possible. You should, however, always take these situations where you a customer was left unsatisfied as learning opportunities, to help improve your customer service, and the product and services your organisation offers. 

Sometimes things may also go wrong on your side, but it’s the way that you handle things that makes all the difference. This often doesn’t involve a big monetary investment instead good customer service and personal interaction with a customer can help with expectations and improve the provision.

What should always be remembered is that without the customer, there would not be an organisation as ultimately, they are the ones that make it all possible. By consistently meeting and exceeding the expectations of customers you can help guarantee a stable, loyal, and growing customer base which can in turn then help an organisation expand and go from strength to strength.
