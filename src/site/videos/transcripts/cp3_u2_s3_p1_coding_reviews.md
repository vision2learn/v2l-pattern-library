**Leah:** Coding reviews are an integral part of the software development process, but there are several different ‘review methods’ that can used and each has its own specific benefits and downsides depending on your project and team structure. 

Let’s look at some of the most common methods for conducting coding reviews. 

**Chris:** Instant code review—also known as pair programming title on screen

In this type of review, there are two developers, one will be on the keyboard producing the code and the other reviewing it as it is being written, paying attention to potential issues and giving ideas for code improvement on the go as well as identifying potential future problems. 

**Leah:** The developer typing is identified as the driver and the developer reviewing the code is the navigator. When using this method, the two programmers will switch roles frequently.

By working together through the process of finding a solution you increase the opportunity to get it right. The ‘driver’ is able to focus all their attention on the task of coding knowing that the ‘navigator’ is there to observe, give guidance and support when required. 

**Chris:** This type of review is useful when aiming for fast high-quality code and a reduced cost of development. It is also useful for personal development, as the pair can learn from each other and can help to improve morale. 

But there are also downsides to this type of review as the pair need to work effectively together as a team, and personal egos may impact on this by one or other of the team feeling intimidated. You may also have the junior developer ‘driving’ and the more experienced developer ‘navigating’ who may get frustrated that the driver is too slow ultimately resulting in conflict. 

**Leah:** Inspection 

**Chris:** title on screen

**Leah:** This type of review is typically carried out by peers, who are trained to review code by looking at errors using well defined processes. 

It is commonly referred to as a Fagan inspection after the inventor of the process, ‘Michael Fagan’.

**Chris:** The principle behind this is that the requirements are agreed at the beginning. For each activity or operation, an entry and exit criteria are specified. The reviewer can then compare the results of the activity to the pre-defined exit criteria, to validate that it has complied with these criteria. 

The steps followed in the inspection are.

Bold titles on screen

Overview- the assignment of roles and the exit requirements for each activity are established.

Preparation- all those involved in the review prepare for their roles, familiarise themselves with the requirements for each section, inspect any supporting materials and note down any questions or concerns that they may have. 

Inspection- this is when the actual review takes place, and any errors are identified.

Rework- developers correct any errors that were found during the inspection.

Follow up- a moderator checks over the rework to ensure that all the identified errors have been corrected and no new ones have been created during the fix. 

Walkthrough title on screen

Is a form of software peer review where the lead designer or coder walks the members of the development team and other stakeholders through the product. Those involved can ask questions or comment on errors or other concerns they may have. This type of review is useful in that it gives a broad overview of the product but is not as in depth as other review types and lacks the direct focus of on training and process development. 

Technical review title on screen

This type of review is carried out by a team of qualified developers who will undertake a peer review of the product and look at it in detail, to ensure that the product meets the suitability criteria for its intended use, and any variance from the original design and specification requirements that were agreed. 

The review team may also make recommendations for alternative solutions. Technical reviews differ from inspections in their ability to suggest direct alterations to the product reviewed, rather than any focus on training or process improvement.
