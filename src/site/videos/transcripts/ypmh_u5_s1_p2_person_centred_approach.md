RHIANNON:  
Person-centred care with Children and young people, means that they are at the centre of the support offered. That support includes choice and is holistic and specifically tailored to the young person’s needs and wishes.

 
In the past, people were expected to fit in with the routines and practices that health and social services felt were most appropriate. But in order to be person-centred, services have had to change to be more flexible to meet individual needs in a manner that is best for them.

 
LEAH:  
Being compassionate, thinking about things from the person’s point of view and being respectful are all important. This might be shown through sharing decisions with a young person and their family and helping them to manage their health. 

 
It is as much about the way professionals and individuals think about care and their relationships, then the actual services available.

 
RHIANNON:  
There are also different ways this partnership can work, such as on a one-to-one basis, where children and young people take part in decisions about their health and care directly, or on a collective group basis, whereby groups are involved in decisions about the design and delivery of person centred services. The underlying philosophy is the same, it is all about doing things with people, rather than ‘to’ them.

 
LEAH:  
Including young people in the decision making around their mental health care leads to better involvement and engagement and can help in creating a successful recovery model.
