BETH: Supporting individuals with autism to take positive risks in their lives is not about encouraging them to do dangerous activities or things. It’s about getting them to take control of their lives and make choices and then learn from the consequences of those choices whether positive or negative. 

Everyone has the right to take risks, no matter how scary it might be, especially for those supporting them. However, there is still a need for ‘safeguarding’ or ‘duty of care’ with all risks that may be taken.

JOE: Risk is a necessary and important part of life for everyone, but we need to think about how risk affects those with autism and manage this risk. When managing risk, there is the potential for carers to be overcautious and overprotective of an individual, depending on the severity of their disorder.

BETH: By identifying the risks associated with an activity for an individual and assessing how that activity can be completed effectively and safely, can help them to build confidence and develop new skills.  Also understanding that there are consequences to taking risks both positive and negative, can help to promote the ability to learn from mistakes. 

JOE: Carers can empower the individual to have a more fulfilling life through positive risk-taking, and this can bring real benefits, when it takes into account the needs and preferences of the individual and their circumstances. The individual is then enabled to grow in confidence, learning from their experiences, and developing new skills and abilities, and make full use of their opportunities and potential.

BETH: Supporting individuals with autism to take positive risks puts them more in control of their own lives. Risk shouldn’t be seen as a negative experience that’s best avoided as this can lead to an individual having a more limited life experience.
