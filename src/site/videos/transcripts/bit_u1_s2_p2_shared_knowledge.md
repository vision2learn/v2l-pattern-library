Hello and welcome to this video in which we'll look at the importance of sharing knowledge, information, and performance measures with other members of the team and other groups. Sharing information and knowledge can increase the productivity of a team that helps it to work faster and smarter. Sharing knowledge will generate improvement ideas and ensure the best methods are applied to work practices or processes.

Let's take a look at these three areas in more detail. Let's start by thinking back to teams and their strengths and weaknesses. We learned that each individual within a team has different skills that they bring to the team and for good, productive teams, these compliment each other. While the same applies to knowledge, different people within an organisation or team bring with them their own knowledge.

Some may know a little information for what's needed, others may be experts, and some may not know anything for the particular topic. For example, imagine you are asked to join a team to brainstorm where a new product hasn't been as successful as expected. On that team, some people will have been involved in the initial idea and carried it though to deployment.

Others may work in customer services and be the ones receiving complaints. Other team members may not know much about the product but have experience of problem solving. By sharing their experiences and knowledge, the team can collaboratively work together to understand what's gone wrong and discuss solutions to improve the product.

Information sharing. Let's begin by looking at how information differs from knowledge. Knowledge that we've already looked at is deeper than information. Knowledge draws on experience and expertise. It's the wisdom a person has from knowing a lot about a subject. Information on the other hand is the data or instructions that anyone can pick up from a sheet, as an example.

Think about baking a cake. Anyone can get the information for how to bake a cake from a recipe and instructions. It's only those with knowledge of baking that know how to turn it into a successful cake that you can't wait to try. Within our team of problem solvers, customer services can prepare information that's been gathered from customer complaints and feedback.

This may be displayed in a graph, survey or verbally passed on. Those with the knowledge of how and why the product was developed can use this information to understand what's gone wrong. By collaborating together, all members of team can share their information and knowledge to reach a solution.

Performance measures allow an organization to know if things are going right. They have targets that tell the management if expectations are being met. In our example, they are used as a tool to know if the new product is as successful as expected. By setting a target that a certain number of units will be sold within, say the first three months, or that it will score a three stars on TripAdvisor in the same timeframe, the company can monitor its success.

So not only has the customer service department received a lot of complaints, the product is also below its performance Measures. By bringing together the information gathered by customer service and the performance measures, the team can share their knowledge to solve the initial product difficulties and develop a successful product.
