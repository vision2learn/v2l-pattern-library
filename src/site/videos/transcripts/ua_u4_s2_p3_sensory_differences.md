JOE: Children on the autism spectrum are more likely to have a restricted diet, eating a more limited range or refusing more foods than their typically developing peers.

BETH: A child with autism can be helped to be distracted from their anxiety overeating, by stimulating their other senses. Making them feel relaxed can help, by making sure their seat is comfy and maybe playing some music or a story to keep them relaxed.

JOE: Individuals who find food smells overpowering, may prefer to eat bland food whilst those who have an under sensitivity to smell and taste, may prefer stronger flavours and some strong smells may be a source of pleasure.

Some people find hard food or sloppy food unbearable, while others may be supersensitive to the texture of food and have to touch everything with their fingers to see how it feels before putting it in their mouth.

BETH: Modifying food to improve the sensory experience, such as puréeing, and introducing a new food or textures in small steps, can help with gradual desensitisation. 

To introduce a new food a series of small steps can be tried. The individual should be able to just look at it, touch it, put it on their plate, then smell it, lick it, put it into their mouth, bite it, chew it, and swallow it. If they don’t like it, they may spit it out, but this should not be looked at negatively, as it might not happen next time. This can go on potentially for months until an individual gets more comfortable with the new sensations.
