There are several ways in which standardised working methods benefit the workplace, which include reduced waste, increased productivity, increased customer satisfaction, and compliance. In this video, we'll look at each of these in more detail. Let's start with reduced waste. There are a few areas within the working environment where waste can be reduced through standardized working methods.

Firstly, standardised working methods can help ensure that resources are optimised. What does this mean? By using standardised procedures, the amount of resources can be measured, thus minimising waste. We've previously looked at just in time ordering for keeping inventory lean. Having a standardised process ensures that only what's needed is ordered, which prevents additional inventory and overspending.

Such processes also help to minimise errors. By following set guidelines, employees are less likely to make errors, thus reducing the likelihood of rework and wasting time, materials, and money. Increased productivity, set guidelines, as we've just seen can be very important. It's important that these are clear, well defined, and employees can follow them.

When done correctly, guidelines help employees to carry out their tasks efficiently. It minimises thinking time and helps to ensure tasks are completed to the correct standard every time. It's easy to see how such systems can reduce variations and maintain consistency across output. Customer Satisfaction Consistency, as we've just looked at, ensures that products are produced to the same standard time after time.

This builds trust for the customer who know that they can rely on good quality every time. Not only is product quality reliable, but so is time. We've already seen that standardised working methods reduce time, and this also ensures that customers will get their product when they expect it. Meeting customer expectations in this way ensures that trust is built up, and the customer is consistently satisfied with the product and service, resulting in repeat business and positive reviews.

Compliance. In Unit 1 we saw how important legislation, procedures and processes are to businesses. Often, an industry will need to comply with certain regulations to ensure that they remain safe and offer good quality. Think of an electrician, they must be qualified to carry out electrical work and work to set safety guidelines.

When this is not done, organisations face heavy fines and they are unlikely to get repeat business. Standardised processes help an organisation to identify potential risks and ensure that the necessary controls are in place to manage them effectively.
