**Leah:** Quite a lot of the time the focus of effective teamwork relates to have good working relationships with other members of the team and while this is critical to the successful completion of projects, what about relationships with other teams? 

**Chris:** It is just as important to develop good working relationships with other teams as there are very few instances when a project relies solely on the input and output of one team. To successfully complete a project there will likely be several teams working on different elements.

**Leah:** As we mentioned in session 1, when working in an ‘Agile’ environment, projects will involve teams with a variety of skillsets who will work on different elements of a project. These teams will rely on each other as the project progresses and frequently use ‘Scrum’ and ‘Kanban’ methodologies to track progress across the teams. This enables the teams to monitor progress and identify any potential risks or holdups and develop solutions to move forward. 

**Chris:** Multi team projects can help to maintain effective and develop positive working relationships. This way you can gain a holistic view of the project and working with other teams on collaborative projects are also a useful way to gain an understanding about the organisation’s other moving parts, and how those parts can work together to better achieve the organisation’s mission. When members of different teams communicate frequently, you should always feel able to ask questions and have a firm grasp of the nature of one another’s work and therefore productivity is considerably boosted.

**Leah:** Collaborative work also allows team members to bring their individual work styles, skills, and perspectives to projects which creates opportunities for new solutions and better results. 

Imagine not knowing that the perfect approach, team member, or resource for a task your team is stuck on, has been under your nose the entire time. 

**Chris:** Good communication provides opportunities to improve your own teams’ methodologies. When teams from different departments are transparent about how they work, it gives you the opportunity to review the way you do your own work which can highlight any weaknesses that you or another team might have. When working together, take note of what questions others ask about your department, what is confusing to them about the way you do your work, and consider how that might help you to find ways to improve your own ways of working.

**Leah:** It can also help you to gain a greater sense of the challenges that might be faced in terms of deadlines and discover ways you can continue to support one another, coordinate your work with each other, and provide support to others.
