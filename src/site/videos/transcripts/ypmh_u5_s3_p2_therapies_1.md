Psychotherapy, talking therapy, counselling, or simply therapy, are some of the terms used as alternatives to medication and can really benefit people struggling with emotional difficulties, life challenges, and mental health concerns.

 
Medication can reduce some of the symptoms of mental health conditions, but therapy teaches people skills to address symptoms on their own, and these can stay with the individual after therapy ends, and as symptoms improve, the need for treatment may reduce.

 
Untreated mental health issues often get worse and may have other negative effects affecting work, study, relationships and general health. A therapist can give support going forward, and once a crisis is over, they can be a safe haven for when things get tough.

 
Psychological therapies services are also known as Improving Access to Psychological Therapies [IAPT] services. Examples of these are, talking therapies like counselling for depression, and cognitive behavioural therapy [CBT] and are available on the NHS with referral from a GP or self-referral.

 
There are many different types of ‘talking therapy’, but they all involve working with a trained therapist. The therapist helps find answers to the problems the child or young person is having. One type of talking therapy may be better than another, and different talking therapies suit different people.
