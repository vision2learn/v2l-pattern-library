Children and young people from the poorest households can be up to three times more likely to have a mental health problem than those growing up in better-off homes. This may be related to the financial strains and stresses put on the family.

 
Parental mental illness is associated with increased rates of mental health problems in children and young people, with an estimated one-third to two-thirds of children and young people whose parents have a mental health problem, experiencing difficulty themselves.

 
If the family is given support financially and helped to overcome these issues, then the potential impact on mental health of the children or young people should be lessened.

 
A child cannot change their life in the short term, but by getting the right support from family and friends or even from professionals, a child can be helped to realise that they are not to blame, and they can overcome these barriers.

 
Physical well-being is a great way towards improving mental wellbeing and if children and young people are encouraged to get involved with physical exercise, this can help, as it influences the release and uptake of the feel-good chemicals ‘endorphins’ in the brain. 

 
By preventing or handling mental health problems and building resilience in children and young people, their future mental health and well-being can be improved.

 
A child or young person’s ability to manage emotions can support or impede their learning, their academic engagement, work ethic, commitment and ultimately their school success.

 
Schools need to educate children and young people to understand how to look after their mental health as well as their physical health.

 
Children with higher levels of emotional, behavioural, social and school wellbeing, have higher levels of academic achievement and are more engaged in school.

 
Emotional wellbeing is a key indicator of academic achievement, and subsequently improved outcomes in later life. Good mental well-being on leaving school has a much greater impact on life chances and outcomes than exam success. 

 
Mental health and wellbeing programmes in schools, can lead to significant improvements in children’s mental health, and social and emotional skills. Well-being provision can also lead to reductions in classroom misbehaviour and bullying.

 
Schools are much more than centres of learning and children need an education that prepares them academically and emotionally for the challenges they will face inside the classroom, and for when they leave.
