JOE: Processing everyday sensory information can be a problem for individuals with autism as they may be oversensitive, known as ‘hypersensitive’, or under sensitive, known as ‘hyposensitive’ or even both, at different times. These differences in their senses can affect how they feel, or act and can have a profound effect on their lives.

BETH: Individuals who have problems with their vision may, if they are under sensitive, have poor depth perception, causing clumsiness or have peripheral vision problems. If they are oversensitive, they may need to focus on a detail rather than the whole thing and they may have trouble being in rooms with bright light such as fluorescents, or even sleeping without complete darkness.

JOE: If an individual has under sensitivity to sounds, they may not respond to verbal communication, they might enjoy crowded noisy rooms or bang doors and objects for stimulation. An oversensitivity to sound can mean that sounds seem magnified, distorted or confused and the individuals can have difficulties concentrating because they are distracted.

BETH: Every day smells can be problematic for Individuals who are under sensitive they cannot tell what things are, whether they are safe to drink or eat and they can fail to notice extreme odours such as body odour. Those who are oversensitive dislike strong smelling products like cleaning products, cat and dogs, and even perfumes, shampoos and deodorants. They may also dislike the smells of foods and cooking.

JOE: Some individuals with autism may have a restricted diet due to taste issues. If under sensitive, they may eat things that aren’t edible and shouldn’t be eaten or really spicy food. If over sensitive, they might find most things too strong and overpowering choosing to only eat bland foods.

GREEN: Problems with touch can manifest in different ways. Individuals who are under sensitive maybe having a high pain threshold and be unable to feel things, such as food in the mouth. Individuals with over sensitivity may find textures of certain foods or clothing intolerable. They may find being touched painful and have difficulties washing or brushing their hair. 

Each individual with autism has their own sensory issues and these can impact greatly on their lives and the way people perceive them.
