BETH: A range of strategies can be used to support communication and social interaction let’s look at these in more detail.

JOE: PECS is a unique alternative/augmentative communication system that works for children who do not speak, who are unintelligible or who are minimal communicators. It consists of six phases and begins by teaching an individual to give a single picture of a desired item or action to a “communicative partner” who immediately honours the exchange as a request. The system goes on to teach discrimination of pictures and how to put them together in ‘sentences’ and may in fact allow learners to develop speech by transitioning  onto a speech generating device (SGD).

BETH: Makaton is a language programme using symbols, signs and speech in spoken word order, to enable communication. Signing can help those with no speech, or those whose speech is unclear and helps provide extra clues to what is being said. Symbols can help with limited speech and cannot or prefer not to sign. Makaton takes away the frustration and enables individuals to connect with other people and the world around them.

JOE: Social stories can help autistic people develop greater social understanding. They are short descriptions of a particular situation, event or activity, and what to expect in that situation and why. Social stories can be used to develop self-care skills such as how to clean teeth, social skills like saying thank you, and academic abilities. They present information in a literal way, which may improve a person's understanding of a difficult situations or activities. 

BETH: Music cues can be used for children with autism as they seem to respond to emotions conveyed through music better than to other forms of communication. Engaging with music is thought to help children become more flexible, responsive and attentive, leading to better social and communication skills.

JOE: Comic strip conversations are simple visual representations of conversation. They show the things that are actually said in a conversation, how people might be feeling, what people's intentions might be. Comic strip conversations use stick figures and symbols to represent social interactions and abstract aspects of conversation, and colour to represent the emotional content of a statement or message.
 
BETH: Visual supports and technology can be used as communication tools in most situations, and are adaptable and often portable. They help to provide structure and routine and improve understanding, avoiding frustration. Visual supports can be real objects of reference or tactile objects, for example food labels, pictures, models, coloured squares, symbols or written words. They can also be created using technology, showing photographs, short videos, pictures and symbols or actions on a smartphone, tablet or computer. Visual supports however are very personal and what works for one person may not work for another.
