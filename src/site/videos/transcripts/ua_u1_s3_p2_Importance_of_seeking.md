BETH: Seeking a diagnosis of Autism spectrum disorder, can be helpful as it can give a better understanding of why a person behaves the way they do and why they always may have felt different, therefore working to increase their confidence.

JOE: It can give parents and their child a clear sense of the limitations and challenges they may face as a result of the disorder, and help gain more understanding regarding behaviour, strengths, needs and the appropriate support available. 

It can help parents feel more able to cope with the reactions of others to their child and have confidence in explaining their behaviour, and helping them understand that their child is not just being difficult.

BETH: A diagnosis can also help gain the correct support in all areas, especially financially if needed, and in educational settings at all levels. 

However, support in school is not solely dependent on a diagnosis, but is based on individual needs, and some families may be entitled to benefits such as DLA if their disability impacts their ability to function at home or in the community.

JOE: If a parent wants a diagnosis and the child or young person is old enough
and has the capacity to comprehend the process, they should be involved in the decision making where possible. 

What needs to be remembered is that every person is different whether diagnosed with a disorder or not, and it is important to consider the implications to the people involved, not just in the present but for the future..
