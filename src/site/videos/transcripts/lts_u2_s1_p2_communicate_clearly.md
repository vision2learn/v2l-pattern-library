Communication is all about giving, receiving, and sharing information but this must be done clearly and effectively to be of value. 

Different forms of communication are suitable for different occasions, and there's no one way that's better than all the others. 

So, how can you achieve clear and effective communication using the main forms of communication? 

Let’s look at these whilst remembering that different methods will often be combined in different ways.

Written communication, this includes emails, handwritten memos, typed letters, digital messages, SMS messages, and much more besides. 

When using written communication, you need to be precise, clear, and unambiguous. Make sure you're being explicit with what you want to communicate, and don't leave it to interpretation. It is therefore, important to review everything you write before you send it, just in case what you have written doesn’t impart what you meant it to.

It is also important that when receiving written communication, you make sure, you take the time to read it carefully, so you don't miss anything.

Oral communication includes face-to-face conversations, phone calls, video calls, and giving presentations.

When communicating orally you should make sure that you speak slowly and clearly. You don't want the other person to mishear you, so make sure you're being precise with your words. If you need to give someone a lot of information this way, take your time, so as not to overwhelm them by speaking too quickly. And make sure you use language and terminology that they'll understand.

Although all forms of communication involve a back-and-forth, this is especially true for oral communication. It is therefore important to make sure the other person has time to reply and ask questions, and don't assume they've understood everything. Gently ask them if they want you to clarify and listen carefully to what they say at all times.

Nonverbal communication means communicating &quot;without words&quot;. Typically, it's about body language and appearance. It's your facial expressions, your tone of voice, and what you're doing with your arms and legs. It's everything you're doing except the writing or speaking.

Try to be aware of things like posture and stance. It's important to appear confident and calm, but also be wary of appearing physically imposing or defensive. Try to be aware of your expressions too. Sometimes when we're having a difficult conversation, it's easy to say a lot with just how your face looks, so it’s essential that your body language matches your words.

Nonverbal communication also includes other things like the way you dress. Think about the environment you're in and who you're going to be speaking to, when deciding what you're going to wear. What impression are you giving with your outfit?

Visual communication is when images or video are used to communicate information. This is often used along with words or speech. For example, including graphs or animations in a presentation.

The important thing is that the visual element of communication should complement the other elements. Sometimes people will include visual elements which can distract attention and don't enhance the message or contain any relevant information on their own. Communication today has become more visual in general, especially with the use of digital media, but it is also important that imagery is not overused, so that the message gets lost or misinterpreted.

Listening. Understanding how communication takes place can help you to communicate more effectively, and as you learn to communicate more effectively, you start to understand how listening is an important part of communication. 

Active listening is a way of effectively engaging with those who are communicating with you and is an important part of the negotiating process. By letting others speak and showing that you understand what they are saying, by the way you respond to, both verbally and nonverbally, helps ensure that effective communication is taking place. 

So, we can see that communication can take place in numerous ways but unless you understand what is being communicated to you, and you can show that you have understood that communication, then it is not going to be effective.
