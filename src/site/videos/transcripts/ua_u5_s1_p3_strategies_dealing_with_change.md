JOE: If change is inevitable then individuals with autism can be helped and guided through the process by using strategies to prepare and support them through those changes.

BETH: If a change is due to happen at school, parents and carers can be proactive in finding out about what the changes will be. If there is to be a new teacher or new classrooms, by finding out what is involved in advance, parents and carers can work out any impact it might have on a child.

JOE: The day of the change can be marked on a calendar and the child can be encouraged to count down to that day. Describing the change clearly gives the child time to process the information along with visual supports.

Visual supports like social stories and photos can be used to show the outcome of activities such as going on holiday and all that goes with it. 

BETH: Showing a photo of a plane and a hotel may make an individual nervous to go on a plane or concerned about the hotel.  But if the whole process is documented from start to finish and shown visually including lots of photos of the destination, this will help them to understand more. The whole process can then be reversed to show the journey back and show that they are going to be returning home to somewhere and something familiar. Visual timetables can also help to make the processes more straightforward and prepare them for the trip.

JOE: Visual supports such as photos can help in preparing individuals with autism for a new place for a visit such as a stately home, a new person in their life, like a support worker or a new activity such as swimming. 

Making a book of photos, or a collection of images on their device, can give them an opportunity to look at something before and during the change. 

BETH: If the change is because of a move to a new school or care service, staff that the individual will interact with, should be a key part of the preparations. They could be introduced to them in familiar surroundings and then be introduced to the new environment by photos and a visit.

JOE: Problems with sequencing can often cause problems with behaviour when transitioning between activities, for example becoming anxious at school or college over unstructured break times. Visual timetables either digital or physical can help to alleviate stress and timer alerts can inform when things are over or due to start making a timeframe more structured.

BETH: Whatever the change, if the individual can be prepared in some way for what is about to happen, then they have the time to process the information and deal with it more appropriately from an informed position.
