LEAH: If a child or young person suffers any risk factors or starts to show signs of distress, families, communities and schools should support them to cope better with the challenges that life throws at them and try to be there to listen to them when needed.

RHIANNON: Schools can often play a critical role in giving young people the skills they need to form strong interpersonal relationships, which will support resilience such as buddy systems, peer to peer support networks, and anti-bullying initiatives. 

LEAH: Schools that promote strong social relationships and teach practical coping skills, are likely to have more resilient pupils, who are able to learn more effectively.

Schools as well as families and friends can help young people to learn how to regulate their emotions, solve problems and help them plan things that might help them cope better when life gets tough. 

RHIANNON: A child or young person can help themselves, by keeping a diary to aid focus on more positive memories. They can also keep busy doing things they enjoy, such as sports, music or drama, and try and stay away from the sources of stress. 

Once the risk factors have been recognised and understood by all concerned, they can then be addressed, and the child or young person can be better supported to maintain good mental health and well-being.
