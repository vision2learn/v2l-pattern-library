LEAH: Physical bullying, is the most obvious form of bullying and physical bullies, usually tend to be bigger, stronger, and more aggressive than their targets. They use physical attacks like kicking, hitting, punching and shoving. 

Physical bullying is the easiest to identify and most likely, what people think of when they think of bullying as this receives more attention from schools than other more subtle forms.

RHIANNON: Verbal bullying uses words, and name-calling to gain power and control over a victim, 
relentlessly insulting and belittling them. This is often based on the way a child or young person looks or acts and is often directed towards those with disabilities or special educational needs.

Verbal bullying almost always occurs, when adults aren’t around and when victims do speak out, they are told to ‘ignore it’ but they should be listened to, as it can leave deep emotional scars.

LEAH: Emotional bullying is sneaky and insidious and often goes unnoticed by parents and 
teachers. It is often used by young adolescents to ruin their target’s social standing, by spreading rumours, or breaking confidences. This type of bullying is most often used by girls.

RHIANNON: Cyberbullying is bullying via the Internet, using a smartphone, or other technology to 
harass, threaten, embarrass, or target another person. It often involves the posting of  
hurtful images, online threats, and sending offensive or discriminatory emails or texts. 

Technology makes cyberbullies feel anonymous and detached from the situation whilst enabling them to target others anytime, even in their own homes.

LEAH: Sexual Bullying can involve, sexual name-calling, crude comments, vulgar gestures, 
uninvited touching, sexual propositioning, and use of pornographic materials. In extreme cases, it can lead to sexual assault. Girls are most often the target of this, but they can be targeted both by boys and other girls. 

‘Sexting’ (texts with sexual content) also can lead to sexual bullying. If a girl sends a photo of herself to a boyfriend, he may, in anger share that photo publicly if they break up and contrary to her wishes or intentions.

RHIANNON: Prejudicial bullying, is based on prejudices children and young people may have towards different races, religions, or sexual orientation. Often, this type of bullying is severe and can lead to hate crimes. Any time a child is bullied for their sexual orientation, race, or religion, it should be reported.

LEAH: The motivation of bullies, are all different and not all bullies will fit neatly into a category. Some bullies will fall into several categories and some may appear to be in a category all their own.

RHIANNON: Some individuals become bullies because they were once bullied themselves and this is very common. Others have big egos and a group of followers and bully to keep their 
Power or social standing. Some bully out of jealousy and others seem pleasant on the outside, but are cold and calculating on the inside.

LEAH: Bullies can also work in groups or cliques, but when they are on their own, behave 
differently. Some bullies are just totally indifferent to the feelings of others and can have deep psychological problems.

RHIANNON: Whatever the type of bully, all bullying is wrong and should not be tolerated, as it can have a devastating effect on children and young people's mental well-being. It is something we should always be mindful of and on the lookout for.
