BETH: Depending on the level of ability and skills an individual with autism has, they can once they gain employment, become a valued member of a company’s workforce. They can offer loyalty, dedication and can be extremely skillful once trained. They may even have advanced skills to offer if they are high functioning.

JOE: However, they may find the usual process of gaining and starting employment very challenging because of their disorder and not their ability to do the job. If they are supported through the process by both the employer and their carers, they can find seeking and gaining employment easier to navigate and understand.

BETH: The more employers are open to taking on individuals with autism, the more they will understand their needs, and the skills and expertise they have to offer. It is important that employers realise that many individuals with autism do not see themselves as ‘disabled’ but ‘neurodiverse’ meaning they just look at life differently, so they can also bring this original thinking to a job role.

JOE: High functioning individuals can be very successful in many different fields including science, research, finance, banking and the arts. If employers are more open to looking at candidates who are on the spectrum, they have opportunities to make their workforce more diverse, with a chance that they may become their best employees.

BETH: Individuals with autism can be supported by employers offering paid work placements to see how individuals fit into their workforce. This can help them to make any adjustments that might be needed before offering a full-time role, and it can give individuals with autism a step into employment, to see what it involves and gain that valuable work experience.

JOE: Making the application process for individuals with autism, simpler and more inclusive will help in the beginning. This can be done by companies becoming more autism aware, especially about the skills individuals with autism have to offer. Being clear in the job application and interview process, can support individuals directly, making it easier for them to feel that they have a chance of gaining employment.

BETH: Being aware of sensory sensitivities and communication difficulties, can help prepare them for the differences individuals present in social interaction, such as in the interview and job induction phase. Also going forward to when they accept a role within a company, their sensory needs within their working environment.

Little changes and reasonable adjustments can make a lot of difference to an individual with autism and can help them to settle in a job and become a valued member of the workforce.
