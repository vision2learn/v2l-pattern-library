BETH: An individual with autism communicates differently to their peers often responding to verbal communication literally and they also use their behaviour as a way of communicating their feelings and emotions. It is helpful when communicating with a person with autism spectrum disorder to be aware of their likely tendency towards concrete thinking.

They often don’t understand that other people have different thoughts, desires and needs to them and cannot put themselves in someone else’s shoes. They have difficulty predicting and interpreting the behaviours of others and may also have trouble understanding the effects of their own behaviour on the people around them.

JOE: Individuals with autism often have difficulty recognising and understanding social cues and therefore do not instinctively learn to adjust their behaviour to suit different social contexts.

Their behaviour however may sometimes exhibit itself as challenging. This challenging behaviour is often a way of showing their frustration anxiety or confusion or a way of getting attention.

BETH: Behaviour exhibited by individuals with autism can often be deemed as inappropriate by their peers or others around them. This may involve them standing too close, not showing eye contact, interrupting conversations and not sticking to the usual social etiquette. If others are not aware of their autism these behaviours can seem rude.

JOE: If an individual has sensory problems, they may cause them to exhibit repetitive behaviours and actions such as hand flapping, flicking, spinning, jumping and rocking or respond adversely to sensory stimuli, this can bewilder others who are unaware or unsure of these
behaviours.

BETH: It is therefore important to know what the function of that behaviour is and try to deal with it as all behaviours serve a purpose. If a trend is recognised, then that will be the reason for the behaviour that regularly appears, and it can help to see what the triggers are and how to deal with it.

JOE: It is important then to be prepared when communicating with an individual with autism that they have their own individual challenges to social interaction and having some knowledge of the potential for behavioural issues, can help to prevent any situations becoming more challenging through ignorance.
