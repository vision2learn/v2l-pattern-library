When providing information to customers it is not only good to know what you are talking about, but you should also be aware of the different rules associated with that information.

There are three categories of rule you need to follow, these are Legal, Organizational, and Ethical. These rules are put in place to protect customers by making sure the information they’re given is accurate and not misleading. 

• Legal rules are rules you’re required to follow by law, and if you don’t follow these you may be liable to criminal penalties like fines or even a prison sentence. 

• Organisational rules are the rules your organization requires you to follow. They’re set by the organization, and although you might not be breaking the law, by not following them, you could face disciplinary proceedings, or even the sack if you don’t.

• Ethical rules are rules that aren’t in law or enforced by your organization, but which are still the right thing to do. 

It is also important to note that sometimes the same rule will be a legal, organizational, and ethical all at once, whereas other might only fall into one or two of these categories. 

So, let’s look at some examples of the rules that may relate to your role.

Legal

It’s against the law to deliberately give false or misleading information to a customer. For example, if you’re selling a health product and make wild claims and exaggerations about what it can do, like claiming it can cure serious illnesses, but you know that it can’t, then you’re committing fraud. This is a criminal offence, and the consequences can be severe, especially if people are hurt as a result. 

There are also laws about the type of information that you can share with a customer, such as personal or sensitive information about another customer or colleague which might be legally protected.

Organisational rules

Organisational rules often cover things like maintaining discipline and promoting a particular kind of culture in the workplace. For example, if you worked as part of the team in a retail environment, there’s probably a dress code you must follow. This is important to maintain the integrity of the brand, as you are the face of the business.

When providing information to customers, the organization might require its people to use a particular style and tone of voice. This is usually to try and enhance their reputation and make their product or service seem more attractive. For example, they might require their people to make sure they mention certain features about a product or service, early in the conversation with a customer to try and get their attention. Or there might be a rule that you should mention other products or services, that complement the one the customer is interested in.

Organisational rules will also require their people to follow all the legal rules associated with their role in the organisation too.

Ethical rules

There are ways of communicating information with customers which are legal, permitted by organizational rules, but are not always ethical. 

For example, you might encourage a customer to believe that a particular product or service is very popular. This can be done by just providing smaller bits of information, so they form a particular picture, like mentioning you’ve recently had to order more stock of a product as it is selling out. This might be true, but you could be presenting it to the customer in such a way, to make them think that this product is popular and in high demand, which might not be the case. You may even get commission for the amount you sell. So, this isn’t technically lying, and you’re not making false claims about the product or service, but it is still deceitful and isn’t ethical.

Likewise, using highly charged emotional language in a negotiation, or expressing exaggerated sadness, if it looks like the customer is walking away, isn’t illegal and might not be breaking any organizational rules, but it isn’t very ethical, because you’re manipulating the customers emotions.

By making sure you follow all legal, organizational, and ethical rules, you help to encourage a more open, honest, and safe working environment for your team and for customers. It’s especially important to remember that breaching these rules, can also put customers safety in jeopardy, and there can be severe consequences for this.
