One of the responsibilities you have as a team leader is ensuring your team is an equal and diverse environment.

Respecting one another’s differences and recognising that everyone deserves to be treated with dignity and respect, is how an organization keeps its culture vibrant and healthy, as well meeting the legal requirements, legislated by the Equality Act 2010. 

It is against the law to treat people different because of protected characteristics, so as a team leader you need to be alert and sensitive to this to protect your team.

Protected characteristics defined in law are:

• Age

• Gender reassignment

• Marital or civil partnership status

• Pregnancy or being on maternity leave

• Disability

• Race, colour, nationality, or ethnic or national origin

• Religion or belief

• Sex

• Sexual orientation

Your own organization will have policies in place, based on the requirements of the law, to protect equality and diversity, so your first responsibility as a team leader is to familiarize yourself with these polices. But you shouldn’t think of equality and diversity as just something you should be reactively protecting. As a team leader you should be actively promoting it and trying to expand the diversity of your team.

So, let’s look at some of the things you can do to promote equality and diversity in your team:

Take time to educate yourself about different cultures, traditions, and religions- The more you know about different cultures, traditions, and religions, the more you’ll be able to relate to members of your team who come identify with them. Think about doing small events to mark major religious holidays, such as inviting members of team who belong to a particular faith or follow a specific tradition to give a short presentation about the holiday if they’d like.

Learn about different minorities and the prejudices they face- This includes LGBTQ+ people, people with disabilities, and people from minority ethnic groups. By taking time to learn about their history and the discrimination they still face, you’ll be more sensitive to their needs, especially if they experience discrimination in the workplace. 

Look into diversity training schemes for you and your team- There are lots of different organisations, that offer training to help you and your team, become more aware of issues of equality and diversity. Speak to your HR department to see if there’s training available. Some charities and advocacy groups also offer partnership schemes, that can improve your organisation’s reputation as a diverse employer too.

Be aware of unconscious biases- Everyone has biases that affect their beliefs and behaviours, even if they’re not aware of them consciously. It’s part of being human. Learning to be more aware of your own biases, sets a good example for your team and will help you more effectively promote equality and diversity.

Make your own stance clear- There should be no room for doubt that you’re committed to equality and diversity, and that you won’t tolerate discriminatory actions or language. You can do this by sending out messages to your team affirming your commitment, perhaps tying it into company values or using real-life examples. 

Talk to your team- This is one of the most important things you can do. Actively engage with your team so that they have opportunities to talk to you, about their concerns and their ideas. This will again show your team that you’re committed to promoting equality and diversity, and it’ll help create an atmosphere that’s more inclusive. Your team will know that you’re approachable and will feel more secure because of it.

Organisations may have policies in place regarding equality and diversity, but it is down to you as a good leader, to ensure that the needs of all those in your team, are met in a respectful and responsive way. It is also important that you realise, that you have the potential to influence others, through your own inclusive, supportive, and respectful behaviour.
