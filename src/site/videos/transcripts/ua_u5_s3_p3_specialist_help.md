BETH: Sometimes the challenging behaviour of an individual with autism can become so great that the family will need to seek external support of specialists to help and there are many different sources of help and support available.

If the distressed behaviour of a child or family member presents a significant risk to themselves or those around them, specialist help should be sought out to deal with the behaviour.

JOE: They could request a social care needs assessment for their family member and for themselves as a carer. They may be able to get respite care or the help of an outreach team who can support them with behaviour strategies.  

Discussing this with a GP and requesting a referral to a behavioural specialist may be appropriate. 

Getting support from a counsellor who understands autism and can support the individual and their family.

BETH: There are a number of ways in which a parent/carer can get support such as meeting up with other carers, getting ideas from other families, and swapping tips in online communities nationally or locally. 

They could try contacting a local ‘National Autistic Society’ group, community service or family support service in their area.

JOE: The ‘Challenging Behaviour Foundation' has a lot of information to help both parents and professionals to give support and advice on spotting behaviours that challenge and what to do when it occurs.

‘Scope' also has information for parents on challenging behaviour.

Families should not suffer in silence as there is help out there and help and support will make life better for all concerned in the long run.
