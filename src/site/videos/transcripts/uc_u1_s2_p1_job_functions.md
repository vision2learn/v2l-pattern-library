The four main job functions of a programmer are to write, test, debug and maintain the code that creates the programmes that computers follow to perform their functions. 

They are responsible for the design and development and to ensure that the software adheres to best practices in performance, reliability, and security. 

They can work in developing mobile apps, coding video games, programming websites and can work freelance or in a multitude of different industries.

**Jack:** Let's have a look at what other key skills coding professionals should have.

Like many other complex professions programming requires a particular skill set to be successful. However, being a coding professional is not just about knowing all about computers and coding language and programming, there are other key skills that are relevant to the role.

Let’s have a look at some of these.

**Leah:** Positive attitude

When writing code, a positive and proactive attitude is sometimes needed as you may often have to go the extra mile to create a successful product. A positive attitude is also important if you need to provide instructions or comments on a piece of code, which should be clear and instructive. If there are problems, it is important to be constructive and solution orientated. 

**Jack:** Communication

Coders are often required to write code so that others including other coders will be able to understand and maintain it. They will also need to be able to explain complex concepts, like how something works, or the reasons behind things to non-coders like clients in relatively simple terms. The ability to communicate effectively, can make all the difference in getting their ideas over to others.

**Leah:** Teamwork

Most developers are part of a team and should be able to share their ideas and support the ideas of others. This may involve disagreements and debate, but this can help develop differing ideas to reach a common goal. Members of the team may have different functions, but it may involve them all working at different stages of the process to ultimately ensure a successful outcome.

**Jack:** Time Management 

Time management is essential for a coding professional not getting distracted from the task at hand, knowing what is needed, and being able to stick to deadlines and produce high quality work consistently. The ability to meet targets and get it right the first time is the best approach, extra features can be added later when the programme is up and running.

**Leah:** Problem solving

A coder should be good at problem solving because this is what they do, understanding how to solve problems within the software they are programming. It doesn't matter how knowledgeable they are, they may come across something that is far from straightforward and needs to be worked out. Being able to break down a problem into its component parts, then finding a solution, is a vital skill.

**Jack:** Self-motivation

Learning to code can be difficult, so a programmer must be able to motivate themselves to stick to it even when things go wrong. They also need to have patience, so as not to get stressed when de bugging code or working with a difficult system or client.

**Leah:** Analytical skills

Code must be visualised to work out what outcomes need to be created and the processes involved. Coders need to draw analytical conclusions from things that may on the surface seem straightforward. Curiosity can also help a coder push themselves to try new approaches.
