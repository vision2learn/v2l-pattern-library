BETH: A constructive environment is an environment where there is not an overload of sensory stimuli be it visual, auditory, olfactory or related to space and an individual with autism has to feel safe and comfortable to be able to communicate or interact socially.

Let’s look at some of the issues, starting with the lighting is it natural or artificial? 

JOE: Lighting plays a large part in the sensory experiences of many autistic people. We now know that fluorescent lights can be very distracting even to the point of debilitating for some people because they can see the lights flickering.

BETH: Is the environment cluttered or minimalist. Many people with autism need space around them and feel overwhelmed by crowds or clutter. They can feel hemmed in by corridors and need to know there is a quick escape route or a place they can remove themselves to which is calmer.

JOE: Individuals with Autism can become overwhelmed by too much visual information to process. Patterns can be distracting and overwhelming and can even cause visual distortion. 

BETH: What about sounds internal or external? Are there any regular ‘external’ sounds, such as traffic, children playing or construction noise? Are there regular ‘internal' sounds, such as ticking clocks, machines humming, music or radio noise?

JOE: Is it possible to reduce these sounds? Many individuals with autism can hear sounds at many decibels above those others can hear. They hear sounds that are further away and the intensity can often be deafening. 

BETH: Are there any strange smells? There may be smells from outside permeating through the walls or from within such as cleaning products, perfumes or foods. Some people with autism find smells so overwhelming that they cause extreme nausea and some can even smell something for a long time after it has been removed from the room.

JOE: To focus on social interaction and communication many individuals with autism need to have reduced sensory distractions to be able to listen to conversations, to respond appropriately, keep calm and relate to what is being said, rather than be distracted to some extent or in extremes suffering from sensory overload.
