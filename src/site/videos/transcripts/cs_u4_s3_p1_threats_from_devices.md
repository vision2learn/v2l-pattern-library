**Abraham:** Recently using personal devices has become more commonplace in working environments. But it means that employees will hop between corporate tools and personal applications introducing several risks associated with allowing the use of personal devices in conjunction with company networks. 

Many companies have a BYOD policy of 'bring your own device' to work, which allows their employees to use their own personal devices to save time and money, as they are often more productive than when using those provided in the working environment. 

Individuals are much more inclined to update their devices more often and keep all their apps and information up to date in their own time, therefore, saving time and money for the business. However, businesses that let their employees use their own personal devices are at risk from data theft, malware, legal problems and lost and stolen devices. 

It also makes it difficult in case there is an incident in which the company would have to take the personal device off the employee to investigate, leading to potentially embarrassing, whilst the team conduct forensic investigations. 

Employees should be fully aware of any issues regarding the use of personal devices regarding security of data and responsibility for that. Mobile security should be as strong as network security, so as not to put the company at risk.

The use of removable devices such as laptops and tablets or even hard drives, can cause conflicts when they are used for personal reasons as well as for business. This has become more of an issue with remote working. 

Company devices should be kept strictly for work so as not to open employees and business to conflict of interest and security issues. Also damage and theft as well as misuse, that may open all parties to legal issues with regards to data protection and security, and exposure to other cyber crimes.
