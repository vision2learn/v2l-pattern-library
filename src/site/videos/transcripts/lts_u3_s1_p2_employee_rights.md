In this video we are going to look at and focus on employees’ rights. 

Employees are defined as people who work under an employment contract and working this way means they have some rights that other people who work in different circumstances, don’t have, although, there are some rights that all workers enjoy. 

So, let’s have a look at some of the other rights employees have in addition to general workers’ rights.

Sickness 

Statutory sick pay is available If you’re too sick to work, and your employer has to pay you at minimum amount a week for up to 28 weeks. Some employers also have their own sick pay schemes, for example, workers who pass their probationary period, might be eligible for full pay when they’re sick.

Statutory maternity leave for mothers of a new baby, means they can take up to 52 weeks off work. This is divided into two blocks of 26 weeks, called Ordinary and Additional Maternity Leave. Your employer has to let you return to your old job at the end of Ordinary Maternity Leave, or to a similar role that pays the same and has the same terms and conditions if you take Additional Maternity leave. 

As well as maternity leave an employee gets Statutory maternity pay. This means that your employer has to pay you 90% of your average weekly earnings before tax, for the first 6 weeks, then a minimum amount 90% of your average weekly earnings [whichever is lower] for the next 33 weeks. 

Statutory paternity leave and pay is also available for fathers. As a father of a new baby, you’re eligible to one or two weeks of paid leave and the pay you’ll receive is a minimum amount or 90% of your average weekly earnings [whichever is lower].

For those parents who are adopting there are similar rules. With Statutory adoption leave, one partner can take 52 weeks leave, divided in the same was as maternity leave, while the other partner can take the two weeks paternity leave, as already described and Statutory Adoption Pay works the same way as maternity pay.

Shared parental leave and pay is for when new parents including through adoption, fostering, or surrogacy and who want to share their statutory leave and pay. You can share up to 50 weeks of leave and 37 weeks of pay and the pay is the same as it is for other forms of statutory leave. Protection against unfair Dismissal

In certain situations, you may be able to take legal action if you’re dismissed: 

Unfair dismissal is when your employer dismisses you without good reason, or if they fail to follow their own formal disciplinary or dismissal process. 

Constructive dismissal is when you’re forced to leave your job because of how your employer has treated you, for example if they’ve refused to pay you or demote you for no reason. 

Employees should try and sort out with the employer any disputes directly or they can exercise their rights by going to an employment or industrial tribunal.

You have the right to ask for Flexible working. This means being able to move your hours, starting and finishing times, or work location to be more convenient for you. For example, working from home, or starting early so you can finish early. However, your employer doesn’t have to meet your requests, but they must deal with them in a reasonable way, such as by having a meeting with you to discuss your request. 

Time off for emergencies. If an emergency has affected a dependent, your employer must let you take time off to care for them, this may include a spouse, parent, grandparent, child or grandchild. There’s no limit to how much time you can take off, but your employer isn’t obliged to pay you for this time. 

Statutory Redundancy Pay 

If you’re made redundant by your employer, which means your employer has decided that they no longer require the job you perform and that there’s no replacement role available for you, you’re entitled to receive redundancy pay. However, you’re only eligible for this, if you’ve worked for your current employer for at least two years. You’ll get half a weeks’ pay for each full year you were employed under 22, or a full weeks’ pay for each full year between 22-41. You receive one and a half weeks pay for each full year you were 41 and older. Length of service is capped at 20 years.
