RHIANNON: A mental health crisis is an emergency that poses a direct and immediate threat to physical or emotional wellbeing. There is no one set definition of what a crisis entails, as it is highly personal to each individual.

LEAH: A mental health crisis in children and young people, is usually when they show signs of being extremely anxious, having panic attacks, feeling suicidal or self-harming. They may also show signs of feeling very high, or having a very low mood, or even suffering from episodes of psychosis. 

RHIANNON: They may be facing extreme challenges in their life, such as bereavement, financial problems within the family, family break-up, exam stress or housing problems. They may also already have a mental health diagnosis. Or they may have challenges that are not obvious, which may just be affecting their mental well-being.

LEAH: How do you plan for something like a mental health crisis, especially if you hope it won’t happen or have no idea about the possibility it might happen?

As in any health crisis the first place to go is Accident & Emergency (999) by phone or directly at the local hospital. Also, emergency GP appointments are available, even if not registered and if talking is needed, there are multiple helplines available.

RHIANNON: There is always an option and an answer, so make sure to stay aware of what these are and how to make use of them.
