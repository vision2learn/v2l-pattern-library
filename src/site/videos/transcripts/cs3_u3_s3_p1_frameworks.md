**Abraham:** Cyber security frameworks are a system of standards, guidelines for best practice to manage and mitigate cybersecurity risks. 

**Nicole:** There are many different types of frameworks according to the specific areas that they need to cover. Following an appropriate cyber security framework will help to manage an organisations risk, as long as it takes into consideration specific needs and priorities of the different types of businesses and organisations.

**Abraham:** There are three broad types of cyber security frameworks these consists of 

• Control frameworks- these are often used by those who are new to cyber security, these offer a basic set of controls to follow. 

• Program frameworks -these assess the overall state of a security program and help to build and analyse a system conducting industry comparisons. 

• Risk frameworks- these focus on risk and prioritise security.

**Nicole:** Defining and communicating an organisation’s risk regime is key to their overall cyber security. The NCSC recommends implementing 10 key steps to help to protect a business from cyber attack. Let’s have a look at what is involved.

**Abraham:** Step 1 is setting up a risk management regime. This should be embedded across an organization and supported by senior management. This can involve conducting an inventory of all resources and risks.

**Nicole:** Step 2 secure configuration of all systems should be ensured and maintained by creating an inventory and defining a baseline build model. Also developing a strategy to remove or disable unnecessary functionality from any systems.

**Abraham:** Step 3 Network security. Networks should be protected from attack by making sure all connections in the network are defended for example by firewalls to filter out any unauthorized access, including all cloud services and remote working sites.

**Nicole:** Step 4 Managing user privileges. Effective management processes should limit user privileges, as unnecessary system privileges can give rise to potential misuse. Sensitive information should only be accessible for those who need to use it.

**Abraham:** Step 5 User education and awareness. Policies should be in place to provide acceptable and secure use of systems only. Staff training should make cyber security a priority to maintain awareness of risks.

**Nicole:** Step 6 Incident management. Effective incident management services will help to maintain resilience and help potentially reduce any impact to both the business and stakeholder confidence. Reporting any criminal activity to law enforcement is also important.

**Abraham:** Step 7 Malware protection. Developing an appropriate antimalware policy can help to make staff aware of how malware can infiltrate a system via phishing scams etc.

**Nicole:** Step 8 Monitoring. Establishing a monitoring strategy, can help to detect attempted attacks by constantly monitoring all systems and networks and analyzing logs.

**Abraham:** Step 9 Removable Media Controls. Organisations should create a policy to control all access to removable media and limit its use. All media should be scanned for malware before it enters the system.

**Nicole:** Step 10 Mobile and Home working can expose new risks. It is important to have remote working policies and train all staff on the use of remote access and portable systems. Also making sure all devices have a baseline security system, can help to secure systems as can protecting data in transit and at rest.

Now let’s look at the CIS framework. The Center for Internet Security [CIS] has developed a 20-point action plan that helps organisations to develop a cyber security framework to prevent the most common types of attack.

**Abraham:** This is broken down into 3 catagories.

1, Identifying the security environment using basic controls which covers:

Performing an inventory, documenting all software assets, checking for software or hardware that may have been introduced without permission or knowledge, checking user permissions, updating passwords and implementing audit logs and other management systems.

**Nicole:** 2, Protecting assets with foundational controls which covers:

Checking email and browsers are up to date and protected, malware and virus protection, preventing unauthorized connections, backup and recovery plans, secure network configurations, boundary defences, data protection measures, additional access controls, wireless access controls, account monitoring controls.

**Abraham:** 3, Developing a security culture using organizational controls which covers:

Implementing security awareness training, managing the security lifecycle, developing incident response procedures, performing penetration tests.

As we have discussed there are many different types of cyber security frameworks, but they all do a similar job of helping organisations to design their own cyber security system.

**Nicole:** It is the cyber security professional’s role to evaluate and apply the appropriate framework for their organization. Focusing on the business needs and what elements are the most important according to the type of traffic an organization has.

Control frameworks work well for those organisations that are new to managing a security program identifying the current state and prioritizing future developments. 

**Abraham:** Program frameworks like the National Institute of Standards and Technology [NIST] can help build a mature program and improve information security. Risk frameworks includes more controls and management across a wider range of business operations taking a systematic approach to risk management.
