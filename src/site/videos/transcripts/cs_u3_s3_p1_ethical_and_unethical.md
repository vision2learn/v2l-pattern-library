**Nicole:** All organisations and companies will have an IT policy in place, and some may even have a specific cyber security policy. All policies should be adhered to consistently from the top down. They should be clear and current being reviewed and updated by the senior management team at least annually, and in touch with ever changing laws and regulations. There should be no barriers to complying with policy guidelines and procedures and where necessary training should be provided. There should also be regular audits to check that policy is being followed and all employees are on track.

Most new employees must sign up to a policy agreement as part of their induction to say that they will follow the guidelines before they are given their emails and log in details.

Policy guidelines may be different depending on the business or organisation and the data that employees are working with but overall, the guidelines will be the same for everyone. Where any specific sensitive data is concerned there may be extra level of security involved and different rules may apply. Where data is concerned guidelines should be always followed strictly adhering to GDPR rules.

There are a range of ethical rules that link to legislation when it comes to using computer systems that apply such as: 

Not using computer equipment to harm others. 

Not interfering with other’s computers and work

Not looking through other’s files without their permission.

Not using a computer to steal or cause mischief.

Not copying software.

Not using other people’s resources without permission.

Acting without regard for consequences.

And having respect for data security.

With remote working, many more employees are working from home and these rules should still apply. Many employees now sync their work emails to their smartphones, which may involve employers expecting workers to check emails outside office hours. Cyber security is even more important especially when personal emails are accessed alongside business emails. Many businesses will set up their employees for remote working but in some cases, it may involve an employee taking their laptop home and relying on their own internet security set up. All IT policy guidelines regarding data security and misuse of computers should be still adhered to even more so.

Unethical conduct can be something as simple as leaving a 'post it' around with log details for anyone to access a computer system as this can open a system up to unethical security issues. This may just be thoughtless behaviour or a lack of judgement, but it can lead to so much more. It may involve deliberately copying information and data to use or supply to others for unethical activities. Unethical behaviour can involve sabotage, disclosing or misusing confidential information or maliciously injuring the reputation or prospects of an individual or business.

It’s also important to remember that as cyber security professionals we have a great deal of access to sensitive data and it’s important that we do not use this high level of access for the wrong purposes.
