**Leah:** Lean project management focusses on ‘value’ delivery in project management terms from the customers perspective by cutting down on ‘waste’ whilst optimising efficiency therefore delivering more with less.

Whereas traditional project management is structured in stages, lean project management enables teams to deliver faster by managing workflow more efficiently with continuous improvement. It means working to deliver what the client needs rather than just what they asked for.

**Jack:** Lean project managers identify objectives, requirements, deliverables and acceptance criteria and the value is what the client is willing to pay for. 

Anything not focussed on solving the problem is seen as wasted resources. Cutting out wasted resources can have a positive effect on delivery time and therefore time spent on the project. It can also help to create continuous workflow.

**Leah:** A key instrument in Lean Project Management is the Plan-Do-Check-Act cycle [PDCA]. This is a four-stage method that includes solutions testing, analysing results, and improving the process to enable teams to avoid recurring mistakes and improve operations. It is a good way of establishing a culture of solutions testing on a smaller scale which in turn minimises potential for reworking. The goal is to plan, execute and test a potential solution and then analyse the results from a client’s perspective.
