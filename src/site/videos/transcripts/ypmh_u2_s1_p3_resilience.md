RHIANNON: We all need to become better at understanding, how we can help children and young people to thrive and be mentally healthy. By having resilience, children and young people can gain the strength to cope when something goes wrong and have the ability to solve problems, so they can remain mentally healthy, or bounce back from times of stress. 

LEAH: Family and social relationships, self-esteem and personal values, aid resilience and there are skills that can be learnt to cope better with stress and improve resilience further.

Resilience in children and young people can be boosted by support from family, friends, neighbours and even pets, as positive interactions with those who are close can help relieve stress and shape good memories.  

RHIANNON: Recalling good memories also improves resilience in vulnerable young people which helps them to better cope with the stresses they’re experiencing, such as remembering the fun times they have had with their friends and family.

LEAH: Building resilience in young people may help to protect against engaging in health risk behaviour, such as smoking, drinking alcohol, illicit drug use and unprotected sex and can also help to delay ‘transitions’ such as parenthood, which can in turn help to avoid the potential negative health consequences of early pregnancy for both parents and babies.

RHIANNON: Resilience can contribute to healthy behaviours, higher qualifications and skills, better employment, better mental wellbeing, and quicker or more successful recovery from illness.
