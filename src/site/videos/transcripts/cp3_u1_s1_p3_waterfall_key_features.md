**Leah:** So, what is the Waterfall approach?

**Chris:** Waterfall project management emphasises a linear approach from the beginning to the end of a project. It flows like a waterfall through all phases of a project with each phase wrapping up before the next phase begins. It is often used by engineers and is front loaded relying on careful planning, detailed documentation, and consecutive execution. It follows a chronological process and works on fixed dates, requirements, and outcomes.

**Leah:** In Waterfall Project Management there are typically four main roles across the team Let’s have a look at what these are.

The Project Manager, who is the Leader of the team and responsible for quality and deadlines. They are responsible for managing the project and subdividing the tasks to the other team members.

**Chris:** A Business Analyst, whose role involves research and helping the team to understand the requirements to make the product competitive in the market.

Developers who build the product and create and write the code. 

Testers who test the final product making sure it functions correctly and there are no bugs.

**Leah:** There are three main principles with the Waterfall model

**Chris:** Low customer involvement is connected to strong documentation as this is one of the reasons that they don’t need to be involved in every stage of the project. For most projects there are only two meetings with the client, initially before the project starts which is where the learning and documentation of the client’s requirements takes place, and then a second meeting which is after the final product is ready to be deployed.

**Leah:** Strong documentation is created at every stage of the project. This includes initial documents created in agreement with the customer which would include the requirements of the client for the final software product.

**Chris:** Sequential structure is the main principle of the Waterfall model, and most projects have 5 to 7 stages. 

**Leah:** Let’s have a look at these stages in more detail.

Stage 1. Analysis and requirements

**Chris:** This is the stage where all the customer requirements are gathered by the team in the form of a document which would include costs, assumptions, risks, dependencies, measurements, and a timeline. This meeting would be very thorough to ensure that there wouldn’t be a need for further communication with the client until the end of the project.

**Leah:** Stage 2. Design

This is the stage where the team would design a technical solution for the project. It would include logical and physical design and would involve the team and the Project Manager.

**Chris:** Stage 3. Implementation 

the team would start to create the actual code based on the requirements and specification that has been identified in the previous stages.

**Leah:** Stage 4. Testing and verification

Testing is done at this stage by the QA team to make sure that there are no errors and that all the requirements have been completed. User testing may also be done at this stage.

**Chris:** Stage 5. Deployment and release

Once the client is happy with the product and it has been reviewed and signed off, the product can be deployed.

**Leah:** Stage 6. Maintenance

This stage would be to ensure if there are any errors or bugs, updates, and or new features that are needed once the product has been released.
