**Abraham:** So how does Cyber crime affect the individual?

As individuals we can have our finances and social status heavily impacted by cyber crime. Unsolicited emails and texts and those often pertaining to be from our own bank or the Inland Revenue can have consequences when interacted with which may be financial. Once details have been unwittingly given, the cyber criminals have access to more than just our details, they can access bank accounts and commit crimes using our identity. This can then expand into our social media circles or work-based contacts. Phones and smart technology around the home can be hacked and taken control of with the opportunity for being surveilled by cameras.

Having 'up to date’ antimalware software and making sure all programs are up to date can help to keep systems secure. Also taking extra precautions like having good password protection, cautious when giving out details online, only accessing secure sites, keeping phones and storage devices safe and away from prying eyes and being careful with any documentation that may have details on it and destroying it.

**Nicole:** Let’s now look at how it can affect Businesses. 

Cybercrime can devastate a company’s finances and reputation quickly or can cause a great deal of disruption to an organisation. In the long term it can cause irreparable financial or brand damage, or even threaten an individual’s way of life. Companies need to realise what a threat cyber crime is and to stop their business falling victim to the cyber criminals should invest in reliable cyber security services.

A DDoS cyber attack involves a network being flooded with excessive traffic causing it to shut down and becoming unavailable to legitimate users. Hacking involves the cybercriminal in trying to take advantage of vulnerabilities in a company’s software and systems. Hackers will ask for a ransom to end the attack, however by using trusted and reliable technology service providers, increasing bandwidth, and designing systems to scale with traffic using the cloud this can be prevented.

There is also a significant impact to the business from regulatory bodies. An example of this would be British Airways who were fined £20 million one of the highest under GDPR by the ICO for their lack of due diligence that led to the compromise of 429,612 staff and customer details.

**Abraham:** The effects of cybercrime can be minimised by businesses making employees aware and wary of downloading attachments or clicking on links to reset passwords without knowing exactly who they have come from or checking their authenticity directly.

Personal information and contact details should not be given freely, so as to give access to hackers and scammers. Businesses should also limit the amount of information they put out on their website. 

Passwords should be long, complex, and original for each individual account. They should not be easy to identify from personal information, for example, not the name of a child or pet, or based on the month or season of the year ‘Summer2021’. These sorts of password relatedly come up on the list of most common passwords. 

To minimise insider attacks all data storage should be encrypted and software should be updated regularly to maintain security. Companies can also enforce two-factor authentication for all their user accounts so that attackers are less likely to be able to gain access even with the right credentials.

**Nicole:** So how are nations affected?

Nation state attacks are usually thought of as highly complex, and technology based. However social engineering is commonly used to convince individuals to download malware to create security breaches. “Spear Phishing' attacks are often used to infiltrate, inserting malware which may remain dormant until triggered by an outside agency making it difficult to guard against. 

State-backed actors are responsible for gaining access to computer networks around the world. Countries like China seem intent on committing large scale espionage even though as part of the G20 they agreed not to conduct or support cyber enabled theft of intellectual property or personal data. In the UK The National Cyber Security Centre [NCSC] supports the government giving advice to organisations affected by state run cyber intrusions stealing trade secrets such as those by the Chinese Ministry of State Security [MSS] in 2018.

**Abraham:** To guard against these often changing and increasingly sophisticated attacks, organisations must have a multi-layered approach to their cyber security [referred to as “defence in depth”], creating nation state threat risk models and educating employees on being hyper aware of nation state attacks. They also need to be constantly aware of the threat and training should be ongoing.
