Poor communication has a wide range of negative effects for all types of workplaces, so let’s look at some of the possible impacts in detail.

Damage to timelines 

If there is ineffective communication in the workplace, people won’t know what the deadlines are. These can lead to increased time pressures and possibly sloppy work, as people race to hit deadlines, or deadlines are missed altogether. This will almost always have a knock-on effect.

Say, for example, due to poor communication, someone in the IT department of a company is under the impression, that a new system doesn’t need to be put in place for a month. They then learn it is business critical and must be finished before the end of the week! Even if it is completed on time, it’s more than likely it will not be executed to a good standard. And the person working on it, will have experienced unnecessary stress and anxiety and felt highly pressured.

Reduced productivity 

As poor communication is likely to mean that people don’t always know what they’re expected to be doing, some may put their energy and resources into the wrong places. They might start work on something that’s being worked on by someone else or has already been completed. They may misunderstand a business need and create something that doesn’t meet the goals and targets. They may even think that there’s nothing to do that day, because no one’s told them otherwise!

Competing communication styles 

Although everyone has their preferred method of communication, and that’s fine! - there does still need to be some common understanding throughout a workplace, about how people should communicate. People need to know what channels to use, and the tone of voice they’re expected to use in written and spoken communication especially with customers, and such. Without this, you’ll find that people just use whatever channel or style they prefer, which may not be the most suitable or efficient one. For example, a manager who hates to use phone or video calls, only communicates via emails and instant messaging. This might lead his team to feel very distant from him, like he isn’t in the loop and doesn’t care. This will then be bad for their morale.

Lack of engagement and lowered wellbeing 

Poor communication may also mean that people don’t feel like they belong to their team or organization. They’ll feel isolated, lonely, and their morale will be low, so causing an impact on productivity and retention. 

Closed minds 

Without open and effective lines of communication between people and teams, employees won’t be regularly exposed to new ideas and approaches. This can mean that inefficient methods don’t get corrected and that problems don’t get solved. 

Poor retention 

Without effective communication helping people to feel like they’re a part of their organization, there will be a reduced desire to be loyal to their employer. They won’t feel like their contributions are being noticed or their ambitions respected. This means it will be much harder to retain talent and time and money will be spent having to hire new employees, to replace the ones who go, possibly starting the whole process over again.

Ineffective or poor communication in the workplace can be frustrating at all levels and can create a breeding ground for mistrust and confusion and help create stressed employees. It also makes it difficult for people to work together closely, collaboratively, and successfully as a team. This can then impact greatly on the success of the business or organization overall, lowering morale and motivation and ultimately affecting a businesses reputation.
