BETH: While not all children with autism have speech and language delays, autism frequently causes social communication challenges and therefore delays in social development.

Children with autism may have a harder time using or understanding non-verbal communication such as pointing and smiling and conversation tends to come across with little animation or inflection in their speech.

JOE: Individuals with High functioning Autism can use language to communicate with other people. But their core communication problems, often make it impossible for them to learn language in the normal way, which relies heavily on social context, and non-verbal communications.

They may be more motivated by their own interests rather than by social responses and may rarely or never imitate others' actions.

BETH: Some individuals with autism use language intellectually and are highly literal in how they use and understand it, in contrast to the figurative way most other people use language. 

They find it difficult to understand and rarely use sarcasm and irony and other forms of communication where one thing is said while another is meant. 

JOE: Because of their difficulty understanding body language and nonverbal communication they often cannot pick up on hints and clues that people drop to indicate interest or disinterest in what is being discussed or an activity that is happening. 

BETH: People with the most severe cases of autism spectrum disorder typically require very substantial support from others. They may be completely isolated. They may not use language at all, or they may even be very limited in their ability to communicate.
