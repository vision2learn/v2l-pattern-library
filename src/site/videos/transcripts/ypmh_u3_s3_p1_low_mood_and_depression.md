LEAH: Most young people experience ups and downs during their childhood and teenage years and occasionally will feel down or upset by certain things going on in their lives. 

RHIANNON: Feeling sad or fed up is a normal reaction to experiences that are difficult or stressful. 

A low mood often involves sadness, an anxious feeling, worry and low self-esteem. It may even involve frustration and anger. 

LEAH: However, it will tend to improve after a short time, especially if the child or young person makes changes to resolve their problems, by discussing them, facing them head on or even getting more sleep. 

If children and young people are able to challenge their negative thoughts and replace them with positivity, they are more likely to improve their mood.

RHIANNON: But some young people feel sad, lonely, down, and anxious or stressed for much longer periods of time, to the extent that it can affect their everyday lives and can prevent them from doing things they would normally do. 

LEAH: It is then, when a low mood doesn’t go away and if anything, gets worse, that it needs to be looked at as clinical depression and a mental condition that needs to be addressed.
