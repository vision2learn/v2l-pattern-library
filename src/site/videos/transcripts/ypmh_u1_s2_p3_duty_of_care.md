RHIANNON: Individuals who work with children and young people play a crucial role in shaping their lives and are responsible for managing risks, using the correct resources, establishing a safe environment and safeguarding them from harm or unprofessional conduct.

LEAH: Children and young people have a right to be treated with respect and dignity and It follows, that trusted adults are expected to take reasonable steps to ensure the safety and well-being of children and young people as part of that ‘duty of care’.

RHIANNON: All workplaces, whether a school, a care provider or justice service organisation have a moral and a legal obligation to ensure that everyone associated with that establishment, whether employee, volunteer, student, tradesperson or the general public, is fully protected from any personal physical or emotional harm, either on the premises or when engaged in activities relating to that establishment.

LEAH: Typical areas of concern are fire safety, health and safety, food safety, personal safety, child and adult protection, safeguarding such as safer recruitment, equality, bullying, violence, harassment, stress, or discrimination from any source.

RHIANNON: A breach of ‘duty of care’ occurs when a person or an organisation fails to live up to that standard. A person may be liable for negligence in a personal injury case if their breach of duty caused another person's injuries or mental ill health.

LEAH: In education ‘duty of care’ is measured as being that of a ‘reasonable parent’ as when parents leave their child at the school gates, they are in effect agreeing to allow the teachers and other staff at the school to act ‘in loco parentis’.  

RHIANNON: This means, that ‘in place of a parent’, all teachers, including trainees, have a 'duty of care' towards their pupils and are required to apply their education and acquired skills to safeguard pupils.

LEAH: Any facilities need to be safe and fit for purpose and all pupils of all abilities have the right to be protected from harm, especially those who may be vulnerable. For example, if a school arranges trips, there needs to be enough help and support to keep all children safe with all potential 
risks assessed.

RHIANNON: A care provider is expected to be trustworthy, and act in accordance with their code of practice. They should be up to date with knowledge and skills and ensure that service is provided safely, and records up-to-date, accurate and confidential except where the wider duty of care or public interest might at risk.

LEAH: In the criminal justice system, the aim is for every child to have a safe and crime-free life and make a positive contribution to society with a focus on child first, offender second. 

RHIANNON: To maintain ‘duty of care’ all inspectors should make sure that youth justice services meet statutory requirements, and no one is taken advantage of.

LEAH: All individuals working with children and young people need to understand and acknowledge the responsibilities and trust inherent in their role and do what a reasonable person, with their training and background, can be expected to do, to maintain a duty of care.
