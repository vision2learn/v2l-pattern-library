JOE: All individuals transition from one activity to another and from one setting to another every day, at home, school, or in the workplace. These may involve major transitions or minor transitions.

Individuals with autism often find it difficult to predict what is happening in their day as well as planning and executing transitions. Transitions whether minor or major for individuals with autism can be a stressful and confusing process that often results in emotional distress or disengagement.

BETH: Individuals with autism spectrum disorders may have greater difficulty in shifting attention from one task to another or handling changes of their routine or setting. This may be due to a greater need for predictability, challenges in understanding what activity will be coming next, or difficulty when a pattern of behaviour is disrupted. 

JOE: When transition strategies are used, they can help with reducing the amount of transition time and improving appropriate behaviour during transitions and disruptions to activities, settings, or routines. 

Strategies can be used before a transition occurs, during a transition, and after a transition, and can be verbal, auditory, or visual. They can help to increase predictability for individuals on the autism spectrum and to create positive routines around transitions. 

BETH: When looking at transitions there are a number of strategies that can be used to support individuals with autism. 

Visual supports such as pictures or real objects can indicate what the transition may involve and be can be used as a prompt. 

Visual timers, digital alerts and day planners can give prior notice and warning when transitions may occur.

JOE: Preparing individuals for transitions in advance though a range of different methods can help them become aware of what to expect and making them part of that transition by involving them in the process can help them to feel more in control.

BETH: Making sure everyone concerned is aware of the issues, can make things more consistent in approach and detail, so that they can reinforce any guidance and support that might be needed.

Using these strategies and types of supports can help prepare children and adults to initiate and carry out a transition and to demonstrate the appropriate behaviour relying less on prompting.
