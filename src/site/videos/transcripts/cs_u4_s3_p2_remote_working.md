Remote working has been around for some time but has become essential for many businesses over the last year or so due to the pandemic and looks like continuing trend for many companies for the foreseeable future. This means that cyber security has become of greater concern for businesses. Rigorous and powerful cybersecurity is needed to protect employees working remotely such as VPN or investing in the more secure zero trust model. 

In a world where employees could be working from many places such as at home, a coffee shop, or on holiday; zero trust works on the principle of don’t trust by default and uses additional factors to assess whether to grant access. These could be where is the user signing in from? What time are they signing in? Is this a normal time when they usually sign in between 9am and 5pm? What device are they accessing from? And many other potential factors that could indicate a risky sign-in attempt.

On site security is easier to handle than using unsafe WiFi networks. When using untrusted WiFi networks, employees should be sure to use a corporate VPN so that their connection is encrypted end-to-end. We must also be aware of human error along with other vulnerabilities such as weak password or the use of the same password. 

Working should always take place in safe environments, so as not to have security impacted by having others present. That's why it’s not ideal to work on a train, in a cafe or in a hotel or business reception or even in the park, where others can see important information and security cannot be guaranteed. 

Whilst working in public, employees should be wary of shoulder-surfing that people can’t view any potentially sensitive data that they may have on screen. Privacy screens can be used which blur the screen of anyone who is not sat directly in front of the computer.
