**Abraham:** So, what are the consequences and implications of inadequate cyber security?

**Nicole:** Cyber security should be one of the top priorities in all companies large or small,

as the sophistication and the number of threats is increasing.

Data is the lifeblood of most organisations and is the key component of day-to-day operations, therefore security breaches that lead to the loss of data can be catastrophic.

Companies and organisations need a security strategy to cover networks, servers, workstations, cloud, applications, and data. 

**Abraham:** Not taking cyber security seriously can lead to irreparable reputational damage, fines, and issues with licensing in case of regulated businesses. It can also cause adverse media coverage, increased anxiety and stress levels in employees and problems related to areas such as GDPR. 

So, let’s look in a little more detail at what the consequences of an attack could be.

**Nicole:** Operational Disruptions- 

Ransomware can lead to hackers holding a company to ransom by infiltrating and accessing their system, and its core datasets, and then asking for money, or else! This can lead to the shutting down of the company’s entire IT infrastructure and business critical systems, to isolate the damage, investigate and recover. For companies that do not have a recovery plan or back-ups in place, it may take weeks or months to get back to where they were before the attack. This can cause disruptions to business operations, have a negative impact on productivity, causing customers to go elsewhere, resulting in a loss of revenue.

**Abraham:** Legal Ramifications 

Data breaches involving customer’s and commercial partner’s personal information, can lead to lawsuits against the company. These can have the impact of lowering trust and confidence, and may involve hefty fines from regulatory bodies, legal fees and expensive court cases.

 In case of regulated businesses, the authorities may even restrict companies from carrying certain operations until legal investigations are concluded, to see how an attack could take place, and to make sure it won’t happen again.

**Nicole:** Financial loss

All cyber criminal acts, whether direct or indirect, can have a negative financial impact. Loss of business, legal fees, fines, cost of containing an attack or breach, compensation pay-outs, and a potential reduction in share price. Data breaches especially, can be hard for a business to overcome and renew trust in former clients and give confidence to new ones, therefore making it possible for companies to go under. 

Some businesses have a ‘never-going-to-happen-to-me’ attitude towards cyber security and have inadequate security measures in place in general. But in this day and age, everyone should air on the side of caution and change that attitude to ‘it could easily happen to me!
