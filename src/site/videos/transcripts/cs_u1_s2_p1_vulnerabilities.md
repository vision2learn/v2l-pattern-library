**Nicole:** Cybercrime is a violation of an individual's privacy and their data security. Data is considered a commodity and is a primary target of cybercriminals. It can be accessed illicitly if not adequately protected by internet security systems or given away to scammers unwittingly by individuals clicking onto bogus links and it can be used to commit further cybercrimes. 

**Abraham:** Businesses are at risk from Phishing, DDoS, and malware. Malware can include different subtypes such as ransomware, trojans, worms, crypto miners or spyware as a few examples. The attack type will often depend on the type of business and the technology they use. It will also be dependent on what information the hacker wants to extract or attack. Cyber attacks can be, as we have already discussed, by external or insider threats. Trojan malware enables hackers to create a backdoor into the business’ systems and access business data and login credentials. Social engineering covers a broad range of malicious cyber crimes manipulating users into making security mistakes or giving away sensitive information or granting access to resources. Hackers can inject malicious code into a website to gain access to information and login details through keyloggers and hacking a database of passwords.

**Nicole:** There has been a significant growth in high-profile ransomware campaigns. Ransomware involves paying a hefty fee for releasing their data, however this does not always happen even when the fee has been paid. There’s been a recent increase in ransomware because companies have started to use cyber insurance. If a company is affected by ransomware then the insurance company will pay the ransom, so cybercriminals know that it is very likely they will get the money. There is, however, no guarantee that the criminals will unencrypt the data even after they are paid.

**Abraham:** This can have a knock on effect, an example of this is when the AXA insurance firm was ‘ransomwared’ in response to them publicly announcing they will no longer offer ‘pay out’ clauses for cyber insurance
