**Abraham:** Even in the most efficient well-run companies, teams can be affected by conflict. This may involve a clash of team members over their contributions to a shared project or team leaders who have battled over their positions with managers who don't see eye to eye with them or simply a clash of personalities within the team. 

Whatever the source of the conflict, it often involves a breakdown in communication and trust and can cause resentment. Conflict should be addressed before it gets out of hand, and this can be done using proactive strategies as conflict often starts with small disagreements that escalate fast.

**Nicole:** So, what are the signs of conflict within a team? 

Things that can suggest conflict are the members of the team’s body language, facial expressions, and tone of voice in discussions. Team leaders and managers who know the team members should be able to spot any changes in the team dynamic.

**Abraham:** What can be done to resolve this?

Using an intermediary or trusted third party that both sides can have faith in, can help with resolving conflict by being fair and impartial and identifying the issues. By remaining objective they can give everyone involved the time and opportunity to present their own perspective on events associated with the conflict. Dominant individuals should not be allowed to bully more reserved members of the team, and everyone should be able to have their say.

**Nicole:** Let's look at what process should be followed.

First, team members should be spoken to individually and each should be asked the same question. They should also be made aware that all discussions are confidential. Individuals should be allowed to put their points of view forward in their own time without making assumptions.

Once the intermediary or facilitator understands the different perspectives, then they need to bring the different parties together and encourage them to discuss the issues between themselves in a calm and non-confrontational manner, giving opportunity to listen to and respect each other’s views. 

If the conflict affects the whole team, then this discussion should involve the whole team.

Once the grievances have been aired then a plan should be drawn up to detail and agree on actions for resolution and reconciliation. This should be followed up by getting feedback on how things are progressing going forward.

Sometimes by airing the grievances that may have started the conflict, things can be resolved quite amicably to suit all parties.

If, however, a resolution to the conflict cannot be reached, then formal procedures may need to be taken and disciplinary action may follow depending on the nature of the conflict.
