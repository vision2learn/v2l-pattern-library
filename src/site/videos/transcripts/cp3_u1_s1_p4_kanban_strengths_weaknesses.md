**Leah:** Let’s now look at Kanban starting with its strengths

**Chris:** Easy to learn and use-Kanban is very straightforward with its approach and is easy to learn and use. It can be used in small simple projects such as for children to help plan their homework; to complex business projects using the Scrum framework. 

**Leah:** Visual-It is a visual system, and the flow of work is managed using a Kanban project board. In its simplest form there are three columns which are labelled: ‘To Do’, ‘In Progress’, and ‘Done’.

**Chris:** Adaptable and flexible-It is adaptable and flexible as there can be no planned timings and priorities and workload can be changed during the project. It is also flexible in that it can be used with another framework such as Scrum.

**Leah:** Collaborative-As a simple visual system, it helps the project team to collaborate as it simplifies planning, helps to manage work and inventory, and increase productivity and efficiency. 

**Chris:** Low overheads-It has low overheads as there is no training needed and all you need is a Kanban project board to get started.

**Leah:** Reduces costs and waste-As it can be used with LEAN’s ‘Just In Time’ methodology it helps to reduce cost and waste. 

**Chris:** And the Weaknesses

Needs another framework to work-Kanban would need a framework such as Scrum or LEAN ‘Just in Time’ to work as it needs the supporting elements that they would give. 

**Leah:** Not suitable on plans that are not stable-Kanban would need plans that are stable and consistent to work effectively, so this may be an issue if the plans need to be dynamic.

**Chris:** Is not Iterative-Another framework would be needed for iterations to be used which would normally end up being a separate project, as iteration is not essential to Kanban at a ticket level.

**Leah:** Lack of timing-Because Kanban is adaptable and flexible, there are often no planned timings for each stage of the project. This also means that items can be changed during the project, and this could cause issues.

**Chris:** A Kanban project could be as simple as cleaning your house or planning school homework or college exams. In the workplace examples could include giving a project manager visibility to a project status; using a Kanban board to delegate work across a team; or connecting related work items across different projects or project boards.
