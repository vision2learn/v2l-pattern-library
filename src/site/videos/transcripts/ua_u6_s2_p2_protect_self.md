JOE: Abuse is when an individual is hurt or treated badly, it is always wrong and should never happen, but often does. It is never the fault of the individual concerned it is always the fault of the abuser. It can happen once or many times and can include different types of abuse and may get more serious the more it happens.

BETH: Individuals with autism may have problems protecting themselves against harm, abuse or being taken advantage of as they might not know what abuse is, or what has happened to them and how it could affect them. 

They should be taught how to recognise potential abuse and they should be able to identify at least two trusted adults they can report abuse to or go to for help.

JOE: An individual should always be listened to and believed when they report abuse, they should also be told about their rights and be supported when reporting abuse.

Anyone who works with or cares for individuals with autism should understand what abuse is, and work to make sure it doesn’t happen.

BETH: Families can educate themselves and their loved one, on how to identify warning signs and how to report if abuse or neglect is suspected. 

They should talk openly to their family member about personal safety, boundaries, saying ‘no’, and what is a healthy and unhealthy touch. They may even try doing role play to show what is a dangerous situation and how to respond.

JOE: The problem is that as a child with autism grows up, they are taught to respond more positively to people and to be more responsive in social interactions. But when the potential is there for abuse, it is harder for them to understand that not all people are honest and trustworthy and have their best interests at heart.

BETH: Individuals need to remember that they do not have to do things because others tell them to, and if something feels wrong it probably is. If an individual has the confidence to assert themselves and stand up to a potential abuser, knowing that when they say ‘no’ it means ‘no’, they are more likely to prevent themselves from harm and abuse.
