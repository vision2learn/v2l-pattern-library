**Nicole:** So why is cyber security testing so important?

**Abraham:** Cyber security testing helps to identify weaknesses or vulnerabilities before they are found by malicious threat actors.

**Nicole:** By simulating the behaviour of threat actors, cyber security experts can help to identify any issues with a system and how these might potentially be exploited.

Companies often do security testing after installing new systems, apps and infrastructure to make sure that every aspect of their system is secure. By identifying these, at the time of installation, weaknesses or vulnerabilities can be assessed and organisations can prioritise and handle any risks more effectively.

**Abraham:** Different types of testing can show up different weaknesses. For example, social engineering testing can highlight issues with procedures and identify how susceptible staff might be to social engineering attacks. This can then be used can be used to develop training to raise staff awareness. 

**Nicole:** Pen testing can help personnel learn how to handle a malicious attack if it happens for real. Red teaming or ethical hacking can be used to systematically identify an attack path looking for all the unnoticeable gaps such as backdoors, open windows, and other ways of breaching an organisation’s system.

**Abraham:** Cyber security testing can be costly, but it can save more money in the long term as it can protect against expensive cyber-attacks that could impact an organisation in the way it functions and its reputation. 

**Nicole:** Think of the costs involved in an expensive ransomware attack, losing data to competitors, replacing software and hardware or even data, and any fines that go along with it for breaching GDPR. 

**Abraham:** Imagine how costly it could be for an organisation to suffer significant downtime especially if they have to close down the system and go offline to fix the problem. 

**Nicole:** Some standards require organisations to perform regular penetration or other types of security testing to meet industry standards and regulations. Such as ISO 27001 the standard for information security and GDPR where organisations that process Personal information and data, have to make sure that they have appropriate security measures in place to identify vulnerabilities. The PCI DSS Payment card industry data security standard requires vulnerability scanning as part of its standard.

**Abraham:** Security testing and evaluating testing is a proactive approach that helps to identify the potential security risks and help to show how to fix issues. It helps to reduce threats and risks in systems to minimize the chance of loss resulting from a cybersecurity breach and taking data protection seriously, by regular testing, helps organisations to cement a relationship of trust with their customers.
