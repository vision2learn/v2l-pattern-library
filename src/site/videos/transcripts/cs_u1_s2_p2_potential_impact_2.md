**Abraham:** Reporting cyber security incidents is important in maintaining the resilience of public communications networks and understanding the threat environment. This enables companies and organisations to strengthen their procedures and put contingencies into place by learning from other’s mistakes and keeping up to date with trends.

**Nicole:** Staff need to be made aware of the importance of cyber security as they represent the business and are often handling sensitive data. Companies should make sure that all staff are fully aware of issues and what steps to take to maintain security and what to do in case of a breach. This training should be delivered in a regular cadence to ensure staff are always aware.

**Abraham:** A cyber security issue usually means that something has, or could have, put critical systems or sensitive data at risk. It can be anything from a malware attack to an employee clicking on an email or link from an untrusted source or losing a storage device with sensitive data.

**Nicole:** It maybe something as small as someone putting their password on a 'post it' by their computer or sharing their log in details with others. This might seem trivial and not a 'reportable offence' but it should be addressed as it could lead to a potential security risk with serious ramifications. It may cause loss of trust in a company and financial loss with legal implications.

**Abraham:** There are many kinds of cyber security incidents but those that involve personal data breaches need to be reported to the ICO [Information Commissioner’s Office] within 72 hours of becoming aware of it. If this affects an individual’s rights and freedoms, then they must also be told without delay.

**Nicole:** Where appropriate this will be shared with the National Cyber security centre [NCSC] and other law enforcement and cyber crime agencies or regulators such as NCA National Crime Agency, the Financial Conduct Authority [FCA], and the Payment Cards Industry [PCI].

**Abraham:** A key principle of the UK General Data protection regulation is that personal data is processed securely and means that all systems and services should have the correct security to maintain confidentiality.

**Nicole:** In some more exceptional cases, a cyber security incident can result in actual physical harm. This is especially true when it comes to cyber-attacks on the healthcare industry such as those affecting the NHS. Also, with a wide range of new smart appliances being developed especially for the home including security systems there is an opportunity for hackers to take over personal devices to surveil or gain control of. The rise of IOT devices has created new problems with technology being used in more day-to-day things meaning that there are new risks emerging.

**Abraham:** Vulnerability to cyber attack should never be underestimated and hidden. It is important to remember that thinking that 'you won't be hacked because nobody would want to', is not a good defence and is sometimes referred to as 'security through obscurity'. To be able to maintain cyber security at all levels it is important that any issues, breaches, or attacks are reported immediately to assess and minimise any current or future damage.
