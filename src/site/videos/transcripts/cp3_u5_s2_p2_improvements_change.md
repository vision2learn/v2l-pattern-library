**Leah:** Earlier in this session we talked about the different elements of software maintenance. Let’s now look at the ‘improvements’ that can be made during maintenance, and the potential negative consequences that this may have. 

**Chris:** Preventive software maintenance 

As we have mentioned previously, ‘Preventive Maintenance’ refers to software changes carried out to futureproof a product and prepare for any potential changes ahead.

This includes making code easier to scale or maintain and managing legacy content. It also covers finding and fixing latent faults in the product before they evolve into operational faults.

**Leah:** ‘Preventive software maintenance’ mainly takes place behind the scenes and relates more to tidying and preparation, rather than major changes.

Users of the software are unlikely to notice preventive software changes directly, but they may see the positive effect later. This is because preventive maintenance can mean the smoother implementation of large-scale changes further down the line. However, a negative consequence of this may be the introduction of new bugs or errors that may go undetected and affect functionality. 

**Chris:** Corrective software maintenance 

Corrective changes in software maintenance fix bugs and defects in the software. These are often carried out by implementing quick, small updates as and when required.

Users are less likely to give negative feedback as they will be happy any issues or bugs are fixed making their experience smoother, as well as making a system or application more reliable.

**Leah:** There may, however, be occasions when a corrective fix may cause some disruption to users. Imagine if a bug or flaw in the software has been present for some time and the user has grown used to its presence and therefore adapted their behaviour to accommodate the flaw. Fixing the issue may mean that they will again have to change their behaviour leading to negative feedback. 

A corrective change may upset a user if they have problems with a certain bug or flaw which isn’t fixed when other issues are.

**Chris:** With corrective software maintenance, it’s important to work fast and frequently which will help to minimise any potential user problems and keep your product working as expected. 

**Leah:** Adaptive software maintenance 

The tech environment is constantly changing. New knowledge, hardware, and cybersecurity threats mean that software quickly becomes outdated. ‘Adaptive software maintenance’ addresses this issue.

Adaptive changes focus on the infrastructure of the software. They’re made in response to new operating systems, new hardware, and new platforms, to keep the program compatible.

**Chris:** Adaptive software changes tend to be low impact for users as they deal with the internal workings of the software such as making sure it can integrate with new tech. Users may notice a small improvement in speed or scalability but are otherwise unaffected.

Users are, however, more likely to notice when adaptive maintenance isn’t completed, as it may mean their software stops working on their devices or they encounter compatibility issues.

**Leah:** Perfective software maintenance 

Perfective software maintenance is the biggest category as it addresses the functionality and usability of the software. It involves changing existing product functionality by refining, deleting, or adding new features and as well as changing the way a product works, it might also change the way it looks. This may impact and change the user interface or in-app user journey.

**Chris:** The scope and visibility of these changes make perfective maintenance the most likely to generate a negative response from users. Users won’t notice a bit of refactored back-end code, but they will notice any changes that are clearly visible in the way the product looks and performs. Managing perfective changes means communicating with your users to emphasis the positives and mitigate any potential negative sentiment. This can be done by advertising, offering support and responding effectively to product feedback.
