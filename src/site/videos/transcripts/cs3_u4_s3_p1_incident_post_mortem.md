**Nicole:** An Incident post-mortem takes place after the event to help teams better prepare for future incidents. 

**Abraham:** These are also known as ‘post incident reviews’ or ‘root cause analysis’ and doing this can help create a culture of continuous improvement.

**Nicole:** It shouldn’t specifically apportion blame but should be about understanding why and how the incident took place and how future attacks can be prevented. All parties should be able to speak more freely when blame is taken out of the equation.

**Abraham:** At the time of the attack the first response is to restore service and mitigate the incident. But the post-mortem gives chance to take the whole incident apart and see what went wrong. The person in charge is the incident post-mortem coordinator and will be chosen by the person in charge of responding to the incident.

**Nicole:** The coordinator will then involve and liaise with the other members of the appropriate teams who were involved in responding to the incident. Once they have accumulated the relevant information, they’ll organise a discussion and assemble a report. A post-mortem is usually a collaborative process orchestrated by the coordinator.

**Abraham:** Most organisations will develop their own post-mortem process, structure and documentation according to their needs. But on the whole, they often follow the same steps:

**Nicole:** The meeting- this will involve all those who were involved in the incident response. Any documentation such as incident logs should be presented. There may also be more individual meetings to gain more details with those involved in mitigating the incident directly. It should also be made clear to all team members, that the incident was blameless!

**Abraham:** The Post-mortem Report-this presents all the information and conclusions gained from the meeting and a record of the event that can be referred to in the future. It should include incident details, root cause analysis, the incident timeline, any actions, and any conclusions and steps to be taken.

**Nicole:** Dissemination of the report and findings- this should be distributed to all involved and to management and decision makers. It may also need to be made public to reassure customers and clients.

**Abraham:** Follow up actions- If recommendations are made these should be acted on.

An incident post-mortem should be a valid process and not just a tick box exercise. It should be clearly detailed enough to draw conclusions as to what works and what doesn’t and how this can be remedied. 

**Nicole:** Remember incidents are a learning opportunity, a chance to uncover vulnerabilities and mitigate future incidents.
