**Nicole:** We can see that threats to organisations can come from a range of sources external and internal, ‘threat actors’ may even attack home workers or users via a phishing email, text, phone call, or by using cleverly worded messages on other communication systems such as Slack or Microsoft Teams. This can then make them become the 'threat actor' unintentionally, therefore everyone should be aware of the importance of safeguarding their data and protecting their devices.

So, what are some of the motives for external threats

One of the most common motives for external threats is for financial gain by getting access to customers financial details such as credit or debit card data. They also can access email addresses and login credentials gaining access to a great many personal computers and subsequently more financial gain. Bank details can be used to access bank accounts and misdirect individuals into believing they are being contacted by their own banks. 

By using ransomware external threat actors can also hold a company to ransom and affect its security credibility. They can also impact a company's profitability as they may not be able to function. This can make it easier for other businesses to take their place.

Now let’s look at motives for insider threats.

Financial gain can also be a motive for insider threats where unscrupulous employees may access customers financial details, data and databases and sell them on to others. They may also want to act maliciously passing on secrets or information to other companies or organisations for financial gain or to undermine a company. 

It’s also becoming increasingly common for cybercriminals to try to “recruit” people working inside companies, who have privileged access to systems. They get them to share their access and in return they get a cut of the profits.
