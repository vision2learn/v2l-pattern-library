BETH: Many individuals with autism are perfectly able and willing to work, but only 21.7% are in full or part-time employment, due to the barriers they face in gaining employment.

JOE: Individuals with autism can offer a wide range of skills and knowledge, qualities and attributes to an employer, however the field of employment is a challenging and full of barriers which many struggle to overcome. These barriers may be different for each individual depending on where they are on the spectrum.

BETH: Some employers do not recognise the difficulties that these barriers pose and may be unintentionally or purposely restricting employment opportunities for individuals with autism. 

Most of these barriers can be removed, or reduced, by making 'reasonable adjustments' when necessary. 

JOE: Barriers may start whilst trying to find a suitable job. Search results may be confusing and the information vague especially through recruiters.  This can create uncertainty as to who is the employer, how much does the job pay, what are the hours?

BETH: Filling in application forms may cause problems with difficult questions to answer about gaps in work history and why they left a previous job, and psychometric or personality tests.

Questions about health or disability may mean disclosing their autism at the application stage which may put employers off reading their CV or offering an interview. Negative assumptions and limited knowledge can stop an employer processing the application.

JOE: Some individuals are themselves put off applying, due to negative experiences of previous applications or interviews.

Attending an interview can be really daunting as they may find it hard to know what to do, what to expect. They may have problems anticipating questions and will be worried about the process if they have not done it before. There may be other things involved such group interviews, tests, activities or presentations, these can all cause great concern.

BETH: Eye contact and verbal and non-verbal communication which is often key in interviews may be a problem, and how to respond to ‘question and answer’ sessions.

Once the interview is over, they may be unsure about how they did or how it went or when they might hear.

JOE: Success at the application and interview stage may not alleviate the issues of starting the job and going through the process of signing contracts and other formalities can itself cause issues. Let alone other elements such as when they start the job, the daily routine and deadlines for projects, what do they wear, how should they behave, when are their breaks, what are their hours, holiday allowance, and adjusting to the workplace environment.
