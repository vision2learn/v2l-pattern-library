**Jack:** What is meant by an Agile development?

We have already mentioned ‘Agile’ in previous units but now let’s look at it in more detail and see how this process is used for software development.

**Leah:** ‘Agile Development’ which is also more commonly known as Agile Software Development, is an umbrella term for a range of frameworks, such as ‘Scrum’, and practices such as the ‘daily stand-up meeting’. It adopts the principles set out in the ‘Agile Manifesto’ and the ‘Agile 12 Principles’.

Agile came about as a process to address issues that software developers faced, focussing on a project being flexible, so it could respond to any changes and deal with any uncertainty.

**Jack:** Agile is people-oriented and result-focused in respect to software delivery and instead of delivering all the product at the end of the project, the software is built incrementally from the start in time-boxed 'sprints'. It is an iterative approach used in software development projects with a focus on continuous releases that takes customer feedback into consideration. 

So, how does this work? 

Development tasks are broken down into bite size chunks, which can be achieved in a certain period known as a ‘sprint’. The agile team itself would normally consist of different skillsets, working independently and collaboratively, to achieve the tasks within that set time.

**Leah:** Let’s now look at the ‘Agile Manifesto’ and how it originated.

The Agile Manifesto is a short document, which was created in 2001 from a group of leading professionals who met to discuss the future of software development. Its focus is on value to individuals and interactions, working software, customer collaboration and responding to change. The Manifesto itself is short and made up of 68 words and has since been embraced by many different teams and companies, and it is agreed by many that it is still relevant today.

**Jack:** What are the 12 Principles?

The 12 Principles of Agile Software, expand on the values within the Manifesto. The focus is on the customer, continuous delivery of product, change, frequency, team collaboration, motivation, support, communication, measurement, excellence, simplicity, self-organisation, and reflection.
