**Abraham:** When changes are made to a system or network there is the potential to create new vulnerabilities. It is therefore imperative that further testing and retesting is done to mitigate this. 

**Nicole:** This may be done on a regular basis and is called remediation verification testing.

Remediation verification testing makes sure that any vulnerabilities identified during pen testing have been dealt with successfully. It focusses on ensuring that issues that arose have been properly identified, fixed and are no longer a threat. 

**Abraham:** It works by attempting to reproduce each vulnerability and then checking if it is fixed.

Insurers and regulators often require this to be done by an independent authority to make sure the testing and solution is robust.

**Nicole:** A penetration test can only however, validate that an organisations IT systems are not vulnerable to any known issues as of the day of the test. Unless tests are done on a regular basis, vulnerability can last in a system for some time before it is checked again.

How are the outcomes of cyber security testing reported?

**Abraham:** Once testing is done the most common form of reporting is the detailed reports usually sent to senior management. Smaller tests and regular scans may be reported as memos, incident logs or as a short report. 

Reports are responsible for detailing all the tasks performed by a security tester or ethical hacker and are responsible for detailing key findings and steps needed to act on these by remediation. 

**Nicole:** Reports may be viewed by senior management, IT teams, clients or even the regulators and should be clear and concise whilst setting out any details of the problems and the solutions.

**Abraham:** A good report should also include a summary or overview of any findings and recommendations as well as a comprehensive detailed list of issues and proposed solutions. This then should make it clear to all levels of interested parties.

**Nicole:** A test report should include:

• any security issues that are uncovered, 

• an assessment of the level of risk that each vulnerability exposes the system to.

• a method or resolving each issue.

• An opinion on the accuracy of the vulnerability assessment and advice on how to improve the process.

The process of debriefing is also very important, and vulnerabilities can be discussed according to their severity and if a test shows vulnerabilities then solutions can be found.

**Abraham:** So, why should the outcomes of cyber security testing be reported?

There are many reasons for reporting the outcomes of cyber security testing but whatever the reason these results should only be shared with those who need to know. They can include sensitive highly confidential information which should only be made available to trusted parties. 

**Nicole:** However, a summary version of a report can be more widely shared detailing the scope and approach of an attack and how this can be mitigated.

**Abraham:** Websites, organisations and software developers often offer a ‘bug bounty’ to uncover issues and vulnerabilities that may impact their product or systems. For those who find complex security vulnerabilities the payout can be high but on average payouts are low. 

**Nicole:** There are also companies that offer the service of connecting ‘bug bounty hunters’ to companies to help with finding their vulnerability issues.

However, for some organisations, there may be other reasons for reporting the outcomes of cyber security testing. One of these may be regulatory to demonstrate to the appropriate authorities that they are taking steps to identify and eliminate any vulnerabilities. They may also be required by regulatory bodies to conduct regular testing especially after a breach.

**Abraham:** A key principle of GDPR regulations is that personal data is processed securely, and all systems and services have the correct security to maintain this. The National Cyber Security Centre [NCSC] even offers a service to organisations to report cyber security incidents simplifying the reporting landscape for users. 

**Nicole:** It is therefore especially important that if an organisation has had a data breach it shows that it is taking all the appropriate steps to stop this from happening again.

**Abraham:** Reporting testing results is the best way that an organisation can regain trust after a breach and reassure their clients and customers that their system is secure. 

**Nicole:** This can also help an organization to better plan for any future threats and allocate the appropriate resources to help prevent this happening.
