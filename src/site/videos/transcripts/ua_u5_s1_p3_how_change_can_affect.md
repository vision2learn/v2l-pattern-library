BETH: The world can seem a really unpredictable and confusing place to Individuals with autism, and they can find any kind of change difficult. 

They often develop routines and rituals as strategies they can use, to cope with confusing sounds and sights, events, people and places.

JOE: They often prefer to have a daily routine so that they know what is going to happen every day, travelling the same way to and from school or work, or eating exactly the same food for breakfast. 

BETH: Some individuals may find minor changes upsetting like the positioning of simple things around the house or introducing something new. Others find big events which create major changes and upheaval distressing, like going on holiday, starting a new school or moving to a new house. 

JOE: Some autistic people manage their behaviour by using daily timetables to help them see the structure of their day. 

They often have a need for structured routine around daily activities such as meals or bedtime and routines can become almost ritualistic in nature, followed precisely with great attention to detail.

BETH: Some have rigid preferences about things like food, such as always wanting the same cereal or even only eating food of a certain colour. Some may only wear clothes made from specific fabrics or again of a certain colour.

JOE: Others find the need for routine and sameness can go so far, that it becomes an obsession.

People's dependence on routines can increase during times of change, stress or illness and may even become more dominant or elaborate at these times. 

BETH: Individuals with autism can find changes to their routines difficult, but they can be prepared for change and supported through it.
