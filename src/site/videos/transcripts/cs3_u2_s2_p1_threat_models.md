**Abraham:** What exactly is threat modelling?

**Nicole:** Threat modelling is an overall risk-based approach to identify and resolve any potential threats and vulnerabilities that may compromise a business or organisation.

**Abraham:** There are many different threat models and many different frameworks involved but they all follow similar steps and have the same goal in preparing an organisation or business on what might happen if they fall prey to malicious attacks. By proactively anticipating scenarios that may impact an organisation they also give an opportunity to look at what might possibly happen even in extremes cases.

**Nicole:** At the beginning of the process, it is important to plan what framework is needed for the threat model and who and what it needs to cover.

So, let’s look at an overall view of the stages involved in threat modelling.

The Discovery stage identifies any desirable assets, any attack vectors or threats surrounding them, and how hackers may reach them.

**Abraham:** The Implementation stage, uses and analyses information gathered in the discovery stage. This may be looking at potential impact and how to prioritise the most damaging attacks, and how to resolve the vulnerabilities associated with these.

Let’s look at these stages in more detail starting with the discovery stage.

**Nicole:** All assets including data and applications should be catalogued. This could be business assets, the various data components and applications that are necessary to run a business and if targeted could disrupt the activities of an organisation or business. It could also be data assets which could be crypto mined to sell on the dark web.

**Abraham:** Hackers are often opportunists who look for easiest way in to exploit already-known vulnerabilities, using tried and tested routes such as unprotected admin accounts and default passwords. The areas at risk that hold the data are the attack surface. By mapping out all the components of these areas that communicate with the asset, threat modelling teams can see how data flows to and from the asset and any potential risks.

**Nicole:** Identify the paths that hackers use to reach these areas from system penetration to asset removal. Hackers may have multiple ways to exploit paths so these should be analysed also. This can be done by collecting information on known exploits and vulnerabilities that relate to each path.

**Abraham:** This is where the good actors or white hat hackers come in. Thinking like a hacker makes it easier to stop an attack by being prepared and secure.

So, we have looked at the discovery stage, now let’s look at the implementation stage in more detail.

**Nicole:** The implementation stage of threat modelling begins with a comprehensive analysis of previously gather discovery stage information.

Analysis of information collected should be used to assess the potential impact for each attack type. This should be done using any assumptions made and any threat intelligence or indicators. As an attack can cause a chain of events it’s important that the analysis covers as much potential damage as possible. This may include reputation, leaked data, legal action, general costs to put things right such as relacing equipment and the downtime that may be caused of organisational services.

**Abraham:** Prioritisation is one of the last steps of threat modelling which prioritises previously discovered vulnerabilities by focussing on the most dangerous high impact attacks. Once these have been addressed then a hierarchy would be followed to address the ‘less’ important threats.

The final step is finding security controls to mitigate all vulnerabilities and remove and counter any threats. It is also the time when any security gaps not discovered in the initial stage can be addressed.

**Nicole:** Threat modelling is important to all businesses and organisations although it can be costly and time consuming. It is often done manually by a competent cyber security team but can also be automated by using threat modelling tools for those who don’t have a range of cybersecurity personnel available.

**Abraham:** How do we evaluate a threat model? 

**Nicole:** Each threat model has its own specific process and sometimes threat models are used in conjunction with each other. Some focus on valuable assets, some more on the types of attack, some on who uses the system and some on prioritising. But whichever model is chosen it should meet the purpose and needs of the organisation or business and the strengths of their cyber security team, and when it comes down to it, a lot depends on what the business is or what the organisation does to help with the evaluation of suitability.
