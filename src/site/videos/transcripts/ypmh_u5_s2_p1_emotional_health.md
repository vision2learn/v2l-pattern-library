RHIANNON:  
The emotional health of children and young people is increasingly recognised as being fundamental to their wellbeing. As emotional health is nurtured primarily in the home, as long as this is a calm and loving environment, it can only be positive for the future prospects of individuals and communities.

 
LEAH:  
The good emotional health of children and young people is vital to them as individuals, as being emotionally healthy is an important end in itself and is also linked with a range of positive outcomes in later life. It does not necessarily mean being happy all the time, but it does mean having the resilience to face the challenges that occur in life.

 
RHIANNON:  
The good emotional health and well-being of children and young people is also vital to society, as poor mental health in children and young people is associated with teenage pregnancy, bullying, violent and criminal behaviour and substance misuse. The majority of adults with mental health problems experienced mental health concerns in childhood. These problems not only persist through to adulthood but can also have an impact the next generation.

 
LEAH:  
The development of emotional health starts even before a child is born, with the first two years of life being critical for putting in the foundations for emotional health throughout childhood. This then follows onward into adult life, through the parent to child relationship.

 
Promoting the emotional health of children and young people can be done by supporting friendships, peer relationships and personal development, within early years, school and college settings and by providing additional advice and support if problems arise.


RHIANNON:  
Conduct disorders can be difficult to diagnose, as some of these troublesome behaviours can be a result of environmental factors or other mental health conditions.

 
LEAH:  
A child who has a poor emotional environment with poor role models may struggle to develop positive relationships with their peers. They may fall behind academically and risk later involvement in risky behaviours. They may also develop physical and mental health issues in their adult life and are unlikely to reach their full potential.

 
A good emotional environment will provide a secure base for children to grow into well-rounded resilient adults with good mental health. A child’s social and emotional development is vital for their future, if they are to achieve their full potential socially, academically and career-wise as an adult. 

 
Strong bonds with a parent or carer can help to develop a resilience to stress, an ability to balance emotions and enable the child to have meaningful interpersonal relationships in the future.
