Ransomware is one of the most common threats to an organisations cyber security and this is increasing all the time. The techniques used by criminals are getting more sophisticated and extortion attacks are on the increase. This has a major economic impact on organisations from not only the cost of paying the ransom but also the loss of revenue during any disruption and the subsequent loss of business and brand reputation.

**Nicole:** Traditionally in the past companies were able to create secure networks in their office with a firewall perimeter around it. This allowed them to then trust access from that network to sensitive corporate networks. With most of the workforce now working from home, this poses a new challenge of how to ensure that only authorised employees are accessing potentially sensitive data. Many IT departments rely on VPNs to access their networks, but these are proving inadequate against the threat of phishing as cyber attacks are on the increase. Zero-trust network access or ZTNA is a new more secure option for controlling remote access to sensitive data, reducing the likelihood of an attack. This involves using a default approach of “trust nothing” and only allowing access using several contextual authentication metrics, such as the device that’s being used to connect, the time they’re connecting, the location they’re connecting from, and the behaviour of the user.

**Abraham:** Multi-factor authentication or MFA can offer an additional defence against data breaches and malicious attacks, forcing individuals to use two or more separate devices to confirm their identity. This often involves a one-time passcode being sent to two or more devices. This can also take the form of a physical USB device that a person carries with them [much like their actual house keys] which they plug in to their computer to prove that they are who they claim to be. This works on the fact that whilst it’s somewhat likely at any given time that a criminal may have taken your credentials, it’s extremely unlikely that they will have both your credentials and your second factor, such as your phone or your USB key.

**Nicole:** I am sure we all are getting used to having one-time passcodes sent to our phones that we must input into the verification process. However, Microsoft has recently urged users to move away from phone-based MFA due to the weak security in telephone networks and the fact that SMS messages are not encrypted giving access to attackers. Companies should use more secure MFA methods like Google or Microsoft authenticators. 

AI and machine learning are growing in sophistication and capabilities. AI is being used increasingly to build automated security systems analysing large quantities of data at a much faster pace. This can offer an opportunity for greater threat detection, but this technology can also work both ways, offering cyber criminals the opportunity to automate their attacks. Criminals are then able to take large data sets of emails and passwords found on the ‘dark web’ from previous breaches, and then automatically run these through all the websites they want to try to hack, with the hope that people use the same password and email for several different sites. Once one logs in successfully, they can then change that password and take over that account.
