BETH: Reasonable adjustments are a way of ensuring that every individual with autism has equal access to services and facilities, by reducing or alleviating disadvantage. Some services may need to provide more suitable support to individuals with autism, in order for them to achieve the same outcomes as others. 

Each individual with autism spectrum disorder is unique, with different needs and challenges, therefore a comprehensive assessment may need to be carried out, to identify their specific areas of need.

JOE: The Equality Act 2010 requires reasonable adjustments to be made, so as to avoid individuals with a disability being put at a "substantial disadvantage" compared with those without a disability. This also applies to individuals with autism.

BETH: All service providers and public bodies have a duty, to ensure that individuals with autism, have equal access to healthcare, education, leisure and all other services regardless of their specific needs. Therefore, if they cannot access those services and facilities, then reasonable adjustments need to be made. 

JOE: To benefit individuals with autism, reasonable adjustments, should be relevant to their needs. Some adjustments may be straightforward or simple, others may be more complex. This may involve things like lighting, ambient noise, visual aids, quiet rooms and easy access. 

BETH: Some reasonable adjustments can be done quite easily and cheaply, some may be more involved and costly, but it is important that they should be made if they benefit the individuals with autism.
