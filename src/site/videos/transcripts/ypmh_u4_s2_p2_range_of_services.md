Children and young people may need help with a wide range of issues at different points in their lives, and parents and carers may also need help and advice to deal with behavioural or other mental health problems their child is experiencing.

 
For those experiencing mental health problems GPs are often a starting point, and they can give advice or refer children and young people to counsellors and therapists who will be able to give more specialist assistance whether this is through the NHS or privately. 

 
Counsellors and therapists can also be approached directly and there are many organisations online that can recommend or showcase these services.

 
Children and Young People’s Mental Health Services or CAMHS as they maybe referred to, cover all services that work with children and young people who have difficulties with their mental health or wellbeing. They include psychiatrists and psychologists and primary health care workers and specialists. A referral to CAMHS for an assessment can be via a parent or carer, the individual if they are old enough, a GP or teacher, or via a school or social service. Getting help from a specialist CAMHS is different dependent on area and waiting times can vary.

 
Youngminds.org.uk is committed to improving the emotional well-being and mental health of children and young people. They campaign, research and aim to influence policy and practice based on the experiences of children and young people they work with, as well as their families and the professionals who support them, to help them to develop better well-being.

 
Mind.org.uk is perhaps the most well-known of all mental health charities. Mind’s aim is to provide advice and support to empower anyone experiencing a mental health problem. They campaign to improve services, raise awareness and promote understanding and offer specific information and support for young people aged 11 to 18 who are going through a difficult time. They also offer supported housing, drop-in centres, crisis helplines, talking therapies, employment and training schemes, counselling and befriending services.

 
Stem4.org.uk is a charity that promotes positive mental health in teenagers and those who support them including their families and carers, education professionals, as well as school nurses and GPs. through the provision of mental health education, resilience strategies and early intervention. This is initially through the provision of mental health education, resilience strategies and early intervention, provided digitally, through their website.

 
There are many other charities and local initiatives involved in supporting good mental well-being, and role of the voluntary and community organisations in supporting mental health issues, plays a very important role and they sit outside of clinical settings. Some local areas may also provide drop-in sessions or support groups run by volunteers, where children and young people can get together.

 
The good thing about the support framework within the charity and volunteer sector, for those with mental health problems, are that they can influence and participate in projects or activities. Helping them to increased levels of confidence and boost their self-esteem also helping their mental well-being.
