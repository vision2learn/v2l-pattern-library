There are many advantages to working in teams but there are also disadvantages. Being aware of both of these, can make it easier for you to consider, how to approach different projects and tasks. 

In a team leadership context, knowing the disadvantages you tend to find in teams, will help you mitigate their effects. And knowing the advantages will help you act to reinforce them. 

So, let’s start by looking at the Advantages 

Productivity – Teams tend to be more productive than individuals. This is because a team has a wide pool of individual talents, experience, and knowledge to draw from. And with more pairs of hands, more work can get done in a shorter amount of time, if tasks are appropriately allocated.

Ideas – In a team, ideas should be shared frequently and openly. This not only allows everyone to have their say, but it also exposes them to ideas they might not otherwise have considered. 

Belonging – In a team, you’re not just one individual on your own, you are part of a group of people working together, for the same goal. This can create a powerful sense of belonging, which is good for people’s wellbeing and morale. 

Mentorship – Teams give people access to mentors who can support and guide them on their career pathway. This can be official or unofficial. 

Networking and relationships –In a team, you’re dealing with a wider group of people, this increases your professional and social circle. This can also lead to unexpected development opportunities, you might not otherwise have encountered. 

So, now let’s look at the Disadvantages 

Loss of individuality – For a team to function properly, everyone needs to put the team’s needs first. What this means in practice can be variable, but it can result in some team members feeling like they’ve lost their autonomy and individuality, by not being able to prioritise themselves, their ambitions, their desires, and their ideas. 

Groupthink – Teams need to have consensus to function and that can sometimes mean teams tend towards groupthink. That’s a situation where new ideas don’t get a hearing because “That’s not how we do things”. In a situation like that, the team has become too inward looking and conservative. 

Cliques – Because teams often become quite tightknit, this can leave people not in the team, feeling left out. The team may even come to think of itself as superior to “outsiders”, and sometimes cliques can form within the team, making some team members feel alienated.

Competition with other teams – There’s nothing wrong with a bit of healthy competition between teams in a workplace, in fact, workplaces often encourage it. But it can go too far, and lead to teams prioritising themselves, over the strategic goals of the organisation.

Inequality in the team – although everyone in an effective team should be collaborating and working together, inevitable situations will arise where some people are given more to do than others. This can be for a lot of reasons such as scheduling, professional experience, and so on. It can produce problems if some members of the team start to perceive this as favouritism or that their willingness to work is being taken advantage of. 

On the whole teams have very strong advantages, and will often outperform less closely organised groups, but we can’t ignore the disadvantages that are inherent in teams. But we shouldn’t consider these disadvantages as “failures” of teams, they inevitably occur every now and again, because they are totally reflective of human nature. What is important though, is that we are realistic in our understanding of the problems, that even the most effective teams will eventually have to deal with.
