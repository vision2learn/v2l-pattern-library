**Chris:** There are many different reasons why conflicts arise in the workplace and failure to deal with these issues quickly and effectively can have a serious impact on the wellbeing of those involved, the wider team, and any projects that they are working on. 

**Leah:** Conflicts will happen and having people who are able to identify and handle conflicts fairly and sensibly will remove or minimise the impact that conflicts may likely have.

Conflict situations can be very different in cause or effect and there is no one solution that fixes all. This is where conflict management skills come in to play. Those familiar with conflict management will look at each situation and utilise a style that is likely to be effective, so let’s have a look at them.

There are five conflict styles that can be utilised to resolve conflicts

**Chris:** Accommodating

This style is about putting the needs of others before your own. This can be useful if there is one party that has different feelings about an issue to others. For those who don’t feel strongly about something, keeping the peace is more important.

Adopting an accommodating style can be useful to resolve small issues quickly and can create a sense of openness within the team as people feel that voicing their opinion will not automatically create conflict. The downside of this method is that you can be seen as weak and prepared to give in quite easily. This is a technique that would not be appropriate for dealing with more serious issues that are affecting the team.

**Leah:** Avoiding

This approach can be useful but can also cause more problems that it solves. It involves removing one or more of the conflicted parties from the project. This can be useful as it gives the removed party or parties time and space to reflect on the issues and it may resolve itself. Alternatively, if used in the wrong situation this may cause more conflict as it can cause resentment with team members, and it may also have a detrimental impact on the projects progress. 

**Chris:** Compromise

This style is all about getting those that are in conflict to agree to concede some of their issues so that a solution can be reached. This style can be useful if there are critical time factors, or it is imperative that a solution is required immediately.

The downside is that none of those involved in the conflict is entirely happy as they may feel that they have conceded to much which may make them less likely to compromise in the future. 

**Leah:** Competing

This is a strategy that is only effective if there is a serious issue and can only be used by those in authority. This involves forcing the view or opinion onto others without giving any opportunity for disagreement. This leads to a win-lose outcome and may leave those who disagree feeling aggrieved. This is a style that should be used when there is a conflict situation that is unnecessary or having a seriously damaging effect on the team. 

**Chris:** Collaboration

This method can be the most useful style to use as it brings those that have differing opinions together to discuss the problem with an open mind encouraging them to focus on resolving the conflict by finding solutions that are agreeable to all involved. Personal emotions are left out of this as the only purpose is to find out what is the best solution for the team which leads to a ‘win-win’ outcome. A downside to this is that it can be very time consuming, and deadlines may be at risk while time is taken to find a solution. 

**Leah:** In any conflict situation and no matter what style is used there are several steps to take that will make a significant contribution to reaching a resolution.

The first step is to remain calm and be prepared to actively listen. When someone else is talking others involved in the conversation are already mentally preparing a response. Active listening involves focusing on what is being said and the points being made rather than thinking about your own response. Being attentive and listening to the other person gives you the opportunity to understand their point of view and creates the opportunity to compromise and collaborate to find a solution. 

**Chris:** Being positive and patient are two other factors in successful conflict resolution. By slowing down your emotional response and considering that others involved may have positive intentions rather than negative ones, will make a significant contribution to finding a resolution. Finding a solution may take time as you explore all the options but gives confidence to others to give opinions and discuss their concerns to reach agreement on an acceptable way forward. 

**Leah:** Once a conflict has been resolved it is important to maintain open communications between those involved. Having an environment where team members can talk freely and constructively on issues or concerns will reduce the potential for conflict situations and raise morale and therefore in turn increase productivity.
