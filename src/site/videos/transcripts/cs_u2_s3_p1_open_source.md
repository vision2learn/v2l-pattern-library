**Abraham:** What is open source information?

Open-source information is information that is publicly available, and anyone can obtain by request, purchase or observation. Information to be considered open source can be published or broadcast for a public audience, available to the public by request, subscription or purchase. It can also be seen or heard casually or made available at a public meeting or by visiting a place or event open to the public. 

Open-source intelligence involves data and information that is available to the general public and can be used by security specialists and threat actors alike to focus their efforts on specific areas of interest.

Cyber criminals will build an image of who they are pretending to be, so they can talk confidently about it and make you feel for them to increase your sense of familiarity. They make you more likely to do what they want, such as click a link. This is called pretexting. They may, for example, research Instagram and see that you have a pet dog and when they make contact, could talk about how they’ve been working at the dog shelter. This will build an emotional connection and establish themselves as a good and trustworthy person. 

**Nicole:** So, what useful information can be gained through social engineering?

Cybercriminals through social engineering use multiple tactics to gain information such as passwords, usernames and banking details. They create websites which look legitimate using trademarks and logos. They rely on an individual’s trust that it must be 'legit'.

By accessing one person’s email password they also have access to all their contacts and many people use the same password so they may have access to their social media and all their social media contacts.

Think of all those emails about winning money or prizes, be eligible for a tax refund, helping someone out abroad by accepting their money into your account. They are all ways social engineering takes place so just think have you ever been a victim.
