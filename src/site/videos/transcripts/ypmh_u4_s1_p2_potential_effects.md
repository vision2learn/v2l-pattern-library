RHIANNON: Mental health concerns within the family may involve a parent, a child, or a sibling or even a grandparent, but whoever is affected, the general effects of mental health concerns, can have an impact on the whole family and their relationships.

 
Knowledge that a parent is mentally ill can be very difficult for a child or young person as they may feel they are to blame for any related behaviours.

 
LEAH: Having a loved one, act violently towards someone in the family will always have a negative effect on mental health. Children who witness or are subject to violence at home, are likely to suffer from depression or develop more severe mental health issues.

 
Children may also have other concerns; they may feel they are missing out, because they’re looking after a family member and doing jobs around the house most people their age don’t do. Some children feel ashamed about their family member and do not want to bring friends home, some are even worried that they will suffer from the same mental illness.

 
RHIANNON: As mental illness can often appear in someone’s teens, it is common for someone to be living in the family home when their issues become apparent, most commonly anxiety disorders and depression. However, in severe cases, children or young people may express suicidal thoughts or attempt suicide or may use drugs or alcohol to cope with their feelings. In these instances, a child’s mental health concerns will most likely require professional help and support.

 
LEAH: The school environment has a significant impact on a young person’s emotional welfare and mental well-being. An estimated three children in every classroom suffer from a diagnosable mental health problem and there is a general trend to increased levels of stress and lower levels of wellbeing.

 
Students with a poor standard of education are more likely to experience mental health problems, this has been attributed to the fact that children from poorer backgrounds, often do not have access to the best standards of teaching.

 
RHIANNON: Some young people suffer a lot of distress going to school it could be due to relationships with their peers, body image, classroom anxiety or exam stress. and the growing number of children and young people experiencing poor mental health is one of the biggest challenges in the classroom.

 
LEAH: Mental health problems in children and young people cause distress and can have wide-ranging effects, including impacts on educational attainment and family life and relationships, as well as affecting physical health and in severe cases, life chances.
