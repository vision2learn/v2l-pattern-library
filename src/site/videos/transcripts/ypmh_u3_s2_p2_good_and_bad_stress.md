LEAH: Learning to cope with stress can require some trial and error and what works for one, might not work for another. Childhood stress can be present in any setting that requires the child or young person to adapt or change. Lots of children and young people face ongoing challenges they can’t quickly resolve, fear kicks in, they get overwhelmed, and they try to escape from it.

RHIANNON: Bad stress happens when a threatening situation goes on and on, and that can erode confidence and may seem overwhelming. The level of stress they feel depends on past experience. 

LEAH: Not all stress is bad however, there’s ‘good stress’, too. 

Good stress is the type of stress that accompanies excitement. The pulse quickens and adrenaline increases, even though there is no threat or fear such as experienced when riding a roller coaster.

RHIANNON: Stress may be caused by positive changes, such as starting a new activity. Stress is more likely to result in positive outcomes if it is seen as a challenge or something to strive to overcome and therefore can help motivate. 

When a child or young person confronts a challenging situation and they feel they can manage or control it, it gives them confidence and makes them stronger, ready to take on new challenges.

LEAH: Ongoing challenges and fears can create stress in children and young people, but remembering past successes, can help a child feel more in control. This can help children and young people rise to challenges such as in sport or resolve problems and build confidence in life.

RHIANNON: The body’s ability to deal with stress can get the brain and body ready to solve problems, and kick into ‘fight or flight’ mode. Dealing with danger quickly, can help people to feel safe again, and when the problem is overcome, the brain ‘feels good’ more confident and resilient.

LEAH: Confident and resilient individuals see stress as a challenge that they can and will master, and maybe even enjoy. If they do, their success will carry over to the next challenge.

RHIANNON: When children and young people have support, and learn how to support themselves when handling stress, they’re better equipped to handle challenges life may throw at them. The more success they have, the more confident and in-control they’ll feel, then when they are feeling that success, they are less likely to experience and dwell on the bad forms of stress.
