BETH: For most people, going to the doctor or dentist is a routine experience, but for individuals with autism, accessing health and care support can be a difficult and stressful experience. 

JOE: New environments and sensory changes can be challenging and cause anxiety. It’s important that healthcare professionals, are aware of the needs for those with autism, and are able to make the experience easier to handle. Health and care settings should also provide more flexibility, to adapt to the needs of people with autism.

BETH: GPs should have a good understanding and knowledge about autism, and the appropriate services to refer individuals to. Appointments should also be long enough to meet the communication needs of an individual with autism. 

JOE: Long waiting times for diagnosis can put extra pressure on parents and carers unnecessary stress to people with autism, as this may mean longer waits for treatment and support.

In some areas, there may be a lack of support services for people living with autism, including getting an Education, Health and Care plan ‘EHC’, or a lack of information available to accessing the support they need.

BETH: A high proportion of children with autism attend mainstream schools, which should all endeavour to do their utmost in providing the necessary support for any student who has special educational needs. However, the needs of individuals with autism as we have discussed in other units often go further than extra learning support.
 
There are specialist schools available and other mainstream schools are well-equipped with facilities to accommodate students with autism, but this is not consistent.

JOE: Autism is clearly established as a learning difficulty requiring special education provision, and a child with autism may be eligible for an ‘EHC’ plan once an assessment of their needs has been made by the local authority. This will describe their needs and any specialist provision to meet those needs and can be accessible from 0-25 years as long as they are in education or training, but not at university.

The opportunities for progression onto further education or training will vary depending on where an individual lives.
  
BETH: Every further education college should provide their students with special educational needs, the extra help that they need to support them with their studies. This includes students on the autistic spectrum. The special educational needs coordinator or SENCO will be in charge of organising any extra learning support or general needs. 

JOE: Students with autism may be able to access extra support at university through the institution's own additional needs team, or SEN support team. This may involve having access to a specialist service such as a note-taker during lectures or a mentor to provide support such as balancing workloads and accessing social opportunities and leisure facilities. They may also be entitled to extra time in exams.
 
BETH: Engaging in leisure activities can lower stress levels and improve mood which can help to increase psychological engagement and avoid unhealthy behaviours.  

Sport has so many benefits, not only as a great way to stay fit, it can also offer a sense of belonging and a way to make friends. Regularly taking part in sport can provide routine and structure and is a good way to release stress and frustration. Some sports clubs cater specifically for the needs of individuals with autism.

JOE: Visiting the theatre or the cinema can be exciting, and a great way to explore a passion for something, but also very challenging for individuals with autism as they might find it difficult to cope with big crowds. However, some cinemas run autism friendly screenings, where they lower sound levels and there is more freedom to move around.

BETH: Museums can also be a great place for individuals with autism to explore their special interests, and they are a great learning resource. Many now have interactive displays which involve all the senses.

JOE: The most important thing about attending a leisure facility is to first see if they have any specific support already available, and informing them about the visit, may help them to give guidance about what is available and for them to be prepared.
