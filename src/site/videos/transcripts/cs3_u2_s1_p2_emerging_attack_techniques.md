**Abraham:** Cyber security professionals are always on the lookout for new threats and use many different sources to find them. By combining information from different sources, a better picture can be seen. Let’s look at some of the sources they use.

**Nicole:** Indicators of Compromise are pieces of forensic data that serve as evidence of potential intrusions on a system or network and may be unusual traffic, unknown files, or suspicious activity. These enable information security professionals and system administrators to detect intrusion attempts. This information can offer actionable threat intelligence that can be shared with the cyber security community.

**Abraham:** Below the surface of the ‘open web’ we all use, lies the Deep, and Dark web and these are not accessible by standard search engines. This part of the internet hides someone’s identity and location and is only accessible through specialist browsers. The dark web is a channel for all kinds of illegal activities and cyber criminals use them to purchase hacking tools and stolen data.

**Nicole:** Social media platforms are a conduit for cyber attacks as data sharing is an inherent part of the apps. They provide a ready resource of personal data which is meant to be shared between family and friends but can instead be accessed by cyber criminals. Privacy settings are in place, but most users don’t give these a second glance. Phishing is common on social media and fake accounts are easy to set up. 

**Abraham:** Messaging platforms are used by threat actors to communicate as well as finding out about data leaks etc. Chat networks are used in a number of ways by cyber criminals for advertising their products and services such as cashing out, carding and crypto currency fraud.

**Nicole:** Cyber attacks and cyber offensive capabilities, are blurring the lines between spies and malicious hackers. Nation state and non-state actors are reshaping the cyber threat landscape. Threat actors can inject high levels of geopolitical instability and some nations use cyber espionage and criminality as a tool, to destabilise or gain economic or commercial advantage. Current geopolitical tensions are constantly monitored to offer clues of who might be targeted in the next attack.

**Abraham:** Malware analysis can help to show indicators of compromise to look for as signs of a system breach it cand also give an insight into the latest tactics and techniques be used by threat actors. 

**Nicole:** Exploit databases are archives of public exploits and vulnerable software. They are developed by ‘pentesters’ and ‘vulnerability researchers’ and give insights into which exploits are available to threat actors and which vulnerabilities need to be prioritised for patching. 

**Abraham:** Paste sites are sites where users can share plain text blocks as a link. These can be used innocently but more often than not, are used to leak breached data, source codes, links to dark web sites or targets of upcoming attacks.

**Nicole:** Information sharing platforms are sites that provide information about current threat actor activities. They may be government funded, or independent, and include the UK ‘National Cyber Security Centre’s Cyber Security Information Sharing Partnership’ and the ‘Financial Services Information Sharing and Analysis Centre’.
