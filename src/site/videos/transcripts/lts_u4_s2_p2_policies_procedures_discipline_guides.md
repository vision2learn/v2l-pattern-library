Organisations have employment policies and procedures that exist to help team leaders and managers, and, in this video, we are going to be looking at how these can help guide a manager in dealing with disciplinary issues.

Although it can often be best if appropriate, to address disciplinary issues informally at first, more serious issues need to be addressed formally and organisations should have clearly defined policies and procedures for this in place, to make sure everyone is treated fairly. Organisations that don’t follow policies and procedures put themselves at risk of breaking the law.

There are two categories of problem that disciplinary procedures are designed to respond to Misconduct and Capability. 

Misconduct occurs when someone is behaving inappropriately or against the organisation’s values

Capability issues are to do with not working to the standard that the organisation expects from its employees

So, what should a disciplinary procedure include?

• A meeting with the employee to tell them that the disciplinary procedure is underway

• An investigation to find out what’s going on

• An option to suspend the person involved, if necessary

• A formal disciplinary hearing, which will be a meeting of everyone involved

• If necessary, a sanction against the person, which can include being dismissed

• An option to appeal the decision and an appeal meeting

• Final confirmation of the decision that’s been made

And, throughout this process, the person in question has the right to be accompanied by a colleague or a union representative. 

This is the minimum disciplinary policy an employer should have, but what other policies can an employer have, that can help a team leader or manager with disciplinary issues?

Timekeeping

Having a formal policy around timekeeping can be useful to prevent absenteeism, when employees take time off without permission or a good reason. 

Typically, a timekeeping policy would mean that most employees are required to accurately log the time they arrive and leave work. They may also have to log the time that they spend on individual projects or pieces of work. This can make it easier to spot absenteeism and makes it less likely that people will be absent without leave.

Conduct Policy

This formally describes the conduct that an employer expects from its employees. Typically, it will state that employees are expected to follow the organisation’s values, treat each other respectfully, and not bully, harass, or discriminate. It may also have rules specific to the organisation, like defining how equipment should be treated, and attitudes expected when dealing with external clients, and so on. 

This helps show what behaviour is acceptable or unacceptable and is also a useful reference for a team leader as to when and if it’s necessary to take disciplinary actions or not.

Gross misconduct

There are some actions, like theft or physical violence, where an employee can be dismissed immediately, however, these still need to be investigated properly giving an employee chance to respond. This allows for a quick response to very serious disciplinary issues.

Capability policy 

This defines the standard of work that an organisation expects from its employees and gives a framework for responding to the problem of underperforming. These policies should be focused on identifying capability issues and giving the employee opportunities to develop and improve. They should also support the employee so their performance can be improved and provide them with training where necessary.

By having policies like this to refer to and in place, you should be in a better position as a team leader to respond to various disciplinary issues before they become too serious.
