BETH: The purpose of a risk assessment is to evaluate risks or hazards, then to remove that hazard or minimise the level of risk by adding measures to control it.

JOE: Any event or activity can have an element of risk to it, so it’s important to assess any risks ahead of time and be prepared for those risks to happen whilst minimising the chance of them happening. Establishing what actions can be taken can help to minimise the impact if it does happen. Planning should help to prioritise risks that are more likely happen and cause greater impact.

BETH: When looking at risk assessment that involves an individual with autism, this can involve specific new activities, or more routine everyday things.  It may be needed for a day out or a school trip, an event, activity or a holiday, or something really simple like preparing a meal.

JOE: Usual methods of risk assessment are made up of structured forms and charts documenting any prospective activity and the problems associated with it such as health and safety issues. However, this doesn’t always take into consideration the person, their needs, objectives and dreams.

BETH: When it comes to looking specifically at-risk assessment focused on activities individuals with autism might do daily, then more consideration needs to be given to how the ‘risks’ can be outweighed by the benefits of taking those risks. 

JOE: A risk assessment can be completed as part of other assessments to assess the environment both physical and social for any risks to an individual with autism, taking a holistic approach.

BETH: Risk assessment can give an insight into the level of risk an individual will face during any activity within a home, educational or work environment, but can be more specifically focused on new risks or new challenges going forward. It can also consider how an individual’s behaviour within the situation or activity may affect the outcome.

JOE: Individuals with autism are often so single-minded about their interests that they are unaware of the effect that their actions may have on others, or any consequences to themselves.

Therefore, risk assessments need to not just focus on the immediate health and safety risks, but the overall well-being of an individual going forward long-term, and the benefits that can be gained by positive risk taking.
