The way written communications are put together and the tone used to impart the information, are important and should be set at a level that the different audiences reading them can understand for them to be effective.

Let's look at some of the elements that make written communications effective.

**Abraham:** Written communication should have a clear purpose and should not be vague. The purpose should be clearly stated at the beginning of the communication defining what it wants to achieve. The reader knows what they are reading about straight away about without having to wade through ramblings. A good example of this would be documenting a threat and clearly putting together a report on any imminent threat attacks.

**Nicole:** Not only should it be clear about the purpose the message behind the written communication should be clear what the goal is. An example of a clear message would be to ensure that a team understands what is expected and can align themselves with that goal such as being aware of any cyber security protocols related to current activities or future threat actors.

**Abraham:** It is important that written communication is accurate so that people reading it can have confidence on any data displayed. This is really important when creating manuals or giving directions to other members of a team.

**Nicole:** Attention to detail means that all the information needed is contained in the written communication and the reader doesn't have to look elsewhere for further clarification. This can also help give clear guidance that’s easy to follow in manuals and instruction booklets prevent mistakes being made or misunderstandings.

**Abraham:** The correct use of spelling, grammar and punctuation are very important as errors can change the meaning of a message which might result in misinformation. Correct usage also gives the reader more confidence in the communication and more likely to act on the information imparted.

**Nicole:** The 'tone of voice' of the written communication should be right for the audience concerned, so that it communicates how it should be responded to. If the tone is too relaxed it won’t communicate any urgency that maybe involved in the communication. 

**Abraham:** The written communication should have an appropriate layout which makes it clearer to read and understand the key points. It can also make it easier to find the appropriate sections within the communication. This is especially important when putting reports together
