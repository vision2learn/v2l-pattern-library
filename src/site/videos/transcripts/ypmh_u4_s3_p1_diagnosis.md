When a child’s behaviour seems different from his or her peers, there may be concerns that, “something is wrong with them.” Although children develop at different rates, a clinical assessment from a professional and possibly some early intervention, can be helpful.

 
As they get older, the question of a mental health diagnosis may come up because a child or adolescent who has been progressing fine, suddenly becomes very angry, sad, or worried all the time. 

 
LEAH:  
There are no definitive medical tests to detect whether someone has a mental health disorder such as a blood test or scan. Mental health diagnoses are based on clinical interviews, observations, and sometimes tasks and tests the child is asked to complete.

 
The positives of having a mental health diagnosis are that it gives access to specialised services and can help by giving the problem a name. By giving it a name, it means someone has seen it before, they are not alone, and information can be shared with others experiencing similar problems. 

 
RHIANNON:  
By having a name, it also makes it easier to explain the issues to others and maybe access specific help as the illness becomes the problem not the child.

 
Mental health diagnoses can be presented as a fixed part of a person, but it is also important to consider that things change over time and a mental health diagnosis is not always a diagnosis for life.

 
LEAH:  
There are also alternatives to having a mental health diagnosis. Mental health professionals may work to understand the problem in the child or young person’s family, social and educational context, without necessarily feeling the need to give a formal diagnosis. 

 
RHIANNON:  
They may also look at what is the overall problem that is creating the mental health issues, and how does the child’s interactions with others effect the problem? 

 
Clinical interventions and strategies can be used with both parents and children without the need for a formal diagnosis, and a diagnosis is not always necessary to access therapy or support.
