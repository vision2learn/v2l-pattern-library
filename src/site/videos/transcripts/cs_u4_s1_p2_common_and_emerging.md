**Abraham:** As we’ve had more time on our hands during lockdown many more of us have been using technology to entertain ourselves, as well as to continue our work remotely. Children have been remote learning often sharing devices with others in a family. Mobile devices are commonly used by everyone including those who have not used technology before, such as the elderly who now regularly 'facetime' or 'chat' to family all over the world. We are all using our mobile phones more to order food, book appointments, organise parcel deliveries, do our banking, purchasing goods and book medical tests and appointments. This has opened us all up to Phishing attacks.

**Nicole:** Covid19 has itself helped to increase the level of phishing attacks on individuals. Any kind of change within the world, produces an opportunity for criminals to exploit people’s confusion. As we search for more vaccine and Covid19 testing information, vaccine and test suppliers are becoming targets of phishing attacks as are the individuals who seek their services. Bogus emails and SMS messages about their services can affect organisations and individuals alike. This is also impacted by remote working and the use of home networks.

**Abraham:** Phishing covers a range of different social engineering attacks which trick victims into sharing sensitive information such as passwords, usernames, and credit card details for malicious reasons. They can be random or more sophisticated and targeted known as spear phishing. This involves malicious emails being sent to a well-researched victim. Other forms of phishing like 'vishing' or voice phishing and 'smishing' or SMS phishing involve the use of mobile phones and phone calls or SMS messaging. Victims are contacted by what appears to be trusted companies, banks or organisations and told they have to act immediately to organise something or respond to something by giving their private details. 

**Nicole:** Most of us have had a message from a parcel delivery company or some other organisation asking us to click on a link. Most of us know that this is bogus, but individuals can be caught out if they are expecting something to be delivered or are too trusting! A common example of vishing is when an employee would be called by someone posing as ‘IT Helpdesk’ asking them to install something on their computer which turns out to be malware. A common example of smishing is a text pretending to be ‘Royal Mail’ saying there is an import charge for a fake parcel that requires payment.

**Abraham:** Other common threats come from 'water holing' a computer attack strategy where an attacker guesses or observes which websites an individual or organisation often uses, and they then affect them with malware. Identity theft and 'synthetic identities' are becoming more commonplace with the potential to affect us all. These involve creating fabricated credentials that are not associated with the real person.
