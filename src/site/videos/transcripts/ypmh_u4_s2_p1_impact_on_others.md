Mental health concerns can be painful and traumatic for all of the family and can have a huge impact both financially and emotionally. Also a child or young person’s mental health problems, can have real consequences for others within their family.

 
When a family member such as a child or a sibling has mental health concerns, it can be an extremely stressful time and may in turn create health problems such as headaches, loss of appetite, fatigue, and insomnia for other members of the family. 

 
It may also cause cognitive and emotional problems such as anxiety, depression, guilt, anger, fear and confusion or behavioural troubles such as changes in attitude, and social withdrawal. 

 
Often family members develop psychological or social problems of their own, to such an extent that they also need help and support.

 
The consequences of poor mental health in a family can further compound the mental health of the child or young person. If they can see the effects their mental health is having the family, such as loved ones becoming upset and worried about them, they may try to remove themselves from family life, so that normality can return.

 
Children who are too young to fully grasp the consequences of mental illness can find it difficult to understand why their sibling isn't well or is acting unusually and may tend to blame themselves and grow up feeling different, lonely, and isolated. 

 
Ultimately this may lead to others developing psychological, behavioural, and social problems of their own.

 
Family life can become unsettled and unpredictable causing a family member to maybe leave their job, give up socialising or following recreational pursuits. They may feel nervous about inviting people into their home and find it difficult to open up to people about their relative's problems.

 
It’s important therefor that we look at the family as a whole when looking at the potential knock on problems connected with Mental Health and look to provide support options beyond the immediacy of the person in focus
