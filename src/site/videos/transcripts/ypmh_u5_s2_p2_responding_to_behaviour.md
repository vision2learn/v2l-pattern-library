How a person feels about themselves and many different pressures growing up can affect a child’s or young person’s self-esteem, such as social media, bullying, exams, family problems, gender issues and abuse. 

 
RHIANNON:  
Most children will have dips in self-esteem as they go through life. Starting a new school, moving house, changes in the family and many other factors can affect a child’s confidence, but with support from parents and other adults in their lives they usually get them through this.

 
LEAH:  
Children and young people with high self-esteem often have a positive image of themselves and are confident, make friends easily, and will try new things and adapt to change. They often are happy on their own or in a group and will try to solve problems for themselves and if they make mistakes will learn from them, ultimately becoming proud of their achievements.

 
Children and young people with low self-esteem often have a negative image of themselves, they might feel bad, ugly, unlikeable or stupid, lack in confidence and are constantly comparing themselves to their peers in a negative way. They may feel lonely and isolated finding it hard to make and keep friendships or feeling victimised by others. They tend to avoid new things, finding change hard and can't deal well with their own failure, putting themselves down and not proud of achievement, always thinking they could have done better.
