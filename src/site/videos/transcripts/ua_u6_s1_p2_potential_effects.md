JOE: The level of an individual’s autism disorder can be a factor in making a decision about education, as those who are high functioning may fit more easily into mainstream schools but may not get that extra attention they might thrive from. 

Some mainstream schools have centres providing specialist support incorporated into them, this can provide support from trained staff, such as learning in small groups and therapies, and a calm setting to retreat to.

BETH: Before parents choose a school, they should check up on what extra facilities are available such as extra teaching support, different approaches to teaching and separate lessons. This may be all a child with autism may need, but for some this may not be enough.

There are different benefits and challenges to all of these types of education, such as inclusion and good practice, that are crucial for enabling children on the autism spectrum to be educated to their greatest potential.

JOE: Once an individual with autism has progressed through education, they are going to have to gain employment if they are going to be able to become more independent and self-sufficient. 

Those who find employment may not always be able to maintain this due to their disorders and associated problems. However, if employers are more aware of the issues and work with occupational health professionals often employers can benefit greatly from employees on the spectrum. 

BETH: The nature of their disorder in some high functioning individuals with autism can make certain professions ideal, such as using finely tuned special interests such as maths abilities in finance and banking. 

Many local initiatives are now evolving, helping companies to see an individual with autism as an asset to their company rather than having a disability.

JOE: Some individuals with autism can struggle to get access to services as they can be very articulate and seem to show that they don’t need support or the help of different services. 

BETH: They therefore can be blocked from some social care services or from learning disability or mental health specific services.  Social care assessors can sometimes lack sufficient awareness of autism to identify an individual’s specific needs.

JOE: To overcome any of these potential effects an individual with autism and their family, have more hurdles to deal with on top of the nature of their disorder, and this only make life more difficult at every stage.
