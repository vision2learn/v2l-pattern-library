Communication is a key component for any workplace, no matter what the industry is, or the line of work involves. But for communication to be effective, everyone should know what and how they’re expected to communicate with each other and who they should be reporting to. 

Everyone should know what their responsibilities are, or work will stop, timelines will be crashed, and goals won’t be achieved, as people won’t know what they’re meant to be doing! 

Effective communication boosts productivity. When people are communicating, they’re coordinating and they’re cooperating, meaning it’s much more likely that work is being done on time to the right standard. People will feel more connected with both their colleagues and their organisation, making them happier and more focused on their work, therefore improving productivity and efficiency. Without effective communication people will feel disconnected from each other, even if they know what their responsibilities and duties are.

Effective communication drives engagement A business where people are being effectively communicated with about their responsibilities and priorities is one that is probably hitting its targets. But if efforts aren’t made to make employees feel engaged, this will only last a certain amount of time. 

By effectively communicating things like the business’s purpose and ambitions, sharing success stories, and encouraging social communication, employees will feel more like they’re part of something bigger than themselves.

Effective communication encourages loyalty, and it means that if information about the business and career possibilities within the organisation, is being spread, people will be more likely to want to stay for longer. By encouraging loyalty and employee retention a company can avoid potentially expensive and disruptive searches for new employees. 

Let’s consider for example that a company has internal fast tracks to promote promising employees to management positions, but it doesn’t communicate this fact about itself to its people. They then won’t know that’s an option, so they might look for opportunities elsewhere. But, if its existence is made clear through effective communication, their retention of talented staff should improve.

Effective communication creates a health ideas ecosystem. If people and teams are able to easily share their experiences, knowledge, and ideas, that means there is a constant information flow in the business. This is fertile soil for the growth of novel ideas and approaches, helping the business stay competitive and efficient. 

Effective communication helps with employee wellbeing. When people feel engaged and know they’re being heard by their colleagues, this is positive for their wellbeing. No one likes to feel on their own or unheard. Effective communication is a powerful tool to resist that feeling, especially important given how many people now work remotely. 

Imagine for example, a company that has a lot of remote workers, a model adopted during the pandemic. This has continued as it offers a lot of benefits, meaning they can hire talent from a much wider geographic range. But they’ve noticed that some employees are talking about feeling isolated and lonely. So, a dedicated social channel is set up on the company’s internal instant messaging platform to encourage people to have those casual “get to know you” conversations that would happen naturally in an office. This helps people feel less lonely and improves employee wellbeing. 

We can see that there are many ways that effective communication is of great importance to a business and that it is essential for building and managing working relationships. Without it, organisations, and employees within them will struggle to reach their full potential. We can also see that effective communication impacts a business at all levels, helping with decision making, encouraging innovation, and keeping a business competitive, alongside helping to maintain a productive and engaged workforce.
