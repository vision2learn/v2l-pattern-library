JOE: It is not always obvious when an individual with autism is going to behave in a challenging manner, but there are ways to help prevent this happening and if it does happen there are also ways to de-escalate the situation or cope with the behaviour.

BETH: The first thing a person should do in all situations is to remain calm so that they can in turn calm the behaviour. Being calm, gentle and patient and giving an individual with autism space can sometimes be all that is needed.

JOE: If an individual show signs of behaviour that challenges it can be good to find alternative activities or objects to focus on to relieve the sensory stimuli that may be affecting them.

BETH: Providing alternative objects to play with, that provide a similar sensory experience to that provided by the behaviour, can help to distract them and this can be incorporated into their daily routine. For a person who bites, this could be to provide chewy tubes, gum, carrots, raw pasta or sultanas, or for a person pinches, they could be given playdough.

JOE: Opportunities for relaxation could be created wherever possible, such as watching bubble lamps, smelling essential oils, as long as they are not oversensitive to smells, listening to music, or using a swing or rocking chair. Distressed behaviour can be diffused by an activity that releases energy like punching a punch bag, bouncing on a trampoline or running around the garden. 

BETH: Reducing stressors by removing distracting elements, going to a less stressful place or providing a calming activity or object, can help to focus on returning to a calm state.

JOE: When supporting somebody who is stressed, it is essential to keep calm and quiet and to be a consistent, safe presence to help them feel they can begin to relax. If this is not the case it may make them feel less secure and more anxious. Visual supports can be used to help with de-escalating a situation.

BETH: It can also be helpful to discuss the situation with the individual once calm has been achieved and try to teach them alternate and more appropriate responses. This should be done providing clear directions and using simple language and attempts by the individual with autism to self-regulate should be praised.
