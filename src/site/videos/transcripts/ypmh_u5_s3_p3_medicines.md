Each time a new medication is prescribed, a doctor or other professional will not know how well it is going to work until the individual has been taking it for a while. It will also be difficult to predict whether they will suffer from any side effects. 

 
Not all medication works for every person and many medications take time to start working and longer to reach their full potential. 

 
LEAH:  
All medications have side effects, but not everyone gets them, and each person is affected differently. Some side effects improve or go away over time, some don’t happen straight away, and some may seem as bad as the illness being treated. 

 
RHIANNON:  
An individual may need to try a few different medications and different dosages before they find the right one. Medication used to treat depression for example, normally takes a few weeks to start having a beneficial effect. 

 
LEAH:  
In most cases an individual has a choice not to take a medication if they don’t want to, but it can be worth a try, to see if it can help and getting a parent or carer to give support and understand the treatment plan, gives it the best chance of success. 

 
RHIANNON:  
If an individual’s mental health problem is severe, it may be that they ‘have to’ take the medication to maintain good mental health.

 
If medication works and makes improvements to mental health, repeat prescriptions will be issued, but regular monitoring and occasional checkups will follow.
