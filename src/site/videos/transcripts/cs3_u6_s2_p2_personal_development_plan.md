**Abraham:** Now that you have conducted your personal skills analysis and assessed the skills you have against various roles in cyber security it is time to create a personal development plan, to help you decide what you need to do in order to achieve your goal of working in cyber security.

**Nicole:** To do this you should use SMART goals or targets. 

So, what exactly are these?

SMART is an acronym which stands for 

**Abraham:** Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Timebound.

**Nicole:** Specific means each goal or target should be clear and focussed on what you want to accomplish. Think about why it is important, who is involved, where will it happen what resources are needed! For example:

**Abraham:** ‘I want to be able to gain the experience and skills to become a team leader in cyber security within my organisation’

**Nicole:** Measurable is a way of tracking your progress and keeping motivated. It should ask questions like ‘how will I know that I am on track to do this?’ And ‘what will I need to do to help make it happen?’ Try setting a timeline for your progress.

**Abraham:** Achievable means that the goals and targets you set are realistic. By breaking down your goals into smaller steps can make them easier to achieve. Don’t forget we all have to walk before we try to run! Think about how long some of the skills you need may take to get and remember set goals that you can control and are not down to others.

**Nicole:** Relevant is about making sure that you goal means something to you, and it really matters, is worthwhile and achievable at this time.

**Abraham:** Time-bound is all about setting target dates giving you something to focus on and work towards. These should be realistic and are best broken down into smaller goals and remember don’t be too specific, if something is worth doing it’s worth doing well and shouldn’t be rushed. 

**Nicole:** And finally, it’s always worthwhile factoring in contingencies as we are all well aware of what can happen when we least expect it!
