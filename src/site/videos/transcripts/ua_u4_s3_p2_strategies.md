JOE: Understanding how an individual with Autism handles information processing, is a good way to work out how to find the right strategies to help the individual. Once their difficulties are identified then suitable strategies can be introduced to help with those difficulties.

BETH: Other people such as family members, teachers, employers, or friends may need to have a lot of involvement implementing the strategies, as they may be used in more than one place; such as at home, at school or college, or in work. It is, therefore, important that everyone involved, uses them consistently. 

Some individuals with autism may develop enough to be able to use the strategies independently however, some may always need support.

JOE: Visual references can be a good way of illustrating what to do and at what time, but they can also be helpful in showing the consequences of an action, such as missing an appointment or a deadline.

Sequence cards, timers and calendars can help with structuring the day at home, in education or work.

BETH: Education and work can cause great anxiety for everyone when homework, assignments and deadlines are involved. Imagine how much more problematic this is for individuals with autism, who may have little or no concept of time and consequences. 

JOE: They may need extra help with using strategies to organise their work, using lists, calendars and colour coding to emphasise importance and timing. Using different coloured files to identify and clarify importance, can help with deadlines and making a list and ticking off as things are completed in sequence, can help to show progress. 

BETH: Processing of instructions can be difficult, so it may be useful to use communication books, online learning environments, and voice recordings to reduce the pressure on the individual of trying to remember what they are supposed to be doing. Computer programmes, digital calendars and alerts can also be a reminder for appointments, deadlines and meetings and help to keep them on track.

JOE: Like most adults, many individuals with autism will need to manage their own money or bills, to varying extents. Children may have to learn to handle pocket money, giving them an allowance and getting them to save with a savings account, can help as they get older. Money management apps could be used to help show how saving works and the 
the consequences of not spending wisely should be stressed.
 
BETH: Some Individuals with autism may also need help in understanding what is the end goal of what they are working to, with all the continuous work and deadlines. Having that goal evidenced visually will help them to see this and focus on it going forward.
