**Leah:** Let’s have a look at Scrum in more detail

**Chris:** Scrum is an Agile methodology often called a framework to help teams to work together on a project. Scrum is quick and easy to adapt, it has continuous learning cycles, it enables fast feedback loops, has clear visibility and low risk, protects the team from un-prioritised work requests, and is focused on delivering value. 

**Leah:** The sprint is a fixed length event which has a sprint goal. All events are time-boxed so once a sprint begins, it is fixed. The sprint itself, involves a collection of ‘tasks’ for the team to achieve. Each task or ‘user story’ is allocated a level of complexity called a ‘story point’. A sprint project board which is also known as the ‘Sprint Backlog’ is used to track the tasks, and sub-tasks are positioned in three columns - ‘To do’, ‘In progress’, and ‘Done’. 

**Chris:** The Scrum Team [also known as the Scrum Development Team] organises and manages its own work. They deliver the agreed product increment inside a sprint. The team consists of different skillsets and abilities needed to deliver the product which would likely include programming, designing, testing, content writing, and/or business skills depending on the project.

**Leah:** Sprint planning is the first meeting of the sprint. It is where achievable items or user stories, are identified from the product backlog to be moved to the next sprint. The team would decide in this meeting, what can be done within the next sprint, and then refine the user stories; for example, they would determine story-points, how tasks will get done, the definition of done, dependencies, and sub-tasks. 

**Chris:** The Scrum Master manages the Scrum Process and remove impediments. They ensure that the framework is followed and would predominately manage the planning meeting taking into consideration priority, capacity, and the wider business expectations. 

However, before the planning meeting can take place, the Product Owner would need to determine the priority tasks and decide on the sprint goal. The Product Owner manages the Product Backlog and optimises the value of the Product. 

Team members also need to allocate their own tasks on the product ‘backlog’. 

**Leah:** The Daily Scrum, also known as the ‘Daily Standup’, is a 15-minute meeting for the internal scrum team, which is arranged at the same time, each working day of the sprint. Its main objective is to determine progress and update the project board, also known as the ‘sprint backlog’, and adapt the ‘product backlog’ if needed. The focus is on progress and actions, and self-management is encouraged, as well as promoting collaboration, improvement, and transparency amongst team members. 

**Chris:** The Sprint Review is where the scrum team present their results from the work they have done in the current sprint, to the stakeholders and/or the customers. [The Product Owner would have direct relationships with the Business Owner and the Stakeholders.] It is where feedback is given, and adaptations, alterations, changes and or improvements can be planned for. The sprint review is a working session, not just a presentation and the product backlog is adjusted to reflect new information and or feedback. It is usually one of the last events of the sprint. 

**Leah:** The Sprint Retrospective or retro is the last meeting and concludes the sprint. This is where the team determine what went well, what could be improved, what needs greater understanding, and the actions that need to be taken, moving forward.

The retro is a safe place where the team can come together, give support and productive feedback. The purpose of the retro is to act on what has been reviewed, and plan ways to increase quality and effectiveness. One of the biggest benefits of working in Agile is ‘continuous improvement’, and in Scrum the driver of improvement is the ‘Retrospective’.
