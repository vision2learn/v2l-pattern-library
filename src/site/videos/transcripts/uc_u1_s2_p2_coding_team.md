**Jack:** So, what are the roles in coding teams and software development

**Leah:** Project Managers are responsible for budgets, risk, scheduling and contract management. They might not know the product that they are creating that well, but they are in control overall.

**Jack:** Product Owners are more or less analysts and concentrate on a product and its features.

**Leah:** Team Lead is the role given to a developer with leadership qualities and who can maintain communication between remote development teams and clients.

**Jack:** Tech Lead has the greatest knowledge and can solve problems not linked with the development, they are also known as the 'architect'.

**Leah:** Full Stack Developer is a programmer not specialised in one area but has a broad range of skills and can implement a solution on every architectural layer.

**Jack:** Front End developers are the point of contact between the end client or user and the system being developed.

**Leah:** Back End Developer is the programmer focused mainly on developing logic and data layers.

**Jack:** QA Lead is responsible for building the quality assurance team and its management and is accountable for relations between QA team members.

**Leah:** QA engineer is responsible for preparing tools that allow for automating processes to verify software quality.

**Jack:** Testers are responsible for conducting manual tests doing system verification. They will try alternate paths to help eliminate any errors.

**Leah:** UI Designers are responsible for preparing, designing the user interface and transferring content, style, and graphics.

**Jack:** UX Designers are responsible for the users experience analysing user behaviour and changing human computer interaction methods.

**Leah:** There are two different approaches to coding teams and methods for conducting projects. One is a 'traditional' development team and the other is a 'scrum' team. 

A traditional development teams is based on a hierarchy between team roles, so there are leaders and managers. This allows the project manager control and it's always clear who is responsible for making decisions.

**Jack:** In a 'scrum' development team there isn't an organisational leader, it is self-managed. Team roles don't overlap, and team members decide work principles on their own, giving a degree of autonomy, which can help make the experience more enjoyable. However, even though a scrum team is self-managing, the structure in place, ensures, that there is a person responsible, the 'scrum master' for the right flow of the project and that everyone in the team is on track.

**Leah:** So why is teamwork important in coding?

When team members work together effectively towards a common goal like developing a product, this will provide the best experience for end users. Developers, quality assurance personnel and software testers should all work closely together as this can help to increase efficiency, enhance creativity as well as drive innovation. It can also help to get a product to market faster and prevent coding and other development mistakes. 

**Jack:** When team members feel comfortable expressing themselves, they can gain more confidence in expressing and introducing new ideas, concepts and approaches. This can encourage creativity which may result in innovations that may not have been thought of in the planning stage.

**Leah:** When teams don't work well together, team members will be more inclined to follow their own goals or show a lack of commitment to a project. The fear of conflict or avoidance of accountability may lead to trust issues and prevent team members from sharing ideas.

So, it's important for teams to work on their skills of communication and collaboration to support efficiency, creativity, and innovation.
