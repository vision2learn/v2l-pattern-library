LEAH: Let’s look at Media influences in a little more detail. 

Mental health and mental ill health are commonly portrayed in the media both negatively and positively. This can be through news articles both on TV and in newspapers, in films and tv programmes both fictional and non-fictional, on children's TV and also across digital and social media.

RHIANNON: If mental health is shown as a good news story with positive endorsements, for example, fund raising success, support and feel-good factors such as ‘comic relief’, this will influence people in a positive way and they will remember the good things.

LEAH: If mental health is shown negatively such as showing violence and bad behaviour in a news article, documentary or a storyline for a drama, this will have negative influences on attitudes.

RHIANNON: However, some media reporting, even though the content shows negative things, can have a positive effect as it raises everyone’s awareness. Such as cases of abuse for example, child neglect, human trafficking, child endangerment and child sexual exploitation.

LEAH: By this being shown widely in the media, it can help encourage more people to come forward with information and make more of the public open to discussion of these topics, ultimately raising the profile of mental health issues as a consequence.

RHIANNON: Journalists, script writers and other media professionals have to ensure fictional and factual portrayals of people with mental health problems in the media are accurate and sensitive. 

LEAH: More and more ‘bloggers' and ‘vloggers' are now responding to these portrayals, relaying their own experiences and raising awareness of the different attitudes they have personally encountered.

RHIANNON: Digital and social media is a big part of most young peoples lives and they can gain a lot of positivity from what is out there, however there is also a lot of negativity carried in these formats too.

LEAH: If what children and young people see online and how mental health issues are portrayed only leaves them angry, sad, worried, stressed, or annoyed, this can build up and start having a negative impact on their life and their own mental health. 

RHIANNON: For example, they may start worrying more about how they look or what they’re missing out on. A lot of people maintain an overly idealised online presence or world, which many others then fail to see as such, instead struggling to keep a positive view of their own lives by contrast.

LEAH: We cannot ignore the media as individuals are continuously consuming its output however we need to be aware that the way things are represented need to be realistic or for a specific storyline and not used in a derogatory or flippant manner just to make good TV or for sensationalism. 

RHIANNON: Mental health needs to be shown appropriately and should be relevant if society is to become more accepting.
