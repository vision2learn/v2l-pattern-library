Different organisations will have different policies and procedures, but there are some standard principles that tend to apply everywhere. As a Team Leader you should be fully aware of these to be effective in your role.

Disciplinary and Dismissal procedures.

When it comes to disciplinary and dismissal policies, your first responsibility as a team leader is to take the time to understand your own organisation’s policies. Talking to HR or your own manager is a great place to start. It’s really important you understand this because, as a team leader, you’re likely to be the first person involved in any disciplinary issues in your team. ACAS provides standardized policy and process templates, that lots of organisations use or refer to, in forming their policies, so you might want to take a look at them too.

Disciplinary procedures are in place to make sure that everyone respects each other and their employer’s rules and values, and that everyone’s work is to the right standard. Disciplinary procedures aim to solve problems and prevent them from happening again and shouldn’t be thought of as ways to punish people.

Dismissal procedures are there as a last resort. Generally, organisations don’t want to have to dismiss their people unless they have to, because it can cause a lot of disruption and emotional distress. But there are situations where dismissal is necessary and unavoidable.

Taking the Informal approach

When a disciplinary issue occurs, if possible, the best way to address it initially is informally. This saves everyone time and energy. So, if things aren’t going right for someone in your team, don’t rush to trigger the formal disciplinary process right away. You need to be cautious, patient, and understanding of everyone involved. 

Start by arranging to have a private conversation with the person or people involved. 

You shouldn’t go into this with any biases about who you think is in the right or in the wrong. That would affect your judgement and won’t help resolve the situation. 

Make sure you listen to everyone’s point of view neutrally. 

People will have different opinions about even simple disciplinary matters, so it’s really important that you listen to everyone and stay impartial. 

Aim to create an agreement about what changes or improvements need to be made. 

If the issue is to do with someone having an inappropriate attitude at work, you might take time to find out where this is coming from and what changes can be made to make this better. If the disciplinary issue is to do with performance, you might create a development plan for the person involved. The important thing is to focus on finding ways to improve the situation, not to pass down judgements and punishments.

Time for the Formal Process

When we say that it’s generally better to try and deal with disciplinary issues informally first, this doesn’t mean you should try to sweep difficult issues under the carpet. 

There are some issues, like bullying, harassment, discrimination, and other acts of gross misconduct, where the formal disciplinary process is the correct one to follow. This wouldn’t mean you’d abandon all of the elements of informally tackling the issue that we’ve described; it just means you’ve made an appropriate escalation according to your organisation’s policies.

You can see that as a leader it’s very important that you’re an expert in your own organisation’s procedures and policies because members of your team may come to you for advice. So, remember If you’re ever unsure of anything, make sure that you find it out before someone asks you. That will help you to be in a position to help your team, as well as knowing how to navigate these situations yourself.
