So why is it important to have product or service knowledge?

Well let’s think about this by using an example. Imagine you go to a computer shop to buy a new laptop. You want it to be able to be powerful enough for streaming and gaming, to have good battery life, a cutting-edge graphics card and a host of other up to date functions. But when you get to the shop and ask the staff about the products and which one best meets your specifications, none of them seem to be able to answer your questions, and they don’t have any real knowledge, only what’s written on the label!

In most cases you wouldn’t be very impressed and would choose to go elsewhere, where you can get that advice and service.

This example demonstrates how important it is that you have the appropriate knowledge of the product or services that you provide when dealing with people outside of your team.

So, who are those outside your team? These will probably be internal and external customers. 

• Internal customers are members of the same organization as you, who rely on the service or product being available for them to do their jobs, such as a salesperson who needs the product to be finished, so they can sell it. 

• An external customer is someone outside of your organization, who is interested in purchasing your product or service.

There may also be situations where superiors within your organization, regulators, or law enforcement may come to you, to find out more about your product or service. 

Knowledge of your product and services allows you to give a positive impression of your team. It shows that you and your team take what they do seriously. It shows that the team takes the time to understand what it is they’re doing and how it relates to people outside of the team too.

This makes the team look more professional, showing that they’re engaged with their work and are enthusiastic about it. This not only reflects well on how the team works, but it makes the product or service appear more desirable too. 

Imagine if you were eating in a restaurant and you asked what the specials were for that day, only for the waiter or waitress to just say, 

“Oh! I never bother to check the specials, they’re never that good…”

It would make it look like the team in that restaurant don’t care about what they’re doing, that they want to undermine each other, and it will probably make you unlikely to eat there again. 

Having good knowledge of your product or service also makes it more likely that internal and external customers will be able to get what they want. If the service or product they enquire about initially, isn’t suitable for them, wouldn’t it be great if you knew that you could supply one, that was a closer fit, to meet their needs? Maybe the product or service is unavailable, but with your help and advice an alternative can be found.

And remember, even if you don’t have anything suitable for them, the fact that you showed knowledge and passion for the product or service you provide, this will impress them and make them more likely to come to you in the future, if their needs change. 

It also demonstrates that your team is made up of people who are engaged with high morale, and who care about their job, team, and the product or service they provide. They want to make that experience for the customer both internal and external, a positive one and by having good knowledge you can help to do this.
