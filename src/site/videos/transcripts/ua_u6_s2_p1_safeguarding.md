BETH: The internet and mobile phones mean bullying can now happen during the day, when an individual is mixing with others and when they are in the safety of their own home. Online or cyber bullying can be via text messages, emails, online gaming, chat rooms, instant messaging and social networks. 

Some autistic young people may need extra support and guidance to stay safe online and educational settings have a duty and responsibility to protect individuals in their care.

JOE: If an individual is being bullied either at school or at work, then it’s best to talk to them and find out more, to see if help can be given. They should be calmly listened to making sure that they know they are believed and it’s not their fault. Some individuals with autism will find it easier to write about the incident or draw a picture about what happened, and they could be encouraged to keep a diary of events. 

BETH: The individual may not realise that they are being bullied and may need this explained to them, differentiating between being friendly or being a bully. They should be told why behaviour that hurts or harms them emotionally or physically, is not acceptable and is bullying.

JOE: An individual may show sudden changes in behaviour, which may be due to bullying. This might be increased anxiety, difficulty sleeping or outbursts at home. A child may mimic the acts of bullies, at home, by bullying their siblings because they don't understand that this behaviour is unacceptable, they are simply acting out what their peers are doing.
 
BETH: Sometimes an individual with autism may bully others. This is more often not intentional bullying, but a way of communicating and trying to get attention. It may be because they are being told to do it by others, exploiting their need to fit in, and they may be unaware of the consequences, and that they are hurting others. 

JOE: Discussing and showing them the consequences of their actions can help them to understand what they are doing is wrong and maybe linking it to what has been done to them can help to show this.

BETH: The long-term effects of bullying of individuals with autism can be serious and they can end up with long-lasting insecurities, behavioural issues and low self-esteem, and it can affect their concentration. They may become more inclined to refuse to take part in social situations because they are afraid of being bullied or they may experience stress-related illnesses. 

JOE: If the bullying becomes really serious and a potential criminal offence is committed, it may be necessary to contact the police. Harassment and stalking, threats of harm or violence to a person or property, evidence of sexual exploitation such as grooming and the distribution of sexual images or inappropriate sexual contact or behaviour are all criminal offences.

BETH: Addressing bullying issues as soon as they are recognised and taking the appropriate action, will help to reduce the effects of bullying and exploitation and hopefully reduce any long-term issues that could be experienced.
