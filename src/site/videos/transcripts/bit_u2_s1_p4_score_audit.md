A key method of maximizing the safety and efficiency of a workplace is to score and conduct a workplace audit. So what exactly is a workplace audit, and how does scoring work?

Let's take a look at this in more detail. A workplace organization, audit is a systematic evaluation of the cleanliness, organization, and efficiency of a workplace. The purpose of an audit is to identify areas for improvement, and to ensure that the workplace is operating safely and efficiently. So what are the steps involved in a workplace audit?

The first step in auditing a workplace organization is to develop a scoring system. This involves determining the criteria that will be used to evaluate the workplace. Once the criteria have been determined, you can then assign a numerical value to each criterion to create a scoring system. This can then be incorporated into an audit checklist, and the scores from each aspect are then combined to give an overall score for the workplace.

These scores are then used to determine the overall performance of the workplace and to identify areas for improvement. The scores can be compared to previous audits or to industry standards to determine whether the workplace is performing well or needs improvement. In the context of auditing and scoring workplace organization, the 5S methodology can be used as a tool for assessing the level of workplace organization and cleanliness.

Let's take a look at how this can be done. The first step is preparation. Review the 5S principles and gather any relevant documentation and data that relates to the area being audited, such as standard operating procedures, process maps, the number of accidents, the amount of waste generated, and the time it takes to complete tasks.

This data will be used to support the observations made during the visual inspection. Once you have all the relevant documentation, you can then begin the audit. The first 5S step in the audit is sort. This is where you evaluate the physical organization of the workplace, including the storage of items and the placement of equipment.

In this step, you are identifying what is needed and what is not needed. All of the items that you believe are not required should be read tagged and moved to a holding area for evaluation. After sorting, the next step is set in order. Put simply, this means arranging things so they are easy to find and use.

Once all the clutter has been removed, this step is about organizing the remaining items in a way that is intuitive and efficient. This may involve creating designated storage areas, labeling items, or establishing clear visual cues for where tools and materials should be kept. Once items are set in order, the next step is shine, which simply means to keep the workplace clean and organized with good maintenance.

In this step, the workspace is cleaned thoroughly and any remaining dirt or debris is removed. Regular cleaning should take place as it is important to maintain a safe and sanitary working environment. Step 4 of the audit process is standardize. This step is all about developing and implementing standard operating procedures to control and keep the workplace clean and organized all of the time.

This may involve creating checklists, documenting procedures, and training employees on the new processes. The fifth and final step in the audit process is sustain. This final step involves ensuring that the new processes and standards are maintained over time. This may involve regularly reviewing and auditing the workspace, providing ongoing training and support to employees, and continuously improving processes based on feedback and new ideas.
