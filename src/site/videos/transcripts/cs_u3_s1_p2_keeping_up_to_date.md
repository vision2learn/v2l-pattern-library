**Abraham:** In a time where almost half of UK residents have purchased at least one smart device since the start of the covid19 pandemic, the threat to users and their networks from cyber attacks has never been greater. 

Everyday products such as smart watches, TV's cameras and other smart appliances offer huge benefits but are vulnerable to cyber attack.

To counter this threat the Government is planning a new law to make sure that virtually all smart devices meet new security requirements. 

Some of the main points of the law will include.

• “Customers must be informed at the point of sale the duration of time for which a smart device will receive security software updates”

• A ban on manufacturers using universal default passwords, such as ‘password’ or ‘admin’, that are often pre-set in a device’s factory settings and are easily guessable

• Manufacturers will be required to provide a public point of contact to make it simpler for anyone to report a vulnerability.” 

Smartphones are also to be covered by the planned 'Secure by Design' legislation. Many people keep their phones for more than four years whilst some companies only offer security updates for just over 2 years. Security updates are important in protecting users against cyber criminals and tech firms need to be more up front about how long they support the security of devices.

Tech associations including the Internet of Secure Things [IoXT] which includes large companies like Google, Amazon and Facebook support this new legislation to boost the security of smart devices.
