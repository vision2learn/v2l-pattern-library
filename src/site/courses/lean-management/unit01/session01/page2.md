### What is lean organisation and why should you study it?

Lean is a term used to describe a wide range of business improvement techniques that help to eliminate waste and increase efficiency. While lean approaches were originally designed to improve quality and reduce production costs in the manufacturing industry, the concepts are now applied to a whole range of non-manufacturing processes such as offices or service industry environments.

As a result, anyone involved in business at any level should have some understanding of business improvement techniques. Lean production techniques and lean office principles can be generally applied to provide continuous improvements to quality, processes and costs across a wide range of sectors and industries. 

Even if you're seeking employment, knowledge of lean management and continuous improvement techniques will provide useful underpinning knowledge about business techniques, expanding your skill set which will prove attractive to employers.

It's important to set goals in your studies, as goal-setting gives you **control** over your learning. 

Research has proven that people who write their goals down have a greater **motivation** to achieve them. 

Setting yourself a specific **deadline** to achieve each goal also keeps you focused and increases your chance of success. 

* * *

Motivation is all about the **desire** and **drive** to achieve something. The sense of pride when you achieve something is one of the rewards of strong motivation, but first you must consider your reasons for pursuing this goal. 

An activity called Motivation Mapping™ can help you to do this. It provides a practical and visual way to record your goals and link them to what motivates you.

### Step 1: What and when

List what you want to achieve and when you want to achieve it. A useful acronym to use for this is SMART. You'll learn more about SMART goals in Lean Organisation contexts later in this course. ClickTap through the letters to see what SMART stands for: 

SMART

### _S_pecific

Give yourself exact and clear goals. For example, completing this course within the prepared timeframe to gain your Level 2 Certificate in Lean Organisation Management Techniques! 

### _M_easurable

Make sure you can measure how successful you are at achieving your goal. Your certificate is proof of your success. 

### _A_cceptable

Your goals must have reasonable and acceptable outcomes. For example, your Level 2 Certificate in Lean Organisation Management Techniques may lead on to you completing further business management related qualifications. 

### _R_ealistic

Is your goal achievable? If you're realistic about your strengths and weaknesses, you can set goals that play to your strengths. For example, if you're better at studying in the evening, plan this in. 

### _T_imed

You need to have a timescale within which you want to achieve your goal. For example, you have an Action Plan to help you submit your Unit Assessments on time. If you need a reminder of these, check your Action Plan in your My Study area now! Why not write it on your Motivation Map? 

### Step 2: Why those goals?

When filling out the **Why** section of your map, you can follow these steps:

  1. List the reasons **why** you want to achieve your goal.
  2. Add as many reasons **why** as possible. (The more strong reasons you have for wanting to achieve something, the higher your motivation will be.)
  3. These reasons can be practical, for example "So I can go to college with good grades," or personal, for example "So I can build confidence in my knowledge of effective business strategies and techniques."

### Step 3: How to achieve your goals

When filling out the **How** section of your map, you can follow these steps:

  1. List all of the practical actions that you will take to show **how** you will achieve your goals.
  2. It is good to set high standards, but be **realistic** about what you will do. For example, no-one can revise for 10 hours every day!

The **How** section of your Motivation Map™ may also include two or three **milestones**. These are important targets that you need to reach on the way to achieving your goal, such as completing an important piece of coursework by a certain date.
